Yes, they can enter. Announcement No. 110 admits fresh jackfruits from Cambodia: the Cambodian side examines orchards and packing houses, the Chinese side approves and registers them and publishes the list; growing is monitored, each fruit washed and brushed, and two per cent of every lot sampled in the first year after the Protocol takes effect.

For a consignment, check the Chinese registration numbers and Cambodian monitoring records; after two years without problems the sampling rate falls to one per cent; mark each box and pallet for the People's Republic of China, attach the Cambodian phytosanitary certificate and put the full protocol sentence in the declaration. A number given only by word of mouth does not count; the list and certificate govern.

At the Chinese port of entry, China Customs checks the permit, certificate and marks. Fruit from unregistered orchards or packing houses is denied entry; lots with live pests, soil or plant residues are returned, destroyed or treated; lots failing food safety standards are returned or destroyed. The country and lot are only an illustration; the list and certificate decide.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries of the Kingdom of Cambodia (hereinafter the Cambodian side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh jackfruits from Cambodia to China, fresh jackfruits from Cambodia meeting the following requirements are permitted for import into China from the date of this Announcement:

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) The Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) The Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(c) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(d) The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruit;

(e) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries of the Kingdom of Cambodia on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Cambodia to China.

II. Permitted Commodity

Fresh jackfruits (hereinafter referred to as jackfruits), scientific name Artocarpus heterophyllus, English name Jackfruit.

III. Permitted Production Area

Jackfruit production areas in Cambodia.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses exporting jackfruits to China shall be examined by the Cambodian side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information, including names, addresses and registration numbers, shall allow accurate tracing when a consignment does not comply with this Announcement. Before each export season, the Cambodian side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side which, after review and approval, shall publish it on the GACC website and update it regularly.

V. Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Lepelley mealybug Dysmicoccus lepelleyi

(2) Pineapple mealybug Dysmicoccus neobrevipes

(3) Sugarcane cluster mealybug Exallomochlus hispidus

(4) Intercepted mealybug Paracoccus interceptus

(5) Coffee mealybug Planococcus lilacinus

(6) Pacific mealybug Planococcus minor

VI. Pre-shipment Requirements

(a) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting jackfruits to China shall, under Cambodian supervision, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, apply good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain orchard hygiene by keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting fruit production and promptly removing fallen and rotten fruit, and apply integrated pest management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring surveys, physical, chemical or biological control and cultural practices, so as to avoid or minimise the occurrence of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side on jackfruits.

(2) The Cambodian side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organise orchard monitoring. For monitoring and control of pests, in addition to visual inspection, a range of physical or chemical control methods shall be used.

(3) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel, who shall have received training from the Cambodian side or its authorised institutions.

(4) The Cambodian side shall retain orchard pest monitoring and control records and provide them to the Chinese side on request. The control records shall include the names of chemical agents used during the growing season, their active ingredients, application rates and dates.

(b) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of jackfruits exported to China shall take place under the quarantine supervision of the Cambodian side or its authorised officers.

(2) Packing houses for jackfruits exported to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors and separate areas for raw material and finished products.

(3) The storage, processing and handling areas for jackfruits exported to China shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and shall be separated from living quarters by appropriate measures and distance.

(4) During packaging, jackfruits exported to China shall be manually selected, graded and cleaned to remove diseased, insect-infested, rotten and misshapen fruit, twigs and leaves or other plant residues and soil. High-pressure water guns or water guns shall be used to blow, spray and brush the fruit surface, and where necessary a soft clean cotton cloth shall be used to wipe the surface by hand, so as to remove insects, eggs and pathogen spores attached to the fruit.

(5) Packaged jackfruits requiring storage shall be placed in a warehouse immediately and stored separately to avoid re-infestation by pests.

(6) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system so that jackfruits exported to China can be traced to registered orchards, recording the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the quantity, the export date, the export quantity, the country of destination and the container number.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging material shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall meet the relevant Chinese plant quarantine and hygiene requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall bear, in Chinese or in English, traceable information such as the name and variety of the fruit, the exporting country, the place of origin (province and city), and the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packing house. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or in English with "Destined for the People's Republic of China" or "Exported to the People's Republic of China".

(3) Containers holding jackfruits exported to China shall be checked at loading for satisfactory hygienic conditions. The containers shall be sealed, and the seal shall remain intact on arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(d) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) During the first year after the Protocol takes effect, the Cambodian side shall sample 2% of every lot of jackfruits exported to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling rate shall be reduced to 1%.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, twigs and leaves, or soil are found, the whole lot shall not be exported to China and, depending on the circumstances, exports of jackfruits to China from the orchard or packing house concerned may be suspended for the remainder of the export season. The Cambodian side shall investigate the cause and take corrective measures, and shall retain the detection records and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(e) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

For jackfruits that pass inspection, the Cambodian side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packing house and carrying the following additional declaration: "This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Cambodia to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China." (this consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Cambodia to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side).

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Treatment of Non-compliance

On arrival at a Chinese port of entry, jackfruits exported to China shall be subject to inspection and quarantine by China Customs in accordance with the following requirements.

(a) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verification that an Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit has been obtained for the imported jackfruits.

(2) Verification that the phytosanitary certificate complies with paragraph (e) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verification that the marks on the boxes or wooden packaging comply with paragraph (c) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(b) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Jackfruits exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruit.

(2) On arrival at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall examine the documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this Announcement. Lots passing inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(c) Treatment of non-compliance.

(1) Where jackfruits are found to come from unregistered orchards or packing houses, the lot shall be denied entry.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or other quarantine pests newly occurring in Cambodia, or soil or plant residues, are found, the lot shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(3) Where jackfruits fail to comply with Chinese national food safety laws, regulations and standards, the lot shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

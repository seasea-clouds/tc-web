From 15 December 2026 imports are allowed. GACC Announcement No. 147 of 2026: origin = African countries with diplomatic ties to China; goods = unroasted cocoa (Theobroma cacao L.) beans, fermented, dried, screened; São Tomé and Príncipe under Announcement No. 120 of 2024.

Ghana lot, full process: plant reviewed by the exporting-country authority, approved/registered by GACC; monitoring records 2 years; wood packaging per ISPM 15; boxes/pallets marked “For export to the People's Republic of China”; container sealed; first two years each lot sampled at min 2%, cut to min 1% if no problem arises; certificate additional declaration in original English; sample filed first.

List or sample missing? Hold your lot; live organisms, soil or residues mean return, destruction or disinfestation, and exports to China may be suspended. Country and month only illustrate the process; registered list, contract and effective date prevail.

<!-- original -->

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and following a risk assessment, cocoa beans from African countries having diplomatic relations with China that meet the following requirements are permitted to be imported.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(i) the Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China; (ii) the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its Implementing Regulations; (iii) the Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its Implementing Regulations.

II. Name of the Commodity Permitted for Import

“Cocoa beans (Cocoa Beans)” in this Announcement means the unroasted cocoa (Theobroma cacao L.) beans grown and processed within the territory of the exporting country, from which the pulp and the pods are removed and which are made through processes such as fermentation, drying and screening.

III. Origin

African countries having diplomatic relations with China.

IV. Enterprise Registration

The production and processing enterprises exporting cocoa beans to China shall be reviewed by the competent authority of the exporting country (hereinafter “the official authority of the exporting country”) and approved and registered by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter “the Chinese side”). The registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so that cocoa beans exported to China can be accurately traced when they do not comply with the relevant provisions of this Announcement. Before trade begins, the official authority of the exporting country shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side. Upon review and approval by the Chinese side, the Chinese side will publish it on the website of the General Administration of Customs.

V. Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Groundnut bruchid, Caryedon serratus (2) Cocoa flower gall fungus, Nectria rigidiuscula (3) Cocoa black pod fungus, Phytophthora megakarya (4) Black root rot fungus, Dematophora bunodes (5) Phellinus brown root rot fungus, Phellinus noxius (6) Rubber white root rot fungus, Rigidoporus lignosus

VI. Pre-shipment Requirements

(i) Planting management.

(1) Planting enterprises shall, under the supervision of the official authority of the exporting country, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, apply Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), maintain planting hygiene conditions such as no sources of pollution nearby and timely removal of diseased plant residues, and implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and control measures such as agricultural practices.

(2) Planting enterprises shall, under the supervision of the official authority of the exporting country, carry out pest monitoring and integrated control management for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side. Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technicians. The technicians shall receive training from the official authority of the exporting country or its authorized institution. Planting enterprises shall keep pest monitoring and control records for at least 2 years and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. The pest monitoring and control records shall include at least detailed information such as the time of monitoring, the names of the pests found, the control measures taken, and the date of use, active ingredient and concentration of the chemicals used. Pesticides prohibited by the Chinese side shall not be used in pest control work.

(ii) Processing management.

(1) Production and processing enterprises shall establish a traceability system to ensure that cocoa beans exported to China can be traced back to the planting base. (2) Processing, packaging, storage and shipment shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the official authority of the exporting country or its authorized personnel; the relevant areas shall be relatively independent and reasonably laid out, clean and hygienic, with hardened floors, and separated from living areas at an appropriate distance. (3) During processing, procedures such as manual sorting, screening, cleaning and removal of impurities shall be applied so as to ensure that no mouldy beans, beans damaged by insects, live insects, insect eggs, soil, weed seeds, plant residues, gravel or other impurities are present.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Raw and finished cocoa beans shall be stored separately to prevent contamination. Cocoa beans shall be packed in clean, hygienic, new materials that meet phytosanitary requirements. If wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15). (2) Each packaging box shall be marked in Chinese or English with the product name, country, place of origin, and traceability information such as the name or registration number of the production and processing enterprise. Each packaging box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “For export to the People's Republic of China” or “Exported to the People's Republic of China”. (3) The means of transport or container carrying cocoa beans exported to China must be inspected at the time of shipment to verify good hygienic conditions. The means of transport or container shall be sealed, and the seal shall be intact upon arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(iv) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Before export, the official authority of the exporting country shall carry out inspection and quarantine of cocoa beans exported to China. Cocoa beans that pass inspection and quarantine by the official authority of the exporting country and are confirmed to meet the requirements of this Announcement are permitted to be exported to China. (2) During the first two years from the start of trade, the official authority of the exporting country shall sample each lot of cocoa beans exported to China at a rate of not less than 2%. If no phytosanitary problem occurs within the two years, the sampling rate may be adjusted to not less than 1%. (3) If quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, plant residues or soil are found, the entire lot shall not be exported to China. The official authority of the exporting country shall identify the causes and take improvement measures. At the same time, records of the findings shall be kept and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(v) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For cocoa beans that pass quarantine, the official authority of the exporting country shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the name or registration number of the production and processing enterprise, and shall fill in the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Cocoa Beans from Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (This consignment complies with the phytosanitary requirements for the export of cocoa beans from Africa to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.)

For lots in which live pests are found, fumigation and other disinfestation treatments shall be carried out before export, and the phytosanitary certificate shall state information such as the treatment method, name of the chemical, dose, time and temperature. (2) Before trade begins, the official authority of the exporting country shall provide a sample phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side for confirmation and filing.

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine and Handling of Non-compliance

When cocoa beans exported to China arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Verification of relevant certificates and markings.

(1) Verify whether the accompanying phytosanitary certificate is authentic and valid. (2) Verify whether the markings on the packaging boxes and pallets comply with Item (iii) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(ii) Inspection of the goods.

In accordance with the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules, imported cocoa beans shall undergo inspection and quarantine, and those that pass inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(iii) Handling of non-compliance.

(1) If the lot is found to come from a production and processing enterprise that is not registered, the lot shall be returned or destroyed. (2) If no valid phytosanitary certificate is found attached, the lot shall be returned or destroyed. (3) If live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or other quarantine pests, or plant residues or soil, are found, the lot shall be returned, destroyed or subject to phytosanitary treatment. (4) If the lot is found not to comply with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards, the lot shall be returned or destroyed. (5) If other cases of non-compliance with Chinese entry quarantine requirements are found, they shall be handled in accordance with the relevant laws and regulations.

If the above non-compliance is found, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the official authority of the exporting country and, depending on the circumstances, suspend imports of cocoa beans from the production and processing enterprise concerned. The official authority of the exporting country shall identify the causes and take improvement measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side shall decide whether to lift the suspension on the basis of the rectification results of the official authority of the exporting country.

VIII. Other Requirements

Cocoa beans from São Tomé and Príncipe have already been granted quarantine access to China, and the relevant import phytosanitary requirements shall be implemented in accordance with Announcement No. 120 of 2024 of the General Administration of Customs.

This Announcement shall enter into force on 15 December 2026.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

15 October 2026

Yes, they can enter. GACC Announcement No. 107 opens the door to fresh jackfruit from Bangladesh with effect from today: orchards and packinghouses must be reviewed by the Bangladeshi side and approved and registered by the Chinese side, and the Chinese side publishes the list before each export season.

For a consignment, check the orchard and packinghouse registration numbers and monitoring records: fruit is bagged during growth and unbagged at the packinghouse; 2% of plants are sampled every 2 weeks from flowering to harvest; fungi are monitored at least 1 time a month; every fruit is blown or brushed clean; each box and pallet is marked in Chinese or English; plus a phytosanitary certificate and the protocol compliance statement.

At the Chinese port of entry, China Customs verifies the permit, certificate and markings and carries out inspection and quarantine. Fruit from unregistered orchards or packinghouses is refused entry; live quarantine pests of concern to China, soil or plant debris lead to return, destruction or quarantine treatment; and consignments failing Chinese food safety standards are returned or destroyed.

<!-- original -->

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Bangladesh to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture of the People's Republic of Bangladesh (hereinafter the Bangladeshi side), fresh jackfruit from Bangladesh meeting the following requirements is permitted for import into China with effect from today.

I. Legal Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(c) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(d) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(e) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Bangladesh to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture of the People's Republic of Bangladesh.

II. Name of Commodity Permitted for Import

Fresh jackfruit (hereinafter jackfruit), scientific name Artocarpus heterophyllus, English name Jackfruit.

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Jackfruit producing areas in Bangladesh.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packinghouses

Orchards and packinghouses exporting jackfruit to China shall be reviewed by the Bangladeshi side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information shall include the name, address and registration number, so that a consignment failing to meet this Announcement can be traced accurately. Before each export season, the Bangladeshi side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side; once reviewed and approved by the Chinese side, it shall be published on the GACC website and updated regularly.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Carambola fruit fly Bactrocera carambolae

(2) Breadfruit fruit fly Bactrocera umbrosa

(3) Cashew bark borer Indarbela tetraonis

(4) Jackfruit shoot borer Glyphodes caesalis

(5) Mango shield scale Milviscutulus mangiferae

(6) Jackfruit bud weevil Ochyromera artocarp

(7) Pacific mealybug Planococcus minor

(8) Phyllosticta leaf spot Phyllosticta artocarpina

(9) Rhizopus soft rot Rhizopus artocarpi

(10) Jackfruit rust Uredo artocarpi

VI. Pre-export Management

(a) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting jackfruit to China shall, under the supervision of the Bangladeshi side, establish a sound quality management and traceability system, apply Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), maintain orchard hygiene such as keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting fruit production and promptly removing fallen and rotten fruit, and implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring and surveys and physical, chemical or biological control as well as cultural practices.

(2) The Bangladeshi side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to China, organise orchard monitoring throughout the year, and bag the jackfruit during fruit growth until the bags are removed at the packinghouse after harvest (see the Annex for bagging specifications).

(3) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the supervision and guidance of technical personnel of the Bangladeshi side. Such personnel shall be trained by the Bangladeshi side or its authorised institution.

(4) Orchards and packinghouses exporting jackfruit to China shall retain records of pest monitoring, control and bagging and provide them to the Chinese side on request. Control records shall include the names of chemical agents used during the growing season, their active ingredients, application rates and timing.

(5) For Bactrocera carambolae and Bactrocera umbrosa, orchards and packinghouses exporting jackfruit to China shall, under the supervision and guidance of the Bangladeshi side, monitor and control them using methyl eugenol and protein hydrolysate baits and other suitable methods, and shall provide the Chinese side with the relevant procedures and results of those plans on request.

(6) For Indarbela tetraonis, Glyphodes caesalis, Milviscutulus mangiferae, Ochyromera artocarp and Planococcus minor, from flowering to harvest 2% of plants in each orchard shall be sampled at random and monitored every 2 weeks, focusing on branches, stems, leaves, flowers and fruit. If pests or their symptoms are found during monitoring, control measures including chemical or biological control shall be taken promptly to keep pest occurrence under control or at a low prevalence level.

(7) For Phyllosticta artocarpina, Rhizopus artocarpi and Uredo artocarpi, monitoring shall be carried out at least once a month from bud break to harvest, focusing on stems, leaves and fruit. If suspicious symptoms are found, samples shall be taken and sent to a laboratory for testing. If the pathogen is detected, the orchard shall take control measures.

(b) Packinghouse management.

(1) Processing, packaging, storage and shipment of jackfruit exported to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Bangladeshi side or its authorised personnel. Packinghouses shall be kept in good sanitary condition and have measures to prevent reinfestation by pests, such as insect-proof screens.

(2) Jackfruit exported to China shall be packed at registered packinghouses. During processing and packing, diseased or rotten fruit shall be removed and the fruit washed and graded so that it is free of insects, mites, rotten fruit, plant debris and soil. An air gun or brush shall be used to clean the surface of every fruit one by one to remove scale insects and other insects; packinghouse inspectors shall also examine every fruit and remove any showing surface cracks, damage, softening, pest infection or decay.

(3) Packinghouses shall establish a traceability system so that jackfruit exported to China can be traced to the registered orchard and packinghouse, recording the processing and packing date, source orchard, name or registration number of the packinghouse, quantity, export date, export quantity, destination country and container number.

(4) Packed jackfruit that needs to be stored shall be placed in storage immediately and kept separately to avoid reinfestation by pests.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused and shall meet the relevant Chinese phytosanitary requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each box shall be labelled in Chinese or English with the name of the fruit, the country, the place of origin (province, city or district), and the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packinghouse. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with "To be exported to the People's Republic of China" or "Exported to the People's Republic of China".

(3) Containers holding jackfruit exported to China shall be checked for good sanitary condition at the time of loading. A seal shall be applied, and the seal shall be intact on arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(d) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

If live quarantine pests of concern to China, branches, leaves or soil are found, the consignment shall not be exported to China. The Bangladeshi side shall identify the cause and take corrective measures, keep records of the findings and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(e) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For jackfruit that passes quarantine inspection, the Bangladeshi side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packinghouse, and shall state in the additional declaration: "This consignment complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Bangladesh to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China." (That is, this consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) Before formal trade begins, the Bangladeshi side shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and record.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Handling of Non-conforming Consignments

When jackfruit exported to China arrives at the port of entry, China Customs shall verify the relevant documents and markings and carry out inspection and quarantine.

(a) Verification of certificates and markings.

(1) Verify whether an Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit has been obtained for the imported jackfruit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Chapter VI (e) of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the markings on the boxes or wooden packaging comply with Chapter VI (c) of this Announcement.

(b) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Jackfruit exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for fruit imports.

(2) On arrival at the Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this Announcement. Consignments that pass shall be permitted entry.

(c) Handling of non-conforming consignments.

(1) Jackfruit from orchards or packinghouses that have not been approved shall be refused entry.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China or newly occurring quarantine pests in Bangladesh, soil or plant debris are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to quarantine treatment.

(3) If the consignment fails to meet Chinese food safety laws, regulations or national standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

This Announcement is hereby made.

Colostrum powder comes into China on 1210, the bonded e-commerce channel; 9610, direct purchase shipping, is not among the routes open to it. Two public documents settle the matter. Classification decision W2020-29, published in GACC Announcement No. 108 of 2020, places the product under tariff code 0404.9000. Item 5 of the adjustment table annexed to Announcement No. 7 of 2022 by the Ministry of Finance and seven other departments writes a remark into that row: bonded online retail goods only. A third column sits on the quarantine side. The same can of powder is declared for entry inspection and quarantine under 0404900000201, filled in separately from the tariff code used on the declaration.

A can of powder passes through exactly these columns between arrival and listing. When someone tells you that this category has always been able to go by direct mail, that all dairy needs quarantine approval, or that an official health certificate is mandatory, there is one way to test the claim: ask for the tariff code, the wording of the list row, and the quarantine number, then read what the announcements actually say. All of these documents are on government websites, and whoever opens them receives the same text.

## Which columns get checked first when your goods arrive

The commodity number on a declaration comes from classification. Only once the tariff code is settled can you find its row in the positive list and read whether any condition is attached to it. Eligibility for a channel comes from that row, not from a logistics proposal. The inspection and quarantine number is a different field: it belongs in the entry quarantine column, exists alongside the commodity number, and neither can be copied into the other.

Each of the three is decided by a different document, sits in a different place, and none of them substitutes for another. Most disputes arise at the first one. Which tariff code the goods fall under decides which row of the list applies, whether that row carries a remark, and which quarantine treatment follows. The other two are largely lookups, on the condition that the first was done correctly.

## On what basis customs says it is not milk

Headings 0401, 0402 and 0403 cover milk and milk products. Colostrum powder is not among them, and classification decision W2020-29, published in GACC Announcement No. 108 of 2020, gives the answer: the described goods are powder made from bovine colostrum taken inside 24 hours after calving, and the conclusion places them in subheading 0404.90, that is, products containing natural milk not elsewhere specified. The announcement took effect on 1 October 2020.

Decisions of this kind bind generally. Article 21 of the Regulations on the Classification of Import and Export Goods of the Customs (GACC Order No. 252) states that the General Administration of Customs may make classification decisions with universal binding force on import and export goods and publish them, and that identical goods shall be subject to the same classification decision. For the same product in the same form, every port and every customs broker works from the same text, and the answer has nothing to do with who is quoting.

Pinning a tariff code to a published decision buys something specific: you leave the border no opening for a follow-up question. Goods whose classification cannot be explained are the ones that end up detained or shipped back, sitting in a bonded warehouse with their paperwork open in the system, unable to move in either direction. Where a decision can be cited, one such event is simply removed.

One thing should be said up front: a decision answers to the form described in it. A compounded powder whose ingredient list adds vitamins, minerals or other substances is not the same commercial item as powder made from colostrum, and its tariff code may fall under a different heading. A single decision does not cover a product line. Each SKU is judged on its own.

Published does not mean permanently valid either. Article 22 of the same regulation: where the laws, administrative regulations or other relevant provisions on which a classification decision was based change, the decision lapses at the same time, and the lapse is to be published by the General Administration of Customs. Articles 23 and 24 handle amendment and revocation initiated by the administration. The validity of the decision in your hand depends on whether its underlying basis has moved, and that is something you have to go back and check.

## Why a listed tariff code still does not allow direct mail

The 2022 version of the positive list of cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods took effect on 1 March 2022 and covers 1,476 tariff codes, among them 0404.9000. Whether a code is on the list decides eligibility. What is written on the row where the code sits decides which route you may take.

The key is in the annexed adjustment table. Item 5 gives 0404.9000 the remark bonded online retail goods only, and other dairy codes in the same table carry the same constraint. The 2019 version of the list had no remark on this row; the 2022 version added one, and that is a tightening. Bonded online retail corresponds to supervision code 1210, direct purchase to 9610, and this remark folds what looked like two routes into one.

A remark says nothing about rates. It answers only which channel is open. So the reasoning that a clear tax calculation buys you a choice of channel does not hold. The opposite claim, that this category can also go by direct mail, does not need an argument either: go back to that row of the annex, and a few words end the conversation. The list will be adjusted again, so before each stocking round check the current version and the wording of the remark rather than building a long-term plan on an old list.

## What goes in the inspection and quarantine column

Colostrum powder under 0404.9000 has a separate number for entry inspection and quarantine: 0404900000201. It is formed by appending an inspection and quarantine suffix after the commodity number, and at declaration it occupies its own column beside the tariff code. Copying the tariff code into the quarantine column, or assuming the quarantine column will follow the tariff code automatically, is the usual consequence of collapsing two systems into one field.

This number corresponds to dairy, and industry practice sets immunoglobulin (IgG) content at 10% or above. To be precise about it: 10% is a figure from industry and testing practice, not a number written in the announcement, and your test report has to cover it. When the classification decision, the tariff code, the quarantine number and the test report all point at the same item, a question at the border is answered along one line rather than four.

The boundary of quarantine approval is also drawn too wide in conversation. GACC Announcement No. 114 of 2021 sets out inspection and quarantine requirements for imported dairy and limits the dairy that requires entry quarantine approval to raw milk, raw milk products, pasteurised milk, and modified milk produced by pasteurisation processes. Colostrum powder is a milk powder made with heat treatment and is not among them. The same announcement notes that the commodity codes listed are only those involved for these categories, and whether a particular product needs approval still depends on its actual process. A generalisation such as all dairy entering China needs quarantine approval therefore does not survive; the judgment has to land on the specific category and the specific process.

## What a wrong code costs on a 50-dollar can

Under the current tax policy for cross-border e-commerce retail imports, within the limits the customs duty is provisionally 0% and import-stage value added tax is levied at 70% of the statutory amount payable. The 13% band therefore becomes 9.1%. A 50-dollar can converts at 7.2 yuan per US dollar to 360 yuan, and consolidated tax comes to about 32.8 yuan, roughly 4.5 US dollars. The per-transaction limit is 5,000 yuan and the annual limit 26,000 yuan, about 700 US dollars and 3,600 US dollars at the same rate, for reference only. Quota accumulates per purchaser and is deducted by customs at clearance.

A correct tariff code saves you nothing here, because that tax was owed in any case. A wrong code saves nothing either and adds two exposures. Article 15 of the Regulations on the Implementation of Customs Administrative Penalties (State Council Order No. 420) sets the bands by the nature of the declared item: where the product name, tariff number, quantity, specification, price, trade mode, origin, place of dispatch, place of arrival, final destination or any other item requiring declaration is undeclared or falsely declared, and national tax collection is affected, a fine of not less than 30% and not more than twice the underpaid tax applies; where licence and certificate administration is affected, a fine of 5% to 30% of the value of the goods; any illegal gain is confiscated.

Where customs finds a classification inaccurate on its own initiative, it redetermines the classification under Article 18 of the classification regulation and handles the matter under the rules on amending and cancelling declarations. If you ask for release of the goods before the classification is settled, Article 19 requires you to provide security. Where classification gives rise to a refund, a supplementary collection, a recovery of tax or late-payment surcharges, Article 25 refers those to the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules of the General Administration of Customs. For goods already in the warehouse, in transit or on the shelf, several of these can arrive at once.

Splitting orders, renaming products, and swapping out declared items are all moves to "get around the limits", and they touch precisely the contents that Article 15 lists one by one. Someone who can tell you how a border office operates is not telling you what is lawful.

## Whether it is worth applying for an advance ruling first

An advance ruling is a decision that customs is asked to make beforehand, before goods actually move, on matters including classification. The basis is the Interim Measures for the Administration of Advance Rulings of the Customs (GACC Order No. 236, promulgated on 26 December 2017, effective 1 February 2018, amended by GACC Orders No. 262 and No. 273). Article 3(1) places the commodity classification of import and export goods among the matters for which an application may be filed.

Who may apply, and when, is where people miss. Article 4 requires the applicant to be a foreign trade operator connected with actual import and export activity and registered with customs; customs agencies and logistics providers fall outside that scope. Article 7 sets the timing at three months before the intended import or export, filed with the direct customs office of registration. If the thought occurs when the goods are already at sea, the window has closed.

Customs issues the advance ruling decision within 60 days from acceptance, and time spent on chemical testing, examination, expert review or other means of establishing the facts does not count against that period. A decision is valid for three years. Where the laws, administrative regulations, customs rules or relevant announcements of the General Administration on which it rests change in a way that affects its force, it lapses automatically, and the same matter may not be applied for twice within the validity period. A decision has no retroactive effect on goods that already moved before it took effect, so it cannot rescue shipments that have gone.

What people hit most often is acceptance. Article 9 provides that where customs rules or an announcement of the General Administration has already laid down clear provisions on the matter applied for, customs shall decide not to accept the application. A category that already has a published classification decision sits squarely inside that sentence. Colostrum powder needs no advance ruling and would not get one: the answer is already in the announcement, and a further request returns only a notice of non-acceptance.

So when is one worth applying for? When no classification decision corresponds to the product, when the tariff text and the explanatory notes support more than one code, or when the formula or the process is new, with compounded products being the typical case. In those situations, obtaining a decision before you stock is far cheaper than being asked to redetermine the classification with the goods standing at the port.

## When to run the classification judgment again

W2020-29 is already in force, and the three lines each sit locked and consistent with one another, so this case needs no fresh judgment. What does need one is a new product or a change of ingredients.

External documents also move. A new version of the list, a revision to the remark on that row of the adjustment table, a new classification decision published or a current one amended or revoked, or a change to the higher-level provisions behind a decision: on any of these the original judgment has to be redone. The first few you see by reading announcements, the last one by reading your own product file. Dates on classification material exist precisely so that you know when it is due for review.

Specification changes are routinely underweighted. Moving the same product from a 300-gram can to stick packs usually leaves classification alone, while the dutiable value and the limit calculations have to be recomputed and the correspondence between quarantine material and labelling re-checked. The tariff code is the most stable of these columns. The others move with price and form.

## What the page you hand to customs should say

A judgment has to land on paper, and the file should answer a border question on its own:

- Product description and form: ingredients, processing steps, the window for collecting colostrum after calving, dosage form and pack size, compared word by word with the description in the classification decision, with reasons written for why it applies or does not.
- The basis cited: classification decision W2020-29, GACC Announcement No. 108 of 2020 which published it, and that announcement's effective date.
- The list row and the wording of its remark: the current version of the list, the row holding 0404.9000, the remark copied verbatim, sourced to item 5 of the adjustment table annexed to Announcement No. 7 of 2022.
- The quarantine treatment: entry inspection and quarantine code 0404900000201, and the judgment on whether it falls among the dairy requiring entry quarantine approval listed in Announcement No. 114 of 2021, with the basis for that conclusion.

Platform review, questions from customs and an internal sign-off all read the same page, and the figures and document numbers have to match from one to the next. Every one of these bases comes from a public government document, and the numbers can be traced back to the original text one by one. That is why the judgment holds up when someone asks.

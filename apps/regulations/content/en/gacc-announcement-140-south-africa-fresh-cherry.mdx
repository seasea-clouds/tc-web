On (21) September 2026, GACC issued Announcement No. 140, permitting imports of South African cherries meeting phytosanitary requirements. Orchards, packinghouses and treatment facilities must be audited by South Africa and jointly approved and registered by both sides.

The announcement lists 6 quarantine pests including Mediterranean fruit fly: 2 traps per km2 checked weekly, control required if FTD > 0.7; pre-export cold treatment at three regimes (1.11 °C/14 d to 2.22 °C/18 d) or methyl bromide fumigation, with 2% sampling.

The phytosanitary certificate must state orchard and packinghouse registration numbers, container number and the English additional declaration; pre-export fumigation must also list methyl bromide dose, duration and temperature. Non-compliant consignments face on-arrival cold treatment, return, destruction or disinfestation.

Pursuant to the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Department of Agriculture of the Republic of South Africa (hereinafter the South African side) on the phytosanitary requirements for export of fresh cherries from South Africa to China, with immediate effect, fresh cherries from South Africa meeting the following requirements are permitted importation.

I. Phytosanitary Basis

(i) Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Cherries from South Africa to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Department of Agriculture of the Republic of South Africa.

II. Commodities Permitted for Import

Fresh sweet cherries from South Africa (hereinafter cherries), scientific name Prunus avium.

III. Permitted Production Areas

Cherry-producing areas in South Africa.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packinghouses

Orchards, packinghouses and treatment facilities for cherries exported to China shall be audited by the South African side and jointly approved and registered by the Chinese and South African sides. Registration information shall include the name, address and registration code to enable accurate traceability where exported consignments fail to comply with this Announcement. Before each export season, the South African side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side; upon review and approval by the Chinese side, it shall be published on the GACC website and updated regularly.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(2) Quilici fruit fly Ceratitis quilicii

(3) Natal fruit fly Ceratitis rosa

(4) Fig wax scale Ceroplastes rusci

(5) Obscure mealybug Pseudococcus viburni

(6) Bacterial canker of stone fruit trees Pseudomonas syringae pv. morsprunorum

VI. Pre-export Management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards for export to China shall, under the supervision of the South African side, establish and implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), including maintaining orchard hygiene and promptly removing fallen fruit, and carry out Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including pest monitoring, chemical, biological and cultural control measures, so as to avoid or minimise the occurrence on cherries of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.

(2) The South African side shall organise and implement orchard monitoring for quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side. Where pests or corresponding symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated management measures including chemical, physical or biological control shall be taken to ensure that South African cherries exported to China are free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.

(3) Orchards for export to China shall conduct monitoring of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side by technical personnel familiar with plant quarantine under the guidance of the South African side; such personnel shall be trained by the South African side or its authorised bodies.

(4) Orchards for export to China shall retain pest monitoring, survey and control records for at least two years and provide them at the request of the Chinese side. Pest monitoring and control records shall include at least the monitoring date, names of pests found and control measures taken; chemical control records shall include detailed information such as the names, active ingredients, application dates and dosages of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(5) For Mediterranean fruit fly, Quilici fruit fly and Natal fruit fly, orchards for export to China shall adopt integrated field management, using chemical or biological control and other methods, and conduct fruit fly monitoring and trapping from flowering to harvest to reduce fruit fly population density. Orchards shall use fruit fly traps (e.g. triangular or bucket type) for monitoring, with lures using registered Mediterranean fruit fly bait. Trap density shall be 2 traps per square kilometre (orchards smaller than 1 square kilometre shall have at least 2 traps); lures shall be replaced as required by the product instructions, and traps shall be inspected once a week. Where the density of fruit flies captured per trap per day exceeds 0.7 (FTD > 0.7), effective control shall be carried out.

(6) For bacterial canker of stone fruit trees, an orchard monitoring system shall be established and orchard management strengthened; during the growing season in cherry-producing areas, field monitoring and surveys shall be conducted at least once a month. Where suspected infection symptoms are found, samples shall be taken promptly and sent to a laboratory for molecular biological identification. Where infection with bacterial canker of stone fruit trees is confirmed, integrated control measures shall be taken to control the pathogen; where control is incomplete, the orchard shall lose its eligibility to export to China for the current export season.

(7) For scale insects and other pests, orchards for export to China shall conduct monitoring and surveys from flowering to the end of harvest at least once every 2 weeks, focusing on fruits, fruit stalks, leaves and other parts. Where pests or corresponding damage symptoms are found, control measures shall be taken promptly to contain pest occurrence or maintain a low pest prevalence level, ensuring that cherry fruits are free from these quarantine pests.

(ii) Packinghouse management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and shipment of cherries for export to China shall be carried out under the supervision of the South African side or its authorised personnel.

(2) During post-harvest processing and packaging, cherries shall undergo washing, sorting and grading so as to ensure they are free from any live insects, defective fruits, twigs, leaves, soil and the like.

(3) The receiving, processing, treatment and storage areas for cherries for export to China shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and shall be segregated from fruit not for export to China.

(4) Registered packinghouses shall establish a traceability system to ensure that cherries for export to China can be traced back to registered orchards; the registration information shall include the processing and packaging date and the name or registration number of the source orchard.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused and shall comply with the relevant phytosanitary requirements of China. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Packaged cherries requiring storage shall be placed in storage immediately and stored separately to avoid re-infestation by pests. Each package shall be labelled in English with the fruit name, country, place of production (e.g. district, city or county), orchard registration number and packinghouse registration number. Each package and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “for export to the People's Republic of China” or the label “Exported to the People's Republic of China”.

(3) Containers used for cherries exported to China shall be inspected at the time of loading to ensure good hygienic conditions, and necessary insect-prevention measures shall be taken during loading.

(iv) Phytosanitary treatment requirements.

Cherries for export to China shall, under the supervision of the South African side or its authorised personnel, undergo cold treatment or fumigation.

Cold treatment shall be carried out under the supervision of the South African side or authorised officials in accordance with the Pre-export Cold Treatment Operational Procedures (see Annex 1) or the In-transit Cold Treatment Operational Procedures (see Annex 2); the cold treatment indicators are as follows:

(1) 1.11 °C or below (fruit core temperature), continuous treatment for 14 days or more;

(2) 1.67 °C or below (fruit core temperature), continuous treatment for 16 days or more;

(3) 2.22 °C or below (fruit core temperature), continuous treatment for 18 days or more.

Fumigation shall use methyl bromide fumigant; the treatment indicators are as follows: at normal atmospheric pressure (NAP), at a temperature of 21.1 °C or above, the dose is 32 g/m3 for a treatment of 2 hours; 0.5 hours after the start of treatment the concentration shall be not less than 26 g/m3; 2 hours after the start of treatment the concentration shall be not less than 16 g/m3.

(v) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The South African side or its authorised personnel shall sample and inspect each consignment of cherries for export to China at a rate of 2% per consignment. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side are found, the entire consignment shall not be exported to China. The South African side shall identify the cause, take corrective measures, retain inspection records and provide them to the Chinese side upon request.

(vi) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For consignments that pass quarantine, the South African side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the orchard registration number, packinghouse registration number and container number, and shall state in the additional declaration: “This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Cherries from South Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (The consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol on phytosanitary requirements for export of fresh cherries from South Africa to China and is free of quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) For consignments subjected to pre-export cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment temperature, duration and the name or registration number of the treatment facility. For consignments subjected to in-transit cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall bear the marking “Cold treatment in transit” (in-transit cold treatment) and state the cold treatment temperature, duration, container number and seal number.

(3) For consignments subjected to pre-export fumigation, the treatment column of the phytosanitary certificate shall state the methyl bromide dose, duration and treatment temperature used, as well as the name or number of the fumigation facility.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Non-compliance Handling

Upon arrival of cherries for export to China at Chinese ports of entry, China Customs shall verify the relevant documents and labels and carry out inspection and quarantine.

(i) Verification of certificates and labels.

(1) Verify whether the imported cherries have obtained the Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Chapter VI (vi) of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the labels on packages and pallets comply with Chapter VI (iii) of this Announcement.

(4) For consignments subjected to pre-export cold treatment, check the cold treatment report signed and confirmed by the South African side as well as the fruit temperature probe calibration records; for consignments subjected to in-transit cold treatment, check whether a cold treatment report, fruit temperature probe calibration records and the like are attached.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Cherries for export to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruit.

(2) Upon arrival of cherries at Chinese ports of entry, Customs shall verify the relevant documents and labels and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and the requirements of this Announcement. Consignments that pass inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(iii) Non-compliance handling.

(1) Where a consignment is found to come from an orchard, packinghouse or treatment facility not approved by the Chinese side, it shall not be permitted entry.

(2) Where cold treatment is found invalid or failed, the consignment shall be subject to on-arrival cold treatment (where still possible within the same container), return or destruction.

(3) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or quarantine pests newly occurring in South Africa, or soil, plant residues and the like are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(4) Where non-compliance with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards is found, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

Annexes: (1) Pre-export Cold Treatment Operational Procedures; (2) In-transit Cold Treatment Operational Procedures.

General Administration of Customs

(21) September 2026

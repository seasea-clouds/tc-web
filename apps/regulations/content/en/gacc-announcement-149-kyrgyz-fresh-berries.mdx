On (20) October 2026, GACC issued Announcement No. 149: effective immediately, fresh berries from Kyrgyz Republic that meet phytosanitary requirements are permitted for import, covering raspberry, blackcurrant and blackberry. Orchards and packing houses exporting to China must be audited by the Kyrgyz side and approved and registered by the Chinese side.

The announcement lists 5 quarantine pests including Byturus tomentosus and Erwinia amylovora. Orchards must establish ISPM 6 management plans and monitor every 2 weeks from bud stage to harvest; packing houses must remove insects, mites, rotten fruit and soil, and wooden packaging must comply with ISPM 15.

The Kyrgyz side samples 2% of each consignment, reducible to 1% after two problem-free years; phytosanitary certificates must be issued under ISPM 12 with an English additional declaration, and a certificate sample must be filed with the Chinese side before trade begins. Non-compliant consignments are denied entry or returned, destroyed or treated.

Pursuant to the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Water Resources, Agriculture and Processing Industry of the Kyrgyz Republic (hereinafter the Kyrgyz side) on the phytosanitary requirements for export of fresh berries from Kyrgyz Republic to China, effective immediately, fresh berries from Kyrgyz Republic complying with the following requirements are permitted for import into China.

I. Legal Basis for Quarantine

(i) Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Berries from Kyrgyz Republic to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Water Resources, Agriculture and Processing Industry of the Kyrgyz Republic.

II. Names of Commodities Permitted for Import

Fresh berries (hereinafter berries) include raspberry (scientific name Rubus idaeus, English name Raspberry), blackcurrant (scientific name Ribes nigrum, English name Blackcurrant) and blackberry (scientific name Rubus fruticosus, English name Blackberry).

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Berry-producing areas of the Kyrgyz Republic.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses exporting berries to China must be audited by the Kyrgyz side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information includes the name, address and registration code, so as to allow accurate traceability when an exported consignment fails to comply with this Announcement. Before each export season, the Kyrgyz side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side, which, after review and approval by the Chinese side, shall be published on the official website of the Chinese side.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Raspberry beetle Byturus tomentosus

(2) Fire blight Erwinia amylovora

(3) American gooseberry mildew Podosphaera mors-uvae

(4) Tobacco ringspot virus Tobacco Ringspot Virus

(5) Verticillium wilt Verticillium dahliae

VI. Pre-export Management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting to China shall, under the supervision of the Kyrgyz side, establish and implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), including maintaining orchard hygiene and promptly removing fallen fruit, and apply Integrated Pest Management (IPM), such as pest surveys, monitoring, chemical or biological control and cultural practices, so as to avoid or minimise the occurrence on berries of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.

(2) The Kyrgyz side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organise orchard monitoring activities. If any quarantine pest of concern to the Chinese side is found, corresponding control measures shall be taken immediately.

(3) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel of the Kyrgyz side. Such personnel shall receive training from the Kyrgyz side or its authorised institutions.

(4) The Kyrgyz side shall retain records of orchard pest monitoring, surveys and control and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. The monitoring and control records shall include the monitoring time, the names of pests found and the control measures taken; chemical control records shall include detailed information such as the names, active ingredients, concentrations and application dates of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(5) For Byturus tomentosus, orchards exporting to China shall be monitored from bud stage to harvest, once every 2 weeks. The focus shall be on checking soil, stems, leaves, flowers and fruit for the said pest. If Byturus tomentosus is found, chemical or biological control measures shall be taken immediately to contain its occurrence.

(6) For Podosphaera mors-uvae, Erwinia amylovora, Tobacco Ringspot Virus and Verticillium dahliae, orchards shall be cleaned regularly, diseased branches, leaves or roots removed, and fallen branches and leaves on the ground cleared by burning or deep burial. From bud stage to harvest, monitoring shall be carried out once every 2 weeks, focusing on branches, leaves, buds, stems and fruit for suspicious symptoms. If any suspicious symptoms are found, samples must be taken for laboratory testing. If the disease is confirmed, chemical or biological control measures shall be taken immediately.

(ii) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of berries for export to China shall be carried out under the supervision of officials of the Kyrgyz side or its authorised institutions.

(2) Packing houses for berries exported to China shall maintain good hygienic conditions, with hardened floors, and be able to prevent re-infestation by pests (for example by installing insect-proof nets).

(3) Berries for export to China shall be packed separately from fruit not destined for China and stored separately.

(4) During packaging, berries for export to China shall undergo removal, sorting and grading to ensure they are free of insects, mites, rotten fruit, branches, leaves, roots and soil.

(5) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system to ensure that berries for export to China can be traced back to registered orchards, recording information such as the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the quantity, the export date, the exported quantity, the country of destination and the container number.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused and shall comply with Chinese phytosanitary and hygienic requirements. If wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall be marked in Chinese or English with information such as the fruit name, country, place of origin (province, city), and the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house. Each package and pallet shall be labelled in Chinese or English with "for export to the People's Republic of China" or "Exported to the People's Republic of China".

(3) Containers used for berries exported to China shall be inspected before loading to ensure good hygienic conditions. Necessary insect-prevention measures shall be taken during loading.

(iv) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The Kyrgyz side or its authorised institutions shall conduct sampling inspection of each consignment of berries for export to China at a rate of 2% per consignment. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, branches, leaves or soil are found, export of the entire consignment to China is prohibited. The Kyrgyz side shall investigate the cause and take improvement measures. It shall also keep inspection records and provide them to the Chinese side upon request.

(v) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For berries that pass quarantine, the Kyrgyz side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), indicating the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house and the container number, and shall state in the additional declaration:

"This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Berries from Kyrgyz Republic to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China." (The consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Berries from Kyrgyz Republic to China and is free of quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) The Kyrgyz side shall provide a sample of the phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side before trade begins for confirmation and record-filing.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Non-compliance Handling

When berries for export to China arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Verification of certificates and labels.

(1) Verify whether the imported berries have obtained the Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Section (v) of Chapter VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the labels on packages and pallets comply with Section (iii) of Chapter VI of this Announcement.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Berries for export to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for importing fruit.

(2) When the berries arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall verify the relevant documents and labels and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this Announcement. Those that pass inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(iii) Non-compliance handling.

(1) If berries are found to come from orchards and packing houses not registered by the Chinese side, the consignment shall not be permitted entry.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or newly occurring quarantine pests in the Kyrgyz Republic are found, or if soil or plant residues are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to treatment.

(3) If non-compliance with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards is found, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

(20) October 2026

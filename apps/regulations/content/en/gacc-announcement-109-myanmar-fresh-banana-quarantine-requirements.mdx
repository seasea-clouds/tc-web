It enters on 1 September 2026. GACC Announcement No. 109, issued on 28 July 2026, admits immature green bananas harvested 10–11 weeks after flowering from Myanmar's banana growing areas; any overripe yellow fruit is barred, and Announcement No. 49 of 2022 is repealed at the same time.

One container walks the whole chain: orchards and packing houses must be audited by Myanmar and registered with China; orchards run GAP and IPM and monitor pests under ISPM 6, scouting mealybugs every 15 days from flowering to harvest and keeping a TR4 pest-free production site that stops on detection; wood packaging follows ISPM 15 and boxes and pallets carry the “Exported to the People's Republic of China” mark; a 1% sample is drawn before departure and travels with an ISPM 12 certificate and the English declaration; at a Chinese port, documents, marks and the import quarantine permit are checked.

Unregistered sources and overripe fruit are refused; pests of concern, newly emerging Myanmar pests or soil lead to treatment, return or destruction; failure against Chinese food-safety law or standards means return or destruction.

Announcement No. 109 (2026) of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (On Phytosanitary Requirements for the Import of Fresh Bananas from Myanmar)

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Banana Fruits from Myanmar to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as China) and the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Irrigation of the Republic of the Union of Myanmar (hereinafter referred to as Myanmar), the import of fresh bananas from Myanmar that meet the following requirements is permitted.

I. Legal basis (1) Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China; (2) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations; (3) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations; (4) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits; (5) Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Irrigation of the Republic of the Union of Myanmar on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Banana Fruits from Myanmar to China.

II. Commodity permitted for entry Fresh bananas from Myanmar (botanical name Musa sapientum, English name Banana) (hereinafter referred to as bananas) means immature green bananas harvested within 10–11 weeks after flowering. No overripe yellow bananas may be exported to China.

III. Permitted place of origin Banana growing areas of Myanmar.

IV. Approved orchards and packing houses Orchards and packing houses for bananas exported to China must be audited by Myanmar and registered with the approval of China. Registration information includes name, address and registration number, so that traceability is possible when exported goods do not conform to this announcement. Before each export season, Myanmar shall provide China with the list of registered orchards and packing houses; after China's review and approval, the list shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs and updated regularly.

V. List of quarantine pests of concern to China 1. Achatina fulica (giant African snail) 2. Aleurodicus dispersus (spiralling whitefly) 3. Bactrocera latifrons (solanum fruit fly) 4. Dysmicoccus neobrevipes (new pineapple mealybug) 5. Fusarium oxysporum Schlecht f. sp. cubense Race 4 (tropical race 4 of banana fusarium wilt) 6. Planococcus lilacinus (coffee mealybug) 7. Planococcus minor (Pacific mealybug)

VI. Pre-export management

(A) Orchard management. 1. Orchards for bananas exported to China shall, under the supervision of Myanmar, establish a sound quality management system and traceability system, implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), maintain sanitary conditions such as the absence of pollution sources affecting fruit production around the orchard and timely removal of fallen and rotten fruit, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring surveys, physical, chemical or biological control measures and agricultural practices. 2. Myanmar shall, in accordance with ISPM 6, draw up management plans for the quarantine pests of concern to China and organise orchard monitoring. Where pests or corresponding symptoms are found in monitoring, integrated management measures including chemical, physical or biological control shall be taken to ensure that Myanmar bananas do not carry the quarantine pests of concern to China. 3. Orchards shall carry out monitoring and integrated control of the quarantine pests of concern to China under the supervision and guidance of Myanmar. Pest monitoring and control shall be implemented under the guidance of technicians trained by Myanmar or its authorised institutions. 4. Orchards shall keep pest monitoring and control records and provide them to China through Myanmar upon request. Records of chemical control shall specify the name of the chemical used during the growing season, its active ingredient, dosage and timing. 5. For mealybugs such as Planococcus minor, Planococcus lilacinus and Dysmicoccus neobrevipes, orchards shall conduct pest monitoring every 15 days from flowering to harvest, checking fruit, stems, branches and leaves for scale insects. In addition to visual inspection, various physical or chemical methods shall be used to monitor pests on branches and stems, and effective control shall follow any detection. 6. For tropical race 4 of Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense (hereinafter referred to as TR4), orchards shall, following the guidance of ISPM 10, establish TR4 pest-free production sites whose status must be approved by Myanmar and China. If TR4 is detected at a production site, Myanmar shall immediately suspend exports from that site and notify China. Exports may resume only after three consecutive months without detection and under Myanmar's supervision. Orchards shall record pest monitoring and control information, keep it for at least two years and provide it to China upon request; records shall include inspection dates, names of pests found, active ingredients, dosage and application dates of agrochemicals used.

(B) Packing house management. 1. Processing, packing, storage and shipment of bananas exported to China shall be carried out under the supervision of Myanmar. 2. Processing and packing shall take place in registered packing houses. Bananas shall undergo immersion and washing, manual sorting and grading to ensure they are free of insects, mites, snails, rotten fruit, weed seeds, plant debris and soil. 3. Packed bananas that need storage before export shall be placed in storage immediately and kept separately to avoid re-infestation by pests. 4. Packing houses shall establish a traceability system allowing bananas to be traced back to registered orchards, and shall record the processing and packing date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the quantity of bananas, the export date, export quantity, destination country, vehicle or container number and seal number. Where chemicals are used in processing, the name, active ingredient, dosage and date of use shall also be recorded. Records shall be kept for at least two years.

(C) Packaging requirements. 1. Packaging materials shall be clean, sanitary, unused and compliant with China's phytosanitary requirements. Wood packaging, if used, shall meet ISPM 15. 2. Each carton shall be marked in Chinese or English with the fruit name, country, producing area (state or region, city or county), and the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house. Each carton and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “Exported to the People's Republic of China”. 3. Containers or closed vehicles carrying bananas to China shall be clean and sealed, and the seals shall remain intact upon arrival at a Chinese port of entry.

(D) Inspection and quarantine before departure. 1. Myanmar shall carry out sampling inspection on each consignment at a rate of 1%. 2. If quarantine pests of concern to China or soil are found, the whole consignment shall not be exported to China. Myanmar shall immediately suspend exports from the relevant orchard or packing house until the cause is identified and effective corrective measures are taken, and shall keep relevant records and provide them to China upon request.

(E) Phytosanitary certificate requirements. For bananas that pass quarantine, Myanmar shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with ISPM 12, stating the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house, and adding the following declaration: “This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Banana Fruits from Myanmar to China, and is free from the quarantine pests of concern to China.”

VII. Entry inspection and quarantine, and handling of non-compliance When bananas exported to China arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall verify the relevant documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine. (A) Verification of certificates and marks. 1. Verify whether the imported bananas have obtained the Permit for Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine. 2. Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Section VI(E) of this announcement. 3. Verify whether the marks on cartons and pallets comply with Section VI(C) of this announcement. (B) Entry inspection and quarantine. 1. Bananas exported to China shall enter through ports allowed by China for fruit imports. 2. On arrival, China Customs shall verify the documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this announcement; consignments that pass are permitted to enter. (C) Handling of non-compliance. 1. Consignments from unregistered orchards or packing houses, or consisting of overripe bananas, shall not be permitted to enter. 2. Where quarantine pests of concern to China, newly emerging quarantine pests of Myanmar or soil are found, the consignment shall be treated, returned or destroyed. 3. Where the consignment fails to comply with Chinese food-safety laws, regulations or national standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

This announcement shall enter into force on 1 September 2026. GACC Announcement No. 49 of 2022 shall be repealed simultaneously.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

28 July 2026

Who carries what in a bonded plan comes down to item-by-item ownership: eight risks are listed one by one, and each has a named owner. Chapter eight, Well Protected, of the source material frames the subject with three questions: who bears what when things go wrong, who owns the assets, and how handover works on change. This article covers the chapter's ownership-boundary page and its risk-register page together, first how the three categories are drawn, then how the eight register rows are read. You do not need to read any other chapter first.

## Three categories of ownership and their boundaries

Three categories cover all eight risks: your confirmation, shared in parallel with us, and our scope that we lead. Boundaries are written in the open, and each side bears its own.

The first card is your confirmation: three core decisions plus port preference and billing method. The three decisions are whether to start the paid S0 diagnostic, the entity path, and category and claims strategy; port preference and billing method are confirmed alongside them. We prepare the evidence: an admission study and compliance basis back the first two, the execution checklist gives the timeline, and the port-execution checklist backs the third, all four delivered at once. The card closes with the split: we prepare the evidence, you make the decisions.

The second card is parallel with us and shared: asset ownership, risk sharing and exit handover. The mechanics are concrete. Asset and data ownership go into annexes, the liability cap into liability clauses, and handover into the exit plan per a six-item list, all three governed by the annex clauses. The four mapped items are asset ownership and data rights, risk sharing and liability cap, exit and handover, and contract annex clauses aligned item by item. The card closes with: shared items all written down, nothing left verbal.

The third card is our scope, which we lead: declaration and inspection, returns exceptions, and compliance records, run by us on standard procedures with progress visible weekly. How it is carried: per-order ledger records and archived answers to regulator inquiries, reviewable with the deliverables, and exceptions fully logged. The four mapped items are four POs (entity, acquisition, data, classification), declaration and inspection duties plus returns and exceptions, compliance records and audit support, and the contingency plan with response deadlines. The card closes with: our scope runs on standard procedures.

A three-step flow connects ownership to execution. Step one: you confirm the three core decisions and port preference. Step two: we start S0, with admission tiering and the cost structure issued in parallel. Step three: both sides confirm schedule and commercial terms, with terms and liabilities written into contract annexes together. The basis: one decision framework throughout, this page and "Three Decisions to Start" share a single source, so each decision is made once.

## The risk register: severity and owner

The next page is Risk Map: Register, Severity, and Owner. Its conclusion is direct: all eight risks have a list entry, a severity and an owner; the two high-severity risks are settled before S1; and the map refreshes monthly with the deliverables ledger, so there is no last-minute re-assessment. On the left is a likelihood-by-impact bubble map. The X-axis is likelihood from low to high, the Y-axis is impact from low to high, and bubble size shows severity. Dark navy with a check is our scope, mid-blue is parallel with us, light blue is provided by you; the quadrants are labelled watch zone, act first and routine. The eight bubbles are port, P0, stock, 9610, claims, data, marks and ingredient list.

Two cards below each cover one high-severity risk. Port execution plan is our scope: we archive the execution ruling and keep a backup port plan ready, matching the item in "Three Lists". Four P0 prerequisites are parallel with us: each is settled before S1, and progress pauses until then, matching "Three Decisions to Start". The basis: every risk has a mitigation and an owner, matching the liability matrix, and the map updates by phase.

## One register row, a full reading

Take row 01, port execution plan, and read ownership, mitigation and refresh as one chain. Start with the register: number 01, severity high, owner our scope, mitigation execution ruling plus backup port plan. Back to the three owner cards: declaration and inspection, returns exceptions and compliance records are led by us on standard procedures, with progress visible weekly, per-order ledger records, archived answers to regulator inquiries and exceptions fully logged, which is why this row sits with us. Then the high-severity card: archive the execution ruling, keep a backup port plan ready, word for word the same mitigation. Finally the refresh rhythm: the map updates monthly with the deliverables ledger and is reviewed by phase, so if port preference changes, this row's execution ruling and backup plan change with it.

Read row 02 against it and the boundary is clearer. Severity is also high, but the owner is parallel with us and the mitigation points to S1, S7, the data path, and the Four Prohibitions and Two Limits; progress pauses until those prerequisites are settled. Both rows are high severity, yet one is led by us and one is gated on shared prerequisites, so the owner sets the response rhythm. Reading all eight rows this way gives every question an address: who bears what, who owns the assets, and how handover works on change.

To close, the terms: the three categories are the contract-level division of work, the register is the row-by-row execution ledger, and both share one source, so every risk lands somewhere specific. The two high-severity risks are settled before S1 starts; the rest are scheduled by severity. Cross-border data transfers are assessed item by item under the outbound clauses of the personal information protection law with separate consent. The map refreshes monthly with the deliverables ledger, not at the last minute.

Fresh pears from Portugal are approved for export to China. Announcement No. 186 of 2025 of the General Administration of Customs takes effect upon publication. The pear is Pyrus communis, limited to mainland Portugal's pear-growing areas; orchards, packing houses and cold treatment facilities are audited by Portugal and registered by China.

Orchards must set up quality and traceability systems, implement GAP and IPM, and establish and monitor pest-free areas for eighteen quarantine pests including the Mediterranean fruit fly; traps are set at one per hectare and FTD must be less than 0.5. Three cold treatment schedules (from 1.11°C/14 days) or fumigation control fruit flies.

Each consignment carries a phytosanitary certificate bearing the prescribed English declaration, and boxes are marked in Chinese or English with export-to-China information. At the entry port, documents and markings are verified and inspection and quarantine are performed; non-compliant consignments are refused, destroyed or subject to quarantine treatment.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Maritime Affairs of the Portuguese Republic (hereinafter referred to as the Portuguese side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh pears from Portugal to China, the import of fresh pears from Portugal that meet the following relevant requirements is permitted effective immediately.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(1) Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(3) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(4) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(5) Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Maritime Affairs of the Portuguese Republic on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Pears from Portugal to China.

II. Names of Commodities Permitted for Entry

Fresh pear (hereinafter referred to as pear), scientific name Pyrus communis, English name Pear.

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Pear-producing areas in mainland Portugal (excluding the Azores and Madeira archipelagos).

IV. Registration

Orchards, packing houses and cold treatment facilities producing pears for export to China shall be audited by the Portuguese side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. Registration information includes name, address and identification code to allow accurate traceability when exported goods do not comply with the relevant provisions of this Announcement. Before each annual export season, the Portuguese side shall provide the Chinese side with the registration list, which shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs after review and approval by the Chinese side.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Pear root aphid Aphanostigma piri

(2) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(3) Fig wax scale Ceroplastes rusci

(4) Codling moth Cydia pomonella

(5) Putnam scale Diaspidiotus ancylus

(6) Pear aphid Dysaphis pyri

(7) Italian pear scale Epidiaspis leperii

(8) Woolly apple aphid Eriosoma lanigerum

(9) Pear sawfly Hoplocampa brevis

(10) Long-tailed mealybug Pseudococcus longispinus

(11) Pear thrips Taeniothrips inconsequens

(12) Blastobasis decolore

(13) Oystershell scale Lepidosaphes ulmi

(14) Obscure mealybug Pseudococcus viburni

(15) Botryosphaeria canker Botryosphaeria stevensii

(16) Phytophthora blight Phytophthora syringae

(17) Pear scab Venturia pyrina

(18) Fire blight Erwinia amylovora

VI. Pre-export Management

(1) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting to China shall, under the supervision of the Portuguese side, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, implement good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain orchard sanitation such as timely removal of fallen and rotten fruit and end-of-season pruning, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including regular surveillance and surveys of quarantine pests, physical, chemical or biological control of pests, and cultural control measures. Fallen fruit may not be used for export.

(2) The Portuguese side shall ensure that growers of pears for export are aware of the quarantine pests of concern to China and their control measures. Pest monitoring and control in orchards exporting to China shall be carried out under the guidance of technicians with phytosanitary knowledge, and the Portuguese side or its authorized body shall train such technicians.

(3) All registered orchards must retain records of surveillance and surveys of quarantine pests of concern to China and of chemical and biological control measures implemented, and shall provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Chemical control records shall include detailed information such as the names of chemical agents used during the growing season, active ingredients, dosages and application times.

(4) The Portuguese side shall ensure that registered orchards are inspected at least 20 days before fruit harvest, focusing on the presence of quarantine pests of concern to China, pest monitoring and control, and the maintenance of field sanitation. If any quarantine pest of concern to China is found, samples shall be sent to an official laboratory for identification and control measures shall be taken immediately. Orchards unable to meet the phytosanitary conditions for pear export to China shall have their export eligibility suspended for the season.

(5) With regard to the Mediterranean fruit fly, pears for export to China shall originate from a Mediterranean fruit fly pest-free area recognized by the Chinese side or from orchards effectively controlled through systemic control measures.

① Pest-free area.

Fruit fly pest-free areas shall be established and maintained based on the relevant requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 26 (ISPM26). The establishment plan and emergency plan for a pest-free area shall be reviewed and approved by the Chinese side before implementation by the Portuguese side. Once fruit flies are detected in a pest-free area, the area shall be suspended. The Portuguese side shall notify the Chinese side within 48 hours and immediately activate the emergency action plan. A pest-free area may be reinstated only after the Portuguese side has eradicated the outbreak and it has been recognized by the Chinese side.

② Systemic control measures.

Orchards exporting to China shall monitor the Mediterranean fruit fly from flowering to harvest using Jackson traps baited with the sex pheromone Trimedlure. Trap density shall be one per hectare, and orchards of less than 3 hectares must have at least 3 traps, checked once a week. Once fruit flies are detected, effective control shall be carried out.

In the pear-producing areas for export to China, the „number of Mediterranean fruit flies trapped per trap per day“ (FTD) shall be less than 0.5; if the FTD value exceeds 0.5, effective prevention and control measures shall be taken immediately, such as insecticide treatment, mating disruption techniques, mass trapping, baits, and collection and destruction of infested fruit. For orchards where control is inadequate, the Portuguese side shall suspend the orchard's eligibility for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. Upon request, the Portuguese side shall provide the Chinese side with pest survey reports or management records.

(6) With regard to the codling moth, pears for export to China shall originate from a codling moth pest-free place of production recognized by the Chinese side or from orchards effectively controlled through systemic control measures.

① Pest-free place of production.

Pest-free places of production shall be established and maintained by the Portuguese side in accordance with the relevant requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 10 (ISPM 10). The establishment plan and emergency plan for a pest-free place of production shall be reviewed and approved by the Chinese side before implementation by the Portuguese side. If codling moth is found in the relevant pest-free place of production, the Portuguese side shall suspend its eligibility for export to China. The Portuguese side shall notify the Chinese side within 48 hours and immediately activate the emergency plan. A pest-free place of production may be reinstated only after the Portuguese side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognized by the Chinese side.

② Systemic control measures.

Orchards exporting pears to China shall monitor codling moth from flowering until the end of harvest. Trap density in orchards shall be one per hectare, and orchards of less than 3 hectares must have at least 3 traps; lures shall be replaced once a month and traps checked once a week. If three or more codling moths are found in a trap, effective control shall be carried out immediately using methods such as mating disruption techniques. For orchards where control is inadequate, the Portuguese side shall suspend the orchard's eligibility for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. Upon request, the Portuguese side shall provide the Chinese side with pest survey reports or management records.

(7) With regard to the pear sawfly, pears for export to China shall originate from a pear sawfly low-prevalence area recognized by the Chinese side. The low-prevalence area shall be established and maintained by the Portuguese side in accordance with the relevant requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 22 (ISPM 22).

Orchards exporting to China shall monitor pear sawfly from flowering to harvest and take both non-chemical and chemical measures for effective control. Trap density shall be one per hectare, and orchards of less than 3 hectares must have at least 3 traps, checked once a week. In autumn, orchards shall be cleaned and infested fruit collected and destroyed. Once pear sawfly is detected, pesticides approved by the Chinese side shall be used promptly for control. For orchards where control is inadequate, the Portuguese side shall suspend the orchard's eligibility for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. Upon request, the Portuguese side shall provide the Chinese side with survey reports or management records on this pest.

(8) With regard to Phytophthora syringae, orchards exporting to China shall conduct surveillance for the pathogen from flowering to harvest once every 2 weeks and carry out targeted preventive spraying based on surveillance results. In addition, pruning shall be carried out in a timely manner to remove low-hanging branches, so as to reduce the risk of near-ground fruit being infected by Phytophthora syringae through rain splash or sprinkler irrigation. Pears for export to China shall be harvested from more than 50cm above the ground, and measures shall be taken to prevent mixing of fruit above 50cm with fruit below 50cm at harvest.

(9) With regard to fire blight, pears for export to China shall originate from a fire blight pest-free place of production recognized by the Chinese side.

① The pest-free place of production shall be established and maintained by the Portuguese side in accordance with the relevant requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 10 (ISPM 10).

② The Portuguese side shall organize fire blight surveillance throughout the national pear-producing area, with at least 3 surveys per region per year. Orchards exporting to China shall be surveyed for fire blight at least 3 times a year, at flowering, young-fruit and fruit-maturity (pre-harvest) stages. If fire blight is detected in an orchard in the season preceding the export season, a buffer zone shall be established within 1000 meters around the pest-free place of production, all susceptible hosts in the buffer zone shall be marked, and they shall be surveyed at least 3 times a year.

③ If fire blight is found in a pest-free place of production or in a buffer zone, the Portuguese side shall suspend that place's eligibility for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. A pest-free place of production may be reinstated only after the Portuguese side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognized by the Chinese side.

(10) For other quarantine pests of concern to China, orchards exporting to China shall conduct surveillance once every 15 days from flowering to harvest, focusing on branches, stems, leaves, fruit stalks and fruit for the presence of target pests, and shall take timely chemical, biological or physical control measures according to pest threshold levels.

(11) During transport of fruit from the orchard to the packing house, closed box vehicles or canvas covering shall be used to avoid pest infestation en route.

(2) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packing, storage and shipment of pears for export to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Portuguese side or its authorized officers. The Portuguese side shall ensure that only pear fruit from registered orchards enters the packing house for sorting and processing.

(2) Packing houses shall have good sanitary conditions. Before processing pears for export to China, the processing workshops and equipment shall be clean and sanitary, and diseased, insect-infested and rejected fruit and residues removed from the previous processing shall have been cleared in a timely manner.

(3) Pear packing, storage and loading areas shall have measures to prevent reinfestation by live pests, such as double doors, air curtains, rubber curtains or insect-proof nets.

(4) Packing houses shall be equipped with a separate inspection room with magnifying glasses, dissecting microscopes and other supplies, located away from unselected fruit, ventilation openings, and corridors and doors through which loading and unloading vehicles pass.

(5) During processing and packing, pears shall be sorted, graded and washed to remove diseased, insect-infested, rotten and malformed fruit, branches, leaves, other plant residues and soil, and effective measures such as high-pressure air guns or water guns and brush washing shall be used to clean the fruit surface, so as to effectively remove scales, insect eggs and pathogen spores adhering to it. If necessary, fungicide treatment against Phytophthora syringae shall be carried out in the packing house.

(6) After packing is completed, packing house technicians shall inspect 600 fruit from each lot and select at least 60 fruit for internal inspection. If any quarantine pest of concern to China is found, the lot shall not be exported to China. If codling moth or fire blight is found, the Portuguese side shall also suspend the status of the corresponding pest-free place of production and immediately notify the Chinese side. The pest-free place of production may be reinstated only after the Portuguese side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognized by the Chinese side.

(3) Packing requirements.

(1) Packing materials shall be clean, sanitary and unused and comply with the relevant phytosanitary requirements of China. If packing boxes have ventilation holes, the holes of each box or the entire box shall be covered with insect-proof mesh (maximum aperture 1.6 mm) or other material capable of preventing reinfestation. If wooden packing is used, it shall comply with the requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM15).

(2) Each packing box shall be marked in English with information such as the fruit name, country, place of origin, orchard name or registration number, and packing house name or registration number. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English as „Exported to the People’s Republic of China“.

(3) Selected and packed fruit shall be kept separate from unselected fruit. Packed pears for export to China shall be refrigerated immediately and stored only with pears of the same phytosanitary conditions and separately from other fruits, so as to avoid reinfestation by pests.

(4) Containers loaded with pears for export to China shall be inspected before packing to ensure good sanitary conditions and freedom from quarantine pests of concern to China as well as from branches, leaves and soil.

(4) Quarantine treatment measures.

Pears for export to China originating from areas infested with the Mediterranean fruit fly shall undergo cold treatment in accordance with the cold treatment operational procedures (see Annexes 1 and 2) under the supervision of the Portuguese side or its authorized officers. The cold treatment parameters shall meet any of the following conditions:

(1) Fruit pulp centre temperature of 1.11°C or below, with a continuous treatment period of not less than 14 days;

(2) Fruit pulp centre temperature of 1.67°C or below, with a continuous treatment period of not less than 16 days;

(3) Fruit pulp centre temperature of 2.22°C or below, with a continuous treatment period of not less than 18 days.

(5) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Pears for export to China shall undergo quarantine inspection at the packing house before export.

(2) During the first two years of trade, the Portuguese side or its authorized officers shall sample and inspect each consignment of pears for export to China at a rate of 2% of each consignment, with a minimum sample size of 1200 fruit, and shall conduct internal inspection of at least 60 of them or of any suspect fruit found during inspection. If no phytosanitary problem occurs within the two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%, but shall be not less than 600 fruit.

(3) If any live quarantine pest of concern to China, or any live pest that cannot be identified to species level or whose quarantine status cannot be determined, is found, the consignment shall not be exported to China. The Portuguese side shall ascertain the cause and take improvement measures. Inspection records shall be retained and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(4) If codling moth or fire blight is found, the status of the corresponding pest-free place of production shall also be withdrawn and the Chinese side shall be notified immediately. The status of the relevant pest-free place of production may be reinstated only after the Portuguese side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognized by the Chinese side.

(6) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) If the quarantine inspection is satisfactory, the Portuguese side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate indicating the name or code of the orchard and packing house, and shall state in the additional declaration column: „This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Pears from Portugal to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.“ (meaning that the consignment meets the requirements of the said Protocol and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China).

(2) For consignments undergoing pre-export cold treatment, the treatment temperature, duration and the name or number of the treatment facility shall be indicated on the phytosanitary certificate. For consignments undergoing cold treatment during transit, „Cold treatment in transit“ shall be indicated on the phytosanitary certificate, together with the treatment temperature, duration, container number and seal number.

(3) Before trade commences, the Portuguese side shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and record-filing by the Chinese side.

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine and Handling of Non-compliance

Upon arrival of pears for export to China at the entry port, China Customs shall verify the relevant documents and markings and carry out inspection and quarantine.

(1) Verification of relevant certificates and markings.

(1) Verify whether the imported pears have obtained the Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Item (6) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the markings on packing boxes or wooden packing comply with the provisions of Item (3) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(4) For consignments that have undergone pre-export cold treatment, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment result report signed and confirmed by the Portuguese side, and the fruit temperature probe calibration records meet the provisions of Item (4) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(5) For consignments that have undergone cold treatment during transit, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment report and the fruit temperature probe calibration records meet the provisions of Item (4) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(2) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Pears for export to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruits.

(2) Inspection and quarantine shall be carried out on imported pears in accordance with the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules; those that pass inspection and quarantine shall be permitted to enter.

(3) Handling of non-compliance.

(1) If pears from unapproved orchards and packing houses are found, the consignment shall not be permitted to enter.

(2) If the cold treatment is deemed invalid, the consignment shall be subject to quarantine measures such as cold treatment on arrival (which may be carried out in the container), refusal or destruction.

(3) If any live quarantine pest of concern to China or any newly occurring quarantine pest in Portugal is found, or if soil, plant residues or the like are found, the consignment shall be refused, destroyed or subject to quarantine treatment.

(4) If the consignment is found not to comply with China's food safety laws, regulations and national standards, it shall be refused or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

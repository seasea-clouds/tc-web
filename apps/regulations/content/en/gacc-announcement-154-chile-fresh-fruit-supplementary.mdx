Effective today, a General Administration of Customs notice revises Mediterranean fruit-fly control-area rules for Chilean fresh fruit to China: cross-area packing, in-transit cold treatment on refrigerated vessels, cold-treatment indicators, pallet labels; existing access stays valid.

Cherries from one control area packed in another: first submit a work plan to the Chilean side, cover boxes with mesh bags of mesh size under 1.6 mm, transport directly in enclosed vehicles; the receiving control area adds one theoretical generation before outbreak lift, with 3 TML traps around it. For in-transit cold treatment on a refrigerated vessel, the calibration record travels with the certificate and the seal opens only at the Chinese port.

Pallets or air-freight cartons keep only the English wording for China, with no extra pallet label; cold treatment follows Annex 3 at 1.11 °C for 16 days or 3 °C for 23 days, with pre-cooling before container loading. Batch and temperature parameters are illustrative; the Chilean approval documents and accompanying certificate prevail.

Pursuant to the relevant laws and regulations of China and to the provisions of the Memorandum of Understanding on Cooperation Concerning Supplementary Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Chilean Fresh Fruit to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture of the Republic of Chile, the phytosanitary requirements for Chilean fresh fruit exported to China are adjusted with effect from today. The relevant requirements are hereby announced as follows:

I. For fruit produced or stored in the control areas implemented following the occurrence of the Mediterranean fruit fly (Ceratitis capitata) in Chile and exported to China, if it is transported to a packing plant in another control area for packing or storage, it shall be authorised and approved by the Ministry of Agriculture of the Republic of Chile, and corresponding risk control measures shall be adopted (see Annex 1).

II. For fruit exported to China produced within the Mediterranean fruit fly control areas in Chile and transported by refrigerated vessel, if in-transit cold treatment is applied, it shall be carried out in accordance with the relevant operational procedures (see Annex 2).

III. Based on updated international phytosanitary standards, the cold treatment indicator requirements for fresh fruit from the Mediterranean fruit fly control areas in Chile are adjusted (see Annex 3).

IV. The label information on pallets and packaging boxes of fresh fruit exported to China is adjusted as follows: each pallet or the packaging boxes of air cargo need retain only the English wording “Export to the People's Republic of China” (for export to the People's Republic of China) as the standard identification element for consignments to China, with no need for an additional pallet label.

It is hereby announced.

Annex 1

Risk control measures for the handling, within Mediterranean fruit fly control areas, of fruit exported to China (cherries and stone fruit) coming from other control areas

I. General requirements

Fruit exported to China produced or stored within the Mediterranean fruit fly (Ceratitis capitata) control areas, including cherries and stone fruit (such as plums, nectarines, peaches and apricots), that is transported to a packing plant in another control area (hereinafter the second control-area packing plant) for packing or storage shall be authorised and approved by the Ministry of Agriculture of the Republic of Chile (hereinafter the Chilean side).

The fruit shall be transported directly from one orchard or packing plant to the second control-area packing plant.

The closed doors of the means of transport may be opened only after arrival at the second control-area packing plant.

For a packing plant receiving fruit from another control area, the time limit for lifting the outbreak status of the area where it is located shall be extended by at least one theoretical generation. That is, the conditions for lifting the outbreak status of that control area shall meet the requirement of completing the fourth or more theoretical generations of the pest. The additional fourth generation is counted from the date on which the last consignment from the supplying control area is received.

II. Fruit transport requirements

Before the fruit is transported, the company operating the orchard or packing plant shall submit an application in advance to the Chilean side, attaching the corresponding work plan, and shall comply with the biosecurity measures established by the Chilean side.

The work plan submitted with the application must include the information on the packing plant to which the fruit is sent for packing, the outward dispatch plan and the quantity dispatched (number of boxes and weight), so as to enable the Chilean side to supervise the outward dispatch of the fruit.

All boxes of fruit sent out for packing shall be covered with mesh bags of mesh size smaller than 1.6 mm or with non-perforated bags, and shall be transported directly to the second control-area packing plant in enclosed vehicles approved by the Chilean side.

III. Requirements for orchards or packing plants sending fruit out for packing

Each box of fruit sent out for packing shall bear a label identifying information such as the orchard, the variety and the harvest time.

The driver of the transport vehicle shall carry documents stating the orchard or packing plant from which the consignment of fruit originates, the quantity and weight, and the type of fruit, and shall hand them over to the second control-area packing plant upon arrival.

The orchard or packing plant shall keep records of each batch of fruit sent out for packing and shall make them available promptly when the Chilean side carries out supervision.

IV. Requirements for packing plants receiving fruit for packing

The packing plant shall notify the competent local authority of the Chilean side of the information on the packing plants from which it intends to receive fruit sent out for packing and of the corresponding fruit batch plan, and shall keep traceable records of the fruit received for supervision by the Chilean side. At the same time, quarantine cold treatment shall be carried out in accordance with the relevant agreement or the requirements of Annex 3 to this Announcement.

V. Responsibilities of the Chilean side

The Chilean side will add 3 TML-baited traps for the Mediterranean fruit fly around the packing plant (preferably on fruit fly host trees) and carry out monitoring until the control area where the packing plant receiving fruit for packing is located has met the requirement of completing the fourth or more theoretical generations of the pest.

In addition, the Chilean side will promptly verify the work plans and supervise the fruit sent out for packing from the orchard or packing plant through to the second control-area packing plant where it is packed for export to China, so as to ensure quarantine security.

Annex 2

Operational procedures for in-transit cold treatment on refrigerated vessels for fruit from the Mediterranean fruit fly control areas during transport

I. General requirements

The holds of a refrigerated vessel may be divided into several treatment zones according to different temperature-control power conditions, and each treatment zone may in turn be arranged as 1 or more treatment chambers.

Each treatment zone (or treatment chamber) in the hold is equipped with an independent temperature detection system, refrigeration system and ventilation system, so that the temperature can be maintained within the authorised treatment temperature range for each type of fruit specified in Annex 3 to this Announcement. Inspectors of the Ministry of Agriculture of the Republic of Chile (hereinafter the Chilean side) will seal each treatment zone (or treatment chamber) for fruit exported to China.

II. Treatment requirements

Refrigerated vessels used to carry out in-transit cold treatment shall be approved by officials of the Chilean side, and the temperature monitoring and refrigeration systems installed in the holds shall be verified, so as to ensure that the pulp temperature during treatment can be controlled.

The probe temperature shall be between -3.0 °C and +3.0 °C, with an accuracy of ±0.1 °C.

The temperature recorder shall be capable of recording the temperature of each probe at least once per hour, and the recorded display shall meet the accuracy required for the probes.

III. Temperature probes

(i) Temperature probe calibration shall be carried out using a standard thermometer approved by officials of the Chilean side in a mixture of crushed ice and distilled water. (ii) Any probe with a reading outside 0 °C±0.3 °C shall be replaced. (iii) A “Pulp Temperature Probe Calibration Record” signed and stamped by officials of the Chilean side shall be issued for each treatment zone (or treatment chamber). (iv) The original of the Pulp Temperature Probe Calibration Record shall be attached to the phytosanitary certificate accompanying the consignment. (v) When the fruit arrives at the Chinese port of entry, China Customs officers shall carry out a calibration check on the pulp temperature probes.

IV. Placement of temperature probes

(i) The packed boxes of fruit or their pallets shall be loaded into the treatment zone (or treatment chamber) of the transporting refrigerated vessel under the supervision of officials of the Chilean side. (ii) The corresponding number of temperature probes shall be placed according to the spatial volume of the treatment zone (or treatment chamber) (see the table below). (iii) To verify the effect of the cold treatment, the pulp temperature probes shall be checked. (iv) All probes shall be placed under the supervision and guidance of officials of the Chilean side.

(1) 1 pulp temperature probe shall be placed in the middle of the load at the centre of the cargo in the treatment zone (or treatment chamber). (2) 1 pulp temperature probe shall be placed at a top corner of the load at the centre of the cargo in the treatment zone (or treatment chamber). (3) 1 pulp temperature probe shall be placed in the middle of the load near the return air vent. (4) 1 pulp temperature probe shall be placed at the top of the load near the return air vent.

The pallet numbers and the positions where the probes are placed in each treatment zone (or treatment chamber) shall be recorded and kept.

(1) Volume 0—283 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 1 or 2, number of pulp temperature probes 4, total number of probes 5 or 6. (2) Volume 284—425 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 2 or 3, number of pulp temperature probes 4, total number of probes 6 or 7. (3) Volume 426—708 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 2 or 3, number of pulp temperature probes 5, total number of probes 7 or 8. (4) Volume 709—1274 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 2 or 3, number of pulp temperature probes 6, total number of probes 8 or 9. (5) Volume 1275—1980 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 2 or 3, number of pulp temperature probes 7, total number of probes 9 or 10. (6) Volume 1981—2830 (cubic metres): number of air temperature probes 2 or 3, number of pulp temperature probes 9, total number of probes 11 or 12.

V. Treatment zones (or treatment chambers) and seals

(i) Quarantine officials of the Chilean side shall apply coded seals to each treatment zone (or treatment chamber) containing the cargo. (ii) The seals may be opened only at the Chinese port of entry by China Customs officers.

VI. Temperature records and commencement of cold treatment

(i) In-transit cold treatment means the cold treatment carried out during the transport of the refrigerated vessel carrying the fruit from its departure from Chile to the first port of arrival in China, or, where necessary after arrival at the port of entry, the cold treatment may be continued. (ii) The treatment time may be officially counted only when all pulp temperature probes in the corresponding treatment zone (or treatment chamber) have reached the specified temperature. (iii) The shipping company shall download the cold treatment temperature records and submit them to the customs at the port of entry. (iv) If part of the cold treatment for a sea voyage has already been completed before the vessel reaches the Chinese port, the treatment records may be downloaded in transit and transmitted to China Customs for review.

VII. Verification of treatment results

China Customs will verify whether the treatment records comply with the relevant treatment requirements and, on the basis of the probe calibration results (subsection (iii) of Chapter III of this Annex), determine whether the treatment is valid.

VIII. Phytosanitary certificate

The temperature and the duration of the cold treatment shall be indicated in the phytosanitary certificate, and the treated pallet numbers of each treatment zone (or treatment chamber) shall be listed in the form of an annex to the phytosanitary certificate. At the same time, the corresponding Chilean seal numbers shall be stated in the phytosanitary certificate. (ii) Upon entry of the fruit, the phytosanitary certificate signed by the Chilean side, the cold treatment report and the pulp temperature probe calibration record shall be provided to China Customs.

Annex 3

Cold treatment indicators for fresh fruit from the Mediterranean fruit fly control areas

The cold treatment of fresh fruit exported to China from within the Mediterranean fruit fly (Ceratitis capitata) control areas shall be carried out in accordance with the cold treatment indicators and operational requirements stipulated in the current phytosanitary requirements.

At the same time, based on updated international phytosanitary standards, the following cold treatment indicator schedules also apply to Chilean fruit exported to China:

(1) Temperature range 1.11 °C or lower, duration (days) 16, applicable fruit cherries, grapes, stone fruit (nectarines, peaches, plums, apricots); blueberries, apples, pears, kiwifruit, citrus. (2) Temperature range 1.67 °C or lower, duration (days) 17, applicable fruit cherries, grapes, stone fruit (nectarines, peaches, plums, apricots); blueberries, apples, pears, kiwifruit, citrus. (3) Temperature range 2.22 °C or lower, duration (days) 18, applicable fruit cherries, grapes, stone fruit (nectarines, peaches, plums, apricots); blueberries, apples, pears, kiwifruit, citrus. (4) Temperature range 3 °C or lower, duration (days) 23, applicable fruit cherries, grapes, stone fruit (nectarines, peaches, plums, apricots).

The fruit shall be pre-cooled before container loading to a temperature close to or below the required treatment temperature.

For consignments subject to in-transit cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate must indicate “in transit cold treatment” (cold treatment in transit), the container number and the seal number.

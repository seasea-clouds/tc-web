Announcement No. 54 of 2026 sets out origin management measures for zero-tariff imports from twenty African countries.

Goods qualify if wholly obtained, produced entirely from originating materials, or meet tariff classification change or regional value content of at least forty percent.

Certificates must be issued by authorised bodies and made out in English; the preferential tariff code is thirty and records are kept for three years.

<!-- original -->

From 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2028, China applies zero tariff to twenty African countries that have diplomatic relations with China and are not least developed countries. GACC has formulated the Measures for the Administration of Origin of Imported Goods under the Zero-Tariff Policy for the Purpose of Implementing Origin Rules, hereby published.

To facilitate customs clearance, GACC launches the Certificate of Origin Issuance System for the twenty African countries from 1 May 2026. Applications based on electronically issued certificates follow Annex 2 of Announcement No. 34 of 2021 for preferential trade agreements with electronic data exchange; those based on non-electronic certificates follow the same annex for agreements without such exchange.

The “preferential tariff code” field under “preferential trade agreement” shall be filled in with “30”.

Hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

28 April 2026

Measures of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China for the Administration of Origin of Imported Goods under the Zero-Tariff Policy for Twenty African Countries Having Diplomatic Relations with China and Not Classified as Least Developed Countries

Article 1 These Measures are formulated to determine correctly the origin of imported goods under the zero-tariff policy for twenty African countries having diplomatic relations with China and not classified as least developed countries, to promote economic and trade exchanges, in accordance with the Customs Law, the Tariff Law, the Regulations on the Origin of Import and Export Goods and other provisions.

Article 2 These Measures apply to the origin administration of goods imported from Algeria, Egypt, Botswana, Equatorial Guinea, Cape Verde, the Republic of the Congo, Ghana, Gabon, Zimbabwe, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, Kenya, Libya, Mauritius, Morocco, Namibia, South Africa, Nigeria, Seychelles and Tunisia (collectively the African countries) and applying for preferential tariff rates under the zero-tariff policy.

Article 3 Imported goods acquire originating status under the zero-tariff policy if they are wholly obtained or produced in an African country; produced entirely from originating materials; or produced using non-originating materials and meeting either the product-specific rules of origin (Annex 1) including tariff classification change, or regional value content of not less than 40%. Changes to the product-specific rules shall be announced separately by GACC.

Article 4 Goods “wholly obtained or produced” under Article 3(1) include: live animals born and raised there; goods obtained from such animals; plants and plant products harvested or gathered there; goods obtained by hunting, trapping, fishing, aquaculture, gathering or capturing; minerals and natural substances extracted from soil, waters or seabed; goods extracted from waters beyond territorial waters which the country is entitled to exploit; fish and marine products obtained by vessels registered and flying its flag; goods produced onboard factory ships registered and flying its flag solely from the aforementioned products; waste and scrap from processing; used goods collected there; and goods produced there solely from the aforementioned items.

Article 5 Tariff classification change means that the tariff heading designation in the Customs Tariff of the People's Republic of China changes to a specified digit after manufacture or processing of non-originating materials in an African country.

Article 6 Regional value content shall be calculated by the relevant formula. “Price of non-originating materials” means the import cost, freight and insurance to the destination port determined under the WTO Valuation Agreement, including materials of unknown origin; for materials obtained in the African country, the first ascertainable price paid or payable there, excluding freight, insurance, packing and other costs from the supplier's warehouse. The price of non-originating materials excludes non-originating materials used to produce originating materials.

Article 7 Originating materials of China used in an African country to produce another good shall be regarded as originating in that African country. Originating materials of one African country used in another African country shall be regarded as originating in the latter, provided both countries have signed and implemented free trade agreements with China including early harvest arrangements.

Article 8 Goods meeting the tariff classification change standard may be regarded as originating if all non-originating materials not meeting the standard do not exceed 10% of the FOB price and all other requirements are met.

Article 9 Goods shall not acquire originating status if they undergo only minimal operations such as preserving goods, simple assembly or disassembly, changing packaging, washing, cleaning, ironing, simple painting, husking or polishing, peeling, sharpening, filtering, bottling, labelling, simple mixing, testing, dilution, drying, salting, freezing or animal slaughter. All operations in the African country shall be considered.

Article 10 Where originating and non-originating interchangeable materials are used, originating status shall be determined by physical separation or an inventory management method recognised by generally accepted accounting principles and used continuously for at least 12 months.

Article 11 Materials used in production, testing or inspection that do not form part of the goods, such as fuel, energy, catalysts, solvents, plant, equipment, machines, gloves, tools, moulds, spare parts, lubricants and other materials, do not affect the determination of origin.

Article 12 Packaging materials and containers used for transport do not affect origin. Retail packaging classified with the goods shall be included as originating or non-originating materials according to their own origin when the regional value content standard applies, and shall not affect origin under other standards.

Article 13 Accessories, spare parts and tools declared with the goods, classified with them in the Tariff and invoiced together, shall be included as originating or non-originating materials according to their own origin under the regional value content standard, and shall not affect origin under the tariff classification change standard. Their quantity and price shall be within reasonable limits.

Article 14 Originating goods retain their status if transported directly, or through other countries solely for geographical or transport reasons, not treated beyond unloading or preserving, under customs supervision, with temporary storage not exceeding 6 months.

Article 15 Certificates of origin (Annex 2) shall cover originating goods, be issued by authorised bodies on application, bear a unique number, cover one or more consignments, state the basis of origin, bear signature or seal consistent with notified specimens, and be made out in English.

Article 16 Certificates shall be issued before or at shipment and be valid for one year from issuance. Retrospective issuance within one year of shipment is permitted for force majeure or other reasonable causes, marked “ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY” and valid for one year from shipment.

Article 17 If a certificate is stolen, lost or damaged, the exporter may apply for a certified copy marked «CERTIFIED TRUE COPY of the original Certificate of Origin number        dated      », valid as the original. Upon submission of the copy the original becomes invalid; if the original has been used, the copy is invalid.

Article 18 Applications for preferential tariff rates shall be accompanied by the certificate, commercial invoice and full transport documents. Goods transported through third countries require certificates from those countries' customs. No certificate is required where electronic data have been received; no third-country certificate is required where transport documents satisfy direct consignment rules.

Article 19 Where no valid certificate is submitted at declaration, a supplementary declaration with a statement of origin (Annex 3) shall be made before customs formalities are completed. With security provided, Customs shall proceed except where the provision of security is prohibited. No adjustment of collected duties shall be made for applications after formalities are completed.

Article 20 The importer may apply to release the bond within the approved period after supplementary declaration with a valid certificate, or after Customs completes the origin verification confirming originating status.

Article 21 No certificate is required for a consignment with a dutiable value not exceeding 1000 in duty-free value; this shall not apply where the consignment is split to evade these Measures.

Article 22 Customs may request supplementary information or verification from the African country to determine authenticity and origin; release against security may be granted during verification unless otherwise provided.

Article 23 The consignee shall retain documents proving origin for 3 years from completion of customs formalities.

Article 24 Preferential tariff rates shall not apply where: no application or supplementary declaration is made; goods are not originating; the certificate does not comply; the certificate does not match the goods; no verification feedback is received within 6 months or the feedback is insufficient; or other violations occur.

Article 25 Definitions: aquaculture; FOB price; interchangeable materials; generally accepted accounting principles; goods; materials; non-originating goods or materials; originating goods or materials; production; and the WTO Valuation Agreement, being the Agreement on Implementation of Article VII of GATT 1994.

Article 26 These Measures are interpreted by GACC.

Article 27 These Measures shall take effect on 1 May 2026.

Dried chilies from African countries with diplomatic relations with China are cleared for import. Announcement No. 50 of 2026 requires growing areas and processing and packaging facilities to be reviewed by the exporting country and approved by China Customs.

Shipments must be free of nine quarantine pests of concern to China, including bean weevil, cowpea weevil and larger grain borer, and growing areas must keep pest-control records for at least two years, with wood packaging meeting ISPM 15 requirements.

Before export, 2% of each consignment is sampled, reduced to 1% if no problems occur; on arrival, China Customs checks the certificate and marking, and consignments with live pests, soil or from unregistered sites are refused, destroyed or given quarantine treatment.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China, and based on risk assessment, imports of dried chilies from African countries with diplomatic relations with China that meet the following requirements are permitted.

I. Inspection and quarantine basis

(1) The Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) The Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(3) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations.

II. Name of commodity permitted for entry

Dried chilies in this Announcement refer to products made from edible chili peppers (Capsicum spp.) grown in the exporting country and processed by natural sun-drying or other drying techniques.

III. Place of production

Chili-producing areas in African countries with diplomatic relations with China.

IV. Enterprise registration

Chili-growing areas for export to China (hereinafter growing areas) and processing and packaging facilities shall be reviewed by the competent authority of the exporting country (hereinafter the exporting country's official authority) and approved and registered by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side). Registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so that accurate traceability is possible when dried chilies do not comply with this Announcement. Before trade begins, the exporting country's official authority shall provide the Chinese side with the list of registered enterprises, which, after review and approval by the Chinese side, will be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs.

V. Quarantine pests of concern to China

(1) Bean weevil Acanthoscelides obtectus

(2) Cowpea weevil Callosobruchus maculatus

(3) Larger grain borer Trogoderma granarium

(4) Potato spindle tuber viroid Pospiviroid fusituberis (PSTVd)

(5) Tomato brown rugose fruit virus Tobamovirus fructirugosum (ToBRFV)

(6) Zebra chip disease bacterium Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum

(7) Tomato canker bacterium Clavibacter michiganensis subsp. michiganensis

(8) Tomato bacterial speck bacterium Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato

(9) Broomrape Orobanche spp.

VI. Pre-export management

(1) Management of growing areas.

(1) Growing areas shall, under the supervision of the exporting country's official authority, establish a sound quality management system and traceability system, implement good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain growing hygiene conditions such as the absence of pollution sources affecting dried chili production and timely removal of plant debris, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and agronomic operations.

(2) Growing areas shall, under the supervision of the exporting country's official authority, carry out pest monitoring and integrated management targeting the quarantine pests of concern to China. Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technicians, who shall receive training from the exporting country's official authority or its authorised body. Growing areas shall keep records of pest monitoring and control for at least two years and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Such records shall include at least the monitoring time, the names of pests found, the control measures taken, and details of the chemicals used, including the date of use, active ingredient and concentration.

(2) Management of processing and packaging.

(1) Registered processing and packaging facilities for dried chilies for export to China shall establish a traceability system ensuring that the chilies can be traced back to the registered growing area, recording the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source growing area, the export date, export quantity, destination country, and the conveyance or container number.

(2) The processing, packaging, storage and shipment of dried chilies for export to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the exporting country's official authority or its authorised officers.

(3) Processing and packaging facilities shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors, and shall have a raw material area and a finished product warehouse.

(4) The storage, processing, packaging and warehousing areas for dried chilies shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, isolated from living areas at an appropriate distance.

(5) During processing, dried chilies shall undergo manual sorting, screening, cleaning and impurity removal to ensure freedom from insects, plant debris, soil and other foreign matter.

(3) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic, unused and comply with China's relevant phytosanitary requirements. If wood packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall be marked in Chinese or English with the product name, country, place of origin, the growing area name or registration number, and the processing and packaging facility name or registration number for traceability. Each packing box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

(3) Means of transport or containers carrying dried chilies for export to China must be inspected at loading to ensure good hygienic conditions. Seals shall be affixed and shall be intact on arrival at the Chinese entry port.

(4) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Before export, the exporting country's official authority shall inspect and quarantine dried chilies for export to China. Only those that pass and are confirmed to meet this Announcement are allowed to be exported to China.

(2) During the first two years of trade, the exporting country's official authority shall sample 2% of each consignment of dried chilies for export to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling ratio shall be reduced to 1%.

(3) If quarantine pests of concern to China, plant debris or soil are found, the whole consignment shall not be exported to China. The exporting country's official authority shall identify the cause and take improvement measures, and shall keep seizure records and provide them to the Chinese side upon request.

(5) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For dried chilies that pass quarantine, the exporting country's official authority shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the names or registration numbers of the growing area and the processing and packaging facility, and containing the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Dried Chilies from Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.”

Consignments in which live pests are found shall undergo fumigation or other treatment before export, and the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment method, the name of the chemical, the dosage, the time and the temperature.

(2) For tomato brown rugose fruit virus, if the country or area of origin is free of the virus, the exporting country's official authority shall state in the additional declaration of the phytosanitary certificate: “This consignment is originated from (country or area), where is free of ToBRFV.”

If the country or area of origin has the virus, the exporting country's official authority shall conduct testing before export to ensure the product is free of the virus and attach an official test report.

(3) Before trade begins, the exporting country's official authority shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen of the phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and record-filing.

VII. Entry inspection and quarantine and handling of non-compliance

When dried chilies for export to China arrive at the Chinese entry port, China Customs shall conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(1) Verification of certificates and markings.

(1) Verify that the phytosanitary certificate complies with Item (V) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(2) Verify that the markings on packing boxes and pallets comply with Item (III) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(2) Cargo inspection.

In accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules, China Customs shall inspect and quarantine imported dried chilies, and those that pass shall be permitted entry.

(3) Handling of non-compliance.

(1) If the chilies come from an unregistered growing area or processing and packaging facility, the consignment shall not be permitted entry.

(2) If no valid phytosanitary certificate is attached, the consignment shall be refused or destroyed.

(3) If quarantine pests of concern to China or other live quarantine pests are found, or plant debris or soil is found, the consignment shall be refused, destroyed or given quarantine treatment.

(4) If the chilies fail to comply with China's food safety laws, regulations and national standards, the consignment shall be refused or destroyed.

(5) If any of the above non-compliance is found, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the exporting country's official authority and may suspend imports of dried chilies from the relevant growing areas and facilities as appropriate. The exporting country's official authority shall identify the cause and take improvement measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side shall decide whether to lift the suspension based on the results of rectification.

VIII. Other requirements

Dried chilies from Rwanda, Egypt, Uganda and Tanzania have already been granted quarantine access to China, and the relevant phytosanitary requirements shall be implemented in accordance with GACC Announcement No. 53 of 2021, No. 67 of 2024, No. 126 of 2024 and No. 134 of 2024.

This Announcement shall enter into force on 1 May 2026.

Hereby announced.

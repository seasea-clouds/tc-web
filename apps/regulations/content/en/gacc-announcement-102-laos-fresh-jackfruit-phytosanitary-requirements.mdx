Yes, they can enter. Announcement No. 102 admits fresh jackfruits from Laos: the Lao side approves orchards and packing houses, the Chinese side reviews, registers and publishes the list; growth is monitored against international standards, each fruit is washed and brushed at the packing house, and shipping to China follows only once registration and certificate are in place.

For a consignment, check the orchard and packing house registration numbers in China and the pre-export monitoring records; a Lao phytosanitary certificate travels with the goods, its additional declaration carrying the full protocol sentence; in the first two years two per cent of each lot is sampled and suspect fruit is cut open; every box and pallet is marked in Chinese or English with the destination statement.

At the Chinese port of entry, China Customs checks the permit, certificate and marks and carries out inspection and quarantine. Fruit from unregistered orchards or packing houses is denied entry; lots carrying live pests of concern to China, soil or plant residues are returned, destroyed or treated; lots failing Chinese food safety standards are returned or destroyed.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic (hereinafter the Lao side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh jackfruits from Laos to China, fresh jackfruits from Laos meeting the following requirements are permitted for import into China from the date of this Announcement:

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) The Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) The Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(c) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(d) The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruit;

(e) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Laos to China.

II. Permitted Commodity

Fresh jackfruits (hereinafter referred to as jackfruits), scientific name Artocarpus heterophyllus, English name Jackfruit.

III. Permitted Production Area

Jackfruit production areas in Laos.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses exporting jackfruits to China shall be examined by the Lao side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information, including names, addresses and registration numbers, shall allow accurate tracing when a consignment does not comply with this Announcement. The Lao side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side which, after review and approval, shall publish it on the GACC website and update it regularly.

V. Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Spiralling whitefly Aleurodicus dispersus

(2) Melon fly Bactrocera umbrosa

(3) Pineapple mealybug Dysmicoccus neobrevipes

(4) Pacific mealybug Planococcus minor

(5) Coffee mealybug Planococcus lilacinus

VI. Pre-shipment Requirements

(a) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting to China shall, under Lao supervision, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, apply good agricultural practices (GAP), and maintain orchard hygiene such as keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting fruit production and promptly removing damaged, fallen and rotten fruit. Integrated pest management (IPM) shall be applied, including regular pest monitoring surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and cultural practices.

(2) The Lao side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organise orchard monitoring throughout the year. For the melon fly, pheromone traps shall be hung in the orchards and yellow sticky traps used for physical monitoring. For the spiralling whitefly, light traps or yellow sticky traps shall be used for physical monitoring. For scale insects and other pests, orchard monitoring shall be carried out at least once every 15 days from flowering to harvest, checking the fruit, stems and leaves for scale insects.

(3) Where pests or their symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated control measures including chemical, physical or biological control shall be taken to ensure that jackfruits exported to China are free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side. Such integrated measures shall be approved by the Lao side and, before trade begins, provided by the Lao side to the Chinese side on request.

(4) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel, who shall have received training from the Lao side or its authorised institutions.

(5) The Lao side shall retain orchard pest monitoring and control records and provide them to the Chinese side on request. The control records shall include the names of all chemical agents used during the growing season, the dates of use, the active ingredients and the concentrations.

(b) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of jackfruits exported to China shall take place under the quarantine supervision of the Lao side or its authorised officers.

(2) Packing houses for jackfruits exported to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors and separate areas for raw material and finished products.

(3) The storage, processing and handling areas for jackfruits exported to China shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and shall be separated from living quarters by appropriate measures and distance.

(4) During packaging, jackfruits exported to China shall be manually selected, graded and cleaned to remove diseased, insect-infested, rotten and misshapen fruit, twigs and leaves or other plant residues and soil. High-pressure air guns or water guns shall be used to blow, spray and brush the fruit surface, and where necessary a soft clean cotton cloth shall be used to wipe the surface by hand, so as to remove insects, eggs and pathogen spores attached to the fruit.

(5) Packaged jackfruits requiring storage shall be placed in a warehouse immediately and stored separately to avoid re-infestation by pests.

(6) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system so that jackfruits exported to China can be traced to registered orchards, recording the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the quantity, the export date, the export quantity, the country of destination and the container number.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging material shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall meet the relevant Chinese plant quarantine and hygiene requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each box shall bear, in Chinese or in English, traceable information such as the type and variety of fruit, the exporting country, the place of origin (province and city), and the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packing house. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or in English with “Destined for the People's Republic of China” or “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

(3) Containers holding jackfruits exported to China shall be checked at loading for satisfactory hygienic conditions. The containers shall be sealed, and the seal shall remain intact on arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(d) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) During the first two years after the Protocol takes effect, the Lao side shall sample 2% of every lot of jackfruits exported to China; where a lot contains fewer than 100 fruit, all of them shall be sampled. Suspect fruit found during the check shall be cut open for inspection. If no phytosanitary problems occur within those two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, twigs and leaves, or soil are found, the whole lot shall not be exported to China and, depending on the circumstances, exports of jackfruits to China from the orchard or packing house concerned may be suspended for the remainder of the export season. The Lao side shall investigate the cause and take corrective measures, and shall retain the detection records and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(e) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For jackfruits that pass inspection, the Lao side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the names or registration numbers of the orchard and packing house and carrying the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Lao PDR to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China.” (this consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Lao PDR to China and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side).

(2) Before trade begins, the Lao side shall provide a specimen phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side for confirmation and record.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Treatment of Non-compliance

On arrival at a Chinese port of entry, jackfruits exported to China shall be subject to inspection and quarantine by China Customs in accordance with the following requirements.

(a) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verification that an Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit has been obtained for the imported jackfruits.

(2) Verification that the phytosanitary certificate complies with paragraph (e) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verification that the marks on the boxes or wooden packaging comply with paragraph (c) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(b) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Jackfruits exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruit.

(2) On arrival at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall examine the documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this Announcement. Lots passing inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(c) Treatment of non-compliance.

(1) Where jackfruits are found to come from unregistered orchards or packing houses, the lot shall be denied entry.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or other quarantine pests newly occurring in Laos, or soil or plant residues, are found, the lot shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(3) Where jackfruits fail to comply with Chinese national food safety laws, regulations and standards, the lot shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

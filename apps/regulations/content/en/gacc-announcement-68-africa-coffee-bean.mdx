Coffee beans from African countries with diplomatic relations with China are cleared for import. Announcement No. 68, issued by the General Administration of Customs on 20 May 2026, allows imports of coffee beans that meet the phytosanitary requirements, with production and processing enterprises subject to review by the exporting country and approval by China Customs.

Coffee beans for export to China must be free of four quarantine pests of concern to China, including the coffee berry borer, the larger grain borer, the cacao canker fungus and the coffee berry anthracnose fungus. Plantations must keep pest-control records for at least two years, and wood packaging must meet ISPM 15 requirements.

Before export, shipments are sampled at no less than 2% in the first two years and may be reduced to no less than 1% if no phytosanitary problems occur; on arrival, China Customs checks the certificate and marking, and consignments with live pests, soil or from unregistered enterprises are refused, destroyed or given quarantine treatment.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China, and following risk assessment, imports of coffee beans from African countries with diplomatic relations with China that meet the following requirements are permitted.

I. Inspection and quarantine basis

(1) The Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) The Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(3) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations.

II. Name of commodity permitted for entry

Coffee Beans, as used in this Announcement, refer to the dehulled, unroasted beans of coffee (Coffea L.) planted and processed in the exporting country, with the outer skin, pulp, parchment and silver skin removed, and produced through processes such as drying and screening.

III. Place of origin

African countries with diplomatic relations with China.

IV. Enterprise registration

Coffee bean production and processing enterprises for export to China shall be reviewed by the competent authority of the exporting country (hereinafter the exporting country's official authority) and approved and registered by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side). Registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so that accurate traceability is possible when the coffee beans do not comply with this Announcement. Before trade begins, the exporting country's official authority shall provide the Chinese side with the list of registered enterprises, which, after review and approval by the Chinese side, will be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs.

V. Quarantine pests of concern to China

(1) Coffee berry borer Hypothenemus hampei

(2) Larger grain borer Prostephanus truncatus

(3) Cacao canker fungus Nectria rigidiuscula

(4) Coffee berry anthracnose fungus Colletotrichum kahawae

VI. Pre-export management

(1) Plantation management.

(1) Plantations shall, under the supervision of the exporting country's official authority, establish a sound quality management system and traceability system, implement good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain plantation hygiene conditions such as the absence of pollution sources nearby and timely removal of plant debris, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and agronomic operations.

(2) Plantations shall, under the supervision of the exporting country's official authority, carry out pest monitoring and integrated control management targeting the quarantine pests of concern to China. Pest monitoring and control shall be implemented under the guidance of professional technicians, who shall receive training from the exporting country's official authority or its authorised body. Plantations shall keep records of pest monitoring and control for at least two years and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Such records shall include at least details of the monitoring time, the names of pests found, the control measures taken, and the date of use, active ingredient and concentration of the chemicals used. In pest control work, pesticides banned by the Chinese side shall not be used.

(2) Processing management.

(1) Production and processing enterprises shall establish a traceability system to ensure that coffee beans for export to China can be traced back to the plantation.

(2) The processes of processing, packaging, storage and shipment shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the exporting country's official authority or its authorised officers, and the relevant areas shall be relatively independent, rationally laid out, clean and hygienic, with hardened floors, and isolated from living areas at an appropriate distance.

(3) During processing, manual sorting, screening, cleaning and impurity removal shall be carried out to ensure freedom from mouldy beans, insect-damaged beans, and impurities such as live insects, eggs, soil, weed seeds, plant debris and gravel.

(3) Packaging requirements.

(1) Raw and finished coffee beans shall be stored separately to prevent contamination. Coffee beans shall be packaged in clean, hygienic, new materials that meet China's phytosanitary requirements. If wood packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall be marked in Chinese or English with the product name, country, place of origin, and the name and registration number of the production and processing enterprise for traceability. Each packing box and pallet shall bear the marking “Exported to the People's Republic of China”.

(3) Means of transport or containers carrying coffee beans for export to China must be inspected at the time of loading to ensure good hygienic conditions. Seals shall be affixed to the means of transport or containers and shall be intact on arrival at the Chinese entry port.

(4) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Before export, the exporting country's official authority shall conduct inspection and quarantine on coffee beans for export to China. Only coffee beans that pass inspection and quarantine by the exporting country's official authority and are confirmed to meet the requirements of this Announcement are allowed to be exported to China.

(2) During the first two years of trade, the exporting country's official authority shall sample no less than 2% of each consignment of coffee beans for export to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling ratio may be adjusted to no less than 1%.

(3) If quarantine pests of concern to China, plant debris or soil are found, the whole consignment shall not be exported to China. The exporting country's official authority shall identify the cause and take improvement measures, and shall keep seizure records and provide them to the Chinese side upon request.

(5) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For coffee beans that pass quarantine, the exporting country's official authority shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the name or registration number of the production and processing enterprise and containing the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Coffee Bean from Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.”

Consignments in which live pests are found shall undergo fumigation or other treatment before export, and the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment method, the name of the chemical, the dosage, the time and the temperature.

(2) Before trade begins, the exporting country's official authority shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen of the phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and record-filing.

VII. Entry inspection and quarantine and handling of non-compliance

When coffee beans for export to China arrive at the Chinese entry port, China Customs shall conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(1) Verification of certificates and markings.

(1) Verify that the accompanying phytosanitary certificate is authentic and valid.

(2) Verify that the markings on the packing boxes and pallets comply with the provisions of Item (III) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(2) Cargo inspection.

In accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules, China Customs shall inspect and quarantine imported coffee beans, and those that pass inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(3) Handling of non-compliance.

(1) If the beans are found to come from an unregistered production or processing enterprise, the consignment shall be refused or destroyed.

(2) If no valid phytosanitary certificate is attached, the consignment shall be refused or destroyed.

(3) If quarantine pests of concern to China or other live quarantine pests are found, or plant debris or soil is found, the consignment shall be refused, destroyed or given quarantine treatment.

(4) If the beans are found not to comply with China's food safety laws, regulations and national standards, the consignment shall be refused or destroyed.

(5) Any other case not meeting China's entry quarantine requirements shall be handled in accordance with the relevant laws and regulations.

If any of the above non-compliance is found, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the exporting country's official authority and may suspend imports of coffee beans from the relevant production and processing enterprises as appropriate. The exporting country's official authority shall identify the cause and take improvement measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side shall decide whether to lift the suspension based on the results of the rectification by the exporting country's official authority.

VIII. Other requirements

Coffee beans from Ethiopia and Burundi have already been granted quarantine access to China, and the relevant phytosanitary requirements for import shall be implemented in accordance with GACC Announcement No. 142 of 2023 and No. 117 of 2024.

This Announcement shall enter into force on 20 July 2026.

Hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

20 May 2026

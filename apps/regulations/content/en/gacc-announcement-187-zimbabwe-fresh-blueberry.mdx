Fresh blueberries from Zimbabwe are now allowed for export to China. General Administration of Customs Announcement No. 187 of 2025 takes effect on the date of issuance. The blueberry, scientific name Vaccinium L., comes from blueberry production areas of Zimbabwe, and its orchards, packhouses and quarantine treatment facilities must be audited by Zimbabwe and approved and registered by China.

Orchards implement GAP and IPM and monitor 5 quarantine pests including the Mediterranean fruit fly from flowering to harvest: fruit flies are trapped at 1 trap per 5 hectares, scale insects are monitored every 2 weeks, and cold treatment before export (1.11°C/15 days, 1 of 3 options) or methyl bromide fumigation for air shipments eliminates fruit flies.

Each consignment carries a phytosanitary certificate with the required English declaration, and packages are labelled in Chinese or English. Certificates and markings are verified and quarantine inspection is carried out at the port of entry; non-compliant consignments are returned, destroyed or treated.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Blueberries from Zimbabwe to China agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People’s Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as “China”) and the Ministry of Lands, Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Rural Development of the Republic of Zimbabwe (hereinafter referred to as “Zimbabwe”), fresh blueberries from Zimbabwe meeting the following relevant requirements are allowed to be imported as of the date of issuance.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(1) Biosecurity Law of the People’s Republic of China;

(2) Law of the People’s Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its Implementing Regulations;

(3) Food Safety Law of the People’s Republic of China and its Implementing Regulations;

(4) Measures for the Inspection, Quarantine and Supervision of Imported Fruits;

(5) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Blueberries from Zimbabwe to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People’s Republic of China and the Ministry of Lands, Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Rural Development of the Republic of Zimbabwe.

II. Name of the Commodity Permitted for Import

Fresh blueberries (hereinafter referred to as blueberries), scientific name Vaccinium L., English name Blueberry.

III. Places of Origin Permitted

Blueberry production areas of Zimbabwe.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packhouses

Orchards, packhouses and quarantine treatment facilities for blueberries exported to China must be audited by Zimbabwe and approved and registered by China. The registration information includes the name, address and registration code, so that goods not complying with the relevant provisions of this announcement can be accurately traced. Zimbabwe shall provide the registration list to China before the export season each year.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Mediterranean fruit fly (Ceratitis capitata)

(2) Mango fruit fly (Ceratitis cosyra)

(3) African tortoise wax scale (Ceroplastes destructor)

(4) Long-tailed mealybug (Pseudococcus longispinus)

(5) Obscure mealybug (Pseudococcus viburni)

VI. Pre-export Management

(1) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting blueberries to China shall establish and implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) under the supervision of Zimbabwe, including maintaining orchard hygiene and the timely removal of fallen fruit, and shall implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), such as pest surveys, monitoring, chemical or biological control and other farming control measures, to avoid or minimize the occurrence of quarantine pests of concern to China on blueberries.

(2) Orchards exporting blueberries to China must have their technical personnel trained or guided by Zimbabwe or its authorized institutions to carry out monitoring surveys of quarantine pests of concern to China; if any such pest is found, corresponding control measures shall be taken immediately.

(3) Orchards exporting blueberries to China shall keep records of pest monitoring surveys and control for at least 2 years and provide them to China upon request. Such records shall include at least the monitoring time, the names of the pests found and the control measures taken; chemical control records shall include detailed information on all chemicals used during the growing season, such as their names, active ingredients, concentrations and application dates.

(4) For the Mediterranean fruit fly and the mango fruit fly, orchards exporting blueberries to China must adopt comprehensive field management measures, including fruit fly monitoring and trapping and chemical or biological control, to reduce fruit fly population density. Monitoring shall start from flowering and last until the end of blueberry harvest. Protein baits (such as Capilure for female fruit flies) and sex pheromone lures (such as Trimedlure or Ego lure for male fruit flies) shall be used to monitor and trap fruit fly populations and shall be replenished and maintained according to the product instructions. 1 trap shall be set per 5 hectares, traps shall be checked at least once every 2 weeks, and effective control measures shall be taken in a timely manner.

(5) For other quarantine pests such as the African tortoise wax scale, the long-tailed mealybug and the obscure mealybug, field monitoring surveys must be conducted in orchards exporting blueberries to China during the growing season. From budbreak to harvest, monitoring shall be carried out at least once every 2 weeks, focusing on branches, stems, leaves and fruit clusters for pest infestation. If scale insects or other quarantine pests of concern to China are detected, comprehensive management measures including chemical or biological control shall be taken immediately.

(2) Packhouse management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of blueberries must be carried out under the supervision of Zimbabwe or its authorized institutions.

(2) During packing, blueberries must be culled, sorted and graded to ensure that they are free from insects, mites, rotten fruit, twigs, leaves, roots and soil and do not carry any quarantine pest of concern to China.

(3) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall meet the relevant phytosanitary requirements of China. If wooden packaging materials are used, they must comply with International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(4) Packaged blueberries that need to be stored shall be placed immediately into warehouses and kept separately to avoid re-infestation by pests. Each package must be marked with the fruit name, exporting country, place of origin (region or county), orchard name or registration code, and packhouse name or registration code. Each package and pallet must be marked in Chinese or English with “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

(5) Before loading blueberries for export to China, containers shall be checked to ensure good hygienic conditions, and necessary pest-proofing measures shall be taken during container loading.

(3) Quarantine treatment requirements.

Blueberries exported to China shall be subjected to cold treatment or fumigation under the supervision of Zimbabwe or its authorized personnel to eliminate fruit flies.

(1) Cold treatment. Cold treatment shall be conducted in accordance with the Operational Procedures for Cold Treatment before Export (see Annex 1), the Operational Procedures for Cold Treatment in Transit (see Annex 2) and the Supplementary Requirements for Cold Treatment in Transit through Third Countries (see Annex 3). The cold treatment parameters must meet any one of the following conditions:

1.11°C or below (pulp temperature) for 15 consecutive days or more;

1.67°C or below (pulp temperature) for 17 consecutive days or more;

2.22°C or below (pulp temperature) for 21 consecutive days or more.

(2) Fumigation treatment. Blueberries exported to China by air shall be fumigated with methyl bromide by personnel authorized by Zimbabwe before shipment. The fumigation parameters are as follows: the temperature shall be above 21.1 °C, the fumigant dosage shall be 32 g/m3, and the duration of fumigation under normal atmospheric pressure shall be not less than 2 h; the minimum concentration during fumigation shall be not less than 26 g/m3 after 0.5 h and not less than 16 g/m3 after 2 h.

(4) Pre-export quarantine.

(1) Zimbabwe or its authorized personnel shall sample and inspect each consignment of blueberries exported to China at a rate of 2% per consignment. If no phytosanitary problems have occurred within 2 years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China are found, the consignment shall not be allowed for export to China. Zimbabwe shall identify the causes, take corrective measures, keep the inspection records and provide them to China upon request.

(5) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For blueberries that have passed quarantine inspection, Zimbabwe shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the names or registration codes of the orchard and the packhouse and the container number, with the additional declaration: „This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Blueberries from Zimbabwe to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.“

(2) For consignments undergoing cold treatment before export, the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment temperature, duration and the name or code of the treatment facility. For consignments undergoing cold treatment in transit, “Cold treatment in transit” shall be stated on the phytosanitary certificate, together with the treatment temperature, start time, duration, container number and seal number.

(3) For consignments undergoing fumigation before export, the methyl bromide dosage, duration and treatment temperature shall be stated in the treatment section of the phytosanitary certificate.

(4) Zimbabwe shall provide the model phytosanitary certificate to China before the start of trade for record and verification.

VII. Inspection and Quarantine upon Entry and Handling of Non-conformities

When blueberries exported to China arrive at a port of entry in China, China Customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(1) Verification of certificates and markings.

(1) Verify whether the imported blueberries have obtained the Quarantine Permit for Entry of Animals and Plants.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of items (3) and (5) of Article VI.

(3) Verify whether the packaging complies with the provisions of item (2) of Article VI.

(2) Inspection and quarantine upon entry.

(1) Blueberries exported to China shall enter through ports of entry approved by China for fruit imports.

(2) Inspection and quarantine shall be carried out in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and other relevant provisions; consignments that pass inspection and quarantine shall be allowed entry.

(3) Handling of non-conformities.

(1) If a consignment is found to originate from unregistered orchards, packhouses or quarantine treatment facilities, it shall not be allowed entry.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China or newly occurring quarantine pests of Zimbabwe are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to pest-eliminating treatment.

(3) If a consignment is found not to comply with Chinese food safety laws and regulations and national standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

22 September 2025

Money in China bonded e-commerce does not leave the country order by order. How soon consumer payments reach the onshore entity depends on the platform settlement cycle: they settle in batches, not per order. Commissions are paid onshore in yuan with individual income tax withheld. Goods payments are pooled into batches and remitted against five documents. Two stages of segregation, three segments of flow, with every step recorded in a node ledger that shows the break point, the owner, and the expected completion date.

Start with the three questions: how money travels from the consumer to the parent company, how promoter commissions are calculated, paid, and withheld, and what must be in place on the money side before launch. This path applies to both bonded stocking and direct import, the two cross-border retail import channels: bonded stocking puts goods into a bonded warehouse first and ships from there after an order; direct import clears customs at the port against the order. The nodes differ, the money rules do not: both go through platform escrow, onshore settlement, and remittance on documents. Two-stage segregation means the onshore and offshore channels share no node; three-segment flow means consumer to onshore entity, onshore to offshore entity, and offshore entity to overseas promoters.

## Consumer Money Reaches Platform Escrow First, Then Settles to the Onshore Entity in Batches

The onshore line runs from consumer payment all the way to the onshore commission: the consumer pays, platform escrow pools the funds, the onshore entity settles in batches, and the onshore commission is paid in yuan with individual income tax withheld. Payment entry points are the channels consumers use, such as WeChat Pay, Alipay, and UnionPay; money does not go straight into the brand's account but first into platform escrow and pooling.

Platform escrow is handled by a licensed payment institution, and customer reserve funds may not be misappropriated; that is the floor set by the Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Non-Bank Payment Institutions (State Council Order No. 768). For the brand, the key at this stage is to confirm the settlement entity, settlement cycle, and settlement currency, and to copy the platform settlement rules into its own money ledger. A collection account alone does not settle the questions of entity and cycle.

The first break point sits between platform escrow and the onshore entity: the platform settles in batches by cycle, not per order. That break point sets the rhythm for everything downstream; commission payouts, goods payment pooling, and document archiving all run by batch, not by order. Collections are pooled within the cycle, settlement releases batches under platform rules, and the brand receives a batch statement rather than an order-by-order stream.

## Why You Cannot Remit Overseas Order by Order

An order-by-order remittance has no customs declaration or contract behind it; in foreign exchange terms it is an outflow without a trade background, and a later audit cannot patch it up. The rule is direct: do not pay overseas individuals per order; settle to the onshore entity first, then remit outward in unified cycles; align remittance batches with customs declaration batches so reconciliation works batch by batch. The second break point sits between the onshore entity and the offshore entity: remit against complete documents, with no skipped steps.

Outward remittance and per-order profit sharing must run on two separate channels. Remittance is backed by contracts and customs declarations; per-order profit sharing has no trade background. Mix the two and the first question from the bank about trade background leaves you without an answer. Hui Fa [2020] No. 14, the Guidelines for Current Account Foreign Exchange Business (2020 Edition), requires banks to review the authenticity and compliance of transaction documents; in practice, that means every remittance batch carries a set of documents that matches item by item.

Check the three questions against this diagram: the onshore commission runs end to end, remittance documents are complete, and the two channels share no node. Once a single payment appears on both channels, segregation is already broken.

## Commission and Goods Payment Are Two Kinds of Money on Two Channels

Onshore commission and overseas compensation run on different channels, with different tax bases, currencies, and paying entities: onshore commission is paid in yuan, and individual income tax is withheld and paid by the onshore entity; overseas compensation is paid by the offshore entity under the global compensation arrangement. Each side keeps its own books and the two are never linked at order level.

Commission is calculated only on attributed transaction value. The promoter receives a promotion service fee, not pay per head or per tier. That rule determines the commission ledger: first attribution, then transaction value times rate, then tax withholding, with a record at every step. When commission and goods payment go through the same pocket, neither the tax side nor the foreign exchange side can explain it; that is the most common and hardest error to fix.

The overseas compensation system itself does not need a structural change for China; what changes is that the China side settles first and remits later. The onshore entity receives goods payments by batch, pays promotion service fees to promoters under the agreement and withholds individual income tax, then remits the remaining goods payment against its trade background. On the books, commission and goods payment for the same order always sit in two separate ledgers.

## Three Segments in Sequence, Never Skipped or Merged

The three fund flows run in sequence, with no skipped segment and no merging. Segment one runs from the consumer to the onshore entity, settled in platform batches, with commission paid onshore. Segment two runs from the onshore entity to the offshore entity, by trade remittance with all five documents complete. Segment three runs from the offshore entity to overseas promoters, paid from the global fund pool. Each segment marks its settlement cycle and currency.

Remittance batches in segment two align with customs declaration batches and reconcile batch by batch. The three segments share no node, and onshore commission and overseas compensation are never linked at order level. Abstract as it sounds, the discipline comes down to three columns in the ledger: which segment, which batch, which set of documents.

## Five Documents to Complete Before Remittance

Five documents must be complete before remittance; missing one blocks the whole batch: contract, invoice, customs declaration, duty-paid voucher, and tax filing. These five map one to one to the trade document flow and are completed item by item at remittance. Each batch is archived and numbered with its documents, so reconciliation can pull the file by batch and see at a glance which document is missing.

Document matching must make the trade background checkable item by item. The contract matches the two parties, the invoice matches value and quantity, the customs declaration matches the batch, and the duty-paid voucher and tax filing match the tax base. Miss one and the bank does not release the payment; the whole batch stops where it is.

## The Node Ledger Puts Break Point, Owner, and Date on One Line

The node ledger breaks the fund segregation diagram into traceable nodes, one node per line, recording channel, node name, node sequence, owner, break-point marker, expected completion date, and status. The owner names the responsible party, such as our customs team, the bonded warehouse, domestic delivery, the port site, or you. The break-point marker states what blocks the node; for example, zone entry tally blocked on awaiting warehouse scan code means the bonded warehouse has not scanned yet.

## The Settlement Batch Ledger Makes Rate and Conversion Verifiable Line by Line

The settlement batch ledger holds one batch per line, with tabs for all batches, pending FX settlement, and settled. Each line records the original-currency amount, currency, FX rate, yuan equivalent, settlement date, and document attachments. The rate and the converted amount sit on the same line, so one multiplication verifies them; status is either pending FX settlement or settled, and attachments are attached by batch, not by order. Every three-segment flow finally lands on a settlement batch, and only when original currency, rate, yuan amount, and attachments are all complete can the line reconcile.

## What Must Be in Place on the Money Side Before Launch

Before launch, settle three things.

- Confirm the platform settlement entity, cycle, and currency, and write the first break-point rule into the contract and system configuration.
- Confirm the onshore entity's collection, FX settlement, and withholding process so commission payouts and individual income tax filings reconcile in one set of books.
- Build the node ledger and settlement batch ledger, and set the archiving and numbering rules for the five documents before discussing a launch date.

If the current system splits commission per order, the China side must change to settle onshore first and remit by cycle. Every batch can reconcile and be audited, and this does not need to wait until after go-live; the three differences in settlement basis, document ownership, and tax bearing are best written down at the design stage.

To close: commission and goods payment run on two channels so both tax and foreign exchange stay clear; if the system splits commission per order, what to change and how is laid out in the batches and ledgers above. Remittance follows China's cross-border payment and tax rules, profit sharing runs the three segments, and each line keeps its own books. The demo ledger has 12 records, and its batch numbers, amounts, and dates are illustrative; rows 09-B and 07-F differ from rate multiplication by 40.00 and 1.00 yuan, row 08-C is rounded to whole yuan, and the text uses the multiplied values. Reconcile line by line, not by the total. Actual FX settlement follows the bank and SAFE requirements in force and the rate on the settlement date.

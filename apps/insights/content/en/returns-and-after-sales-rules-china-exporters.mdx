A return escapes duty only when two statutory clocks line up: the return application is filed within 30 days of the release of the declaration list, and the goods reach the original customs supervision site, the original special customs supervision area, or a bonded logistics center (Type B) within 45 days. Meet both and the duty is not levied, while the consumer's cumulative annual transaction amount is adjusted. The basis is GACC Announcement No. 45 of 2020, a deadline counted in days, not a service level you can negotiate with a warehouse.

## How the two clocks run

The clock starts on the day of release, not the day the order was placed, shipped, or handed to a carrier for the return. From release, day 30 closes the application and day 45 closes the arrival at the original supervision site. The three documents and the bonded warehouse ledger describe the same event: order, payment, logistics, and ledger. If any one of them disagrees, the return stalls even inside the window.

Day 30 governs the application. The consumer asks for a return, you accept it and issue a return order; the whole declaration or only part of it can go back, because Article 2 of Announcement No. 45 allows all or part of the goods on the original declaration list to be returned. Partial returns are the easiest to overlook: two units leave a declaration, the rest keep their original status, and duty and quota are adjusted only for what actually returns.

Day 45 governs where the goods end up. The returned goods must reach the original customs supervision site, the original special customs supervision area, or a bonded logistics center (Type B); a signature at any domestic warehouse is not enough. Arrive in time and the duty is not levied and the quota is adjusted. Miss the day-30 application and the exemption already has a missing leg even if the goods arrive before day 45, so duty is levied on the original import basis. The stretch from day 31 to day 45 is where this rule is most often misread.

Neither clock is negotiable. Announcement No. 45 sets 30 days for the application and 45 days for arrival, leaving no discretion to a platform or a warehouse. Document No. 486 of 2018 from six ministries places cross-border retail imports under the rules for personal-use inbound articles, and GACC Announcement No. 194 of 2018 ties list-based release and return management to the same declaration data. Read together, a return is not something the after-sales desk can carry alone.

## Where the boundary sits and when performance fails

Start with the seven-day no-reason return. Cross-border goods are supervised as personal-use inbound articles, so whether the seven-day rule applies depends on the nature of the goods and on what the page discloses; do not copy the domestic online-shopping habit. The page has to state the exclusions and the return route before the consumer orders, which removes one layer of dispute later.

Then performance failure. Severe delay, or a short shelf life that was not disclosed and leaves the goods unusable on arrival, counts as a failure; the consumer is then entitled to a return, a refund, or compensation. The test rests on two things: whether the goods still work for the consumer, and whether the page stated the limits. A warehouse signature is only an action, never the conclusion.

Service acceptance and the returns ledger share one record set. What support accepted, what the warehouse received, and what the ledger wrote off all point to the same return order, so monthly reconciliation and a regulatory review can follow one line to the end.

## Three return scenarios and the 24-hour correction

- Partial return: only part of the declaration goes back, duty is not levied, and the annual quota is adjusted for the actual return;
- Expired, damaged, or unsellable onshore goods: send them back or destroy them, and do not leave them aging in the bonded warehouse;
- Exception order correction: amend it within twenty-four hours and log the action in the ledger rather than in a chat thread.

The 24-hour rule is operating discipline, not a statutory clock. The later an exception is corrected, the more likely zone entry, write-off, and reconciliation run into each other; if the amendment never reaches the ledger, the days left in the system keep moving while people cannot match the account, and the only fallback is manual digging.

## The return central warehouse cuts handling to three days

The return central warehouse is industry practice, not a mandatory route for every parcel. Returns are consolidated there for sorting and a first inspection, then routed to zone entry or destruction; handling falls from about 10 days to 3. Returned goods can enter the original special customs supervision area or bonded logistics center (Type B), and the records stay on the same source as the ledger. That last point matters more than saving a few days.

The ledger does more than record returns. It backs the monthly progress figures and settlement reconciliation: how much was returned, how much entered the zone, how much was destroyed, and how much duty and quota moved this month. At month-end those numbers should come straight off the ledger instead of being rebuilt in a spreadsheet.

## Which fields to read on a return row

First, see which step you are on. The zone-entry ledger holds one return per row, with tabs for all returns, within window, and overdue; every row needs the return number, days left, order number, reason, entry mode, and 30-day window. Without all the fields, sorting and chasing cannot work.

Second, see what it records. In the demo, RT260922-001 has 24 days left, was returned to the bonded warehouse for a mismatch with the description, and sits at pending zone entry; RT260921-002 has 18 days left and has entered. Both rows are read against the statutory 30-day window, and the days left are not invented by the system; they are counted forward from the release date.

Third, see where it maps. On screen, the 30-day window is the days left: the order with the fewest days is handled first. Overdue rows need to be pulled out separately, first to see whether a remedy is still possible and only then to assign responsibility.

## After-sales and vouchers: Chinese service, 12315, and duty documents

After-sales is more than a return. Chinese-language service, return and exchange acceptance, complaint handling, and 12315 responses all have to hold up; before a consumer calls 12315, the platform's acceptance records, handling times, and reply lines should already be in place rather than assembled after the complaint arrives.

The voucher trail is easier to miss. Voucher-based service scripts need to be pre-loaded, with the goods supervised as personal-use inbound articles; the consumer should receive the customs duty document and the platform's electronic purchase voucher. The duty document proves the transaction was taxed as a cross-border retail import, and the electronic voucher shows which platform and which order supplied the goods. With both in hand, the return and the refund reconcile.

## One batch of supplements from release to zone entry

Walk one batch of cross-border supplements through the whole route. Suppose a batch is released on 18 September, the consumer asks for a return on 22 September because the goods do not match the description, and only two boxes go back while the rest stay. The store accepts the same day and issues return order RT260922-001; on 23 September the screen shows 24 days left.

Start with the clocks. The application closes on day 30 and arrival closes on day 45, and both dates belong on the return order so the warehouse can schedule inbound work backwards from the arrival deadline. The days left on screen are only those two dates converted into today's reading, not a new deadline.

Next, the actions. Only two boxes return, so the order is written as a partial return; the goods go from the consumer to the return central warehouse, where packaging and batch numbers are checked after sorting, and then back into the bonded warehouse, with the status moving from pending entry to entered. Entry happens within 45 days, duty is not levied, the personal annual quota is adjusted for those two boxes, and the rest of the order stays untouched.

Then time and records. Central-warehouse sorting cuts handling from about 10 days to 3, but those 3 days cannot eat into the 45-day arrival window; at monthly reconciliation, the return order, zone-entry record, duty document, and electronic purchase voucher have to point to the same order. When all four agree, the return is truly closed.

## Wrong order of judgment means rework

Two mistakes are most common. The first treats the 30 days as a handling deadline: finish the work within 30 days and forget the 45-day arrival window. The second treats a partial return as a full return and adjusts duty and quota on the whole order, which immediately puts a mismatched figure on the ledger. The first runs past the arrival deadline; the second writes off goods that never came back.

Do not reverse the sequence either. Fix the return scope and reason first, then the entry mode, and only then change the status in the system; align the return order, order number, batch number, and duty document before the warehouse arranges transport. Click the status to entered while the goods are still on the road, and month-end reconciliation will need rework.

Effective immediately, GACC Announcement No. 244 allows fresh durian (Durio zibethinus Murray) from Laos' durian-producing areas; orchards and packing houses must be Lao-audited and China-registered; list published on the GACC website before each export season; check before ordering.

Orchards: GAP and ISPM 6 monitoring; guava fruit fly traps: one per hectare (at least 3 under 3 ha), two-week checks; durian seed borer: bore-hole and cut-open checks for eggs and larvae; mealybugs: two-week monitoring. Packing houses: hand sorting, grading, cleaning, blown clean with high-pressure air guns or water jets, ISPM 15 wood packaging, cartons marked “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

On arrival, customs check the permit, phytosanitary certificate and markings; 2% per batch in the first two years, then 1% if problem-free; unapproved orchards/packing houses, or quarantine pests, soil or leaves: denied entry, returned, destroyed or disinfested; food-safety failures: returned or destroyed.

In accordance with the laws and regulations of China and the provisions on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh durian from Laos to China agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic (hereinafter referred to as the Lao side), fresh durian from Laos that meets the following requirements is permitted for import as of the date of promulgation.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) The Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) The Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its Implementation Regulations;

(c) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its Implementation Regulations;

(d) The Measures for the Inspection, Quarantine, Supervision and Administration of Imported Fruits;

(e) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic on the Phytosanitary Requirements for Fresh Durian Exported from Laos to China.

II. Name of the Commodity Permitted for Import

Fresh durian (scientific name: Durio zibethinus Murray; English name: Durian; hereinafter referred to as durian).

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Durian-producing areas of Laos.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

The orchards and packing houses for durian exported to China shall be audited by the Lao side and approved and registered by the Chinese side; the registration information shall include the name, address and registration number, so as to ensure accurate traceability when exported goods do not comply with the relevant provisions of this Announcement. Before each annual export season, the Lao side shall provide the list of registered establishments to the Chinese side, and the list shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs after review and approval by the Chinese side.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Guava fruit fly (Bactrocera correcta)

(2) Durian seed borer (Mudaria luteileprosa)

(3) Jack Beardsley mealybug (Pseudococcus jackbeardsley)

(4) Lavender mealybug (Planococcus lilacinus)

(5) Pacific mealybug (Planococcus minor)

VI. Pre-export Management

(a) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting durian to China shall, under the supervision of the Lao side, establish and improve sound quality management and traceability systems, implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), and maintain orchard hygiene, such as ensuring that there are no pollution sources affecting fruit production in the surrounding area and that damaged fruit, dropped fruit and rotten fruit are promptly removed; they shall also implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring, physical, chemical or biological control of pests, and preventive and control measures such as farming operations.

(2) The Lao side shall, in accordance with the requirements of International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), formulate a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organize and implement orchard monitoring. Where pests or their corresponding symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated management measures, including chemical, physical or biological control, shall be taken to ensure that durian exported to China is free from the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.

(3) Monitoring and control of pests shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel. The technical personnel shall have been trained by the Lao side or its authorized institutions.

(4) All registered orchards must keep records of pest monitoring and control and provide them upon request by the Chinese side. The control records shall include detailed information such as the names of all chemical agents used during the growing season, their active ingredients, application dosages and application dates.

(5) For the guava fruit fly, orchards exporting durian to China shall use traps to monitor fruit flies from the flowering stage to the harvest stage. The trap density shall be one trap per hectare, and orchards with an area of less than 3 hectares shall install at least 3 traps, to be checked once every two weeks. Once fruit flies are detected, effective control must be carried out; orchards where control is not thorough shall be prohibited from export.

(6) For the durian seed borer, orchards exporting durian to China shall carry out regular monitoring throughout the growth period, focusing on checking whether there are bore holes on the fruit surface; if there are bore holes, the fruit shall be cut open in time to check the eggs, larvae and damage. In addition, pests shall also be monitored in the orchard by means of light traps, pheromone traps and other methods. Once the durian seed borer is found, appropriate physical, chemical or biological control measures shall be taken immediately to eradicate it.

(7) For pests such as the Jack Beardsley mealybug, the lavender mealybug and the Pacific mealybug, the orchard shall conduct monitoring once every two weeks from the flowering stage to the harvest stage of durian, checking for the occurrence of scale insects on fruits, stems, branches and leaves. In addition to visual inspection, pests should also be monitored at branches and trunks by various physical or chemical methods, and effective control shall be carried out once they are found.

(b) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of durian exported to China shall be carried out under the supervision of the Lao side or its authorized officials.

(2) Packing houses for durian exported to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors, and shall have raw material areas and finished product warehouses.

(3) The functional areas for storing, processing, treating and warehousing durian exported to China shall be relatively independent and reasonably laid out, and shall be isolated from living areas with an appropriate distance.

(4) In the packing process, durian exported to China shall undergo manual sorting, grading and cleaning to remove diseased fruit, insect-damaged fruit, rotten fruit, deformed fruit, branches and leaves or other plant residues and soil; the fruit surface shall be cleaned by effective measures such as blowing and scrubbing with a high-pressure air gun or water jet, and, where necessary, the exterior of durian may be wiped by hand with fine, soft and clean cotton cloth to effectively remove scale insects, insect eggs, pathogenic spores and other contaminants attached to the fruit surface. In addition, insecticidal treatment shall be carried out in the packing house if necessary.

(5) The packing process shall comply with China's food safety laws and regulations and national standards, and the illegal addition of non-edible substances is prohibited.

(6) Packed durian that needs to be stored shall be placed in the warehouse immediately and stored separately to avoid re-infestation by pests.

(7) Containers loaded with durian exported to China must be checked at the time of packing to ensure good hygienic conditions.

(8) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system to ensure that durian exported to China can be traced back to registered orchards; the records shall include information such as the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the production volume, the date of export, the quantity exported, the country of destination and the container number.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials for durian exported to China shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall comply with the relevant phytosanitary requirements of China. Where wooden packaging is used, it must meet the requirements of International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each carton shall be marked in Chinese or English with the name of the fruit, the place of origin (province, city, district), the exporting country, the name or registration number of the orchard, and the name or registration number of the packing house and other information. Each carton and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “To be exported to the People’s Republic of China” or “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

(d) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Within the first two years of the start of trade, the Lao side shall carry out sampling inspection at a ratio of 2% for each batch of durian exported to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within the two years, the sampling ratio may be reduced to 1%.

(2) If quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, branches, leaves or soil are found, the entire batch of goods shall not be exported to China. The Lao side shall identify the causes and take corrective measures; at the same time, records of the interceptions shall be kept and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(e) Requirements for phytosanitary certificates.

(1) For consignments that have passed quarantine, the Lao side shall issue phytosanitary certificates in accordance with International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), indicating the names or registration numbers of the orchards and packing houses, and fill in the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Durian from Lao PDR to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China.” (This consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Durian from Laos to China and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.)

(2) Before the start of the durian export trade to China, the Lao side shall provide the Chinese side with samples of the phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and record-filing.

VII. Inspection and Quarantine upon Entry and Handling of Non-conformities

When durian exported to China arrives at Chinese ports of entry, China Customs will carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(a) Verification of relevant certificates and markings.

(1) Whether the imported durian has obtained the Quarantine Permit for Inbound Animals and Plants.

(2) Whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Item (e) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(3) Whether the markings on the cartons and pallets comply with the provisions of Item (c) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(b) Inspection and quarantine upon entry.

(1) Durian exported to China shall enter from ports of entry at which the Chinese side permits the import of fruit.

(2) Inspection and quarantine shall be carried out on the imported durian in accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations, rules and other provisions and the requirements of this Announcement; those that pass inspection and quarantine are allowed to enter.

(c) Handling of non-conformities.

(1) If the goods are found to come from unapproved orchards or packing houses, the consignment shall not be allowed to enter.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or other pests newly occurring in Laos, or soil, plant residues, etc., are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(3) If the goods are found not to comply with China's food safety laws and regulations and national standards, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

Hereby announced.

Referral promotion can be done in China, provided your model sits inside six boundaries: four prohibitions and two limits. Get all six right and your first promoters can start; if any one is wrong, fix it before recruiting.

Referral promotion here means promoters share product links, close sales and earn a service fee on each transaction. Your goods enter through the online bonded import channel (supervision mode 1210, goods treated as articles for personal use) and sit in a bonded warehouse; the onshore entity is the company you set up and register in China for cross-border e-commerce. The six boundaries govern promoter conduct and money; import compliance for the goods is a separate track.

## Four prohibitions and two limits, checked against your model

Start with the shape. The four prohibitions govern what promotion looks like; the two limits govern how money is calculated and who signs the contract.

- No inventory. Orders ship straight to the consumer and promoters never stock goods: the consumer orders, the bonded warehouse ships, and the promoter never holds the product.
- No resale. A promoter's income is a promotion service fee, not a buy-low-sell-high margin; the promoter never buys out the goods, so resale never arises.
- No entry fees. Joining is free with no purchase threshold; any shape that demands a purchase or a payment before granting promoter status gets deleted.
- No multi-level. The team you manage caps at two levels, and nothing beyond two levels is compensated.

The two limits are mandatory. Pay-basis limit: commissions are calculated only on attributed transaction value, GMV being the real sales the promoter closed, and any head-count-based pay is removed entirely. Contracting-entity limit: promoters in China sign promotion service agreements with the onshore entity, which withholds and remits individual income tax; overseas IBOs (Independent Business Owners, the promoter-operators in your overseas system) are not paid directly by the China entity.

## Which two regulations sit behind the six items

The shape of the six items comes from two regulations and three red lines. State Council Decree No. 443, the Direct Selling Regulations, defines what direct selling is, how licenses are issued and how pay is capped; State Council Decree No. 444, the Regulations on the Prohibition of Pyramid Schemes, lists three categories of pyramid-scheme conduct in Article 7, and any one of them makes the conduct a pyramid scheme: pay by head count for recruiting, entry fees paid in money or goods to qualify, and team-based pay calculated on downline performance.

The first two have no safe harbor. The third, team-based pay, has a criminal safe harbor in Article 5 of the 2013 joint opinion of the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate and the Ministry of Public Security (Gong Tong Zi [2013] No. 37): team-based pay calculated on sales performance is not treated as a crime, and Article 224-1 of the Criminal Law on organizing or leading pyramid-scheme activities is its criminal anchor. But the harbor removes only criminal liability, not administrative liability: administrative responsibility remains, and the four prohibitions and two limits keep applying.

Check your own plan in a fixed order: entry fees first, then head-count pay, then whether the hierarchy can spiral out of control. The harbor protects pay split by sales performance; pay split by head count and pay-to-enter both sit in the liability zone, with no exemption.

Check every red line against what you actually do, and list the changes. Lawful referral sales rest on the cited provisions, not on verbal promises.

## Where the changes land and who enforces them

All six are executable: the restructuring lands in agreements and execution rules, a rebuild with a clear path rather than a statement of principle. If you know direct selling supervision in major markets such as the United States, you will recognize the same guardrail logic: no pay for recruiting, no inventory holding. China is more specific: a hard two-level cap, GMV attribution, onshore contracting with tax withheld, all enforced at system level, and the system simply does not let non-compliant shapes through.

The four prohibitions and two limits are the path design for administrative compliance in China. The criminal safe harbor settles non-criminalization; the administrative safe zone is entered by meeting these six items.

## One order end to end, where each of the six takes effect

Walk through one example order. A consumer orders a bottle of fish oil through a promoter's link for 60 US dollars; assume the agreement sets a 10 percent service fee, so the commission is 6 US dollars.

The order enters the bonded warehouse and the warehouse ships straight to the consumer; the promoter never touched the goods, and the no-inventory rule holds. The promoter did not buy out this bottle of fish oil and receives a service fee of 6 US dollars rather than a margin, so the no-resale rule holds. He joined free, with no purchase and no payment, so the no-entry-fee rule holds.

He has only one level of team under him, and team pay is calculated on the team's attributed sales as well, stopping at two levels, so the no-multi-level rule holds. The commission is calculated on the attributed transaction value of 60 US dollars and has nothing to do with how many people he recruited this month, so the pay-basis limit holds. The commission is paid by the onshore entity that signed the agreement, with individual income tax withheld; his overseas upline IBO receives no money from the China entity, and the overseas portion settles inside your overseas system, so the contracting-entity limit holds.

The six items are not six slogans; they are six visible actions inside this one order. If any step has the wrong shape, the system should stop it.

## What this article does not cover

This article draws only the six boundaries of the promotion model. What claim wording may say, how detail pages and livestreams are reviewed, which customer data may leave the country, and how commissions are remitted abroad each have their own rules and are not covered here. The six items are not a one-time approval either: administrative liability persists, and after the model's shape is fixed it must keep running against the red lines.

Write the six items into contracts and systems first, then recruit the first promoters. Only with the boundaries drawn clearly do referral sales last.

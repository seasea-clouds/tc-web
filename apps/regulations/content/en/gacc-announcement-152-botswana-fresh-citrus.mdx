On 27 October 2026, GACC issued Announcement No. 152: fresh citrus from Botswana (Orange, Mandarin, Grapefruit, Lemon) meeting phytosanitary requirements is permitted for import from that date. Orchards, packing houses and cold treatment facilities must be audited by the Botswana side and jointly approved and registered by the Chinese and Botswana sides.

The announcement lists 6 quarantine pests, with fruit fly traps at no fewer than 3 per km2, checked at least once every 2 weeks. All citrus requires cold treatment: Lemon at 3 °C for 18 days, other citrus at -0.2 °C for 16 days or 1.0 °C for 19 days, with pre-cooling for at least 72 hours.

The Botswana side samples 2%, reducible to 1% after two years without phytosanitary problems; certificates must be issued under ISPM 12 with an English additional declaration, and transit cold treatment must be marked “Cold treatment in transit”. Consignments with invalid cold treatment undergo on-arrival cold treatment, return or destruction.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side, GACC) and the Ministry of Lands and Agriculture of the Republic of Botswana (hereinafter the Botswana side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh citrus from Botswana to China, fresh citrus from Botswana meeting the following requirements is permitted for import from the date of this announcement.

I. Legal Basis for Quarantine

(i) Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Citrus from Botswana to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Lands and Agriculture of the Republic of Botswana.

II. Permitted Commodities

Fresh citrus (hereinafter citrus), including:

(1) Orange, scientific name Citrus sinensis, English name Orange.

(2) Mandarin, scientific name Citrus reticulata, English name Mandarin.

(3) Grapefruit, scientific name Citrus paradisi, English name Grapefruit.

(4) Lemon, scientific name Citrus limon, English name Lemon.

III. Permitted Production Areas

Citrus-producing areas of Botswana.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards, packing houses and cold treatment facilities exporting citrus to China shall be audited by the Botswana side and jointly approved and registered by the Chinese and Botswana sides. Registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so as to enable accurate traceability of export consignments that do not comply with this announcement. Before each export season, the Botswana side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side which, after review and approval by the Chinese side, shall be published on the GACC website and regularly updated.

V. Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(2) Natal fruit fly Ceratitis rosa

(3) African wax scale Ceroplastes destructor

(4) Black thread scale Ischnaspis longirostris

(5) South African citrus thrips Scirtothrips aurantii

(6) False codling moth Thaumatotibia leucotreta

VI. Pre-export Management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Registered orchards exporting to China shall, under the supervision of the Botswana side, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), including maintaining orchard hygiene and promptly removing fallen fruit, and implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), such as pest surveys, monitoring, chemical or biological control and cultural practices.

(2) The Botswana side or its authorized institution shall conduct orchard monitoring for quarantine pests of concern to China, and shall immediately take appropriate control measures if such pests are detected.

(3) For Mediterranean fruit fly, Natal fruit fly and false codling moth, orchards exporting to China shall conduct monitoring from flowering to harvest; each type of trap shall be set at a density of no fewer than 3 per square kilometre, and for a single orchard of less than 1 square kilometre, no fewer than 2 per trap type; traps shall be inspected at least once every 2 weeks.

(4) For African wax scale, black thread scale and South African citrus thrips, orchards exporting to China shall conduct monitoring from flowering to harvest, once every 2 weeks.

(5) Orchards exporting to China shall retain pest monitoring, survey and control records for at least 2 years and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Pest monitoring records shall include the monitoring date, names of pests detected and control measures taken; chemical control records shall include detailed information on the names, active ingredients, concentrations and application dates of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(ii) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and shipment of citrus exported to China shall be carried out under the supervision of officials of the Botswana side or its authorized institution.

(2) Packing houses shall maintain good hygienic conditions and be able to prevent pest re-infestation, for example by installing insect-proof nets.

(3) During packaging, citrus exported to China shall undergo removal, sorting and grading, as well as sterilization, washing, brushing and waxing, so as to ensure freedom from insects, mites, rotten fruit, twigs, leaves, roots and soil.

(4) Citrus exported to China shall be packaged separately from citrus destined for other markets and stored separately.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall comply with China's phytosanitary requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall be marked with information such as the fruit name, country, production area (district or county), and the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house. Each package and pallet shall be marked, in Chinese or English, with “Exported to the People's Republic of China”.

(3) Containers used for citrus exported to China shall be inspected before loading to ensure good hygienic conditions; necessary insect-proof measures shall be taken during loading.

(iv) Cold treatment measures.

All citrus exported to China shall undergo cold treatment under the supervision of the Botswana side or its authorized institution. Cold treatment shall follow the Pre-export Cold Treatment Operational Procedure (see Annex 1) or the In-transit Cold Treatment Operational Procedure (see Annexes 2 and 3). The cold treatment indicators shall meet the following conditions:

Lemon: pulp temperature at 3 °C or below, for 18 consecutive days or more.

Citrus other than lemon: pulp temperature at -0.2 °C or below for 16 consecutive days or more, or at 1.0 °C or below for 19 consecutive days or more. Before cold treatment, the fruit shall be pre-cooled for at least 72 hours to reach the treatment temperature.

(v) Pre-departure inspection and quarantine.

(1) During the first two years of trade in citrus exported to China, officials of the Botswana side or its authorized institution shall sample and inspect the citrus at a rate of 2% per consignment. If no phytosanitary problems occur within two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China are detected, export of the entire consignment to China is prohibited. The Botswana side shall investigate the cause and take corrective measures. Inspection records shall also be kept and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(vi) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For citrus exported to China that has passed quarantine, the Botswana side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), indicating the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house and the container number, and shall state in the additional declaration:

“This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Citrus from Botswana to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China.” (The consignment meets the phytosanitary requirements of the Protocol for fresh citrus exported from Botswana to China and is free of quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) For consignments subject to pre-export cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall indicate the treatment temperature, duration and the name or code of the treatment facility.

(3) For consignments subject to in-transit cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall bear the mark “Cold treatment in transit” (in-transit cold treatment), together with the treatment temperature, start time, duration, container number and seal number.

(4) Before trade begins, the Botswana side shall provide a sample phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side for record and verification.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Non-compliance Handling

When citrus exported to China arrives at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall conduct quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verify whether the imported citrus has obtained the Permit for Entry of Animals and Plants.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Chapter VI, Section (vi), of this announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on packages or wooden packaging comply with Chapter VI, Section (iii), of this announcement.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Citrus exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for fruit imports.

(2) Upon arrival at a Chinese port of entry, Customs shall verify the relevant documents and marks and conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this announcement. Consignments passing inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(iii) Non-compliance handling.

(1) If a consignment is found to originate from an orchard, packing house or cold treatment facility not approved by the Chinese side, entry of that consignment shall be refused.

(2) If cold treatment is determined to be invalid, the consignment shall be subject to on-arrival cold treatment, return or destruction.

(3) If live quarantine pests of concern to China or newly occurring quarantine pests in Botswana are detected, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to pest elimination treatment.

(4) If non-compliance with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards is found, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

Annexes: (1) Pre-export Cold Treatment Operational Procedure; (2) In-transit Cold Treatment Operational Procedure; (3) Supplementary Requirements for In-transit Cold Treatment via a Third Country.

General Administration of Customs

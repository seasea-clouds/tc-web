You can launch, but only after all sixteen items on the promotion and payout sides are checked off, written into the project charter, and backed by a retrievable voucher for each. This is a pre-launch condition, not paperwork to complete afterwards: if any item is missing or a prerequisite is still open, the launch stops where it stands. What you put in is one verification meeting and the patience to name an owner and a latest date for every item; the list is reviewed monthly in a shared tracker, not settled by a nod before go-live.

## What has to be on paper before you launch

Writing all sixteen items into the project charter is the one step you cannot skip here. Verification goes by vouchers, not by who nods in the meeting: every item names an owner and a latest date, unfinished entries are never left open-ended, and progress pauses while a prerequisite remains undecided. Each of the two lists shows all eight items in place, the left one with its illustrative basis fully covered and the right one drawn from the two-channel fund design. Launch waits until all sixteen are ticked, and the list is checked again every month in a shared tracker.

## Which promotion items tend to be only half done

Entity and contracting come first. The onshore entity is established, evidenced by its business registration; promoters sign onshore and the onshore entity withholds individual income tax, filing withholding returns under State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 61 of 2018; the tier structure stops at two levels, and commission is calculated only on attributed transaction value; no entry fees are charged to promoters. Article 7 of the Prohibition of Pyramid Selling Regulations (State Council Decree No. 444) lists entry fees, payment by headcount recruited, and the formation of upper and lower lines together. These four items sit exactly on those three categories, and a line in the agreement saying participation is voluntary will not hold them off.

The middle two watch the goods, the last two watch data and claims. Stockpiling for resale is not allowed, and the agreement separately bans stockpiling for resale; check each once, one against dispatch and inventory records, one against the contract wording. Data stays in the onshore system with the cross-border path defined in advance. Where personal information really must be provided outside China, Article 38 of the Personal Information Protection Law sets out the security assessment, certification or standard contract routes, Article 39 requires notice to the overseas recipient and separate consent, and Article 55 requires an impact assessment with records kept. Promotional claims pass three reviews covering artwork, legal and compliance, with the false-advertising categories in Article 28 of the Advertising Law as the check sheet.

## Which payout items need money and documents to agree

The first four cover calculation and remittance. Onshore contracting and withholding need the agreement receipt and the withholding voucher; pay is based only on attributed transaction value, never on headcount recruited; the tier cap is enforced by the system, not corrected by hand at month end; remittance documents and tax filing have to match payment by payment. Under Article 1 of the Announcement of the State Taxation Administration and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange on Tax Filing for Outbound Payments under Service Trade and Other Items (No. 40 of 2013, amended by STA Announcement No. 31 of 2018), an onshore institution making a single service-trade payment above 50,000 US dollars to an overseas party files for tax first; where one contract needs several payments, Supplementary Announcement No. 19 of 2021 requires filing only before the first remittance.

The last four cover where the money belongs and what it leaves behind. Overseas promoters are paid by your parent company, with the onshore entity taking no part in calculation or disbursement; onshore settlement and offshore payment run on two separate channels and never share one bill; the agreement states the income is a service fee rather than a purchase-resale margin; bills and vouchers are filed by period with the deliverables. All eight items on the right are in place too, judged against the two-channel fund design: whichever channel the money moves on, the documents have to appear in that set of books, and a voucher produced later says nothing about the path taken at the time.

## One pre-launch verification meeting, from licence to bill

Take a batch of cross-border health products launching in autumn. The onshore entity has just been set up and the promotion is scheduled for the next settlement cycle, so the meeting walks the four rows of the Chinese verification table one by one: entity setup maps to business registration and is pending confirmation; promoter signing maps to the agreement receipt and is complete; income tax withholding maps to the withholding voucher and is complete; the tier cap maps to a system setting and is complete. The pending row gets a named handler and a latest date on the spot, the other three have their voucher numbers copied into the charter, and nobody writes almost done in the minutes.

A neighbouring column holds the check questions: whether all sixteen items are ticked, whether the vouchers can be pulled, whether they come from the same source as the two lists, and whether the items and statuses in the system interface demo line up. All four point at one thing: can the list, the system and the agreement be traced back to the same batch of business. Entity setup is the usual sticking point here; until the licence arrives, promoter signing and withholding stay open, and an open prerequisite stops progress under the charter.

The meeting records the two pending vouchers as unfinished items: the promoter service fee and the domestic delivery fee each get an owner and a latest date, and the rows move to paid only once the vouchers arrive. That is how the vouchers-checkable question in the list lands on a concrete bill number; a line in the charter saying archived does not count. The six demo rows belong to the same batch of deliverables, so items and statuses have to come from the same source as the lists.

## Where the vouchers come from and how unfinished items close

Left-column vouchers leave records in both the system and the agreement: the agreement template, attribution details, bills and withholding vouchers; right-column vouchers are remittance documents and period bills. Both sides are archived with the deliverables and can be pulled together for a regulator question, without assembling them from several systems at short notice. The launch conditions are plain: all sixteen items complete before launch, the left column listing the handling actions for the four prerequisites and the right column stating the two-channel fund design; the list goes into the project charter and is ticked monthly in a shared tracker. The list itself is a pre-launch prerequisite, not a record filled in afterwards.

If your plan is to launch first and fill in vouchers as you go, that route will cost you: withholding vouchers on the promotion side and remittance documents on the payout side are created at a point in time, and material produced later cannot match the fund path at that moment, so both bank document review and regulator questions come back to that one payment. Checking all sixteen before you schedule launch is less work than rewriting a ledger afterwards. Bill numbers, amounts, rates and statuses in the demo interface are illustrative; actual rates, filing thresholds and voucher requirements follow current rules of the tax and foreign exchange authorities and your real bills.

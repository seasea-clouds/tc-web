Belgian fresh apples are cleared for export to China. Announcement No. 76, issued by the General Administration of Customs on 2 June 2026, allows imports of apples that meet the phytosanitary requirements. Orchards, packinghouses and cold treatment facilities must be reviewed by the Belgian side and approved by China Customs.

Shipments must be free of 16 quarantine pests of concern to China, including the Mediterranean fruit fly, codling moth and fire blight, and must undergo cold treatment: a fruit pulp centre temperature of 1.11°C or below for 14 consecutive days, or 1.67°C or below for 16 days, or 2.22°C or below for 18 days. In the first two years of trade, 2% of each consignment is sampled.

On arrival, China Customs checks the quarantine permit and phytosanitary certificate. Fruit from unapproved orchards, fruit whose cold treatment is judged ineffective, or fruit found to carry live pests or soil is refused entry, re-exported, destroyed or given arrival cold treatment.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions on phytosanitary requirements for the export of Belgian apples to China agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain of Belgium (hereinafter the Belgian side), imports of Belgian apples that meet the following requirements are permitted with effect from today.

I. Phytosanitary basis

(1) The Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) The Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(3) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(4) The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(5) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain of Belgium on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Belgian Apples to China.

II. Names of commodities permitted for entry

Fresh apples (hereinafter apples), scientific name Malus domestica, English name Apple.

III. Permitted places of origin

Apple-producing areas of Belgium.

IV. Approved orchards and packinghouses

Orchards, packinghouses and cold treatment facilities for apples exported to China shall be reviewed by the Belgian side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. Registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so that consignments can be accurately traced if they fail to meet the requirements of this Announcement. Before each export season, the Belgian side shall provide the Chinese side with a list of registered enterprises, which shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs after review and approval by the Chinese side and updated regularly.

V. List of quarantine pests of concern to China

(1) Mediterranean fruit fly, Ceratitis capitata

(2) Codling moth, Cydia pomonella

(3) Cydia lobarzewskii

(4) Apple gall midge, Dasineura mali

(5) Dysaphis devecta

(6) Dysaphis plantaginea

(7) Epidiaspis leperii

(8) Woolly apple aphid, Eriosoma lanigerum

(9) Apple sawfly, Hoplocampa testudinea

(10) Lepidosaphes ulmi

(11) Pulvinaria hydrangea

(12) Rhopalosiphum insertum

(13) Phytophthora syringae

(14) Apple scab, Venturia inaequalis

(15) Verticillium dahliae

(16) Erwinia amylovora (fire blight)

VI. Pre-export management

(A) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting to China shall, under the supervision of the Belgian side, establish a sound quality management and traceability system, implement good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain orchard hygiene, such as keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting fruit production and promptly removing and not harvesting fallen and rotten fruit, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and other control measures such as farming operations.

(2) The Belgian side shall, in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to China and supervise orchards in carrying out monitoring activities. If pests or their corresponding symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated management measures including chemical, physical or biological control shall be taken. Fallen fruit may not be exported.

(3) Pest monitoring and control shall be implemented under the supervision and guidance of Belgian professional and technical personnel. The Belgian side or a training institution authorised by it shall train the technical personnel.

(4) The Belgian side shall keep records of orchard pest monitoring and control and provide them to the Chinese side on request. Control records shall include the names of chemical agents used during the growing season, active ingredients, application rates and times.

(5) The Belgian side shall ensure that, at least before fruit harvest, registered orchards are randomly inspected by the Belgian side or an authorised service provider (ASP), focusing on the presence of quarantine pests of concern to China, the orchard's pest monitoring and control, and the maintenance of field hygiene. If quarantine pests of concern to China are suspected, samples shall be sent to an official laboratory for identification and control measures shall be taken immediately. Orchards that are poorly managed, such as those unable to meet the phytosanitary requirements for apple exports to China, shall have their export eligibility for the season suspended.

(6) For the Mediterranean fruit fly, the Belgian side shall supervise the establishment of a monitoring system. In orchards exporting to China, from flowering to harvest, producers shall use Jackson traps baited with the sex pheromone Trimedlure. Trap density shall be one per hectare; orchards smaller than 3 hectares must have at least three traps. Traps shall be checked once every two weeks. Once the Mediterranean fruit fly is detected, effective control shall be carried out and the Chinese side notified.

(7) For fire blight, apples exported to China shall come from pest-free growing points, established and maintained in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 10 (ISPM 10) and jointly approved by the Belgian and Chinese sides. At the request of the Chinese side, the Belgian side shall promptly provide relevant information on the maintenance of the pest-free growing points.

(7.1) The Belgian side shall supervise monitoring activities for fire blight in registered apple-producing areas. Producers shall monitor fire blight at least three times a year on orchards exporting to China and susceptible plants around them, namely after new bud emergence, 30 to 40 days after flowering, and before harvest. A buffer zone of 1,000 metres shall be established around the pest-free growing point, all susceptible hosts marked, and monitoring carried out at least three times a year. At the request of the Chinese side, the Belgian side shall promptly provide relevant monitoring information.

(7.2) If fire blight is detected whether in a pest-free growing point or in the buffer zone, the Belgian side shall suspend the export eligibility of the relevant pest-free growing point for the season and immediately notify the Chinese side. After the Belgian side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognised by the Chinese side, the pest-free growing point status may be restored in the next growing season.

(8) For the codling moth, orchards exporting to China shall carry out codling moth monitoring under the supervision of the Belgian side and the guidance of plant protection technicians, with surveys beginning at full bloom and continuing until the end of apple harvest. Trap density shall be one per hectare; orchards smaller than 3 hectares shall have at least three traps. Lures shall be replaced according to the product label, and traps shall be checked and recorded once every two weeks. If three or more codling moths are found in a trap, the relevant production site shall immediately take effective prevention and control measures. Orchards where control is inadequate shall have their export eligibility suspended by the Belgian side, which shall immediately notify the Chinese side. On request, the Belgian side shall provide the Chinese side with survey reports or management records for this pest.

(9) For the apple sawfly and Cydia lobarzewskii, orchards exporting to China shall monitor them under the supervision of the Belgian side and the guidance of technicians, from flowering until the end of harvest. Once detected, Belgian-approved pesticides shall be used promptly for control, and monitoring shall be carried out with traps, sticky traps or visual inspection. Trap or sticky trap density shall be one per hectare; orchards smaller than 3 hectares must have at least three sticky traps. Traps shall be checked once every two weeks. In autumn, orchards shall be cleaned and infected fruit collected and destroyed. Orchards where control is inadequate shall be removed from the list of approved orchards for the season, and the Belgian side shall immediately notify the Chinese side. On request, the Belgian side shall provide the Chinese side with survey reports or management records for these two pests.

(10) For other quarantine pests of concern to China, such as the apple gall midge, Dysaphis plantaginea, Epidiaspis leperii, Lepidosaphes ulmi, Phytophthora syringae and apple scab, orchards exporting to China shall conduct monitoring under the supervision and guidance of Belgian technicians. Pest monitoring shall run from flowering to harvest, at least once every two weeks, focusing on stems, branches, leaves, fruit stalks and fruit. If pests or their symptoms are found, biological, physical or chemical methods shall be used for timely control. Orchards where control is inadequate shall be removed from the list of approved orchards for the season, and the Belgian side shall immediately notify the Chinese side. On request, the Belgian side shall provide the Chinese side with survey reports or management records for the pest concerned.

(11) During transport of fruit over long distances from the orchard to the packinghouse, closed box trucks or canvas coverings shall be used to avoid pest infection en route.

(B) Packinghouse management.

(1) The Belgian side or its authorised quarantine officers shall supervise apple packing, storage and shipment through annual inspections of packinghouses. Packing facilities and cold stores shall be kept in good hygiene and capable of preventing re-infection by pests (for example with insect-proof nets or ultraviolet lamps).

(2) During packing, apples shall be sorted, graded, washed and otherwise processed to remove defective fruit, so as to ensure freedom from insects, pathogens, rotten fruit, leaves, branches, roots and soil.

(3) During grading and packing, packinghouse technicians shall inspect 600 fruit in each lot and cut open 60 fruit with suspicious symptoms. If a quarantine pest of concern to China is found, that lot of apples may not be exported to China. If fire blight is found, the Belgian side shall also suspend the export eligibility of the relevant pest-free growing point and immediately notify the Chinese side. After the Belgian side has eliminated the outbreak and it has been recognised by the Chinese side, the pest-free growing point status may be restored in the next growing season.

(C) Packing requirements.

(1) Packing materials shall be clean, hygienic, unused and comply with Chinese phytosanitary and hygiene requirements. If wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Packed apples for export to China shall be placed in storage immediately, stored only with apples of the same phytosanitary status, and isolated from other products to avoid re-infection by pests.

(3) Each box shall be marked in Chinese or English with the fruit name, country, place of origin (city or province), orchard name or registration number, and packinghouse name or registration number. Each box and pallet shall bear the words “For the People’s Republic of China” (exported to the People's Republic of China) in Chinese or English.

(4) Containers loaded with apples for export to China must be inspected at the time of packing to ensure good hygiene. This activity must be recorded for inspection by the Belgian side.

(D) Quarantine treatment measures.

Apples exported to China shall undergo cold treatment for the Mediterranean fruit fly, under the supervision of the Belgian side or its authorised personnel and in accordance with the cold treatment operating procedures (see Annexes 1 and 2). The cold treatment indicators shall meet any one of the following:

(1) A fruit pulp centre temperature of 1.11°C or below for 14 consecutive days or more;

(2) A fruit pulp centre temperature of 1.67°C or below for 16 consecutive days or more;

(3) A fruit pulp centre temperature of 2.22°C or below for 18 consecutive days or more.

(E) Pre-departure inspection and quarantine.

(1) In the first two years of trade, the Belgian side or its authorised personnel shall sample 2% of each consignment of apples for export to China, with a minimum sample of 1,200 fruit, and shall cut open at least 60 of them and any fruit with suspicious symptoms found during inspection. If no phytosanitary problem occurs within two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%, but shall be no fewer than 600 fruit.

(2) If fire blight is found, the Belgian side shall suspend the export eligibility of the relevant pest-free growing point and immediately notify the Chinese side. After the outbreak has been eliminated by the Belgian side and recognised by the Chinese side, the status of the relevant pest-free growing point may be restored in the next export season.

(3) If live specimens of other quarantine pests of concern to China are found, the consignment may not be exported to China. The Belgian side shall determine the cause and take preventive measures for improvement. It shall also keep inspection records and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(F) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For apples that pass inspection and quarantine, the Belgian side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the registered names or codes of the orchard and packinghouse, and shall include in the additional declaration: “This consignment complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Apples from Belgium to China, and is free from the quarantine pests of concern to China.”

(2) For apples given pre-export cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment temperature, duration and the name or code of the treatment facility. For apples given cold treatment in transit, the phytosanitary certificate shall bear the words “Cold treatment in transit” and state the intended treatment temperature, duration, container number and seal number.

(3) Before trade formally begins, the Belgian side shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen phytosanitary certificate.

VII. Entry inspection and quarantine and treatment of non-compliance

When apples for export to China arrive at the entry port, China Customs shall check the relevant documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine.

(A) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verify that the imported apples have obtained the Quarantine Permit for the Entry of Animals and Plants.

(2) Verify that the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Article VI (F) of this Announcement.

(3) Verify that the marks on the boxes or wooden packaging comply with the provisions of Article VI (C) of this Announcement.

(4) For consignments given pre-export cold treatment, verify that the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment result report signed by the Belgian side, and the fruit temperature probe calibration records comply with the provisions of Article VI (D) of this Announcement.

(5) For consignments given cold treatment in transit, verify that the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment report, and the fruit temperature probe calibration records comply with the provisions of Article VI (D) of this Announcement.

(B) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Apples for export to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for importing fruit.

(2) When the apples arrive at the Chinese entry port, China Customs shall check the relevant documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws, regulations, national standards and the requirements of this Announcement.

(C) Treatment of non-compliance.

(1) Apples found to come from unapproved orchards or packinghouses shall not be allowed to enter.

(2) If cold treatment is judged ineffective, the consignment shall be subject to arrival cold treatment (which may be carried out in the container), re-export or destruction.

(3) If live specimens of quarantine pests of concern to China or of pests newly occurring in Belgium are found, or if soil or plant debris is found, the consignment shall be re-exported, destroyed or given quarantine treatment.

(4) If the consignment fails to comply with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards, it shall be re-exported or destroyed.

This Announcement is hereby issued.

Annexes: 1. Pre-export cold treatment operating procedures; 2. In-transit cold treatment operating procedures.

General Administration of Customs

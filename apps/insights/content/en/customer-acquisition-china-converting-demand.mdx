Where your acquisition budget goes, and how much each channel gets, depends on where conversion conditions actually hold, not on filling all four channels at once. The architecture in module S7 of the source pages is three growth stages against four channels: cold start from month zero to six, scale-up from six to eighteen, retention beyond eighteen months, with Xiaohongshu, Tmall Global and JD Worldwide, Douyin, and the WeChat private domain each carrying a different weight at every stage. Compliance sits in from day one: promotion copy passes three reviews, the membership system is rebuilt under onshore rules, and member data stays inside China. Alongside the architecture runs a per-channel paid-media dashboard, so the money out and the return back sit side by side in the same row, verifiable.

## The three-stage four-channel division matrix

The matrix lists three growth stages vertically and four channels horizontally, one bubble per cell, where bubble size is the investment intensity that stage puts into that channel: large for primary push, medium for support, small for slow. Read cell by cell, the budget shifts its main battlefield by stage, loading each stage onto the channel where conversion conditions are most mature at that moment.

Cold start, months zero to six: Xiaohongshu takes the large bubble for ingredient education, Tmall Global and JD Worldwide take the medium bubble for trust-based conversion, Douyin gets no spend yet, and the WeChat private domain takes the large bubble to collect first-order trials. No transaction evidence exists at this stage, so content explains the ingredients first, the marketplace stores catch the trust people search for, and the private domain lands the first order with a trial pack.

Scale-up, months six to eighteen: Xiaohongshu steps down to a medium bubble for content scale-up, Tmall Global and JD Worldwide rise to the largest bubble as the main conversion channel, Douyin rises to the largest bubble for livestream volume, and the WeChat private domain holds a medium bubble for repurchase reminders. The main battlefield moves from Xiaohongshu to the marketplaces and Douyin, and content shifts from education to volume.

Retention, beyond eighteen months: word of mouth, stock-up repurchase, hero-product carryover, and member repurchase all sit at medium-to-large bubbles, with member repurchase in the WeChat private domain the largest. Growth at this stage does not come from new traffic; it comes from people who already bought coming back to talk.

## The compliance sidebar sits in from day one

To the right of the matrix, the compliance sidebar lists four items, all fixed on launch day, not patched in after scale-up.

Promoters: no stock, no resale, two tiers or fewer. Attribution and settlement: GMV attribution on a clear basis, contracts signed with the onshore entity, withholding handled onshore. Creatives: every ad and influencer script passes three reviews before publishing. Membership: not transplanted wholesale from the overseas setup but rebuilt under onshore rules, consistent with the overall “no incidents” basis; member data stays onshore, and any cross-border transfer is assessed item by item under the cross-border provisions of the Personal Information Protection Law.

## Cost structure in magnitudes from public sources

The cost structure carries three lines. Platform commission runs in a 0.5%–5% range by category, taken as directional figures from public sources; the other items cover payment fees, paid media, content production, and commissions; validation means small steps and a weekly payback check. All three feed the sensitivity analysis in the unit economics later, so when the media budget loosens or tightens by one notch, the numbers follow.

## Three growth legs and three private-domain steps

The architecture boils down to three growth legs: content seeding for awareness, platform conversion for transactions, and private-domain repurchase for retention. The private domain runs three steps: the brand's official mini-program plus content-driven follows plus a first-order trial pack; the tools are just three, WeCom groups, a mini-program member card, and repurchase reminders.

The growth-compliance link sits in the same block: promotion content passes three reviews before publishing, and a second supply slot is reserved so scale-up adds channels without restructuring the supply side. On pacing, the plan rules out all-channels-at-once: prove content and conversion in one channel first, then replicate to the second.

Walk the demo data once, end to end. Start at the total row for the headline: the four channels spent 130000.00 yuan in the month, returning 1358500 impressions, 932 orders, and 838800.00 yuan of GMV, an overall ROI of 6.5.

Then read the division of labor by row: content seeding at 42000.00 yuan and short video at 58000.00 yuan take about sixty percent of the spend, deliver 486000 and 724000 impressions at ROI 4.5 and 2.9, and carry the reach job; private-domain community at 18000.00 yuan and member operations at 12000.00 yuan spend far less, click through at 8.1% and 11.4%, return ROI 12.1 and 22.2, and carry conversion and retention, with member operations turning the smallest spend into the largest GMV at 266400.00 yuan. Within one row, where the money went and what came back line up at a glance, and the add-or-hold decision comes from the row itself.

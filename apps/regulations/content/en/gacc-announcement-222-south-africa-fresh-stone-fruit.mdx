South African fresh stone fruits are approved for import. Five fruits: peach (Prunus persica), nectarine (Prunus persica var. nucipersica), plum (Prunus salicina), apricot (Prunus armeniaca) and prune (Prunus domestica). Orchards and packing houses need SA audit and China Customs registration. Announcement 222 (2025) is in force.

15 quarantine pests of concern to China, including Thaumatotibia leucotreta, are prohibited. Orchards must apply GAP and IPM with monitoring and trapping. Pre-export cold treatment at -0.6°C or below for 22 days, or methyl bromide fumigation at 21.1°C and 32 g/m3 for 2 h, is required. A 2% sample of no fewer than 1200 fruits must be checked.

Each consignment needs a phytosanitary certificate bearing the declaration: „This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from the South Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.“ Packages must read „Exported to the People’s Republic of China“. China Customs inspects at entry and returns, destroys or treats non-compliant lots.

Announcement of the General Administration of Customs No. 222 of 2025 (Announcement on Phytosanitary Requirements for Import of Fresh Stone Fruits from South Africa)

Pursuant to the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions on phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh stone fruits from South Africa to China agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter China Customs / GACC) and the Department of Agriculture of the Republic of South Africa (hereinafter the South Africa Department of Agriculture), fresh stone fruits from South Africa that meet the following requirements are allowed to be imported from today.

I. Phytosanitary Basis

(1) Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Quarantine of Entry-Exit Animals and Plants and its implementing regulations;

(3) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(4) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(5) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from South Africa to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Department of Agriculture of the Republic of South Africa.

II. Names of Commodities Permitted for Import

Fresh stone fruits (hereinafter stone fruits) include peach (scientific name Prunus persica, English name Peach), nectarine (scientific name Prunus persica var. nucipersica, English name Nectarine), plum (scientific name Prunus salicina, English name Plum), apricot (Prunus armeniaca, English name Apricot) and prune (scientific name Prunus domestica, English name Prune).

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Stone fruit producing areas in South Africa.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards, packing houses and cold-treatment facilities for stone fruits exported to China must be audited by the South Africa Department of Agriculture and approved and registered by China Customs. Registration information includes name, address and registration code, so that consignments can be accurately traced when they do not comply with the relevant provisions of this Announcement. Before each export season, the South Africa Department of Agriculture shall provide China Customs with the list of registered enterprises, which shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs after review and approval by China Customs.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) False codling moth Thaumatotibia leucotreta

(2) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(3) Mango fruit fly Ceratitis cosyra

(4) Five-spotted fruit fly Ceratitis quinaria

(5) Quilici fruit fly Ceratitis quilicii

(6) Natal fruit fly Ceratitis rosa

(7) Codling moth Cydia pomonella

(8) South African carnation tortrix Epichoristodes acerbella

(9) Apple leafroller Lozotaenia capensana

(10) African armored scale Diaspidiotus africanus

(11) Long-tailed mealybug Pseudococcus longispinus

(12) Blossom thrips Frankliniella schultzei

(13) Black peach aphid Brachycaudus persicae

(14) Bacterial canker of stone fruit Pseudomonas syringae pv. morsprunorum

(15) Plum viroid 1 Apscaviroid latenspruni (Plum viroid I)

VI. Pre-export Management

(1) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards producing stone fruits for export to China shall establish and implement good agricultural practices (GAP) under the supervision of the South Africa Department of Agriculture, including maintaining orchard hygiene and promptly removing fallen fruit, and shall apply integrated pest management (IPM), including pest surveys, monitoring, chemical or biological control and farming operations, to avoid or minimize the occurrence on stone fruits of quarantine pests of concern to China. Specific pest monitoring plans and integrated management measures must be approved by the South Africa Department of Agriculture and provided to China Customs upon request.

(2) Orchards exporting to China must have monitoring and surveys of quarantine pests of concern to China carried out by technicians familiar with plant quarantine who have been guided by the South Africa Department of Agriculture; such technicians must be trained by the South Africa Department of Agriculture or its authorized bodies. If any quarantine pest of concern to China is found, appropriate control measures shall be taken immediately.

(3) Orchards exporting to China shall keep records of pest monitoring, surveys and control for at least two years and provide them to China Customs upon request. Pest monitoring and control records shall include at least the time of monitoring, the pest name and the control measures taken; chemical control records shall include details such as the names of all chemical agents used during the growing season, active ingredients, concentrations and application dates.

(4) For fruit flies and the false codling moth, orchards exporting to China shall take integrated field management measures, including monitoring and trapping of fruit flies (including the Mediterranean fruit fly, mango fruit fly, Quilici fruit fly, five-spotted fruit fly and Natal fruit fly) and the false codling moth, using chemical or biological control to reduce their population density. Monitoring shall start at flowering and end at harvest.

Orchards exporting to China shall use fruit fly traps (such as triangular and bucket types) for monitoring, with registered Mediterranean fruit fly bait as the attractant. Trap density shall be 2 per square kilometre (at least 2 traps in orchards smaller than 1 square kilometre), and traps shall be checked once a week. If the daily capture per trap exceeds 0.7, effective control shall be carried out.

For the false codling moth, sex pheromone trapping shall be used for pest monitoring in orchards. Trap density shall be 2 per square kilometre (at least 2 traps in orchards smaller than 1 square kilometre), and traps shall be checked once a week. If the false codling moth is detected, appropriate physical, chemical or biological control measures shall be taken immediately.

(5) For the codling moth, trapping monitoring shall be carried out in stone fruit producing areas and related areas (orchards, packing houses and surrounding production areas), at a density of 1 per 2 hectares. Monitoring shall start at full bloom and continue until stone fruit packing is completed. Traps shall be checked at least once every 2 weeks, and lures shall be replaced in time according to the instructions. If 3 or more codling moths are found in a trap at any check, control measures shall be taken immediately. Orchards where control is inadequate shall lose their eligibility to export to China for that export season.

(6) For pathogens of concern to China such as the stone fruit bacterial canker bacterium and plum viroid I, an orchard monitoring system shall be established and orchard management strengthened, with field monitoring and surveys at least once a month during the growing season in stone fruit producing areas. Once suspicious infection symptoms are found, samples shall be taken promptly and sent to a laboratory for molecular identification. If infection with the above-mentioned pathogens of concern to China is confirmed, integrated control measures must be taken to control their occurrence; if control is inadequate, the eligibility of the orchard to export to China shall be withdrawn.

(7) For pests of concern to China such as the black peach aphid, African armored scale, South African carnation tortrix, blossom thrips, apple leafroller and long-tailed mealybug, monitoring and surveys shall be carried out at least once every 2 weeks from flowering until the end of harvest, focusing on fruits, fruit stalks, leaves and other parts. If pests or their damage symptoms are found, control measures shall be taken promptly to control the pests or maintain a low level of prevalence, so that stone fruit shall not carry these quarantine pests.

(2) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packing, storage and shipment of stone fruits shall be carried out under the supervision of the South Africa Department of Agriculture or its authorized personnel.

(2) During post-harvest processing and packing, stone fruits shall be sorted and graded to ensure that they shall not contain any live insects, defective fruit, twigs, leaves, soil or the like.

(3) The functional areas for receiving, processing, treating and storing stone fruits exported to China shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and isolation measures shall be taken from fruits not intended for export to China.

(3) Packing requirements.

(1) Packing materials shall be clean, hygienic, unused and comply with the relevant plant quarantine requirements of China. If wooden packing is used, it shall comply with the International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) If packed stone fruits need to be stored, they shall be placed in storage immediately and kept separately to avoid reinfestation by pests. Each carton shall be marked with the fruit name, country, place of origin (such as district, city or county), orchard registration number, packing house registration number and other information. Each carton and pallet shall bear, in Chinese or English, the words „Exported to the People’s Republic of China“.

(3) Containers carrying stone fruits exported to China must be inspected at the time of loading to ensure good sanitary conditions, and necessary insect-proof measures shall be taken during loading.

(4) Quarantine treatment requirements.

For fruit flies and the false codling moth, stone fruits exported to China shall undergo cold treatment or fumigation under the supervision of the South Africa Department of Agriculture or its authorized personnel.

Cold treatment shall be carried out in accordance with the operational procedures for pre-export cold treatment (see Annex 1) or for cold treatment in transit (see Annex 2), with the following treatment specifications: -0.6°C or below (fruit pulp temperature) for a continuous period of 22 days or more.

Fumigation shall use methyl bromide as the fumigant, with the following treatment specifications: a temperature of 21.1°C or above, a dosage of 32 g/m3, and a fumigation duration of not less than 2 h under normal atmospheric pressure; in addition, the minimum concentration during fumigation shall be not less than 26 g/m3 after 0.5 h and not less than 16 g/m3 after 2 h.

(5) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The South Africa Department of Agriculture or its authorized personnel shall sample and inspect each consignment of stone fruits exported to China at a rate of 2% of each consignment, with a minimum sample of 1200 fruits; at least 60 fruits from the 2% sample, or suspicious fruits found during inspection, shall be cut open for examination. If no plant quarantine problem has occurred within two years, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%, but the sample size shall not be less than 1200 fruits.

(2) If any live quarantine pest of concern to China is found, the entire consignment shall not be exported to China. The South Africa Department of Agriculture shall ascertain the cause, take improvement measures, and keep inspection records to be provided to China Customs upon request.

(6) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For stone fruits that pass quarantine inspection, the South Africa Department of Agriculture shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the orchard registration number, packing house registration number and container number, and shall include in the additional declaration: „This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from the South Africa to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.“ (This consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from South Africa to China and does not carry quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) For consignments undergoing pre-export cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall state the treatment temperature, duration and the name or number of the treatment facility. For consignments undergoing cold treatment in transit, the phytosanitary certificate shall bear the words „Cold treatment in transit“ and state the treatment temperature, duration, container number and seal number.

For consignments undergoing pre-export fumigation, the phytosanitary certificate shall state, in the quarantine treatment column, the methyl bromide dosage, duration and treatment temperature used, as well as the name or number of the treatment facility.

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine and Handling of Non-compliance

When stone fruits exported to China arrive at the entry port in China, China Customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(1) Verification of relevant certificates and marks.

(1) Verify whether the imported stone fruits have obtained the Import Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Item (6) of Article 6 of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on cartons and pallets comply with the provisions of Item (3) of Article 6 of this Announcement.

(4) For consignments that underwent pre-export cold treatment, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment result report signed and confirmed by the South Africa Department of Agriculture, and the fruit temperature probe calibration records meet the provisions of Item (4) of Article 6 of this Announcement.

(5) For consignments that underwent cold treatment in transit, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment report, the fruit temperature probe calibration records and the like meet the provisions of Item (4) of Article 6 of this Announcement.

(6) For consignments that underwent pre-export fumigation, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate meets the provisions of Item (4) of Article 6 of this Announcement.

(2) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Stone fruits exported to China shall enter through ports permitted by China Customs for the import of fruits.

(2) When stone fruits arrive at the entry port in China, China Customs will inspect the relevant documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and the requirements of this Announcement.

(3) Handling of non-compliance.

(1) If the fruits are found to come from an unregistered orchard, packing house or treatment facility, the consignment shall not be allowed to enter.

(2) If cold treatment is determined to be invalid or has failed, the consignment shall be subject to cold treatment on arrival (which may be carried out in the container), return, destruction or other measures.

(3) If any live quarantine pest of concern to China, any newly occurring quarantine pest in South Africa, soil, plant debris or the like is found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or treated to eliminate the pests.

(4) If the consignment is found not to comply with Chinese food safety laws and regulations and national standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

This Announcement is hereby issued.

General Administration of Customs

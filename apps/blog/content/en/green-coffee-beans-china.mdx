## The First Question Is Not Paperwork

Exporters of green coffee usually arrive with the same assumption: coffee is food, so coffee needs food registration. That assumption is half right, and the half that is wrong is expensive.

Under China's revised registration rules, **unroasted coffee beans and roasted coffee sit in two different systems**. Unroasted beans are treated as agricultural products. Roasted beans, ground coffee and coffee products are treated as food. The registration channel is different, the declaration code is different, and the registry the buyer checks is different.

This guide covers the unroasted side, which is where most origin exporters actually sit. If you ship roasted beans or coffee products instead, see our guide to [food registration under Decree 280](/en/blog/gacc-decree-280/).

## Green Beans Travel as Agricultural Products

When Decree 280 replaced Decree 248 on 1 June 2026, six raw agricultural categories were moved out of the food registration system altogether: oilseeds, miscellaneous grains, fresh vegetables, dried beans, seasonings, and **unroasted coffee and cocoa beans**. Those categories are now governed by GACC Announcement No. 219 of 2025, which took effect on 15 December 2025 and sets out how overseas enterprises of imported agricultural products are registered and declared. Three practical consequences follow.

**The facility registration is handled through the agricultural channel.** It is not the CIFER food registration path your buyer may have written into the contract.

**Declaration mechanics change.** When the buyer declares a shipment in the covered catalogue, the customs declaration must carry the overseas facility's Chinese registration number in the certificate field for overseas production, processing and storage enterprise registration, licence category code 302. Where a declaration covers several line items, each item needs the registration number of the facility behind it. A correct facility with a wrong or missing code still stops at the port.

**The registry is public and worth checking.** Importers can look up registered facilities through the GACC enquiry service for animal and plant quarantine information. The buyer's compliance team will check it, and so should you. To see whether your own facility is already registered, and how the registration number should read, our [free self-check tool](/en/c/check/gacc) is the fastest route; full filing support is covered under [GACC registration services](/en/services/gacc/).

## Roasted Beans and Ground Coffee Are Food

The moment coffee is roasted, the product moves into the food registration system under Decree 280.

For categories outside the official-recommendation catalogue, the route is a self-application filing through the CIFER system, without a recommendation letter from the exporting country's authority. Registration is valid for five years and renews automatically at expiry, with three exceptions: products placed on the no-automatic-renewal list, which currently covers meat and meat products and edible bird's nest and its products, facilities under rectification for non-compliance, and origins subject to a suspension of imports. Facilities in those situations must apply for renewal between three and twelve months before expiry, with a statement from the competent authority of the country of origin confirming continued compliance; a registration can be cancelled if renewal is not filed.

This is where the practical risk sits. A facility that assumes renewal is automatic, when its product is on the exception list, loses its registration number and only finds out when a container is stopped.

## Origin Eligibility Comes Before Everything

No registration saves a shipment from an origin that is not approved for coffee exports to China. China maintains a list of approved origins, and the list moves.

On 20 July 2026, a new protocol took effect allowing coffee beans from 53 African countries with diplomatic relations with China to enter, provided the phytosanitary requirements are met. Ethiopia and Burundi have their own established protocols under earlier announcements, and origins outside the approved list cannot be remedied with documents.

The first step in any coffee project is therefore not a form. It is confirming that the origin is approved, that the correct system applies to the product form, and that the producing or processing facility is the entity on the registration.

## Quarantine Rules That Stop Shipments

For origins covered by a coffee protocol, the phytosanitary requirements are specific and checkable, and failures are found at the port rather than at contract stage.

**Pests of concern.** Four are named in the African coffee protocol: the coffee berry borer, the larger grain borer, Nectria rigidiuscula and Colletotrichum kahawae. Live pests in a consignment mean return, destruction or treatment, and can trigger a suspension of the facility.

**Packing and marking.** Coffee must be packed in clean, new materials that meet China's phytosanitary requirements, and wood packaging must comply with ISPM 15. Every package carries the product name, country, place of origin and the producing or processing facility with its registration number. Cartons and pallets are marked "Exported to the People's Republic of China", and containers are sealed, with seals intact on arrival.

**Certification.** The exporting country's authority issues a phytosanitary certificate that names the producing or processing facility or its registration number, and carries a defined additional declaration confirming the consignment meets the coffee protocol and is free from pests of concern to China.

**Sampling.** For the first two years of trade under the African protocol, the exporting country's authority samples each consignment at a rate of no less than two per cent; where no phytosanitary problem has arisen in those two years, the rate can be adjusted to no less than one per cent.

## What Actually Gets Coffee Rejected

The failure modes we see are consistent, and most are avoidable before a container moves.

- The consignment comes from a facility that is not registered, which results in return or destruction
- There is no valid phytosanitary certificate, also return or destruction
- Live pests, plant residues or soil are found
- The product does not meet Chinese food safety law or national standards
- The agent name declared at customs does not match the name in the register, character for character

## The Classification Traps

Three questions decide whether a coffee project runs smoothly, and they are the questions exporters most often answer by assumption.

**Is ground coffee covered by a roasted bean registration?** Not necessarily. The registration sub-item describes beans, and ground product has to be checked against the scope of the existing registration rather than assumed into it.

**Where does liquid coffee extract sit?** It registers in the beverage category, not as coffee beans, which is a different registration from the one the roaster holds.

**Whose name goes on the registration?** The facility that actually produces. A trading company filing in its own name is rejected, and an approval obtained with inaccurate information can be withdrawn later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does green coffee need GACC food registration?

No. Unroasted beans are outside the food registration system and are managed as agricultural products under Announcement 219.

### Does the facility still need approval from the exporting country's authority?

For origins covered by a coffee protocol, yes. The producing or processing facility must be audited by the exporting country's competent authority and approved by GACC, and the approved list is published before trade starts.

### What about packaging and labels?

Bulk green beans are marked at package level as the protocol requires. Retail packs of roasted coffee sold in China additionally need Chinese labels that meet the national standards, which we cover in our [guide to Chinese label compliance](/en/blog/china-label-compliance/).

### How long does registration take?

In our filings, approval under the food route has come through in a few weeks once the dossier is correct. The delays we see are almost never system delays; they are classification, entity and translation problems found after submission.

### Does my roasted coffee registration cover green beans?

No. Roasted beans and green beans sit in different systems, and the two are declared and verified differently.

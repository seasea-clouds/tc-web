Ongoing operations are delivered month by month, and each of the fourteen services has a deliverable you can accept. S0 to S6 put the project on track; S10 to S13 bring audit, visibility, content and membership into the daily routine. The four that genuinely settle into steady state are the last four. Every item has a fixed action, a fixed record and one person who signs it, so when something goes wrong you do not start with a meeting to work out whose job it was.

The service list is cut by signing authority, not by system. One row is one thing: the deliverable plus how we execute it. Once a deliverable is issued, we walk your team through it item by item; if any item is missing, the corresponding package is not accepted. Durations are conditional ranges; the conditional upper bound counts from start T and is an upper bound, not a promise. S6 follows the settlement cycle, and S7 to S13 follow the module lists.

## How the first six stages are sequenced

S0 Diagnosis takes 2–4 weeks. Four documents arrive together: the market-entry special report, the compliance basis report, the execution checklist and the port execution basis verification checklist. Diagnosis does not end when the briefing ends; every item in the reports is reviewed with your team. If S0 is not settled, S1 and S3 do not start, and any quotation afterwards is empty.

S1 Entity takes 4–12 weeks. There are only two deliverables: the license and the customs registration. Only when both are in place does the declaration and settlement chain open. This step cannot slip, because it gates both store account opening and the first declaration.

S2 Storefront takes 4–8 weeks. Build the store, open the accounts, connect payment, then run test orders from the consumer's checkout all the way to a successful payment. A test order is not a click on a page; it runs refunds, reconciliation and order status callbacks end to end.

S3 product filing takes 2–6 weeks. Issue the classification ruling per SKU, then collect the documents. A wrong tariff code forces an amendment on every later declaration, and amendments slow inventory turnover, so it is better to be slower at this step.

S4 Warehousing takes 2–4 weeks. Register the bonded ledger and stand up the visibility dashboard. The ledger registers goods and the dashboard watches the books; both share one source, which is what later lets S11 show that goods and ledger agree.

S5 clearance with the three documents takes 1–2 weeks. Three documents plus the first shipment, the timing checked shipment by shipment, and the first release run end to end from declaration to leaving the bonded zone. Only once the first run goes smoothly can you talk about repeating it.

S6 fund settlement follows the settlement cycle. Complete the first full cycle and issue the reconciliation statement and documents. S6 is not something finance remembers at month end; from the very first order it should hang on the same order number.

## The four daily items, each holding one segment

S7 to S13 are seven modules: S7 promotion, S8 after-sales, S9 data, S10 compliance audit, S11 supply chain visibility, S12 content compliance and S13 membership operations. Each has its own module list; we deliver item by item against the list and accept item by item against the same list. Once operations reach steady state, the four you open most often are the ones below.

S10 compliance audit runs monthly. Each month we self-check, keep records and answer inquiries; each month we check Customs' ingredient list and negative list; we assess the impact on SKUs on sale and sync the conclusion to you. The deliverables are the self-check ledger and the filing records. Their value does not lie on the day of the check but the next time a declaration is questioned, when the basis of that time can be pulled out unchanged.

S11 supply chain visibility does one thing: put inventory, orders, clearance and logistics on one screen. All four segments must be visible and exceptions must raise alerts; the dashboard and the ledger share one source, so goods and books agree; alerts go to the person responsible and every step leaves a record. The deliverable is a four-segment visibility dashboard, not a pretty picture.

S12 content compliance is the gate before listing. Detail-page copy, labels and images are reviewed item by item; livestream scripts are reviewed before broadcast; materials are filed so that both records and originals can be retrieved. If content compliance does not pass, the product does not go live. A gate placed before listing is far cheaper than a takedown afterwards.

S13 membership operations follow onshore rules. Members are managed onshore, with no per-head fee; tiers move from trial to repeat purchase and then to compliant referral; subscription, meaning recurring purchase, is offered only for health food used continuously; advisors give advice and do not treat; data stays onshore, and any transfer abroad is assessed under the Personal Information Protection Law. The deliverables are member tiering and the recurring-purchase portal.

## The responsibility table has only three rows

The responsibility table carries no slogans; it sorts only four kinds of thing. Your side owns qualification, the entity path, claims, port and volume; our side owns access, records, liaison and exceptions. There is one contact window, absorbed inside our team, so you are not the one relaying calls between suppliers.

The sign-off page puts primary and joint liability in one place: primary responsibility for product quality and safety rests with the cross-border e-commerce enterprise, that is, the brand; the domestic company the brand commissions to handle business registration is supervised by law and bears civil joint liability, on the basis of Shang Cai Fa [2018] No. 486. The signature is not a process step; it fixes that line of responsibility in place. The brand cannot pass primary responsibility to the service provider, and the service provider cannot push joint liability back by saying that it only executes.

## How to read the two dashboards

The four-track dashboard puts the four data segments you watch that day on one screen. Inventory, orders, clearance and logistics are all here; exceptions are dispatched by owner and the handling is logged.

| Track | Key metric | Current value | Status | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orders track | Orders in transit | 128 | Normal | Details Log |
| Inventory track | Batches in bonded stock | 36 | Normal | Details Log |
| Clearance track | Leaving the zone today | 86 | In progress | Details Log |
| Logistics track | Logistics in transit | 214 | In progress | Details Log |

“Normal” means the current value is within threshold; “in progress” means the process is still running. The two states must not be read together: orders in transit being normal does not mean that clearance will not jam, and today's outbound clearance running does not mean that the logistics segment has no exception. The point of the dashboard is to land exceptions on a specific person early, not to explain them at month end.

The member tiering dashboard records the next delivery date, the recurring-purchase status and every action. Tier, repeat purchase and recurring purchase all sit in one record, visible to advisors and to operations alike.

The ingredient-list check joins the S10 monthly routine rather than opening a separate inspection. One dashboard watches goods and documents, the other watches people and renewals; together they are the daily routine of ongoing operations.

## How a month runs

A batch of 900 bottles of coenzyme Q10 soft capsules enters its first month of steady operations. The brand hands over qualification, label drafts, intended claims, port and monthly volume; we take on access, records, liaison and exceptions. S3 first fixes the classification ruling and documents for this SKU, S4 posts the batch into the bonded ledger, S5 runs the first shipment, and S6 attaches the first settlement to the same order number.

Mid-month, the S10 monthly check finds a new line of questioning from Customs on the ingredient list and the negative list, concerning one excipient of this SKU. We first assess the impact on batches on sale, then sync the conclusion to the brand, and the label draft and detail-page copy are amended at the same time. S12 reviews the detail page before listing and finds that a “helps sleep” claim lacks substantiation; the page is held back and the livestream script is returned for review. Once the copy is revised, the review records and original materials are filed together.

In the same month, the S11 four-track dashboard shows 128 orders in transit, 36 batches in bonded stock, 86 leaving the zone today and 214 in logistics transit. The orders and inventory tracks are normal; the clearance and logistics tracks are still running; exceptions are dispatched by owner and the handling stays in the log. S13 member tiers move from trial to repeat purchase, recurring purchase is opened only for health food used continuously, and member data does not leave the country. At month end S6 issues the reconciliation statement, the brand confirms primary responsibility on the sign-off page, and the domestic company confirms joint liability.

In this way of working, no step depends on watching closely. Every step has a deliverable, a record and one person who signs; when something goes wrong, you first check which item was not delivered, then discuss whose people were involved. What ongoing operations really sells is this line of responsibility that can be reviewed.

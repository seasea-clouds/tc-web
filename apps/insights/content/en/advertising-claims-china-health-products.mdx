In China, whether your copy may claim a health function depends on the status under which the product is imported. Imported as conventional food, detail pages and livestreams may state ingredients and nutrition only. With a health food registration certificate, the blue hat, only the functions listed on that certificate may be stated, and the line "health food is not medicine" must appear. Neither category may carry efficacy or safety assertions, disease prevention or treatment wording, or paid endorser recommendations. Fines published by market regulation authorities land on the copy, not on the product.

There is one hard condition before launch: every batch of detail pages and livestream scripts goes through script review one, asset review two and final launch review, and all three leave an archive number in the ledger. Missing any one of them blocks release. Articles 17 and 18 of the Advertising Law draw the line on disease treatment and health food advertising, and Articles 55 and 58 provide the penalty basis. Health food advertisements must also be reviewed by the advertising review authority before publication under Article 46; an internal three-stage review does not replace that statutory step.

Do not translate structure or function statements from an overseas page straight into Chinese and put them live. A phrase such as "supports metabolism" may be lawful in the country of origin, but when the product is managed as conventional food in China it counts as a health-function claim, and every sentence has to pass the three reviews. Goods under customs supervision mode 1210 are supervised as articles for personal use; that governs import procedure, and the Advertising Law and Food Safety Law still apply to claims.

## What you may say and what you cannot say at all

The comparison table splits into three layers by product status. Conventional food may state ingredients and nutrition; no health-function, disease-prevention or treatment claim may appear. Blue hat products may state only the functions on the registration certificate, not one function beyond it, and must carry the line "health food is not medicine". The layer banned for both has no exceptions: no efficacy or safety assertions, and no recommendations or testimonials from paid endorsers.

![Claims comparison table and published penalty scale: conventional food, blue hat, bans shared by both, and five fine amounts](/images/insights/advertising-claims-china-health-products/claims-allowed-banned-wording-and-penalties.webp)

Published penalties are handled under Articles 55 and 58 of the Advertising Law, and the bar length is proportional to the fine: an actor posing as an expert while claiming efficacy drew 1,224,800 yuan (about 170,000 US dollars at 7.2 yuan per dollar, for reference); claims to "dissolve clots, clear vessels" drew 400,000 yuan; safety assertions for health food drew 250,000 yuan; claims to "improve, relieve, fight cancer" drew 200,000 yuan; claims to "improve sleep, boost immunity" drew 190,000 yuan.

There is a pattern in those numbers: the fine tracks how strong the claim is, and the closer the wording gets to efficacy and disease, the higher the amount. The case with the actor is the largest because a false endorsement was added on top. What gets fined is the script itself; a product that passes inspection does not block the penalty. This is the most frequent penalty point for health products entering China cross-border, and it matches the ranking on the risk map.

## Where the red lines sit for detail pages and livestreams

Six classes must not appear at all: disease prevention or treatment wording, medical terms, absolute claims, the names or images of state agencies, recommendations or testimonials from experts or actors, and fabricated user reviews. Scripts, voiceovers and assets are measured with the same ruler; changing the carrier does not loosen it. Internet advertising is governed by the Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising, Order No. 72 of the State Administration for Market Regulation, whose Article 19 separates the advertising duties of livestream sellers, livestream room operators and livestream marketers, and whose Article 8 bans health food advertising disguised as health or wellness knowledge.

The easy lines to cross are the extra bans on conventional food: all health-function wording counts, including boost immunity, improve sleep and support metabolism; absolute "free" wording must not appear on the page either. Three things must be done: the Chinese electronic label, ingredient list and warnings are complete; claims match credentials item by item; assets are archived for inspection. All three are finished before each batch goes live, and the archive numbers enter the ledger.

## Which three reviews a batch must pass before it goes live

Script review one is signed off by compliance: every line is checked against the banned-word list, producing a compliance opinion and an archive number. Asset review two is signed off by the legal team: claims are matched to credentials item by item, and the Chinese electronic label, ingredient list and warnings are checked at the same time, producing a legal sign-off and a rejection record. The final launch review is confirmed by both sides: the asset may go live only after joint sign-off on the trail page, producing dual sign-off and a ledger number.

None of the three may be skipped; without all three no asset goes live. The banned-word list goes to hosts and influencers in advance, with signed receipts on file. The three sample review conclusions are: completed DA2609-0031 at 09-19 17:12; completed DA2609-0032 at 09-20 10:05; rejected DA2609-0033 at 09-20 14:26, each with details and log links. The basis is the Advertising Law and the Food Safety Law; reviews run per batch, with a legal re-check before launch.

Spoken lines get no protection from page review: scripts, prompts and replies all go through review and are archived. Livestream red lines share the same table as detail pages, with the three absolute-prohibition classes and the tracked boundaries applied verbatim. The same list goes to hosts and influencers in advance, and receipts are filed with each batch.

Gift-with-purchase, trial-size and spend-threshold plans pass review before launch. Whether the gift value counts into the dutiable price is confirmed by us and shared with you, and trial sizes follow the full-size path. Event plans and review conclusions enter the ledger under asset archive numbers and stay retrievable; changes after launch re-run the review and are filed per batch.

## One fish oil script from draft to release

Take this batch's fish oil softgel livestream script as the worked example. The first draft contained "dissolve clots, clear vessels" and "improve sleep, boost immunity", both common function statements on overseas pages. Script review one checked every line against the banned-word list and rejected it at 09-20 14:26, with the rejection reason reading "2 efficacy assertions" and archive number DA2609-0033.

The revision did one thing: it replaced the efficacy assertions with verifiable statements about ingredients and nutrition, without rewording the claim to slip past. Script review one passed at 09-21 09:30 under DA2609-0034. Asset review two then checked the Chinese electronic label, ingredient list and warnings, together with the credentials behind every claim. The final launch review required both sides to sign on the trail page before the stream could start. Hosts and influencers held the same word list, and signed receipts were filed with the batch.

Had the first draft gone live, the penalty scale puts "dissolve clots, clear vessels" in the 400,000 yuan band and "improve sleep, boost immunity" in the 190,000 yuan band. Two sentences cost far more than two extra reviews.

## What this does not cover

This piece covers claim wording and the content review trail only. Whether the product is filed as conventional food or a blue hat product, how tariff codes and tax are calculated, whether customer data may leave China, and how promotion commissions are remitted abroad are all outside it; so are regulator inquiries and sampling inspections. The two ledgers are internal execution records and do not replace the pre-publication advertising review required by Article 46 of the Advertising Law: health food advertising still has to be submitted for review.

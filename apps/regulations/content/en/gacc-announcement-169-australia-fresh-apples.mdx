Australian fresh apples are cleared for export to China. Announcement No. 169 of 2025 requires orchards, packing houses and cold-treatment facilities to be reviewed by the Australian side and approved and registered by the Chinese side; the registered list is published on the GACC website.

When arranging a shipment, verify the orchard and packing house registration numbers and cold-treatment records; apples from fruit-fly areas must complete the required cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate carries the additional declaration on protocol compliance and pest freedom, and packages show product, origin (state), registration number and the export-to-China marking.

On arrival, Chinese customs verify the permit, certificate and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine. Unregistered premises, ineffective cold treatment, live quarantine pests or soil, and food-safety non-compliance lead respectively to arrival cold treatment, return, destruction or quarantine treatment.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions on phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh apples from Australia to China agreed between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Australian Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (hereinafter the Australian side), fresh apples from Australia meeting the following requirements are allowed for import with effect from the date of publication.

Inspection and quarantine basis

(i) Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Australian Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Apples from Australia to China.

Name of the commodity allowed for import

Fresh apples (hereinafter apples), scientific name Malus domestica L., English name Apple.

Authorized origin areas

Apple-producing areas of Australia.

Registration

Orchards, packing houses and cold-treatment facilities for apples destined for China shall be reviewed by the Australian side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. Registration information includes name, address and registration number, so as to ensure accurate traceability when the exported goods do not comply with the provisions of this announcement. Before each export season, the Australian side shall provide the Chinese side with the registered list, which, after examination and approval by the Chinese side, shall be published on the GACC website.

List of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side

(1) Queensland fruit fly, Bactrocera tryoni

(2) Mediterranean fruit fly, Ceratitis capitata

(3) Codling moth, Cydia pomonella

(4) Light brown apple moth, Epiphyas postvittana

(5) Woolly apple aphid, Eriosoma lanigerum

(6) Brown rot, Monilinia fructicola

(7) Botryosphaeria canker, Botryosphaeria stevensii (= Diplodia mutila)

Pre-export management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards destined for China shall maintain good sanitary conditions, establish a sound traceability management system and control pest occurrence. Under the supervision of the Australian side, integrated pest management shall be implemented, including pruning and orchard management at the end of the season, pest and disease monitoring, biological control and chemical control.

(2) The Australian side shall formulate management plans for quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organize orchard monitoring. Pest monitoring and control in orchards destined for China shall be carried out under the supervision and guidance of Australian professional technical personnel, who shall have received training from the Australian side or its authorized institution.

(3) Orchards destined for China must keep pest monitoring and control records and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Control records include the name, dosage and timing of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(4) For the Mediterranean fruit fly and the Queensland fruit fly.

(1) From fruit-fly-free areas: Tasmania is a fruit-fly-free area for the Mediterranean fruit fly and the Queensland fruit fly; orchards of apples destined for China shall establish a monitoring trap system and maintain the existing pest-free status of the state. If the Mediterranean fruit fly or the Queensland fruit fly is detected, the Australian side must notify the Chinese side within 48 hours and immediately launch the national emergency plant pest response plan, including delimiting surveys with additional traps to determine the extent, location, surrounding areas and buffer zones of the outbreak, as well as fruit sampling. Affected areas shall suspend apple exports to China, or the fruit shall undergo eradication treatment under methods recognized and approved by the Chinese side.

(2) From fruit-fly infested areas (areas outside Tasmania, i.e., the Australian mainland): the Mediterranean fruit fly occurs only in Western Australia and nowhere else in Australia; the Queensland fruit fly occurs only in eastern Australia and not in Western Australia. In orchards destined for China, the Australian side or its authorized personnel shall conduct visual inspection or trap monitoring of fruit flies from flowering to harvest.

(5) For the codling moth, apples destined for China shall originate from codling moth-free areas or low-prevalence areas recognized and approved in accordance with the guidelines of the International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures, or adopt systematic management measures recognized by the Chinese side (see Annex 1). Codling moth does not occur in Western Australia.

(6) For Monilinia fructicola and Botryosphaeria stevensii, apples destined for China shall originate from pest-free or low-prevalence areas of the above pests, recognized and approved in accordance with the guidelines of the International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures, or adopt systematic management measures recognized by the Chinese side (see Annex 2).

(7) For the light brown apple moth, woolly apple aphid and other quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, orchards destined for China shall be monitored from flowering to harvest, once every two weeks, with focused inspection of branches, stems, leaves and fruit for these pests. If any pest is detected, measures shall be taken immediately to control its occurrence.

(ii) Processing and packaging management.

(1) Apples destined for China shall be processed and packed in packing houses approved by the Australian side. Packing houses and cold stores shall have good sanitary conditions and measures to prevent pest reinfestation.

(2) During processing and packaging, apples shall be sorted, washed, classified and graded to ensure they carry no insects, mites, rotten fruit, or branches, leaves, roots or soil. Defective fruit shall be removed during processing, and a post-harvest fungicide treatment shall be applied.

(3) Packed apples shall be placed in storage immediately and kept separately to avoid reinfestation by pests.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and comply with the relevant Chinese phytosanitary requirements.

(2) If packing boxes have ventilation holes, insect-proof nets (maximum mesh aperture 1.6 mm), durable plastic bags or liners (including perforated ones) or adhesive tape shall be used to cover the ventilation holes of each box or the entire pallet; alternatively, micro-perforated packaging bags shall cover the ventilation holes of each box or the entire pallet to prevent pests from entering; alternatively, ventilated boxes may be packed directly in sealed safety rooms or containers to avoid reinfestation.

(3) If wood packaging is used, it shall comply with the requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(4) Each packing box shall indicate in English the fruit name, country, origin (state), orchard or its registration number, and packing house or its registration number. Each packing box and pallet shall carry the marking “Exported to the People’s Republic of China” in Chinese or English.

(iv) Quarantine treatment requirements.

Apples destined for China from fruit-fly infested areas (areas outside Tasmania, i.e., the Australian mainland) shall undergo cold treatment against fruit flies. Cold treatment shall be carried out under the supervision of the Australian side or its authorized personnel, in accordance with the pre-export cold treatment procedure (see Annex 3) or the in-transit cold treatment procedure (see Annex 4).

Apples destined for China from Western Australia shall undergo cold treatment against the Mediterranean fruit fly, meeting either of the following indicators: 1°C or below (flesh temperature) for 16 consecutive days or more; or 2.1°C or below (flesh temperature) for 21 consecutive days or more.

Apples from the Australian mainland outside Western Australia shall undergo cold treatment against the Queensland fruit fly, meeting the following indicator: 3°C or below (flesh temperature) for 18 consecutive days or more.

(v) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) For two years following the issuance of this announcement, the Australian side or its authorized personnel shall inspect for quarantine the 2% of cartons of apples destined for China, with a minimum sample of 600 fruit, and conduct dissection checks on 60 of them. If no phytosanitary problem occurs within two years, the sampling proportion may be reduced to 1% of cartons, but the sample shall be no fewer than 600 fruit.

(2) If quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side are detected, the whole consignment shall not be exported to China. If the codling moth, Monilinia fructicola or Botryosphaeria stevensii is intercepted for the first time, exports from the relevant orchards shall be suspended for the current season, and whether to suspend the relevant packing house shall be assessed based on the investigation results. If the consignment comes from a pest-free area or pest-free production site, that area or site status shall be revoked. The Australian side shall identify the cause of non-compliance and take corrective measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side will decide, based on the outcome of the corrective action, whether to lift the suspension.

(vi) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For consignments passing quarantine, the Australian side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate indicating the registration numbers of the orchard and packing house, and the additional declaration shall state: “This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Apples from Australian to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (This consignment meets the requirements of the Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Apples from Australia to China, and carries no quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.)

(2) For apples undergoing pre-export cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall indicate the quarantine treatment method, treatment temperature, duration, and the name or registration number of the treatment facility. For apples undergoing in-transit cold treatment, the phytosanitary certificate shall bear the mark “Cold treatment in transit” (cold treatment in transit), and state the cold treatment temperature, treatment duration, container number and seal number.

(3) Before trade begins, the Australian side shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and filing.

Entry inspection and quarantine and non-compliance handling

When apples destined for China arrive at the entry port, Chinese customs shall carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Certificate and mark verification.

(1) Verify whether the imported fruit holds the Animal and Plant Quarantine Entry Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of item (vi) of Article VI of this announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on packing boxes or wood packaging comply with the provisions of item (iii) of Article VI of this announcement.

(4) For consignments with pre-export cold treatment, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, the cold treatment result report signed by the Australian side, and the fruit temperature probe calibration records meet the provisions of item (iv) of Article VI of this announcement.

(5) For consignments with in-transit cold treatment, verify whether the phytosanitary certificate, cold treatment report and fruit temperature probe calibration records meet the provisions of item (iv) of Article VI of this announcement.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Apples destined for China shall enter through ports authorized by the Chinese side for fruit imports.

(2) Imported apples shall be inspected and quarantined in accordance with the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules; those passing inspection and quarantine are allowed to enter.

(iii) Non-compliance handling.

(1) If apples are found to come from unregistered orchards, packing houses or cold-treatment facilities, the consignment shall not be allowed to enter.

(2) For consignments from fruit-fly infested areas, if the cold treatment is deemed ineffective, the consignment shall be subject to on-arrival cold treatment (which may be carried out in the container itself), return or destruction.

(3) If live specimens of the quarantine pests listed in Article V of this announcement, quarantine pests newly detected in Australia, or soil are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to quarantine treatment.

(4) If the consignment does not comply with Chinese national food safety standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

(5) If the above non-compliance is found, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the Australian side and, depending on the circumstances, suspend apple imports from the relevant orchards, packing houses and cold-treatment facilities; if the consignment comes from a pest-free area or pest-free production site, that area or site status shall be revoked. The Australian side shall identify the cause of non-compliance and take corrective measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side will decide, based on the outcome of the corrective action, whether to lift the suspension.

This announcement is hereby issued.

Annexes: 1. Systematic measures against codling moth; 2. Systematic measures against Monilinia fructicola and Botryosphaeria stevensii; 3. Pre-export cold treatment procedure; 4. In-transit cold treatment procedure.

General Administration of Customs

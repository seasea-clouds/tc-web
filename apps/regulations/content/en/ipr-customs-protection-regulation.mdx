I have seen brands lose out in cross-border work: counterfeit batches leave the country while the brand has no recordal with customs, so customs has no one to notify. These Regulations are the basis for customs protection; they took effect on 1 March 2004, and the current text is the second revision of 2018.

The rights holder first records its rights with the General Administration of Customs. The recordal is valid for 10 years and can be renewed within 6 months before expiry; no fee is charged at present. Once recorded, customs will notify you in writing when it finds suspected infringing goods; you may also apply for detention yourself, but must provide a security not exceeding the value of the goods.

Import consignees and export consignors must also truthfully declare the intellectual property status. My advice: complete the recordal before your next shipment leaves, not when the goods are already at the port.

(Promulgated by Decree No. 395 of the State Council of the People's Republic of China on 2 December 2003; first revised on 24 March 2010 by the Decision of the State Council on Amending the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Customs Protection of Intellectual Property Rights; second revised on 19 March 2018 by the Decision of the State Council on Amending and Repealing Certain Administrative Regulations)

## Chapter I General Provisions

**Article 1** These Regulations are formulated in accordance with the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China for the purpose of implementing customs protection of intellectual property rights, promoting foreign economic, trade, scientific, technological and cultural exchanges, and safeguarding the public interest.

**Article 2** For the purposes of these Regulations, “customs protection of intellectual property rights” means the protection implemented by customs of the exclusive right to use a trademark, copyright and rights related to copyright, and patent rights (hereinafter collectively referred to as intellectual property rights) that are related to imported and exported goods and protected by the laws and administrative regulations of the People's Republic of China.

**Article 3** The State prohibits the import and export of goods that infringe intellectual property rights.

Customs shall implement intellectual property protection in accordance with the relevant laws and these Regulations, and exercise the relevant powers provided for in the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China.

**Article 4** Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to implement intellectual property protection, it shall submit an application to customs for the adoption of protection measures.

**Article 5** The consignee of imported goods or its agent, and the consignor of exported goods or its agent, shall truthfully declare to customs the intellectual property status related to the imported and exported goods in accordance with State regulations, and submit the relevant supporting documents.

**Article 6** Customs shall keep confidential the commercial secrets of the parties concerned when implementing intellectual property protection.

## Chapter II Recordal of Intellectual Property Rights

**Article 7** An intellectual property rights holder may, in accordance with these Regulations, apply to the General Administration of Customs for recordal of its intellectual property rights; when applying for recordal, it shall submit a written application. The application shall include the following:

(1) the name or title, place of registration or nationality, etc. of the intellectual property rights holder;

(2) the name, content and related information of the intellectual property right;

(3) the status of the licensing of the intellectual property right;

(4) the name, place of origin, customs office at the place of entry or exit, importers and exporters, main characteristics, price, etc. of the goods in which the intellectual property rights holder lawfully exercises its intellectual property rights;

(5) the manufacturers, importers and exporters, customs offices at the place of entry or exit, main characteristics, price, etc. of known goods infringing the intellectual property rights.

Where the contents of the application prescribed in the preceding paragraph are supported by certifying documents, the intellectual property rights holder shall attach the certifying documents.

**Article 8** The General Administration of Customs shall, within 30 working days from the date of receipt of all application documents, decide whether to grant recordal and notify the applicant in writing; where recordal is not granted, it shall state the reasons.

The General Administration of Customs shall not grant recordal under any of the following circumstances:

(1) the application documents are incomplete or invalid;

(2) the applicant is not the intellectual property rights holder;

(3) the intellectual property right is no longer protected by laws or administrative regulations.

**Article 9** Where customs finds that an intellectual property rights holder has failed to truthfully provide relevant information or documents when applying for recordal of an intellectual property right, the General Administration of Customs may cancel the recordal.

**Article 10** Recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights shall take effect on the date the General Administration of Customs grants the recordal and shall be valid for 10 years.

Where the intellectual property right remains valid, the intellectual property rights holder may, within 6 months before the expiry of the recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights, apply to the General Administration of Customs for renewal of the recordal. Each renewal of the recordal shall be valid for 10 years.

Where the recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights expires without an application for renewal, or the intellectual property right is no longer protected by laws or administrative regulations, the recordal shall lapse immediately.

**Article 11** Where the circumstances of the recordal of an intellectual property right change, the intellectual property rights holder shall, within 30 working days from the date of the change, complete the formalities for modification or cancellation of the recordal with the General Administration of Customs.

Where the intellectual property rights holder fails to complete the modification or cancellation formalities in accordance with the preceding paragraph, and this seriously affects the lawful import and export of others or the performance of customs supervision duties in accordance with law, the General Administration of Customs may cancel the relevant recordal upon the application of an interested party, or on its own initiative.

## Chapter III Application for Detention of Suspected Infringing Goods and Handling Thereof

**Article 12** Where an intellectual property rights holder discovers that suspected infringing goods are about to be imported or exported, it may apply to the customs office at the place of entry or exit of the goods for detention of the suspected infringing goods.

**Article 13** Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to detain suspected infringing goods, it shall submit an application and the relevant certifying documents, and provide evidence sufficient to prove that the infringement is obviously established.

The application shall mainly include the following:

(1) the name or title, place of registration or nationality, etc. of the intellectual property rights holder;

(2) the name, content and related information of the intellectual property right;

(3) the names of the consignee and the consignor of the suspected infringing goods;

(4) the name, specifications, etc. of the suspected infringing goods;

(5) the ports, time, means of transport, etc. through which the suspected infringing goods may enter or exit.

Where the suspected infringing goods are suspected of infringing a recorded intellectual property right, the application shall also include the customs recordal number.

**Article 14** Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to detain suspected infringing goods, it shall provide customs with a security not exceeding the value of the goods, for compensating losses that may be caused to the consignee or consignor by an improper application and for paying the costs of storage, custody and disposal of the goods after detention by customs; where the intellectual property rights holder pays the storage and custody costs directly to the warehouse operator, such costs shall be deducted from the security. The specific measures shall be formulated by the General Administration of Customs.

**Article 15** Where an intellectual property rights holder applies for detention of suspected infringing goods, complies with the provisions of Article 13 of these Regulations and provides a security in accordance with Article 14 of these Regulations, customs shall detain the suspected infringing goods, notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing, and serve the customs detention receipt on the consignee or consignor.

Where an intellectual property rights holder applies for detention of suspected infringing goods but does not comply with the provisions of Article 13 of these Regulations, or fails to provide a security in accordance with Article 14 of these Regulations, customs shall reject the application and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

**Article 16** Where customs discovers that imported or exported goods are suspected of infringing a recorded intellectual property right, it shall immediately notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing. Where the intellectual property rights holder, within 3 working days from the date of service of the notice, files an application in accordance with Article 13 of these Regulations and provides a security in accordance with Article 14 of these Regulations, customs shall detain the suspected infringing goods, notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing, and serve the customs detention receipt on the consignee or consignor. Where the intellectual property rights holder fails to file an application or provide a security within the time limit, customs shall not detain the goods.

**Article 17** With the consent of customs, the intellectual property rights holder and the consignee or consignor may inspect the goods concerned.

**Article 18** Where the consignee or consignor considers that its goods do not infringe the intellectual property rights of the intellectual property rights holder, it shall submit a written statement to customs with the relevant evidence attached.

**Article 19** Where the consignee or consignor of goods suspected of infringing a patent right considers that the imported or exported goods do not infringe the patent right, it may, after providing customs with a cash security equal to the value of the goods, request customs to release the goods. Where the intellectual property rights holder fails to bring a lawsuit before a people's court within a reasonable period, customs shall refund the cash security.

**Article 20** Where customs discovers that imported or exported goods are suspected of infringing a recorded intellectual property right and, after notifying the intellectual property rights holder, the holder requests customs to detain the suspected infringing goods, customs shall, within 30 working days from the date of detention, investigate and determine whether the detained suspected infringing goods infringe an intellectual property right; where a determination cannot be made, it shall immediately notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

**Article 21** Where customs requests the competent intellectual property department to provide assistance in investigating the detained suspected infringing goods, the competent intellectual property department concerned shall provide assistance.

Where the competent intellectual property department requests customs to provide assistance in handling infringement cases involving imported or exported goods, customs shall provide assistance.

**Article 22** The intellectual property rights holder and the consignee or consignor shall cooperate when customs investigates the detained suspected infringing goods and the relevant circumstances.

**Article 23** After filing an application with customs for the adoption of protection measures, the intellectual property rights holder may, in accordance with the Trademark Law of the People's Republic of China, the Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China, the Patent Law of the People's Republic of China or other relevant laws, apply to a people's court for measures ordering the cessation of the infringing act or for property preservation in respect of the detained suspected infringing goods.

Where customs receives a notice from a people's court requesting assistance in the enforcement of an order to cease the infringing act or of property preservation measures, it shall provide assistance.

**Article 24** Customs shall release the detained suspected infringing goods under any of the following circumstances:

(1) where customs detained the suspected infringing goods in accordance with Article 15 of these Regulations and has not received a notice from a people's court requesting assistance in enforcement within 20 working days from the date of detention;

(2) where customs detained the suspected infringing goods in accordance with Article 16 of these Regulations and has not received a notice from a people's court requesting assistance in enforcement within 50 working days from the date of detention, and the investigation cannot determine that the detained suspected infringing goods infringe an intellectual property right;

(3) where the consignee or consignor of goods suspected of infringing a patent right requests the release of the goods after providing customs with a cash security equal to the value of the goods;

(4) where customs considers that the consignee or consignor has sufficient evidence to prove that its goods do not infringe the intellectual property rights of the intellectual property rights holder;

(5) where the intellectual property rights holder withdraws its application for detention of the suspected infringing goods before customs determines that the detained suspected infringing goods are infringing goods.

**Article 25** Where customs detains suspected infringing goods in accordance with these Regulations, the intellectual property rights holder shall pay the relevant costs of storage, custody and disposal. Where the intellectual property rights holder fails to pay the relevant costs, customs may deduct them from the cash security provided by the holder to customs, or require the guarantor to perform the relevant security obligations.

Where the suspected infringing goods are determined to infringe intellectual property rights, the intellectual property rights holder may include the relevant costs of storage, custody and disposal it has paid in the reasonable expenses incurred to stop the infringing act.

**Article 26** Where customs discovers a suspected criminal case in the course of implementing intellectual property protection, it shall transfer the case to the public security organ for handling in accordance with law.

## Chapter IV Legal Liability

**Article 27** Where detained suspected infringing goods are determined to infringe intellectual property rights after investigation by customs, customs shall confiscate them.

After confiscating goods infringing intellectual property rights, customs shall notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing of the relevant information on the infringing goods.

Where the confiscated goods infringing intellectual property rights can be used for social welfare undertakings, customs shall transfer them to the relevant public welfare institutions for use in social welfare undertakings; where the intellectual property rights holder is willing to purchase them, customs may transfer them to the holder for consideration. Where the confiscated goods infringing intellectual property rights cannot be used for social welfare undertakings and the intellectual property rights holder is unwilling to purchase them, customs may, after eliminating the infringing characteristics, auction them in accordance with law; however, for imported goods bearing counterfeit trademarks, except in special circumstances, they shall not be allowed to enter commercial channels merely by removing the trademark labels on the goods; where the infringing characteristics cannot be eliminated, customs shall destroy them.

**Article 28** Where, after customs accepts an application for recordal of intellectual property protection and for the adoption of intellectual property protection measures, customs fails to discover infringing goods, fails to adopt protection measures in a timely manner, or the protection measures adopted are ineffective because the intellectual property rights holder failed to provide accurate information, the intellectual property rights holder shall bear the responsibility itself.

Where, after the intellectual property rights holder requests customs to detain suspected infringing goods, customs cannot determine that the detained suspected infringing goods infringe the intellectual property rights of the holder, or a people's court rules that they do not infringe the intellectual property rights of the holder, the intellectual property rights holder shall bear liability for compensation in accordance with law.

**Article 29** Where the import or export of goods infringing intellectual property rights constitutes a crime, criminal liability shall be pursued in accordance with law.

**Article 30** Where customs officers, in implementing intellectual property protection, neglect their duties, abuse their powers or engage in malpractices for personal gain, and the act constitutes a crime, criminal liability shall be pursued in accordance with law; where the act does not constitute a crime, administrative sanctions shall be given in accordance with law.

## Chapter V Supplementary Provisions

**Article 31** Goods carried or mailed by individuals into or out of the territory that exceed the reasonable quantity for personal use and infringe intellectual property rights as provided in Article 2 of these Regulations shall be handled as infringing goods.

**Article 32** These Regulations shall come into force on 1 March 2004. The Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Customs Protection of Intellectual Property Rights promulgated by the State Council on 5 July 1995 shall be repealed simultaneously.

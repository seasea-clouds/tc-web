Announcement No. 33 of 2026 publishes origin rules for the China-Congo early harvest arrangement.

Goods qualify if wholly obtained, produced entirely from originating materials, or meet tariff classification change or regional value content of at least forty percent.

Certificates must be issued by authorised bodies and made out in English; the preferential tariff code is twenty-nine and records are kept for three years.

<!-- original -->

On 1 April 2026, the Arrangement on Early Harvest of the Agreement on the Development of Economic Partnership between the Government of the People's Republic of China and the Government of the Republic of the Congo enters into force. GACC has formulated the Measures for the Administration of Origin of Import and Export Goods under the Arrangement, hereby published.

Importers or their agents applying for agreement tariff rates shall complete the Customs import declaration and submit origin documents as provided in Annex 2 of GACC Announcement No. 34 of 2021 for preferential trade agreements without electronic data exchange; the preferential trade agreement code field shall be filled in with “29”.

Certificates of origin under the Arrangement may be self-printed.

Hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

30 March 2026

Measures of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China for the Administration of Origin of Import and Export Goods under the Arrangement between the Government of China and the Government of the Republic of the Congo on Early Harvest of the Agreement on the Development of Economic Partnership

Article 1 These Measures are formulated to determine correctly the origin of import and export goods under the Arrangement on Early Harvest (hereinafter the Arrangement), to promote economic and trade exchanges between China and the Republic of the Congo (Congo-Brazzaville), in accordance with the Customs Law, the Tariff Law, the Regulations on the Origin of Import and Export Goods and the Arrangement.

Article 2 These Measures apply to the origin administration of import and export goods under the Arrangement.

Article 3 Goods are originating goods of the Arrangement and acquire originating status if wholly obtained or produced in China or Congo-Brazzaville; produced entirely from originating materials; or produced using non-originating materials and meeting either the product-specific rules of origin (Annex 1) including tariff classification change, or regional value content of not less than 40%. Changes to the product-specific rules shall be announced separately by GACC.

Article 4 Goods “wholly obtained or produced” under Article 3(1) include: live animals born and raised there; goods obtained from such animals; plants and plant products harvested or gathered there; goods obtained by hunting, trapping, fishing, aquaculture, gathering or capturing; minerals and natural substances extracted from soil, waters or seabed; goods extracted from waters beyond territorial waters which that party is entitled to exploit; fish and marine products obtained by vessels registered and flying that party's flag; goods produced onboard factory ships registered and flying that party's flag solely from the aforementioned products; waste and scrap from processing; used goods collected there; and goods produced there solely from the aforementioned items.

Article 5 Tariff classification change means that the tariff heading designation in the Customs Tariff of the People's Republic of China changes to a specified digit after manufacture or processing of non-originating materials in China or Congo-Brazzaville.

Article 6 Regional value content shall be calculated by the relevant formula. “Price of non-originating materials” means the import cost, freight and insurance to the destination port determined under the WTO Valuation Agreement, including materials of unknown origin; for materials obtained in China or Congo-Brazzaville, the first ascertainable price paid or payable there, excluding freight, insurance, packing and other costs from the supplier's warehouse. The price of non-originating materials excludes non-originating materials used to produce originating materials.

Article 7 Originating materials of China or Congo-Brazzaville used in the other party to produce another good shall be regarded as originating in that other party.

Article 8 Goods meeting the tariff classification change standard may be regarded as originating if all non-originating materials not meeting the standard do not exceed 10% of the FOB price and all other requirements are met.

Article 9 Goods shall not acquire originating status if they undergo only minimal operations such as preserving goods, simple assembly or disassembly, changing packaging, washing, cleaning, ironing, simple painting, husking or polishing, peeling, sharpening, filtering, bottling, labelling, simple mixing, testing, dilution, drying, salting, freezing or animal slaughter. All operations in China or Congo-Brazzaville shall be considered.

Article 10 Where originating and non-originating interchangeable materials are used, originating status shall be determined by physical separation or an inventory management method recognised by the generally accepted accounting principles of the exporting party and used continuously for at least 12 months.

Article 11 Materials used in production, testing or inspection that do not form part of the goods, such as fuel, energy, catalysts, solvents, plant, equipment, machines, gloves, tools, moulds, spare parts, lubricants and other materials, do not affect the determination of origin.

Article 12 Packaging materials and containers used for transport do not affect origin. Retail packaging classified with the goods shall be included as originating or non-originating materials according to their own origin when the regional value content standard applies, and shall not affect origin under other standards.

Article 13 Accessories, spare parts and tools declared with the goods, classified with them in the Tariff and invoiced together, shall be included as originating or non-originating materials according to their own origin under the regional value content standard, and shall not affect origin under the tariff classification change standard. Their quantity and price shall be within reasonable limits.

Article 14 Originating goods retain their status if transported directly from the exporting to the importing party, or through other countries solely for geographical or transport reasons, not treated beyond unloading or preserving, under customs supervision, with temporary storage not exceeding 6 months.

Article 15 Valid certificates of origin under the Arrangement (Annex 2) shall cover originating goods, be issued by authorised bodies of the exporting party on application, bear a unique number, cover one or more consignments, state the basis of origin, bear signature or seal consistent with notified specimens, and be made out in English.

Article 16 Certificates shall be issued before or at shipment and be valid for one year from issuance. Retrospective issuance within one year of shipment is permitted for force majeure or other reasonable causes, marked “ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY” and valid for one year from shipment.

Article 17 If a certificate is stolen, lost or damaged, the exporter may apply for a certified copy marked “CERTIFIED TRUE COPY of the original Certificate of Origin number ___ dated ___”, valid as the original. Upon submission of the copy the original becomes invalid; if the original has been used, the copy is invalid.

Article 18 Applications for agreement tariff rates shall be accompanied by the certificate, commercial invoice and full transport documents. Goods transported through third countries require certificates from those countries' customs. No third-country certificate is required where transport documents satisfy direct consignment rules.

Article 19 Where no valid certificate is submitted at declaration, a supplementary declaration with a statement of origin (Annex 3) shall be made before customs formalities are completed. With security provided, Customs shall proceed except where the provision of security is prohibited. No adjustment of collected duties shall be made for applications after formalities are completed.

Article 20 The importer may apply to release the security within the approved period after supplementary declaration with a valid certificate, or after Customs completes the origin verification confirming originating status.

Article 21 No certificate is required for a consignment with a dutiable value not exceeding 1000 in value; this shall not apply where the consignment is split to evade these Measures.

Article 22 Customs may request supplementary information or verification from Congo-Brazzaville to determine authenticity and origin; release against security may be granted during verification unless otherwise provided.

Article 23 The consignee, the consignor of export goods and the producer of export goods shall retain documents proving origin for 3 years from completion of customs formalities.

Article 24 Agreement tariff rates shall not apply where: no application or supplementary declaration is made; goods are not originating; the certificate does not comply; the certificate does not match the goods; no verification feedback is received within 6 months or the feedback is insufficient; or other violations occur.

Article 25 The consignor of export goods, its agent, and the domestic producer and its agent may apply to Chinese issuing bodies for a certificate of origin under the Measures for the Administration of Issuance of Certificates of Origin for Export Goods (GACC Order No. 270).

Article 26 At the time of export declaration, the consignor of export goods or its agent shall complete the Customs export declaration in accordance with the Customs declaration provisions.

Article 27 Definitions: aquaculture; FOB price; interchangeable materials; generally accepted accounting principles; goods; materials; non-originating goods or materials; originating goods or materials; production; and the WTO Valuation Agreement, being the Agreement on Implementation of Article VII of GATT 1994.

Article 28 These Measures are interpreted by GACC.

Article 29 These Measures shall take effect on 1 April 2026.

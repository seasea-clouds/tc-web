## The Challenge: A Premium Brand, a Fragmented Market

A family-owned pet food company in the US Midwest had spent 25 years building a premium, grain-free brand that was a best-seller across North American retail chains. In late 2023, they made a decision that sounded simple: enter China, the world's second-largest pet food market, where spending on pet nutrition was growing at double digits every year.

The reality was anything but simple.

### The Reality Check

As their export manager later put it: *"Everything we knew about 'compliance' in the US turned out to be the wrong map for China."* The gaps they discovered were substantial:

- **GACC registration of the manufacturing facility.** Under Decree 248, their US plant had to be registered with Chinese customs through the CIFER system before a single bag could ship. They had never heard of it.
- **MOA product registration.** Pet food is classified as feed in China, which means every product needed registration with the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, a process with its own testing and documentation requirements.
- **Chinese labeling under GB 10648.** Labels had to be redesigned in Chinese, with regulated statements and warning text that had no equivalent on their US packaging.
- **Port-level inspection variance.** Different ports applied inspection requirements differently, and they had no way to evaluate which entry strategy was safest.

They first tried to navigate it with a local importer. Their first test shipment was held at customs for weeks. Then the importer asked for a compliance plan they could not produce. Three months of self-research later, they told us: *"The more we read, the less we knew."*

## Why They Came to Us

They found us through a search for importing pet food into China and booked a free assessment. In the first call, we did three things:

1. **Mapped their entire product line against China's import eligibility.** Two of their six SKUs were clear candidates, two needed reformulation to qualify, and two we advised holding back.
2. **Flagged the three approvals they were missing.** GACC plant registration, MOA product registration, and label compliance, in priority order.
3. **Gave them a realistic roadmap.** What could be done in parallel, what had to be sequential, and what they could stop worrying about.

That single call replaced three months of confusion with a plan. They signed on the same week.

## How We Made It Happen

### Step 1: Compliance Audit and SKU Strategy

We started with a full audit: formulas, ingredient declarations, production records, and packaging. The SKU strategy saved them real money. They stopped spending on products that would never clear Chinese customs and focused budget on the two that would.

### Step 2: GACC Registration of the US Plant

This was the gate. We prepared the facility's technical dossier, translated and structured it to GACC's requirements, and guided the plant through CIFER submission. Between document preparation and the authority's review, the registration was approved in early 2024.

### Step 3: MOA Product Registration

In parallel, we prepared the MOA registration for both qualified SKUs: coordinating lab testing in accredited Chinese laboratories, compiling safety and nutrition evidence, and managing the application. This is the step most first-time exporters underestimate. It is product-level, not just plant-level.

### Step 4: Label and Packaging Compliance

We had the Chinese labels designed and reviewed against GB 10648 and MOA labeling rules, including the regulated statements, feeding guidelines, and warning text. Artwork revisions were managed against the US packaging files so the brand look stayed intact.

### Step 5: Import and Channel Readiness

Finally, we helped them select an entry route, vet an import agent and customs broker, and plan the first shipment: which documents needed to travel with the goods, and how to respond if inspection pulled samples.

## The Result

The first container cleared Chinese customs in the summer of 2024 and reached a bonded channel ahead of schedule. Since then, the brand has launched through an online flagship store and a pet hospital distribution network. Just as valuable, they now hold a **reusable compliance file**: every future SKU runs through the same GACC registration, MOA filing, and label pipeline at a fraction of the original effort.

The export manager's summary was simple: *"If we'd tried to do this alone, we'd still be stuck on the first form."*

## What This Case Shows

Three lessons transfer to any food or supplement brand:

1. **Check eligibility before you spend.** SKU-level screening upfront beats a detained shipment later.
2. **GACC registration is the gate, not the finish line.** Product-level approvals, like MOA for pet food, are where most plans stall.
3. **Compliance is a reusable asset.** Done properly the first time, it becomes infrastructure for every product that follows.

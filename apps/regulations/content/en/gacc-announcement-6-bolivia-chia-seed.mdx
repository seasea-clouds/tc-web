On January 10, 2025, the General Administration of Customs (GACC) issued Announcement No. 6, permitting the import of Bolivian chia seeds (Salvia hispanica L.) produced through secondary cleaning, drying, screening, magnetic cleaning and metal detection, meeting phytosanitary requirements, with origin covering all of Bolivia.

Exporting processing enterprises must register with GACC for publication on their website, ensure freedom from four quarantine pests (common ragweed, spiny sandbur, dodder, Johnsongrass), and dry seeds at 65°C for at least 10 hours in sealed equipment. The phytosanitary certificate must include the protocol's specific additional declaration and seal number.

The registered enterprise list is available on the GACC website. All other matters are subject to the original text of this announcement.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the “Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Chia Seeds from Bolivia to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Rural Development and Land of the Plurinational State of Bolivia”, from today, the import of chia seeds from Bolivia meeting the relevant requirements is permitted.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(1) The “Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China”;

(2) The “Entry-Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine Law of the People's Republic of China” and its implementing regulations;

(3) The “Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China” and its implementing regulations;

(4) The “Commodity Inspection Law of the People's Republic of China” and its implementing regulations;

(5) The “Measures for the Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Entry-Exit Grain”;

(6) The “Measures for the Administration of Food Safety of Imported and Exported Goods of the People's Republic of China”;

(7) The “Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Chia Seeds from Bolivia to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Rural Development and Land of the Plurinational State of Bolivia”.

II. Name of the Commodity Permitted for Entry

The chia seeds (English name: Chia Seed) referred to in this announcement are the seeds of Salvia hispanica L. grown and processed in Bolivia, produced through processes such as secondary cleaning, drying, screening, magnetic cleaning, and metal detection.

III. Permitted Origin

The entire territory of Bolivia.

IV. Enterprise Registration

Processing enterprises exporting chia seeds to China must be registered with the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the “Chinese side”). The National Agricultural Sanitation and Food Safety Agency of Bolivia (hereinafter referred to as the “Bolivian side”) shall provide the Chinese side with the relevant enterprise list in advance. After the inspection or review by the Chinese side has been passed, the enterprise will be registered, and the relevant enterprise list will be published on the GACC website.

V. Quarantine Pests

Chia seeds exported to China must not carry the following quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side:

(1) Common ragweed Ambrosia artemisiifolia

(2) Spiny sandbur Cenchrus echinatus

(3) Dodder Cuscuta spp.

(4) Johnsongrass (Sorghum halepense)

VI. Pre-Shipment Requirements

(1) Production Requirements.

(1) The Bolivian side shall adopt internationally recognized survey and monitoring methods to carry out pest survey and monitoring for quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side during the growth and storage periods of Salvia hispanica L., and submit monitoring reports on the occurrence of the above pests at the origin according to the needs of the Chinese side, including monitoring methods and results, and other information required by the Chinese side.

(2) The Bolivian side shall supervise planting enterprises to establish an integrated pest management system (IPM), implement effective field pest control measures, and reduce the occurrence level of pests of concern to the Chinese side. In pest control work, pesticides prohibited by the Chinese side must not be used.

(3) During the production and processing of chia seeds exported to China, cleaning measures such as screening must be taken, and the product must not carry soil, plant residues, impurities, or dangerous toxic and harmful weed seeds.

(4) Chia seeds exported to China must undergo effective drying treatment before export, dried in sealed drying equipment, at a temperature of 65°C or above, heated for at least 10 hours.

(2) Packaging and Transportation Requirements.

(1) Each package must have at least one label indicating the enterprise name and registration number, with markings in Chinese and English clearly stating the product/origin description and “CHIA SEED FROM BOLIVIA TO BE EXPORTED TO THE PEOPLE'S REPUBLIC OF CHINA”.

(2) Containers transporting chia seeds must be cleaned.

(3) Pre-Export Inspection, Quarantine and Certificate Requirements.

(1) Before export, the Bolivian side shall implement inspection and quarantine for chia seeds exported to China, issue a phytosanitary certificate for those meeting the requirements, and state in the additional declaration: “This consignment complies with the requirements described in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Chia Seeds from BOLIVIA to China and is free from quarantine pests concerned to China.” (This consignment complies with the phytosanitary requirements for the export of chia seeds from Bolivia to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side), and also state the enterprise name, registration number, and container number of this batch of goods.

(2) After the goods are loaded into containers, a seal must be applied, and the seal number must be stated in the phytosanitary certificate. The packaging must not be opened or replaced during transportation.

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine

(1) Certificate Verification.

(1) Verify whether it comes from a registered enterprise;

(2) Verify whether the accompanying phytosanitary certificate is genuine and valid.

(2) Goods Inspection and Supervision.

(1) In accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations, rules, etc., and combined with the requirements of this announcement, inspection and quarantine will be carried out on chia seeds; entry will be permitted after the goods pass;

(2) Chia seeds exported to China must undergo inspection and quarantine at the designated supervision site of Chinese Customs.

(3) Handling of Non-Conformities.

(1) Those without a valid phytosanitary certificate will be returned or destroyed;

(2) Those coming from unregistered processing enterprises will be returned or destroyed;

(3) If soil is found, it will be returned or destroyed;

(4) If quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side listed in this announcement are intercepted, the relevant goods will be permitted to enter after passing effective quarantine treatment. If quarantine treatment cannot be implemented, return or destruction will be adopted;

(5) If pests other than those listed in this announcement are intercepted, they will be handled according to the relevant clauses of the “Entry-Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine Law of the People's Republic of China” and its implementing regulations;

(6) If other situations not meeting China's entry inspection and quarantine requirements are found, they will be handled in accordance with the provisions of relevant laws and regulations.

If any of the above violations are found, the Chinese side will notify the Bolivian side and, depending on the severity of the violation, take measures such as suspending the export of the relevant registered enterprise, or even suspending the export of chia seeds from Bolivia to China, until the Bolivian side takes effective improvement measures.

This announcement is hereby issued.

General Administration of Customs

January 10, 2025

## How Long the Money Is Tied Up: a Six-Segment Chain and Four to Six Months

The cash cycle chain that S6 funds settlement lays out has six segments: stocking payment, first leg in transit, zone entry and listing, sales, settlement, outward remittance. You pay the goods money overseas first, the goods travel the first leg into China, sales begin once they are in the zone and listed, the platform settles the goods money to your onshore account by cycle, and finally it is remitted overseas through the outward remittance process. The six segments join end to end; whichever segment runs slow, the capital stays tied up that much longer.

The page lays the six segments out on a six-month timeline: stocking payment sits around the first month, the first leg in transit occupies the second month, zone entry through on-shelf sales spans the third to the fifth month, the settlement inflow starts walking in from the fifth month, and outward remittance closes the chain; the whole bottom band is marked cash tied up, running from the first month all the way to the sixth. The legend has four items: the dark band is the tied-up range, drawn on a four to six month scale; the light blue band is each segment's own usage; the triangles are the compression points; the scale is the number of months the capital stays tied up.

## Which Three Numbers Start the Cash Budget

The cash budget starts from three numbers.

- First stocking amount: your target monthly sales multiplied by two to three months of turnover; the magnitude is set by your own company;
- Cash tied up: a four to six month range, floating with the settlement terms and the first-leg mode;
- Collections rhythm: it follows the platform settlement cycle, and the days are set by platform rules.

With the three numbers in place, the skeleton of the cash flow budget stands up.

How accurate these three numbers are directly decides the size of two things: how much of your capital is tied up, and how much money you are fronting when goods move slowly. The page states the basis plainly: the cycle is a practice range given by platform rules, and the S6 module's output is the reconciliation statement for the first settlement cycle.

## How Settlement Runs, and How to Use the Two Compression Points

Settlement runs in cycle batches, not per single order. One settlement cycle stacks three things: the platform's settlement rhythm, the account terms and the remittance channel. The funds account opens in about six weeks; those six weeks are the practice range produced by bank account opening stacked with the inward and outward payment process, and the opening date directly fixes your first settlement date.

The page marks two compression points. Compression point one: onshore settlement first, so the settlement inflow covers operating expenses before anything else. Compression point two: swap the first leg for air freight, trading freight cost for time on in-transit capital. The two compression points only change the capital rhythm; the rules of settlement itself do not move.

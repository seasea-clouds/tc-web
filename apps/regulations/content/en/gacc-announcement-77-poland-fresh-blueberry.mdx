On June 28, 2023, GACC issued Announcement No. 77, permitting import of Polish fresh blueberries meeting phytosanitary requirements, effective immediately. The scientific name of fresh blueberries is Vaccinium L., originating from Polish blueberry-producing areas. Orchards and packinghouses exporting to China must be approved and registered by GACC.

The announcement establishes nine chapters. Blueberries exported to China must be free from eight quarantine pests of concern, including blueberry end rot and elm oyster shell scale. Orchards shall implement Good Agricultural Practices and Integrated Pest Management, monitoring every two weeks. Packaging must be labeled in English with fruit name, origin, and registration number. Pre-export inspection sampling is at a 2% rate. Additional declarations on phytosanitary certificates must follow the protocol.

The registered list will be published on the GACC website before each annual export season. The list of quarantine pests and inspection measures will be reviewed and adjusted based on epidemic dynamics, while other matters shall be subject to the original text of this announcement.

In accordance with relevant laws and regulations of China and the agreement between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of the Republic of Poland on phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh Polish blueberries to China, imports of Polish fresh blueberries meeting the following requirements are permitted effective immediately.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(i) The Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) The Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its Implementing Regulations;

(iii) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its Implementing Regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) The Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Polish Fresh Blueberries to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of the Republic of Poland.

II. Name of Commodity Permitted for Import

Fresh blueberries (hereinafter referred to as "blueberries"), scientific name Vaccinium L., English name Blueberry.

III. Permitted Origin

Polish blueberry-producing areas.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packinghouses

Orchards and packinghouses exporting to China shall be reviewed and filed with the National Plant Health and Seed Inspection Agency, which represents the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of the Republic of Poland (hereinafter referred to as "the Polish side"), and be approved and registered by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as "the Chinese side"). Registration information includes name, address, and registration number. Before each annual export season, the Polish side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side, which shall be approved after review and published on the official website of GACC.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Blueberry end rot Godronia cassandrae

(2) Blueberry fruit rot Diaporthe spp. (Diaporthe vaccinia, D. eres, D. rudis)

(3) Septoria leaf spot Septoria albopunctata

(4) Elm oyster shell scale Lepidosaphes ulmi

(5) cyclamen mite Phytonemus pallidus

(6) blueberry bud mite of cranberry Acalitus vaccine

(7) Cranberry gall midge Dasineura oxycoccana

(8) Calluna thorn mealybug Peliococcus calluneti

VI. Pre-Export Management

(i) Orchard Management.

(1) Orchards exporting to China shall establish and implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) to ensure traceability of the production process. Maintain orchard sanitary conditions, remove fallen and rotten fruits during harvest, and implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including pest and disease monitoring, chemical or biological control, and agronomic operations.

(2) Orchards exporting to China shall conduct pest monitoring and integrated management for quarantine pests of concern to China during the growing season under the supervision of the Polish side. Phytosanitary measures shall be implemented under the guidance of technical personnel with knowledge of pest prevention and phytosanitary monitoring; such personnel shall receive training from the Polish side or its authorized agencies.

(3) For elm oyster shell scale, primrose plant-eating mite, low-temperature gall mite of cranberry, and cranberry gall midge, orchards exporting to China must monitor from flowering to harvest, once every two weeks, focusing on branches, stems, leaves, and fruits for the presence of the above pests. If any pest is detected, immediate chemical or biological control measures shall be taken to suppress pest occurrence.

(4) For blueberry end rot, blueberry fruit rot, and shell needle spore, orchards shall be regularly cleaned, diseased branches, leaves, or roots removed, and residual ground debris cleared by burning or deep burial. From bud stage to harvest, monitoring shall be conducted every two weeks, focusing on leaves, buds, and stems for signs of the above diseases. If monitoring detects occurrence, immediate chemical or biological treatment shall be applied.

(5) Orchards exporting to China shall retain records of pest monitoring and control, including names, active ingredients, application dates, and concentrations of all chemical agents used during the growing season. These records shall, upon request, be provided by the Polish side to be provided by the Polish side.

(ii) Packinghouse Management.

(1) Processing, packing, storage, and shipping of blueberries exported to China shall be conducted under quarantine supervision of the Polish side.

(2) During packing, blueberries exported to China shall be removal of diseased and deformed fruits, sorted, graded, and compressed air-blown to ensure they are free from insects, mites, rotten fruits, branches, leaves, roots, and soil.

(3) Packed blueberries exported to China requiring storage shall be immediately warehoused and stored separately to avoid re-infection by pests.

(iii) Packaging Requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic, unused, and comply with Chinese phytosanitary requirements.

(2) Each package shall be labeled in English with fruit name, origin (province, city, or county), country, orchard or packinghouse name or registration number. Each package and pallet shall be labeled in Chinese or English: "Exported to the People’s Republic of China" (Exported to the People's Republic of China) Republic of China).

(3) If wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(4) Before loading, containers shall be inspected to ensure good sanitary conditions, and this activity shall be recorded for inspection by the Polish side.

(iv) Pre-Export Inspection.

(1) During the first two years of trade, the Polish side shall sample every lot of blueberries exported to China at a 2% rate. If no phytosanitary issues occur within two years, the sampling rate shall be reduced to 1%.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China are detected, the entire lot shall not be exported to China. The Polish side shall investigate causes and implement corrective measures. Interception records shall be retained and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(v) Phytosanitary Certificate Requirements.

(1) For blueberries that pass inspection, the Polish side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate stating the orchard and packinghouse name or registration number, with the following additional declaration: "This consignment of blueberries complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Polish Fresh Blueberries to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China." (This shipment complies with the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Polish Fresh Blueberries to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) Before trade commences, the Polish side shall provide samples of the phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side for confirmation and filing.

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine, and Handling of Non-conformities

When blueberries arrive at Chinese entry ports, China Customs shall implement inspection and quarantine as follows:

(i) Verification of Certificates and Labels.

(1) Verify whether imported blueberries have obtained the "Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine License."

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Section 6, Subsection (v) of this announcement.

(3) Verify whether package and pallet labels comply with Section 6, Subsection (iii) of this announcement.

(ii) Entry Inspection and Quarantine.

(1) Blueberries exported to China shall enter through ports designated for fruit import by the Chinese side.

(2) In accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations, and rules, imported blueberries shall undergo inspection and quarantine; those passing inspection may be allowed entry.

(iii) Handling of Non-conformities.

(1) If found to originate from unapproved orchards or packinghouses, the lot shall not be allowed entry.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China or newly emerging quarantine pests in Poland are detected, the lot shall be returned, destroyed, or disinfested. The Chinese side shall immediately notify the Polish side and suspend blueberry exports from the affected orchard or packinghouse for the current season. The Polish side shall investigate and supervise relevant enterprises to rectify until Chinese requirements are met again.

VIII. Compliance Review

In the first year of trade, with assistance from the Polish side, the Chinese side shall dispatch experts to conduct on-site or remote video inspections of Polish blueberry-producing areas to ensure management systems meet relevant requirements.

IX. Retrospective Review

Based on the dynamics of blueberry pest outbreaks in Poland and interception records, the Chinese side shall conduct further risk assessment and consult with the Polish side to adjust the list of quarantine pests and related measures. If necessary, the Chinese side may dispatch experts to Poland for a retrospective review.

This announcement is hereby issued.

General Administration of Customs

June 28, 2023

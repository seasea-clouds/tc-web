The upgraded China Peru Free Trade Agreement protocol takes effect on 1 November 2026, and Announcement No. 155 takes effect the same day; the revised rules of origin it publishes are the yardstick for Peruvian goods to claim the agreement tariff rates.

Watch three certificate essentials when filing: issued by the authorised body of the exporting side, completed in English, and valid for one year from issue; without a certificate at declaration, you may file a supplementary declaration with tax security before customs procedures are completed and still claim the rate.

A lot valued at no more than 600 US dollars is exempt from submitting a certificate; certificates can now be self printed, reissued ones must be marked as reissued; storage in a third country must not exceed 3 months, with full transport documents kept.

With approval of the State Council, the Protocol Between the Government of the People's Republic of China and the Government of the Republic of Peru on Upgrading the Free Trade Agreement Between the Two Governments takes formal effect on 1 November 2026. In order to correctly determine the origin of goods imported and exported under the Agreement Between the Government of the People's Republic of China and the Government of the Republic of Peru on Free Trade (hereinafter referred to as the Agreement), the General Administration of Customs has formulated the revised Rules of Origin for Import and Export Goods under the Agreement and hereby publishes them, with effect from 1 November 2026.

Certificates of origin under the Agreement are self printable certificates.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

30 October 2026

Rules of Origin for Import and Export Goods under the Free Trade Agreement between the Government of the People's Republic of China and the Government of the Republic of Peru, as Revised, Administered by the Customs of the People's Republic of China

Article 1　These Rules are formulated in order to correctly determine the origin of goods imported and exported under the Agreement Between the Government of the People's Republic of China and the Government of the Republic of Peru on Free Trade (hereinafter referred to as the Agreement), to promote economic and trade exchanges between China and Peru, in accordance with the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China, the Tariff Law of the People's Republic of China, the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on the Origin of Import and Export Goods, the Agreement and the Protocol on Upgrading the Free Trade Agreement Between the Two Governments.

Article 2　These Rules apply to the administration of origin of goods imported and exported under the Agreement.

Article 3　Goods that meet one of the following conditions and the other provisions of these Rules are originating goods under the Agreement (hereinafter referred to as originating goods) and enjoy originating status under the Agreement (hereinafter referred to as originating status): (1) wholly obtained or produced in China or Peru; (2) produced in China or Peru wholly from originating materials that comply with these Rules; (3) produced in China or Peru from non originating materials, but meeting the tariff classification change, regional value content, manufacturing or processing operation or other requirements specified in the product specific rules of origin under the Agreement (Annex 1). When the product specific rules of origin listed in Annex 1 change, Customs will announce them separately.

Article 4　Goods “wholly obtained or produced in China or Peru” referred to in Article 3(1)(1) of these Rules mean: (1) animals born and raised in China or Peru; (2) goods obtained from animals raised in China or Peru; (3) plants and plant products harvested, picked or gathered in China or Peru; (4) goods obtained by hunting, trapping, fishing or aquaculture conducted in China or Peru; (5) minerals and other naturally occurring substances extracted from the territory, territorial waters, seabed or subsoil beneath the seabed of China or Peru; (6) goods, other than fish, crustaceans and other marine life, obtained or extracted from the waters, seabed or subsoil beyond the territorial waters of China or Peru over which that party is entitled under international law to exploit; (7) fish, crustaceans and other marine life taken from the waters outside the territory of that party by vessels legally flying the flag of China or Peru, in accordance with international law; (8) goods processed on board factory ships legally flying the flag of China or Peru, made wholly from the goods described in item (7) above; (9) waste and scrap generated in the manufacturing process in China or Peru, fit only for the recovery of raw materials; (10) used goods collected in China or Peru, fit only for the recovery of raw materials, where collection does not include goods imported from countries or territories other than China and Peru and subsequently assembled there; (11) goods produced in China or Peru wholly from the goods listed in items (1) through (10) above.

Article 5　The tariff classification change referred to in Article 3(1)(3) of these Rules means that, after non originating materials are manufactured or processed in China or Peru, the tariff heading of the goods under the Import and Export Tariff Schedule of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Tariff Schedule) changes by the specified number of digits.

Article 6　The regional value content referred to in Article 3(1)(3) of these Rules shall be calculated as follows: regional value content = (FOB price of the goods - price of non originating materials) / FOB price of the goods x 100%. The “price of non originating materials” means the import cost of the non originating materials determined under the WTO Valuation Agreement, plus freight and insurance to the port or place of destination, including the price of materials of unknown origin. Where non originating materials are obtained within China or Peru, it shall be the earliest determined actual price paid or payable for the non originating materials there, excluding freight, insurance, packaging and any other charges for moving those materials from the supplier's warehouse to the producer's premises. Where the regional value content is calculated under the first paragraph of this Article, the price of non originating materials does not include the price of non originating materials used in production to produce originating materials.

Article 7　Where originating materials of China or Peru are used in the other party to produce another good, those materials shall be regarded as originating materials of that other party.

Article 8　For goods whose originating status is determined under the tariff classification change standard of the Agreement, where all non originating materials used in production that do not satisfy the tariff classification change standard (including materials of unknown origin), priced as determined under Article 6 of these Rules, do not exceed 10% of the FOB price of the goods, and the goods satisfy all other relevant provisions of these Rules, the goods shall be regarded as originating goods.

Article 9　Goods meeting Article 3(1)(3) of these Rules shall not be granted originating status if, in production, they undergo only one or more of the following operations or treatments: (1) operations to ensure that goods remain in good condition during transport or storage; (2) the breaking up or assembly of consignments; (3) packing, unpacking or repacking for retail sale; (4) slaughter of animals; (5) assembling parts into a complete product, or disassembling products into parts, being simple assembly or disassembly; (6) washing, cleaning, removal of dust, oxide, oil, paint or other coatings; (7) ironing or pressing of textiles; (8) simple painting and polishing operations; (9) husking, simple grinding or simple cutting; (10) filtering, sifting, selecting, classifying, grading or matching, including the composition of sets of goods; (11) placing or printing marks, labels or other distinguishing signs on the product or its packaging; (12) simple mixing of goods of the same or different kinds; (13) bottling, canning, jugging, bagging, crating or boxing. For the purposes of the first paragraph of this Article, “simple” generally describes an operation that requires neither special skills nor machines, instruments or equipment specially produced or assembled for it; “simple mixing” generally means a mix that requires neither special skills nor specially produced or assembled machines, instruments or equipment, and does not include chemical reaction.

Article 10　For sets of goods as defined in General Rule 3 of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, where all components are originating goods the set shall be regarded as originating; where a set consists of originating and non originating goods, the set shall still be regarded as originating if the price of the non originating goods, determined under Article 6 of these Rules, does not exceed 15% of the price of the set.

Article 11　Packing materials and containers used for transport do not affect the determination of originating status. Where originating status is determined under the regional value content standard of the Agreement, the price of retail packing materials and containers classified with the goods shall be counted into the price of originating or non originating materials according to their respective origins. Where originating status is determined under a standard other than the regional value content standard of the Agreement, and the retail packing materials and containers are classified with the goods, the origin of those retail packing materials and containers does not affect the determination of originating status.

Article 12　Where originating status is determined under the regional value content standard of the Agreement, and accessories, spare parts, tools, instructions or information materials are declared together with the goods, are classified with the goods in the Tariff Schedule and are invoiced together, their price shall be counted into the price of originating or non originating materials according to their respective origins. Where originating status is determined under the tariff classification change standard of the Agreement, and such accessories, spare parts, tools, instructions or information materials are declared together with the goods, are classified with the goods in the Tariff Schedule and are invoiced together, their origin does not affect the determination of originating status. The quantity and price of the accessories, spare parts, tools, instructions or information materials described in the first and second paragraphs of this Article shall be within a reasonable range.

Article 13　The following materials and articles used in production that do not themselves form part of the goods do not affect the determination of originating status: (1) fuel, energy, catalysts and solvents; (2) equipment, devices and articles used for testing or inspecting the goods; (3) gloves, eyewear, footwear, clothing, and safety equipment and articles; (4) tools, dies and moulds; (5) spare parts and materials for maintaining equipment and buildings; (6) lubricants, greases, synthetic materials and other materials used in production or for the operation of equipment and maintenance of buildings; (7) any other materials or articles used in production that do not form part of the goods or of any component part, but reasonably show that they took part in production.

Article 14　In determining originating status, interchangeable goods or materials shall be distinguished by one of the following methods: (1) physical separation of goods or materials; (2) an inventory management method recognised by the generally accepted accounting principles of the exporting party and used continuously for at least one fiscal year.

Article 15　Originating goods transported from the exporting party to the importing party retain their originating status if one of the following conditions is met: (1) they do not pass through countries or territories other than China and Peru (hereinafter referred to as other countries or territories); (2) although they pass through other countries or territories, whether or not transport means are changed or temporary storage takes place, all of the following conditions are met: (a) apart from unloading, repacking or handling needed to keep the goods in good condition, the goods undergo no other treatment; (b) the goods remain under customs supervision of those countries or territories throughout; (c) where the goods are temporarily stored in other countries or territories, the stay does not exceed 3 months.

Article 16　A valid certificate of origin under the Agreement (form at Annex 2) shall meet all of the following conditions: (1) the goods listed are originating goods that comply with these Rules; (2) it is issued by an authorised issuing body of the exporting party; (3) it bears a unique certificate number; (4) it covers one or more goods shipped in the same consignment; (5) it states the basis on which the goods enjoy originating status; (6) it carries security features such as the seal of the issuing body and matches the specimen notified by the exporting party to the importing party; (7) it is completed in English.

Article 17　A certificate of origin shall be issued before or at the time of export, and is valid for one year from the date of issue. A certificate of origin may be issued retrospectively after export in any of the following cases: (1) through unintentional error, omission or other reasonable cause the certificate was not issued before or at the time of export, and the exporter provides all necessary commercial documents and the export declaration; (2) the authorised issuing body of China or Peru is satisfied that a certificate of origin was issued, but it was not accepted at import for technical reasons. In the case of item (1) of the second paragraph, the retrospective certificate is valid for one year from the date of issue; in the case of item (2), its validity runs the same as the original certificate. A retrospective certificate shall bear the words “ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY”.

Article 18　Where a certificate of origin is stolen, lost or destroyed, the exporter may apply in writing to the authorised issuing body of the exporting party for a certified true copy. The certified true copy shall bear the words “CERTIFIED TRUE COPY of the original Certificate of Origin number_____dated_____” [certified true copy of the original certificate of origin (number___date___)], and is valid for the same period as the original. Once the certified true copy is lodged with Customs, the original certificate ceases to be valid. Where the original certificate has already been used, the certified true copy is invalid.

Article 19　Imported goods with originating status may claim the agreement tariff rate under the Agreement.

Article 20　An importer or its agent applying for the agreement tariff rate for imported originating goods shall declare under the relevant provisions of Customs and present the following documents: (1) a certificate of origin that complies with these Rules; (2) the commercial invoice of the goods; (3) through transport documents for the goods. Where goods are transported to China through other countries or territories, proof issued by the customs of those countries or territories or other proof accepted by Customs shall be submitted. Where the through transport documents submitted under item (3) of the first paragraph satisfy the documentary requirements for goods transported through a third party under a preferential trade arrangement, the proof under the second paragraph need not be submitted.

Article 21　Unless Customs provides otherwise, for imported goods originating in Peru under the Agreement, where the importer or its agent did not submit a valid certificate of origin at declaration, it shall, before customs procedures are completed, make a supplementary declaration to Customs that the goods have Peruvian originating status, and submit the declaration of originating status for imported goods (form at Annex 3). Where the importer or its agent makes the supplementary declaration under the first paragraph and provides tax security, Customs processes the import accordingly, except where laws or administrative regulations do not permit security. Where security equivalent to the maximum duty payable has already been lodged for early release or similar reasons, it is regarded as meeting the security requirement of this paragraph. Where the importer or its agent applies for the agreement tariff rate after customs procedures are completed, duty already collected is not adjusted.

Article 22　In any of the following cases the importer or its agent may apply to Customs to release the tax security within the security period approved by Customs: (1) the importer or its agent has made the supplementary declaration and submitted a valid certificate of origin under the Agreement; (2) Customs has completed the origin verification procedure and the result is sufficient to establish the originating status of the goods.

Article 23　Where the duty paying value of a single consignment of Peruvian originating goods does not exceed 600 US dollars, the importer or its agent may claim the agreement tariff rate without submitting a certificate of origin. Goods declared for import in split lots to circumvent these Rules do not fall under the preceding paragraph.

Article 24　Where the value of a single consignment of Chinese originating goods does not exceed 600 US dollars or the equivalent in Peruvian currency, or the higher amount set by Peru, the exporter or producer may issue an origin declaration (form at Annex 4) in place of a certificate of origin. An origin declaration is valid for one year from the date of issue.

Article 25　To establish the authenticity and accuracy of a certificate of origin or origin declaration, to verify the originating status of the goods concerned or whether they meet the other requirements of these Rules, Customs may ask the importer or its agent, or the overseas exporter or producer, for supplementary information, or send a verification request to the competent bodies of Peru. During verification, Customs may, at the request of the importer or its agent, release the goods against tax security, except where laws or regulations provide otherwise.

Article 26　Imported goods do not qualify for the agreement tariff rate under the Agreement in any of the following cases: (1) the importer or its agent neither applied for the agreement tariff rate before customs procedures were completed nor made a supplementary declaration as provided by these Rules; (2) the goods do not have Peruvian originating status under the Agreement; (3) the certificate of origin or origin declaration does not comply with these Rules; (4) the goods listed in the certificate of origin or origin declaration do not match the goods actually imported; (5) within 150 days from the verification request, Customs receives no verification response, or the response is insufficient to establish the authenticity of the certificate or the originating status of the goods; (6) the importer or its agent otherwise breaches these Rules.

Article 27　An exporter or its agent may, under the Measures for the Administration of Issuing Certificates of Origin for Export Goods (decree No. 270 of the General Administration of Customs), apply to the issuing body in China for a certificate of origin under the Agreement.

Article 28　When declaring exports, the exporter or its agent shall complete the export goods declaration form of the People's Republic of China in accordance with the declaration rules of Customs.

Article 29　An exporter applying for a certificate of origin under the Agreement shall keep, for 3 years from the date of issue, documentary records sufficient to establish the originating status of the goods. An importer or its agent applying for the agreement tariff rate shall keep, for 3 years from completion of customs procedures, documentary records sufficient to establish the originating status of the goods. The authorised issuing body shall keep, for 3 years from the date of issue, copies of certificates and the related application files.

Article 30　Goods originating in Peru exhibited in other countries or territories and sold to China after exhibition may claim the agreement tariff rate at import if all of the following conditions are met: (1) the goods were shipped to China in the state in which they were exhibited, during the exhibition or immediately after it; (2) since exhibition the goods have been used for display only; (3) the goods remained under customs supervision of the country or territory of exhibition during the exhibition. Sales promotions in shops or commercial premises aimed at selling foreign products do not fall within the exhibitions covered by this Article. In the case of the first paragraph, the importer or its agent applying for the agreement tariff rate under these Rules shall submit the certificate of origin together with documents proving the link to the exhibition, and the certificate shall state the name and place of the exhibition.

Article 31　In these Rules: (1) “material” means any object or substance used in producing another good, including components, ingredients, raw materials, parts or components; (2) “originating goods or materials” means goods or materials that have Chinese or Peruvian originating status under these Rules; (3) “non originating goods or materials” means goods or materials that do not have Chinese or Peruvian originating status under these Rules; (4) “production” means any method of obtaining a good, including planting, raising, extraction, picking, gathering, mining, harvesting, fishing, trapping, hunting, manufacturing, processing or assembly; (5) “producer” means a person engaged in planting, raising, extraction, picking, gathering, mining, harvesting, fishing, trapping, hunting, manufacturing, processing or assembly of goods; (6) “aquaculture” means the farming of aquatic organisms including fish, molluscs, crustaceans, other aquatic invertebrates and aquatic plants, starting from eggs, fry, larvae and roe, with intervention in the rearing or growing process such as orderly stocking, feeding or protection from predators, to raise output; (7) “FOB price” means the free on board price including the freight of the goods to the final port or place of exit; (8) the “WTO Valuation Agreement” means the World Trade Organization Agreement on Implementation of Article VII of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade 1994; (9) “generally accepted accounting principles” means the accounting principles recognised in China or Peru for recording revenue, expenditure, costs, assets and liabilities, disclosure of information and preparation of financial statements; those principles include broad guiding principles of general application and detailed standards, practices and procedures; (10) “interchangeable goods or materials” means goods or materials interchangeable for commercial purposes and substantially identical in nature.

Article 32　These Rules are interpreted by the General Administration of Customs.

Article 33　These Rules take effect on 1 November 2026.

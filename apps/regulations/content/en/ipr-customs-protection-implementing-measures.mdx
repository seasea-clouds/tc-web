The Regulations set the protection framework; these Measures are the operational rulebook of the General Administration of Customs: how to apply for recordal, which documents to prepare, how the security for detaining goods is calculated from the value of the goods.

Let me first remind overseas brands: you cannot file an application with the General Administration of Customs yourself; you must go through an office established in China or a domestic agent. The documents vary by type of right: a trademark registration certificate for trademarks, plus a patent evaluation report for utility model and design patents. No fee is charged at present.

If you do apply to have goods detained, the security comes in three tiers: goods worth less than 20,000 yuan, security equal to the full value; 20,000 yuan to 200,000, 50% of the value with a floor of 20,000 yuan; above 200,000 yuan, 100,000 yuan. Trademark rights holders may arrange a general security, and within the year of approval no separate security per detention application is needed.

(Promulgated by Decree No. 183 of the General Administration of Customs on 3 March 2009; revised on 29 May 2018 by the Decision of the General Administration of Customs on Amending Certain Rules, promulgated as Decree No. 240)

## Chapter I General Provisions

**Article 1** These Measures are formulated in accordance with the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China and other laws and administrative regulations for the purpose of effectively implementing the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Customs Protection of Intellectual Property Rights (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulations").

**Article 2** Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to adopt intellectual property protection measures or handles recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights with the General Administration of Customs, a domestic intellectual property rights holder may file the application directly or entrust a domestic agent with it, while an overseas intellectual property rights holder shall file the application through an office it has established within the territory of China or by entrusting a domestic agent.

Where an intellectual property rights holder entrusts a domestic agent with filing the application in accordance with the preceding paragraph, it shall issue a power of attorney in the prescribed format.

**Article 3** Where an intellectual property rights holder or its agent (each hereinafter referred to as an "intellectual property rights holder") requests customs to detain suspected infringing goods that are about to be imported or exported, it shall file with customs an application for detention of the suspected infringing goods in accordance with the relevant provisions of these Measures.

**Article 4** The consignee and consignor of imported and exported goods or their agents (hereinafter collectively referred to as "consignees and consignors") shall, within a reasonable scope, inform themselves of the intellectual property status of the goods they import or export. Where customs requires a declaration of the intellectual property status of imported or exported goods, the consignee or consignor shall truthfully declare it to customs within the time limit prescribed by customs and submit the relevant supporting documents.

**Article 5** Where the relevant documents or evidence submitted to customs by an intellectual property rights holder or a consignee or consignor involve commercial secrets, the intellectual property rights holder or the consignee or consignor shall state this to customs in writing.

When implementing intellectual property protection, customs shall keep confidential the commercial secrets of the parties concerned, except for information that customs is required to make public in accordance with law.

## Chapter II Recordal of Intellectual Property Rights

**Article 6** An intellectual property rights holder applying to the General Administration of Customs for recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights shall submit a written application to the General Administration of Customs. The application shall include the following:

(1) the name or title, place of registration or nationality, mailing address, name of the contact person, telephone and fax numbers, and email address, etc. of the intellectual property rights holder;

(2) for a registered trademark: the name of the trademark, the class and names of the approved goods, the design of the trademark, the term of validity of the registration, and the assignment, alteration and renewal of the registered trademark, etc.; for a work: the title of the work, the date of completion of its creation, the class of the work, images of the work, and the assignment and alteration of the work, etc.; for a patent right: the name, type and filing date of the patent right, and the assignment and alteration of the patent right, etc.;

(3) the name of the licensee, the licensed goods, the licensing term, etc.;

(4) the name, place of origin, customs office at the place of entry or exit, importers and exporters, main characteristics, price, etc. of the goods in which the intellectual property rights holder lawfully exercises its intellectual property rights;

(5) the manufacturers, importers and exporters, customs office at the place of entry or exit, main characteristics, price, etc. of known goods infringing the intellectual property rights.

The intellectual property rights holder shall submit a separate written application for each item of intellectual property right for which recordal is applied for. Where an intellectual property rights holder applies for recordal of an internationally registered trademark, it shall submit a separate written application for each class of goods covered by the application.

**Article 7** An intellectual property rights holder submitting an application for recordal to the General Administration of Customs shall attach the following documents and evidence:

(1) documents proving the identity of the intellectual property rights holder;

(2) a copy of the Trademark Registration Certificate issued by the administration for industry and commerce under the State Council; where the applicant has, upon approval, had trademark registration matters altered, renewed the trademark registration, or assigned the registered trademark, or applies for recordal of an internationally registered trademark, it shall also submit the relevant certificate of trademark registration issued by the administration for industry and commerce under the State Council; a copy of the certificate of voluntary registration of copyright issued by the copyright registration department and photographs of the work certified by that department; where the applicant has not completed voluntary copyright registration, it shall submit work samples proving that the applicant is the copyright owner and other evidence concerning the copyright; a copy of the patent certificate issued by the patent administration department under the State Council; where more than 1 year has elapsed from the date of announcement of the patent grant, it shall also submit a duplicate of the patent register issued by the patent administration department under the State Council within the 6 months preceding the recordal application; where recordal of a utility model patent or a design patent is applied for, it shall also submit the patent evaluation report made by the patent administration department under the State Council;

(3) where the intellectual property rights holder licenses another person to use the registered trademark or the work or to exploit the patent and a licensing contract has been concluded, a copy of the licensing contract; where no licensing contract has been concluded, a written statement on the licensee, the scope of the license, the licensing period and other matters;

(4) photographs of the goods in which the intellectual property rights holder lawfully exercises its intellectual property rights and of their packaging;

(5) evidence of the import or export of known infringing goods. Where an infringement dispute between the intellectual property rights holder and another person has already been handled by a people's court or the competent intellectual property department, it shall also submit copies of the relevant legal instruments.

The documents and evidence submitted by the intellectual property rights holder to the General Administration of Customs in accordance with the preceding paragraph shall be complete, authentic and valid. Where the relevant documents or evidence are in a foreign language, a Chinese translation shall be attached. Where the General Administration of Customs considers it necessary, it may require the intellectual property rights holder to submit notarized or legalized instruments for the relevant documents or evidence.

**Article 8** An intellectual property rights holder applying to the General Administration of Customs for recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights, or applying anew for recordal after a recordal has lapsed, shall pay the recordal fee. The intellectual property rights holder shall remit the recordal fee by bank transfer to the account designated by the General Administration of Customs. Where the General Administration of Customs collects the recordal fee, it shall issue a receipt. The standards for collecting the recordal fee shall be formulated and published separately by the General Administration of Customs in conjunction with the relevant State departments.

No further recordal fee shall be payable where the intellectual property rights holder applies for renewal or modification of the recordal.

Where the intellectual property rights holder withdraws its recordal application before the General Administration of Customs grants approval, or where its recordal application is rejected, the General Administration of Customs shall refund the recordal fee. Where a recordal already approved by the General Administration of Customs is cancelled or revoked by it, or lapses for other reasons, the recordal fee already paid shall not be refunded.

**Article 9** Recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights shall take effect on the date the General Administration of Customs grants the recordal and shall be valid for 10 years. Where the term of the intellectual property right is less than 10 years from the date the recordal takes effect, the term of validity of the recordal shall be governed by the term of the intellectual property right. For recordals granted by the General Administration of Customs, or recordals approved for renewal by it, before the Regulations took effect, the term of validity shall still be calculated according to the original term.

**Article 10** Within the 6 months before the expiry of the term of validity of the recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights, the intellectual property rights holder may submit a written application to the General Administration of Customs for renewal of the recordal, with the relevant documents attached. The General Administration of Customs shall, within 10 working days from the date of receipt of all renewal application documents, decide whether to grant the renewal and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing; where the renewal is not granted, it shall state the reasons.

The renewed recordal shall be valid for 10 years, counted from the day following the expiry of the preceding recordal. Where the term of the intellectual property right is less than 10 years from the day following the expiry of the preceding recordal, the term of validity of the renewed recordal shall be governed by the term of the intellectual property right.

**Article 11** Where, after a recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights has been approved by the General Administration of Customs, the contents of the written application submitted to customs in accordance with Article 6 of these Measures change, the intellectual property rights holder shall, within 30 working days from the date of the change, apply to the General Administration of Customs for modification of the recordal, with the relevant documents attached.

**Article 12** Where an intellectual property right ceases to be protected by laws or administrative regulations before the expiry of the term of validity of the recordal, or where the recorded intellectual property right is assigned, the former intellectual property rights holder shall, within 30 working days from the date on which the recorded intellectual property right ceases to be protected by laws or administrative regulations or on which the assignment takes effect, apply to the General Administration of Customs for cancellation of the recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights, with the relevant documents attached. Where the intellectual property rights holder relinquishes the recordal during its term of validity, it may apply to the General Administration of Customs for cancellation of the recordal.

Where an application for modification or cancellation of the recordal is not filed with the General Administration of Customs in accordance with Article 11 of these Measures and the preceding paragraph, and this seriously affects the lawful import or export of others, the General Administration of Customs may cancel the recordal of the intellectual property right concerned on its own initiative or upon the application of the interested party concerned.

Where the General Administration of Customs cancels a recordal, it shall notify the intellectual property rights holder concerned in writing, and the recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights shall lapse as of the date of cancellation by the General Administration of Customs.

**Article 13** Where the General Administration of Customs revokes a recordal of customs protection of intellectual property rights in accordance with Article 9 of the Regulations, it shall notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

Where the General Administration of Customs has revoked a recordal, if the intellectual property rights holder applies again for recordal of the intellectual property right of the revoked recordal within 1 year from the date of revocation, the General Administration of Customs may refuse to accept the application.

## Chapter III Detention upon Application

**Article 14** Where an intellectual property rights holder discovers that suspected infringing goods are about to be imported or exported and requests customs to detain them, it shall submit a written application to the customs office at the place of entry or exit of the goods in accordance with Article 13 of the Regulations. Where the intellectual property right concerned is not recorded with the General Administration of Customs, the intellectual property rights holder shall also attach the documents and evidence prescribed in items (1) and (2), paragraph 1, Article 7 of these Measures.

An intellectual property rights holder requesting customs to detain suspected infringing goods shall also submit to customs evidence sufficient to prove that the infringement obviously exists. The evidence submitted by the intellectual property rights holder shall be capable of proving the following facts:

(1) that the goods whose detention is requested from customs are about to be imported or exported;

(2) that a trademark sign infringing its exclusive right to use a trademark or a work has been used on the goods, or its patent has been exploited, without permission.

**Article 15** An intellectual property rights holder requesting customs to detain suspected infringing goods shall provide customs, within the time limit prescribed by customs, with a security equal to the value of the goods.

**Article 16** Where the application filed by an intellectual property rights holder does not meet the requirements of Article 14 of these Measures, or where it fails to provide a security in accordance with Article 15 of these Measures, customs shall reject the application and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

**Article 17** Where customs detains suspected infringing goods, it shall notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing of the name, quantity and value of the goods, the name of the consignee or consignor, the declared date of import or export, the date of detention by customs and other circumstances.

With the consent of customs, the intellectual property rights holder may inspect the goods detained by customs.

**Article 18** Where, within 20 working days from the date of detention of the suspected infringing goods, customs receives a written notice from a people's court requesting assistance in seizing the goods concerned, it shall provide assistance; where it has not received a notice of assistance in seizure from the people's court, or where the intellectual property rights holder requests customs to release the goods concerned, customs shall release the goods.

**Article 19** Where customs detains suspected infringing goods, it shall serve the customs detention receipt for the detained suspected infringing goods on the consignee or consignor.

With the consent of customs, the consignee or consignor may inspect the goods detained by customs.

**Article 20** Where a consignee or consignor requests, in accordance with Article 19 of the Regulations, the release of its goods suspected of infringing a patent right that have been detained by customs, it shall file a written application with customs and provide a cash security equal to the value of the goods.

Where a consignee or consignor requests customs to release goods suspected of infringing a patent right and the requirements of the preceding paragraph are met, customs shall release the goods and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

Where the intellectual property rights holder brings a lawsuit before a people's court over the patent infringement dispute concerned, it shall submit to customs, within 30 working days from the date of service of the written notice of customs prescribed in the preceding paragraph, a copy of the notice of case acceptance issued by the people's court.

## Chapter IV Investigation and Handling upon Own Initiative

**Article 21** Where, in the course of supervision and control of imported and exported goods, customs discovers that the goods involve an intellectual property right recorded with the General Administration of Customs and that the use of the intellectual property right by the importer or exporter or the manufacturer has not been recorded with the General Administration of Customs, it may require the consignee or consignor to declare the intellectual property status of the goods and submit the relevant supporting documents within the prescribed time limit.

Where the consignee or consignor fails to declare the intellectual property status of the goods or submit the relevant supporting documents in accordance with the preceding paragraph, or where customs has reason to believe that the goods are suspected of infringing an intellectual property right recorded with the General Administration of Customs, customs shall suspend the release of the goods and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing.

**Article 22** The intellectual property rights holder shall, within 3 working days from the date of service of the written notice of customs prescribed in Article 21 of these Measures, reply in accordance with the following provisions:

(1) where it considers that the goods concerned infringe an intellectual property right recorded with the General Administration of Customs and requests customs to detain them, it shall file a written application with customs for detention of the suspected infringing goods and provide a security in accordance with Article 23 or Article 24 of these Measures;

(2) where it considers that the goods concerned do not infringe an intellectual property right recorded with the General Administration of Customs, or where it does not request customs to detain the suspected infringing goods, it shall state the reasons to customs in writing.

With the consent of customs, the intellectual property rights holder may inspect the goods concerned.

**Article 23** Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to detain suspected infringing goods in accordance with item (1), paragraph 1, Article 22 of these Measures, it shall provide customs with a security in accordance with the following provisions:

(1) where the value of the goods is less than 20,000 yuan, a security equal to the value of the goods shall be provided;

(2) where the value of the goods is 20,000 yuan to 200,000, a security equal to 50% of the value of the goods shall be provided, but the amount of the security shall not be less than 20,000 yuan;

(3) where the value of the goods exceeds 200,000 yuan, a security of 100,000 yuan shall be provided.

Where an intellectual property rights holder requests customs to detain goods suspected of infringing the exclusive right to use a trademark in accordance with item (1), paragraph 1, Article 22 of these Measures, it may provide a general security to the General Administration of Customs in accordance with Article 24 of these Measures.

**Article 24** An intellectual property rights holder holding the exclusive right to use a trademark recorded with the General Administration of Customs may, upon approval by the General Administration of Customs, submit to it a letter of security issued by a bank or a non-bank financial institution to provide a general security for its applications to customs for customs protection measures for the exclusive right to use a trademark.

The amount of the general security shall be equal to the total of the costs of storage, custody and disposal incurred after the intellectual property rights holder applied to customs in the preceding year for detention of suspected infringing goods; where the intellectual property rights holder did not apply to customs in the preceding year for detention of suspected infringing goods, or where the costs of storage, custody and disposal were less than 200,000 yuan, the amount of the general security shall be 200,000 yuan.

From the date on which the General Administration of Customs approves its use of the general security until 31 December of the same year, where the intellectual property rights holder requests customs, in accordance with Article 16 of the Regulations, to detain imported or exported goods suspected of infringing the exclusive right to use a trademark that it has already recorded with the General Administration of Customs, it need not provide a further security, except where the intellectual property rights holder has failed to pay the relevant costs in accordance with Article 25 of the Regulations or to bear liability for compensation in accordance with Article 29 of the Regulations and the General Administration of Customs has issued a notice to the guarantor to perform the security obligations.

**Article 25** Where an intellectual property rights holder files an application in accordance with item (1), paragraph 1, Article 22 of these Measures and provides a security in accordance with Article 23 or Article 24 of these Measures, customs shall detain the suspected infringing goods and notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing; where the intellectual property rights holder does not file an application or does not provide a security, customs shall release the goods.

**Article 26** Where customs detains suspected infringing goods, it shall serve the customs detention receipt for the detained suspected infringing goods on the consignee or consignor.

With the consent of customs, the consignee or consignor may inspect the goods detained by customs.

**Article 27** After detaining suspected infringing goods, customs shall investigate the suspected infringing goods and the other relevant circumstances in accordance with law. The consignee or consignor and the intellectual property rights holder shall cooperate in the customs investigation and truthfully provide the relevant information and evidence.

In investigating the suspected infringing goods, customs may request the competent intellectual property department concerned to provide advisory opinions.

Where the intellectual property rights holder and the consignee or consignor have reached an agreement on the suspected infringing goods detained by customs, and they file a written application with customs with the relevant agreement attached, requesting customs to lift the detention of the suspected infringing goods, customs may terminate the investigation unless it considers that a crime is suspected.

**Article 28** Where, after investigating the detained suspected infringing goods, customs cannot determine whether the goods infringe the intellectual property right concerned, it shall, within 30 working days from the date of detention of the suspected infringing goods, notify the intellectual property rights holder and the consignee or consignor in writing.

Where customs cannot determine whether the goods infringe the patent right concerned, the consignee or consignor may, after providing customs with a security equal to the value of the goods, request customs to release the goods. Where customs agrees to release the goods, the matter shall be handled in accordance with paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 20 of these Measures.

**Article 29** Where customs cannot determine whether the goods concerned infringe its intellectual property right, the intellectual property rights holder may, in accordance with Article 23 of the Regulations, apply to a people's court for measures ordering the cessation of the infringing act or for property preservation.

Where, within 50 working days from the date of detention of the suspected infringing goods, customs receives a written notice from a people's court requesting assistance in seizing the goods concerned, it shall provide assistance; where it has not received a notice of assistance in seizure from the people's court, or where the intellectual property rights holder requests customs to release the goods concerned, customs shall release the goods.

**Article 30** Where customs makes a decision to confiscate infringing goods, it shall notify the intellectual property rights holder in writing of the following known circumstances:

(1) the name and quantity of the infringing goods;

(2) the name of the consignee or consignor;

(3) the declared date of import or export of the infringing goods, the date of detention by customs and the effective date of the penalty decision;

(4) the place of departure and the place of destination of the infringing goods;

(5) other circumstances relating to the infringing goods that customs can provide.

Where a people's court or the competent intellectual property department, in handling an infringement dispute between the parties concerned, requires the assistance of customs to obtain evidence relating to the imported or exported goods, customs shall provide assistance.

**Article 31** Where customs discovers articles carried or mailed by individuals into or out of the territory that are suspected of infringing the intellectual property rights provided in Article 2 of the Regulations and exceed the reasonable quantity for personal use, it shall detain them, except where the passenger or the sender or recipient declares to customs that they are relinquished and customs consents.

The intellectual property rights holder shall assist customs in investigating the infringing articles. Where an incoming or outgoing passenger or the sender or recipient of incoming or outgoing mail considers that the articles detained by customs do not infringe the intellectual property right concerned or are for personal use, they may state the relevant circumstances to customs in writing and provide the relevant evidence.

**Article 32** Where imported or exported goods or incoming or outgoing articles are determined, after investigation by customs, to infringe intellectual property rights and should be confiscated by customs in accordance with Article 27, paragraph 1, and Article 28 of the Regulations, but the party concerned cannot be identified, they may be collected by customs after 3 months have elapsed from the date of issuance of the relevant public notice by customs.

Where an import or export infringement act is suspected of constituting a crime, customs shall transfer the case to the public security organ in accordance with law.

## Chapter V Disposal of Goods and Costs

**Article 33** Customs shall dispose of confiscated infringing goods in accordance with the following provisions:

(1) where the goods concerned can be directly used for social welfare undertakings, or the intellectual property rights holder is willing to purchase them, the goods shall be transferred to the relevant public welfare institutions for use in social welfare undertakings or transferred to the intellectual property rights holder for consideration;

(2) where the goods concerned cannot be disposed of in accordance with item (1) and the infringing characteristics can be eliminated, they shall be auctioned in accordance with law after the infringing characteristics have been eliminated. The proceeds of the auction of the goods shall be turned over to the State Treasury;

(3) where the goods concerned cannot be disposed of in accordance with items (1) and (2), they shall be destroyed.

Before auctioning infringing goods, customs shall seek the opinions of the intellectual property rights holder concerned. Where customs destroys infringing goods, the intellectual property rights holder shall provide the necessary assistance. Where the relevant public welfare institutions use the infringing goods confiscated by customs for social welfare undertakings, or the intellectual property rights holder accepts the entrustment by customs to destroy infringing goods, customs shall exercise the necessary supervision.

**Article 34** Where customs assists a people's court in seizing suspected infringing goods or releases detained goods, the intellectual property rights holder shall pay the costs of storage, custody and disposal of the goods during the period of detention by customs.

Where customs confiscates infringing goods, the intellectual property rights holder shall pay the costs of storage, custody and disposal according to the actual storage time of the goods after detention by customs; however, where customs cannot complete the disposal of the goods within 3 months from the date of service of the decision to confiscate the infringing goods on the consignee or consignor, and this is not caused by the consignee or consignor applying for administrative reconsideration, bringing an administrative lawsuit, or by other special reasons relating to the disposal of the goods, the intellectual property rights holder need not pay the relevant costs arising after the 3 months.

Where customs auctions infringing goods in accordance with item (2), paragraph 1, Article 33 of these Measures, the expenditure of the auction costs shall be handled in accordance with the relevant provisions.

**Article 35** Where the intellectual property rights holder fails to pay the relevant costs in accordance with Article 34 of these Measures, customs may deduct them from the cash security provided by the intellectual property rights holder or require the guarantor to perform the security obligations.

Where customs confiscates infringing goods, it shall, after the disposal of the goods has been completed and the relevant costs settled, refund the cash security to the intellectual property rights holder or release the guarantor from the security obligations.

Where customs assists a people's court in seizing suspected infringing goods or releases the detained goods in accordance with items (1), (2) and (4) of Article 24 of the Regulations, the consignee or consignor may apply to the people's court for property preservation in respect of the security provided by the intellectual property rights holder. Where, within 20 working days from the date on which customs assisted the people's court in seizing the suspected infringing goods or released the goods, customs has not received a notice of assistance in enforcement from the people's court for the adoption of property preservation measures in respect of the security provided by the intellectual property rights holder, customs shall refund the cash security to the intellectual property rights holder or release the guarantor from the security obligations; where customs has received such a notice of assistance in enforcement from the people's court, it shall assist in the enforcement.

**Article 36** After customs releases detained goods suspected of infringing a patent right in accordance with Article 19 of the Regulations, where the intellectual property rights holder submits to customs, in accordance with paragraph 3 of Article 20 of these Measures, a copy of the notice of case acceptance issued by the people's court, customs shall dispose of the cash security provided by the consignee or consignor according to the judgment of the people's court; where the intellectual property rights holder does not submit a copy of the notice of case acceptance issued by the people's court, customs shall refund the cash security provided by the consignee or consignor. With respect to the security provided by the intellectual property rights holder to customs, the consignee or consignor may apply to the people's court for property preservation; where customs has not received a notice of assistance in enforcement from the people's court for the adoption of property preservation measures in respect of the security provided by the intellectual property rights holder, customs shall, after the 20 working days following the date of disposal of the cash security provided by the consignee or consignor, refund the cash security to the intellectual property rights holder or release the guarantor from the security obligations; where customs has received such a notice of assistance in enforcement from the people's court, it shall assist in the enforcement.

## Chapter VI Supplementary Provisions

**Article 37** Customs shall implement protection of Olympic symbols and World Expo symbols with reference to these Measures.

**Article 38** For the purposes of these Measures, "security" means a cash security or a letter of security issued by a bank or a non-bank financial institution.

**Article 39** For the purposes of these Measures, the value of goods shall be examined and determined by customs on the basis of the transaction value of the goods. Where the transaction value cannot be determined, the value of the goods shall be estimated by customs in accordance with law.

**Article 40** The written notices of customs prescribed in Articles 17, 21 and 28 of these Measures may be served by personal delivery, mail, fax or other means.

**Article 41** The time limits prescribed in paragraph 3 of Article 20 and paragraph 1 of Article 22 of these Measures shall be calculated from the day following the date of service of the written notice of customs. The expiry of the time limits shall be determined in accordance with the following provisions:

(1) where the intellectual property rights holder submits documents to customs or provides a security through the post office or a bank, the time limit shall expire at 24:00 on the expiry date;

(2) where the intellectual property rights holder submits documents to customs or provides a security in person, the time limit shall expire at the end of the normal working hours of customs on the expiry date.

**Article 42** Where an intellectual property rights holder or a consignee or consignor submits to customs copies of the relevant documents in accordance with these Measures, it shall check the copies against the originals of the documents. After the copies are verified, it shall annotate the copy with the words "checked against the original" and confirm it with a signature and seal.

**Article 43** These Measures shall come into force on 1 July 2009. The Measures of the Customs of the People's Republic of China for Implementing the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Customs Protection of Intellectual Property Rights, promulgated by Decree No. 114 of the General Administration of Customs on 25 May 2004, shall be repealed simultaneously.

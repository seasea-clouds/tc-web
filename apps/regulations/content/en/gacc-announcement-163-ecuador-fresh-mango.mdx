Fresh mangoes from Ecuador are approved for export to China. Announcement No. 163 of 2025 requires that orchards, packing houses and heat-treatment facilities be verified by the Ecuadorian side and registered by the Chinese side; registration includes name, address and identification code for traceability, and the list is published on the GACC website.

When exporting mangoes, verify the orchard and packing house registration numbers and heat-treatment records; monitor fruit flies against the daily trap threshold; sort, wash and dip fruit in fungicide before packing; complete hot water treatment at registered facilities before export; the certificate carries protocol-compliance and pest-freedom declarations, and cases are marked with origin, orchard, packing house and the China destination marking.

On arrival, Chinese Customs checks permit, certificate, seals and marks and carries out inspection and quarantine; consignments from unapproved premises, failing quarantine, carrying live quarantine pests or soil, or not meeting food safety standards receive quarantine treatment, return, destruction or entry denial respectively.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Chinese side) and the Agencia de Regulación y Control Fito y Zoosanitario of the Republic of Ecuador (hereinafter referred to as the Ecuadorian side) concerning the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh mangoes from Ecuador to China, fresh mangoes from Ecuador meeting the following relevant requirements are permitted to be imported from the date of this announcement.

Basis for inspection and quarantine

(i) the Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) the Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Agencia de Regulación y Control Fito y Zoosanitario of the Republic of Ecuador on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Mangoes from Ecuador to China.

Name of commodities permitted for entry

Fresh mangoes (hereinafter referred to as mangoes), scientific name Mangifera indica, English name Mango.

Permitted places of origin

Mango-producing areas of Ecuador.

Registration

Orchards, packing houses and heat-treatment facilities for mangoes exported to China must be verified by the Ecuadorian side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information must include the name, address and identification code, so as to enable accurate traceability when the exported consignment does not comply with the relevant provisions of this announcement. Before trade begins, the Ecuadorian side must provide the Chinese side with the registered list; after review and approval by the Chinese side, the list is published on the website of the General Administration of Customs.

List of quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side

(1) Spiraling whitefly Aleurodicus dispersus

(2) Woolly whitefly Aleurothrixus floccosus

(3) South American fruit fly Anastrepha fraterculus

(4) West Indian fruit fly A. obliqua

(5) Serpentine fruit fly A. serpentina

(6) Striped fruit fly A. striata

(7) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(8) Wax scale Ceroplastes spp.

(9) Cacao flower gall fungus Nectria rigidiuscula

(10) Mealybug Pseudococcus spp.

(11) Spiny shield scale Selenaspidus articulatus

Pre-export management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Mango orchards for export to China must, under the supervision of the Ecuadorian side, establish a sound quality management system and traceability system, implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), maintain sanitary conditions in the orchard, such as the absence of pollution sources around affecting fruit production and the timely removal of fallen fruit and rotten fruit; and apply integrated pest management (IPM), including pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control, and agronomic control measures. Fallen fruit must not be mixed with marketable fruit at harvest.

(2) Under the supervision of the Ecuadorian side, mango orchards for export to China must carry out pest monitoring and integrated management targeting the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side. Pest monitoring and control must be carried out under the guidance of professional technicians, who must have received training from the Ecuadorian side or its authorized body.

(3) All mango orchards for export to China must keep records of pest monitoring and control and provide them to the Chinese side upon request. The control records must include detailed information such as the names of all chemical agents used during the growing season, active ingredients, application dates and application concentrations.

(4) With regard to Anastrepha fraterculus, A. obliqua, A. serpentina, A. striata and Ceratitis capitata, the Ecuadorian side must carry out fruit fly monitoring every year during the mango growing, harvest and processing seasons, in accordance with international standards for phytosanitary measures, in the areas where the mango orchards and packing houses for export to China are located. If the average daily capture per trap of the above fruit flies combined exceeds 0.14, mangoes from the relevant orchard may not be exported to China in that production season.

(ii) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packing, storage and shipment of mangoes for export to China must be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Ecuadorian side or its authorized officials.

(2) Before packing, mangoes for export to China must go through sorting, washing and fungicide dipping to remove pest- or disease-affected fruit and foreign matter, so as to ensure that the mangoes carry no quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, nor insects, mites, diseased fruit, molluscs, plant residues or soil.

(3) Mangoes that have undergone hot water treatment and been packed must be stored in clean, sanitary, insect-proof cold storage rooms to avoid reinfestation by pests.

(4) During the production and export season of mangoes for export to China, the Ecuadorian side must inspect the treatment, processing, packing and storage areas of the packing house daily for pests. If live pests are found, the corresponding area must immediately receive the appropriate quarantine treatment; if live adult fruit flies are found during packing, none of the mangoes placed in the packing area during that period may be exported to China.

(iii) Packing requirements.

(1) Packing materials must be clean, sanitary and unused, and comply with the relevant phytosanitary requirements of China. If wooden packaging is used, it must meet the requirements of International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each packing case must be marked in English with information such as the mango variety, origin, orchard, packing house and hot water treatment facility. Each packing case must bear the marking “Exported to the People’s Republic of China” in Chinese or English.

(3) Quarantine-passed mangoes for export to China must be transported in intact and clean containers; the Ecuadorian side affixes seals to the corresponding containers. When the consignment arrives at the entry port of China, both the container and the Ecuadorian seal must be intact and undamaged.

(iv) Quarantine treatment requirements.

(1) Before export and under the supervision of the Ecuadorian side, mangoes for export to China must undergo hot water treatment at treatment facilities registered by the Ecuadorian side and recognized by the Chinese side, with the following technical parameters:

(1) The water temperature for hot water treatment of mangoes for export to China is 46.1°C or above; the treatment time is counted after the water temperature has remained at 46.1°C for 5 minutes. The fruit must be immersed at least 10.2 centimeters below the water surface.

(2) The treatment duration depends on the weight of the fruit treated: for fruit weighing less than 500 grams, the treatment lasts no less than 75 minutes; for fruit weighing between 500 grams and 650 grams, no less than 90 minutes; for fruit weighing more than 650 grams, no less than 110 minutes.

(3) If the fruit requires water cooling after hot water treatment, the hot water treatment time must be extended accordingly by 10 minutes, and the mangoes must be held at room temperature for 30 minutes; the cooling water temperature must not be lower than 21.1°C.

(2) Hot water treatment is a mandatory quarantine treatment measure and must comply with the hot water treatment requirements for mangoes exported to China (see Annex). Each year, before treating mangoes for export to China, the Ecuadorian side must test the hot water treatment facilities to be used and confirm that they operate normally.

(3) After heat treatment, quarantine officials of the Ecuadorian side must cut open fruit for inspection, with a cutting-open rate of 1%. If live fruit fly larvae are found, that consignment of mangoes may not be exported to China.

(v) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The Ecuadorian side must carry out pre-export quarantine on the mangoes for export to China and the containers loading the mangoes, to ensure that they carry no quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, plant residues or soil are found, the whole consignment may not be exported to China.

(vi) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For consignments that have passed quarantine, the Ecuadorian side must issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), stating the heat treatment temperature, duration, date and container seal number for that consignment of mangoes, and include the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Mango Fruits from Ecuador to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (This consignment meets the requirements of the phytosanitary protocol for the export of fresh mangoes from Ecuador to China and carries no quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side.)

(2) The issued phytosanitary certificate must be accompanied by a copy of the hot water treatment temperature record for that consignment of mangoes, stamped and confirmed by the Ecuadorian side, together with the probe calibration report.

(3) Before the program starts, the Ecuadorian side must provide the Chinese side with a sample of the phytosanitary certificate for confirmation and filing.

Entry inspection, quarantine and handling of non-conformities

When mangoes for export to China arrive at the entry port of China, Chinese Customs carries out inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Verification of relevant certificates and marks.

(1) Verify whether the imported fruit holds the Quarantine Permit for Entry of Animals and Plants.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Article 6(vi) of this announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on the packing cases or wooden packaging comply with the provisions of Article 6(iii) of this announcement.

(4) Verify the hot water treatment temperature record and probe calibration report signed by the Ecuadorian side.

(5) Verify whether the container seal matches that stated in the phytosanitary certificate.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Mangoes for export to China must enter through the ports and airports authorized by the Chinese side for fruit imports.

(2) In accordance with the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules, inspection and quarantine are carried out on the imported mangoes; those passing inspection and quarantine are allowed to enter.

(iii) Handling of non-conformities.

(1) If the consignment is found to come from an unapproved orchard, packing house or heat-treatment facility, it is not allowed to enter.

(2) If the container is found not to meet the phytosanitary requirements of China, the container receives quarantine treatment; if that consignment of mangoes is found not to comply with the relevant provisions of this announcement, its entry is not permitted.

(3) If live specimens of the quarantine pests listed in Article 5 of this announcement are found, the consignment is returned or destroyed; if live fruit fly larvae are intercepted, the Chinese side will notify the Ecuadorian side within 15 days and suspend the export of Ecuadorian mangoes to China until the Ecuadorian side has identified the cause and taken effective measures and the Chinese side considers the risk to be effectively controlled.

(4) If non-compliance with China's food safety laws, regulations, national standards and requirements is found, the consignment is returned or destroyed.

(5) If the above non-conformities are found, the Chinese side will immediately notify the Ecuadorian side and, depending on the circumstances, suspend the import of mangoes from the relevant orchards and packing houses for the current export season. The Ecuadorian side must identify the cause of non-compliance and take corrective measures to prevent recurrence. The Chinese side will decide whether to lift the suspension based on the Ecuadorian side's rectification results.

This announcement is hereby issued.

Annex

Hot Water Treatment Requirements for Mangoes Exported to China

Heat treatment site requirements

(1) Heat treatment facilities must be located inside a fruit packing house registered by the Ecuadorian side and recognized by the Chinese side.

(2) The packing house and surrounding areas must be in good sanitary condition, with a relatively independent receiving and quarantine area, a hot water treatment area, and an insect-proof packing area and cold storage area; the latter two areas must at all times be kept clean, sanitary and free of insects.

(3) During the export season of mangoes for export to China, the treatment and processing, post-treatment storage and packing areas of the packing house must be effectively enclosed, that is, not open to the outside.

(4) The main entrances to the packing and storage areas must be a double-door system with an air curtain on the outer side; the inner door may be an insect-proof mesh screen of 30 meshes per inch or more, or a vertical transparent plastic strip curtain.

(5) Windows, vents and other potentially exposed places in the insect-free areas of the packing house must be fitted with mesh of 30 meshes per inch or more.

Heat treatment facility requirements

(1) Hot water treatment facilities handling mangoes for export to China must be registered by the Ecuadorian side and, before the export season, included in the list licensed for filing with the Chinese side.

(2) Heat treatment facilities must have devices and functions for water circulation, water temperature heating, automatic temperature regulation and thermostatic control, and accurate display and recording of water temperature and probe temperature, and must operate normally and pass testing before formal use in treatment.

(3) The heat source of the facility is automatically controlled, ensuring that the water temperature remains at 48°C or above throughout treatment and that the whole process proceeds continuously and stably.

(4) The configured temperature recorder must display, record and print the water temperature, probe temperature and the start and end times of treatment in real time, accurately and tamper-proof, and must also have a backup power supply.

(5) Each hot water treatment tank must hold no fewer than 8 calibrated water temperature probes located at different positions; the mango fruit treated must be immersed at least 10.2 centimeters below the water surface.

(6) The temperature recorder must automatically and unalterably record the temperature of all probes at least once every 2 minutes, and the recorded readings must meet the probe accuracy requirements. If the treatment facility is a continuous system, the temperature recorder must be able to run continuously for 12 hours.

(7) The chart offset scale of the temperature record must not be lower than 5 mm/°C, and the printing speed no less than 30.5 centimeters per hour; the printed temperature record must correspond to the time and temperature recorded for each probe.

Temperature probe requirements

(1) The accuracy of the temperature probes used in treatment must reach ±0.15°C, and the calibration error must not exceed ±0.4°C.

(2) During mango treatment, all probes must be calibrated daily, before formal treatment begins, by the Ecuadorian side according to the method recognized by the Chinese side.

(3) After probe calibration is completed, the Ecuadorian side issues the probe calibration report.

(4) When temperature records show an abnormality or the temperature recorder is replaced, the temperature probes must be recalibrated.

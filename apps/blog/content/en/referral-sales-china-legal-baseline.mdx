The first gate for referral sales into China is a restructured order flow, and the direct selling license does not even rank. Goods under customs supervision mode 1210 enter as articles for personal use, cleared through the cross-border e-commerce track of their own, so the license is out of reach in the short term and out of scope as well. What decides whether you can recruit your first promoters is how the commission is calculated, who signs the contract, and which account the money moves through, and whether those three points have been rebuilt around the red lines.

The basis sits in public texts. State Council Decree No. 443, the Direct Selling Regulations, defines what direct selling is, how licenses are issued and how pay is capped; State Council Decree No. 444, the Regulations on the Prohibition of Pyramid Schemes, lists three categories of pyramid-scheme conduct in Article 7; and the 2013 joint opinion of the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate and the Ministry of Public Security (Gong Tong Zi [2013] No. 37) confirms in Article 5 that team-based pay tied only to sales performance is not treated as that crime. This article puts the three license facts, the three changes restructuring requires, and the verdict order for the three red lines side by side, so you can check your referral plan line by line.

## Why the license does not make the shortlist

Three facts, and the question closes.

First, goods under mode 1210 are supervised as articles for personal use. The Notice Shang Cai Fa [2018] No. 486 states that cross-border e-commerce retail import goods are supervised as articles for personal use and are exempt from the first-import permit, registration or filing requirements. Direct selling, as the Regulations define it, means a licensed company sending its own salespeople to sell face to face outside a fixed place of business; goods under 1210 are ordered by the consumer and shipped from a bonded warehouse, so the shape of the transaction does not match the definition and the license does not apply. Your identity in this market is a registered cross-border e-commerce enterprise, which is a statutory track of its own.

![The compliance starting point for referral sales in China: the license is not the prerequisite, restructuring the order flow is](/images/blog/referral-sales-china-legal-baseline/referral-license-not-required-restructuring.webp)

Second, the license itself is out of reach in the short term. Article 7 of the Regulations lists four conditions: a clean record for five consecutive years before applying, at least three years of direct selling experience outside China for foreign investors, paid-in registered capital of at least 80,000,000 yuan (about 11,100,000 US dollars at 7.2 yuan per dollar, for reference), a deposit paid in full at a designated bank, and information reporting and disclosure systems in place. The deposit starts at 20,000,000 yuan (about 2,800,000 US dollars) and then floats at 15 percent of the previous month's direct selling revenue, capped at 100,000,000 yuan (about 13,900,000 US dollars) (Article 29). For an overseas brand that wants to test a referral channel first, that road locks up time and money far beyond the business itself.

Third, the claim that health products have been removed from the direct selling product catalogue does not hold. Health products remain in the catalogue to this day. That point does not collide with your channel choice: the catalogue governs what licensed direct sellers may sell, while 1210 governs what cross-border e-commerce may import, and the two tracks are independent. On 1210 you read the cross-border e-commerce retail import positive list and the filing requirements, and the direct selling catalogue simply does not enter the picture.

So skipping the license route is not a way around supervision. Notice No. 486 provides a statutory track of its own, the license conditions cannot be met in the short term, and the budget and time belong to the thing that must actually be done, which is restructuring the order flow.

## What must be done is the order-flow restructuring

Restructuring has an anchor. Article 5 of the 2013 joint opinion confirms that team-based pay calculated on sales performance is not treated as the crime of organizing or leading pyramid-scheme activities. That sentence gives every referral plan a safe harbor to anchor to; team-based pay is not guilty by itself. The plan then tightens one step further: hierarchy capped at two levels, no head-count-based pay of any kind, and the margin of safety stays wide.

The architecture is ready-made. Four prohibitions and two limits draw the boundary of promoter conduct, the money flow splits into three segments, and goods payments sit apart from commissions behind two layers of separation. These are mature compliance components, and none of them depends on a new approval in front.

At the execution level, restructuring changes three things. One, commissions are calculated on attributed transaction value, so a promoter's income tracks the real sales the promoter closed, and never the number of people recruited. Two, promoters sign promotion service agreements with the onshore entity, so the contractual relationship sits onshore and the rights and obligations stay checkable. Three, commissions and goods payments run through separate accounts: goods money goes into the supply-chain account and commissions settle on their own schedule. All three changes land in systems and contracts, and once they land, the model can go live.

## Two regulations, three red lines

The Direct Selling Regulations (Decree No. 443) govern the definition of the activity, the license conditions and the pay cap. If you sold under a license, Article 24 would cap a salesperson's pay at 30 percent of that person's own direct sales to consumers; a referral plan does not sit under that cap, but the number tells you how early China wrote its tolerance for multi-level pay into digits.

The real red lines are drawn in Article 7 of the Regulations on the Prohibition of Pyramid Schemes (Decree No. 444), and any one of the three categories makes the conduct a pyramid scheme:

- Pay by head count: requiring recruited persons to recruit others, and calculating or paying rewards on the number of people developed, directly or indirectly, in a rolling chain;
- Entry fees: requiring recruited persons to pay money, or to pay it in a disguised form such as buying goods, to qualify for joining or for recruiting others;
- Team-based pay: forming upline and downline relations, and calculating or paying upline rewards on the downline's sales performance.

Where a crime is constituted, Article 224-1 of the Criminal Law on organizing or leading pyramid-scheme activities also applies.

## The verdict order and the edge of the safe harbor

Check your plan in a fixed order: entry fees first, then pay by head count, then whether the hierarchy can spiral. The third item, team-based pay, sits inside the safe harbor of Article 5 of the 2013 opinion; the first two have no safe harbor at all, and one touch makes the conduct a pyramid scheme.

Read the edge of the harbor precisely. Article 5 shields team-based pay calculated on sales performance; pay by head count is outside the shield. Staying inside the harbor removes the criminal case, and the administrative liability does not disappear with it: the four prohibitions and two limits keep applying. In short, the harbor saves pay by performance, and it does not save pay by head count or pay-to-enter.

## One brand, two routes

An overseas health product brand plans to launch a referral channel in China and recruit a first batch of 200 promoters.

Route A is the license. Raise paid-in capital to 80,000,000 yuan, assemble five clean years and three years of overseas direct selling experience, pay the 20,000,000 yuan deposit into the designated bank, and wait for the approval. Not one promoter can be recruited before the license arrives, and the products would have to be confirmed inside the direct selling catalogue as well. Time and money both end up locked at the gate.

Route B is restructuring. The brand enters as a cross-border e-commerce enterprise under mode 1210 and stocks a bonded warehouse; each of the 200 promoters signs a promotion service agreement with the onshore entity; after each order closes, the commission is calculated on attributed transaction value and settles monthly through a separate commission account that never mixes with the bonded goods payments; the referral system runs two levels deep with no entry condition of any kind. Once the three changes land in systems and contracts, the first batch of promoters can start the same month.

The difference between the two routes is plain. Route A spends everything on the qualification, and Route B spends everything on the business. Referral sales can be done lawfully in China; the precondition is that the order flow and the commission flow pass the red-line check first, and the three changes land in systems and contracts.

A lawful referral program is drawn, and it is not gambled. Draw the boundaries first, then recruit.

Running a bonded order in production, the money and the paperwork each follow their own route, and each carries its own checkpoints. The funds flow is watched at settlement batches: money moves in whole batches to the onshore entity on a cycle, not as per-order remittances abroad. The invoicing flow is watched at documents: only when the five documents are complete do outbound remittance, pre-tax deduction and audit share a common premise. The two lines converge on the same order, and the meeting point is the six milestones from M1 to M6.

## How the money moves, the five nodes of the funds flow

The nodes of the funds flow line up in a row: payment, collection, settlement, onshore receipt, outbound remittance, with the checkpoint at the tail verifying documents and tax filing. Settlement runs in batches on a cycle, collected by the onshore entity as a single counterparty, with no per-order remittances abroad; commission is accrued per order by attribution and withheld and remitted monthly, with bills and withholding vouchers filed period by period; the fund account opens in about 6 weeks, running in parallel with the store build-out and taking no extra launch time.

Each node can be matched to something concrete in the back-office interface: a settlement statement sample, and the records of the funds flow and settlement batches are all in the system, so verification needs no separately gathered material. The batch is the basic unit of this flow; reconciliation, outbound remittance and record-keeping all follow the batch.

## How the paper is kept, what the five documents stack looks like

The invoicing flow has the same five nodes: payment voucher, waybill, customs declaration, tax receipt, settlement statement, and the checkpoint verifies that all five are on file. Five complete documents are the shared premise of three things: outbound remittance, pre-tax deduction and audit readiness; with any one missing, none of the three can proceed. The settlement statement is generated automatically per batch, drawn from the same source as the funds flow ledger, so the numbers match.

The records of the money, goods and information flows are not kept in a separate ledger; they all converge onto these five documents. One stack of documents traces an order end to end, and it also lines the books up with inventory.

## Why the invoicing flow stands as a flow of its own

This stack of vouchers serves four purposes: outbound remittance, pre-tax deduction, audit and regulator inquiries. All four draw on the same underlying documents, and one missing page breaks the chain, so the invoicing flow is run as its own flow with its own checkpoint, in parallel with the funds flow.

The import chain is especially strict about matching: the five documents must line up on the same order, and cross-order mixing gets picked out item by item. The discipline of fund segmentation is written into the agreement, and batch samples are checkable batch by batch in interface screenshots; discipline plus samples is the everyday state of this flow.

## How documents are pulled, how the books reconcile

Reconciliation runs on the batch number, checked batch by batch in the funds flow ledger; document retrieval runs on the archive number, pulled from the checkpoint tracker. Both ledgers are archived with the deliverables, ready on demand, so an inquiry never needs material assembled in a hurry.

## Four flows converge on one order

Placing an order moves four flows at once: the information flow returns the three-document feedback, the compliance flow completes identity verification, the funds flow folds this payment into batch settlement, and the invoicing flow generates the settlement statement in step. The four flows land their entries at the M1 to M6 checkpoints point by point, and every step of an order from product selection to commission booked can be matched to a record.

## One order walks from M1 to M6

The order path has 6 steps and 4 key interfaces, and the accountable A role in the responsibility matrix is exactly 1. M1 product selection and market access: triggered by complete inputs, gated at documents, producing the list and HS codes; we execute, and the inputs come from you. M2 entry into the bonded zone: gated at the bonded ledger, producing the ledger and the inventory view. M3 listing: gated at the claims review, producing the review conclusion and the disclosure text.

M4 order: gated at identity and disclosure, producing the identity and disclosure records. M5 customs clearance: triggered by payment completion, gated at the three-document cross-check and quota, producing the verification conclusion and the quota deduction. M6 settlement and commission: triggered by release, gated at commission compliance, producing the commission list; we execute and you confirm the rules. The steps are orchestrated by us; you provide inputs at M1 and confirm rules at M6, with no need to interface at every link in between.

China Customs admits New Zealand pet food from today under Announcement No. 10 of 2026. Only products made by plants recommended by New Zealand and registered by China qualify; each lot containing terrestrial or aquatic animal ingredients needs the original health certificate.

One lot of extruded dry food follows the full route: the source animals were born and raised in New Zealand, the plant is on the Chinese registration list, extrusion centre temperature reaches at least 100 °C, the pack carries a bilingual label marked "for pet food use only", and the original health certificate travels with the lot. China Customs checks the permit, the registration list and the certificate before release.

If the certificate or the registration does not match, hold this lot: no valid certificate, an unregistered plant or a mismatch between goods and documents means return or destruction; an unsatisfactory safety or hygiene result means treatment, return or destruction. Immediate effect is not an exemption from quarantine: permit approval, document checks and cargo inspection all remain. If bovine spongiform encephalopathy occurs, New Zealand suspends exports to China.

Under the laws and regulations of China and the provisions between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter "the Chinese Party") and the Ministry for Primary Industries of New Zealand (hereinafter "the New Zealand Party") on the quarantine and health requirements for the export of New Zealand pet food to China, New Zealand pet food that meets the following requirements is permitted for import from today.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(i) the Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China; (ii) the Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations; (iii) the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Import and Export Feed and Feed Additives; (iv) the Memorandum of Arrangement between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry for Primary Industries of New Zealand on the Quarantine and Health Requirements for the Export of New Zealand Pet Food to China (hereinafter "the Memorandum").

II. Scope of Products Permitted for Import

Pet food in this Announcement means industrially processed and manufactured products for direct consumption by pets, including dry pet food, canned pet food, pet snacks, pet rolls, pet nutritional supplements and chew products.

III. Disease Control Requirements

(i) Priority diseases. These are bovine spongiform encephalopathy, scrapie and chronic wasting disease. If any of them occurs in New Zealand, the New Zealand Party shall immediately suspend all exports of pet food to China. Exports of compliant pet food may resume after the Chinese Party completes a risk assessment and lifts the relevant disease ban and the two parties reach agreement.

(ii) Other diseases. These are highly pathogenic avian influenza, Rift Valley fever, rinderpest, vesicular stomatitis, anthrax, bluetongue, brucellosis (Brucella abortus and Brucella melitensis), Newcastle disease, African swine fever, swine vesicular disease, classical swine fever and foot-and-mouth disease. If any of them occurs in New Zealand, the New Zealand Party shall immediately suspend exports to China of pet food made by plants in the disease control zone from animal ingredients linked to that disease, and shall provide the Chinese Party with the relevant technical material. After assessment by the Chinese Party and agreement between the two parties, the affected pet food plants may resume exports.

IV. Requirements for Ingredients and Source Animals

(i) Ruminant-derived protein must not be used to feed ruminants (milk excepted). Veterinary drugs and feed additives banned in China or New Zealand must not be used under the law. (ii) Terrestrial animal ingredients (other than dairy ingredients and rendered ingredients) shall meet the following requirements: (1) the source animals were born and raised in New Zealand; (2) farmed animals passed ante-mortem and post-mortem inspection, showed no clinical signs of any of the above animal diseases on the day of slaughter, and were fit for pet food production; (3) wild animals that were hunted or captured comply with the New Zealand rules on the supply of edible products of wild animal origin, are assessed as healthy individuals and are not endangered species; (4) the animals did not die of an animal disease and were not subject to emergency slaughter under a disease eradication programme; (5) production took place in a plant approved by the New Zealand Party and complied with the New Zealand laws and regulatory requirements on pet food safety and hygiene.

(iii) Dairy ingredients shall meet the following requirements: (1) they come from healthy animals born or raised in New Zealand; (2) they were processed in a plant approved by the New Zealand Party and comply with the New Zealand rules on edible products of animal origin; (3) they underwent heat treatment, and/or the raw milk used underwent heat treatment at not lower than 72 °C for at least 15 seconds (or equivalent). (iv) Aquatic animal ingredients shall meet the following requirements: (1) the source aquatic animals showed no signs of infectious disease; (2) the animals were not subject to emergency slaughter under an infectious disease eradication programme; (3) processing took place in a plant or on a vessel approved by the New Zealand Party and complied with the New Zealand rules on edible products of animal origin.

(v) In addition, imported animal ingredients must be lawfully imported from countries or regions that meet New Zealand biosecurity requirements and are approved by the Chinese Party, pass quarantine and be fit for pet food production; ruminant-derived ingredients must not come from countries or regions subject to the Chinese ban on transmissible spongiform encephalopathy (TSE). (vi) Plant-derived ingredients meet phytosanitary requirements, contain no toxic or harmful substances or components banned by New Zealand rules, and contain no genetically modified components not approved by the Chinese authorities. (vii) Rendered animal ingredients comply with the relevant New Zealand laws, safety and hygiene requirements and are fit for pet food production.

V. Requirements for Production and Processing Plants

Plants producing pet food for export to China that contains terrestrial and/or aquatic animal ingredients shall meet the following requirements: (i) they are approved by the competent New Zealand authority and operate lawfully; (ii) they run a risk management plan based on hazard analysis and critical control points (HACCP) approved by the New Zealand Party; (iii) the New Zealand Party has verified that their production and processing comply with the New Zealand rules on safety and hygiene management; (iv) they are recommended by the New Zealand Party and registered by the Chinese Party.

VI. Production and Processing Requirements

(i) Commercially sterile pet food. (1) It undergoes high-temperature high-pressure heat processing with an F0 value of not less than 3.0; (2) an appropriate sealing method is used and the process is validated as achieving commercial sterility; (3) other processing methods are subject to approval by the New Zealand Party and recognition by the Chinese Party.

(ii) Heat-processed dry pet food. (1) For extruded dry pet food, the product centre temperature in the extrusion or drying step is not lower than 100 °C. (2) Other heat-processed pet food (except commercially sterile pet food, extruded dry pet food and chew products) shall be processed in the manner set out in the Annex. (3) If foot-and-mouth disease occurs in New Zealand, pet food for export to China shall meet the following requirements: (1) no ingredients from the foot-and-mouth disease control zone may be used; (2) heat processing at a centre temperature of not lower than 70 °C for not less than 30 minutes is required. (4) Other processing methods are subject to approval by the New Zealand Party and recognition by the Chinese Party.

(iii) Chew products. Heat processing at a centre temperature of not lower than 75 °C for not less than 30 minutes, validated as effectively killing pathogenic bacteria including Salmonella and vegetative fungi; or soaking for at least 8 hours in an alkaline solution with a pH value of 14.

VII. Product Requirements

(i) The product is approved for sale on the New Zealand market; (ii) it has a traceability system meeting New Zealand requirements; (iii) it complies with the relevant New Zealand regulatory requirements and with the Chinese laws, regulations and food safety standards on banned substances, toxic and harmful substances and contaminants; (iv) before pet food is exported to China, plants producing pet food containing aquatic and/or terrestrial animal ingredients shall establish a validated processing flow that meets the following requirements: (1) commercially sterile pet food meets the requirements for commercial sterility; (2) non-commercially sterile pet food meets the following requirements:

Salmonella: not detected in 25 grams: n=5, c=0, m=0, M=0; where: n is the number of samples tested; c is the number of samples with a bacterial count between m and M, and the result is still acceptable if the counts in the other samples are less than or equal to m; m is the minimum bacterial count (threshold), and the result is compliant if no sample count exceeds m; M is the maximum bacterial count, and the result is non-compliant if 1 or more sample counts are equal to or greater than M.

VIII. Packaging, Labelling, Storage and Transport Requirements

(i) Packaging material is new and clean, with good sealing and moisture resistance, and is not easily damaged. (ii) Packs carry labels in Chinese and English that comply with the Chinese labelling rules for imported pet food. (iii) The outer pack states the name and address of the production plant, its registration number and the exporting country, and is marked "not for human consumption" or "for pet food use only". (iv) Effective measures are taken during storage and transport to prevent external contamination.

IX. Pre-Export Inspection and Certificate Requirements

Pet food for export to China shall be inspected and quarantined by the New Zealand Party. Each lot of pet food for export to China containing terrestrial and/or aquatic animal ingredients shall be accompanied by at least one original health certificate.

X. Entry Inspection and Quarantine Requirements

(i) Quarantine approval. The importer shall obtain the Permit for Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine under the relevant rules. (ii) Document checks. (1) Check that the Permit for Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine is attached as required; (2) check that the goods come from a registered plant; (3) check that the health certificate is authentic and valid.

(iii) Cargo inspection. China Customs inspects and quarantines New Zealand pet food for export to China under the relevant laws, administrative regulations and rules and the requirements of this Announcement; lots that pass inspection and quarantine are released for entry. (iv) Handling of non-compliance. (1) Lots are returned or destroyed in any of the following cases: no valid health certificate; a New Zealand production plant that is not registered; goods that do not match the documents; soil, animal carcasses, animal excreta or quarantine pests found where effective quarantine treatment is not possible; labelling that does not meet the standard and cannot be corrected. (2) Where testing shows a safety or hygiene item does not meet the Chinese pet feed hygiene standard, the lot is treated, returned or destroyed. (3) Where bulk packs or containers are found broken, the owner or agent shall put them in order; if the damaged packaging poses a risk of spreading animal or plant diseases, the contaminated site, articles and instruments shall undergo quarantine treatment. (4) For plants with serious problems or repeated non-compliance, the Chinese Party may suspend their eligibility to export to China.

Announcement No. 123 of 2021 of the NMPA issued the Provisions on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics for Children, covering cleansing, moisturizing, body powder, sunscreen and other cosmetics for children under 12, as well as products suggesting child use through “all age groups”, “family use”, trademarks, patterns, homophones, letters or numbers.

The formula follows safety first, efficacy necessity and formula minimalism; new ingredients under monitoring, gene or nanotechnology ingredients, and ingredients for whitening, acne, depilation, deodorization, anti-dandruff, anti-hair-loss, dyeing or perming must not be used; safety assessment and necessary toxicological tests are required before launch.

The Little Golden Shield mark must appear on the sales package display face; non-children cosmetics must not carry it. Products newly registered or filed from 1 May 2022 follow the new labeling rules; existing products must update labels before 1 May 2023. Imported brands must lock formula and label at registration or filing before arranging production and import.

To standardize the production and operation of cosmetics for children, strengthen the supervision and administration of cosmetics for children, and ensure the safety of children using cosmetics, and in accordance with laws and regulations including the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics, the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) has organized the formulation of the Provisions on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics for Children (hereinafter the Provisions), which are hereby published, and the matters concerning the implementation of the Provisions are announced as follows:

Except for the labeling requirements, the other provisions on cosmetics for children take effect on 1 January 2022.

From 1 May 2022, cosmetics for children applied for registration or filed must be labeled in accordance with the Provisions; for cosmetics for children registered or filed earlier whose labels do not comply with the Provisions, the cosmetics registrant and filing entity must complete the update of product labels before 1 May 2023 so as to comply with the Provisions.

The mark for cosmetics for children will be published separately.

This announcement is hereby issued.

Annex: Provisions on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics for Children

**Article 1** To standardize the production and operation of cosmetics for children, strengthen the supervision and administration of cosmetics for children, and ensure the safety of children using cosmetics, these Provisions are formulated in accordance with laws and regulations including the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics.

**Article 2** Production and operation activities of cosmetics for children within the territory of the People’s Republic of China, and their supervision and administration, shall comply with these Provisions.

**Article 3** Cosmetics for children as referred to in these Provisions are cosmetics suitable for children under 12 years of age (including 12 years of age) that have functions such as cleansing, moisturizing, body powder and sun protection.

Products bearing terms such as “suitable for all age groups” or “family use”, or using trademarks, patterns, homophones, letters, Chinese pinyin, numbers, symbols, packaging forms or other means to suggest that the intended users include children, are managed as cosmetics for children.

**Article 4** The cosmetics registrant and filing entity are responsible for the quality, safety and efficacy claims of cosmetics for children.

Cosmetics producers and operators shall engage in production and operation activities in accordance with laws, regulations, mandatory national standards and technical specifications, strengthen the quality management of cosmetics for children, act in good faith and with self-discipline, and ensure product quality and safety.

Cosmetics producers and operators shall establish and implement systems such as incoming inspection records to ensure that cosmetics for children are traceable. They are encouraged to use information technology means to collect and retain production and operation information and build a quality and safety traceability system for cosmetics for children.

**Article 5** The cosmetics registrant and filing entity shall develop cosmetics for children in accordance with the physiological characteristics and possible application scenarios of children, following the principles of scientificity and necessity.

**Article 6** Cosmetics for children must carry the mark for cosmetics for children prescribed by the National Medical Products Administration on the display face of the sales package.

Non-children cosmetics must not carry the mark for cosmetics for children.

Cosmetics for children must use “Attention” or “Warning” as the lead-in and carry warning statements such as “must be used under adult supervision” on the visible face of the sales package.

The cosmetics registrant and filing entity are encouraged to adopt anti-counterfeiting technologies and other means on labels to help consumers identify and choose lawful products.

**Article 7** The formula design of cosmetics for children shall follow the principles of safety priority, efficacy necessity and formula minimalism:

(1) Ingredients with a long history of safe use shall be selected; new ingredients still in the monitoring period must not be used, nor may ingredients prepared with new technologies such as gene technology or nanotechnology be used; where an alternative ingredient is unavailable and such an ingredient must be used, the reasons shall be stated and the safety of its use in cosmetics for children shall be assessed;

(2) Ingredients intended for spot-lightening, acne treatment, depilation, deodorization, anti-dandruff, anti-hair-loss, hair dyeing or hair perming must not be used; where ingredients that may have the above effects are used for other purposes, the necessity of use and the safety of their use in cosmetics for children shall be assessed;

(3) For cosmetics for children, the scientificity and necessity of the ingredients used shall be assessed from the aspects of ingredient safety, stability, function and compatibility, taking into account children’s physiological characteristics, in particular ingredients such as fragrances and perfumes, colorants, preservatives and surfactants.

**Article 8** Cosmetics for children shall undergo product safety evaluation through safety assessment and necessary toxicological tests.

When the cosmetics registrant and filing entity conduct the safety assessment of cosmetics for children, children’s physiological characteristics shall be taken into account in hazard identification, exposure calculation and other aspects.

**Article 9** The National Medical Products Administration organizes cosmetics technical review bodies to formulate dedicated technical guidelines for cosmetics for children and conduct strict review of the registration application materials submitted by applicants.

Drug regulatory authorities shall strengthen post-market supervision and administration of cosmetics for children, focusing on technical verification of product safety documentation; non-compliance found shall be handled strictly in accordance with the law.

**Article 10** Cosmetics for children shall be produced in accordance with the Good Manufacturing Practices for Cosmetics, and the environmental requirements for production workshops of children’s skincare cosmetics shall comply with the relevant provisions.

The cosmetics registrant, filing entity and entrusted manufacturer shall conduct self-inspection of compliance with the Good Manufacturing Practices for Cosmetics as required, so as to ensure continued compliance with those practices.

The cosmetics registrant and filing entity are encouraged to formulate product standards for cosmetics for children that are stricter than the mandatory national standards and technical specifications.

**Article 11** The cosmetics registrant, filing entity and entrusted manufacturer shall formulate and implement onboarding and annual training plans for personnel, ensuring that employees are familiar with their job responsibilities and possess the professional knowledge and the legal knowledge relevant to cosmetics for children needed to perform those responsibilities. Enterprises shall establish employee training files.

Enterprises shall strengthen quality culture building, continuously raise employees’ quality awareness and ability to perform duties, and encourage employees to report unlawful or non-compliant situations found in their work.

**Article 12** The cosmetics registrant, filing entity and entrusted manufacturer shall strictly implement the material incoming inspection record system; where the enterprise considers it necessary after assessment, it shall conduct testing of relevant items to avoid bringing in prohibited ingredients such as hormones and anti-infective drugs, or substances that may harm human health, through raw materials or packaging materials in direct contact with the cosmetics.

Where the cosmetics registrant or filing entity finds prohibited ingredients such as hormones and anti-infective drugs, or substances that may harm human health, in raw materials or packaging materials in direct contact with the cosmetics, it shall immediately take measures to control the risk and report to the local provincial drug regulatory department.

**Article 13** The cosmetics registrant, filing entity and entrusted manufacturer shall take measures to prevent the properties, odor and appearance of cosmetics for children from being confused with food, medicines or other products, so as to prevent accidental ingestion or misuse.

Labels of cosmetics for children must not carry terms such as “food grade” or “edible”, or food-related patterns.

**Article 14** Cosmetics operators shall establish and implement an incoming inspection record system, verify the market entity registration certificate of the direct supplier, the special cosmetics registration certificate or ordinary cosmetics filing information, the mark for cosmetics for children, and the product quality inspection certificate of conformity, and keep the relevant vouchers, truthfully recording the cosmetics name, special cosmetics registration certificate number or ordinary cosmetics filing number, shelf life, net content, quantity purchased, supplier name, address, contact details, purchase date and other content.

Cosmetics operators shall check the label information of the cosmetics for children they operate against the corresponding product information published on the official website of the National Medical Products Administration, including: cosmetics name, special cosmetics registration certificate number or ordinary cosmetics filing number, name of the cosmetics registrant or filing entity, name of the entrusted manufacturer, and name of the domestic responsible person, ensuring that the above information matches the published information.

Cosmetics operators are encouraged to display cosmetics for children in separate sections and to display the mark for cosmetics for children in the sales area. They are encouraged to actively remind consumers to check the product registration or filing information when selling cosmetics for children.

**Article 15** Operators of cosmetics for children on e-commerce platforms, and e-commerce operators selling cosmetics for children through self-built websites or other online services, shall comprehensively, truthfully and accurately disclose on their main operation page the label and other information consistent with the cosmetics registration or filing materials, and continuously display the mark for cosmetics for children at a prominent position on the product display page.

**Article 16** Where cosmetics producers and operators or medical institutions discover or learn of an adverse reaction to a cosmetics for children, they shall report the adverse reaction to the local city or county adverse reaction monitoring institution in accordance with the provisions.

The cosmetics registrant and filing entity shall analyze and evaluate the adverse reaction reports collected or learned concerning cosmetics for children, and self-inspect the possible causes of the adverse reaction. For what may be a serious adverse reaction, investigation and analysis shall be conducted in accordance with the provisions and a self-inspection report shall be prepared, submitted to the local provincial adverse reaction monitoring institution and simultaneously to the local provincial drug regulatory department. Where a product safety risk is found, measures shall be taken immediately to control the risk; where a quality defect or other problem that may harm human health is found, production shall be stopped immediately, the cosmetics already placed on the market and sold shall be recalled, and the relevant cosmetics operators and consumers shall be notified to stop selling and using them, in accordance with Article 44 of the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics.

**Article 17** Where sampling inspection finds quality and safety problems in a cosmetics for children, the cosmetics registrant, filing entity and entrusted manufacturer shall immediately stop production, conduct a self-inspection of compliance with the Good Manufacturing Practices for Cosmetics, and report to the local provincial drug regulatory department. Production may resume only after the risk factors affecting quality and safety have been eliminated. The provincial drug regulatory department may organize on-site inspection according to the actual situation.

Where the cosmetics registrant or filing entity finds a quality defect or other problem in a cosmetic that may harm human health, it shall, in accordance with Article 44 of the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics, immediately stop production, recall the cosmetics already placed on the market and sold, and notify the relevant cosmetics operators and consumers to stop selling and using them.

The cosmetics registrant and filing entity shall analyze and assess other related products according to the reasons for failing the inspection, so as to ensure product quality and safety.

**Article 18** The drug regulatory department shall, on the principle of risk management and in light of local conditions, list the cosmetics registrant, filing entity, domestic responsible person, entrusted manufacturer and cosmetics operators with relatively concentrated sales of cosmetics for children as key supervision targets, and increase the frequency of supervision and inspection.

**Article 19** The drug regulatory department shall treat cosmetics for children as a key category for annual sampling inspection and risk monitoring. Where sampling inspection or risk monitoring finds substances in cosmetics for children that may harm human health, the drug regulatory department may take emergency control measures such as ordering the suspension of production and operation, and issue safety warning information; where the cosmetics for children are imported, the relevant departments shall be requested in accordance with the law to suspend imports.

**Article 20** When the drug regulatory department investigates violations concerning cosmetics for children in accordance with the law, any of the following circumstances shall be deemed a serious circumstance under the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics:

(1) Production of cosmetics for children using ingredients prohibited for cosmetics production, or new ingredients that should be registered but are not registered;

(2) Illegally adding substances that may harm human health to cosmetics for children.

**Article 21** Children’s toothpaste is managed by reference to these Provisions.

**Article 22** These Provisions take effect on 1 January 2022.

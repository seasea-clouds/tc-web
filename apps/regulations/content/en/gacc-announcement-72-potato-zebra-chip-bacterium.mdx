Entry is allowed, but approval comes first. From 1 August 2026, Announcement No. 72 suspends quarantine approval for production-use propagating material: seed potatoes from affected areas and six host seedlings, including tomato from Italy and the Republic of Korea.

For a consignment of Italian tomato seed to China: confirm the growing area is pest free, test by PCR under ISPM No. 27 before export to confirm the bacterium is absent, and note the additional declaration on the certificate before applying to resume approval.

Transshipment via a third country needs a transit certificate plus the certificate of origin; detection of the bacterium or a non-compliant certificate means return or destruction; seedlings from other affected countries face the same testing and declaration.

<!-- original -->

Recently, China Customs has on multiple occasions detected the quarantine pest Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium (Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum) in incoming plant seeds originating from Italy, the Republic of Korea and other countries. This bacterium can harm Solanaceae and Apiaceae host plants such as potato, tomato and carrot, seriously affecting crop yield and quality. To prevent the introduction of this bacterium and protect China's agricultural production and ecological security, and pursuant to the Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China, the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations, the Plant Quarantine Regulations and other laws and regulations, as well as relevant international phytosanitary measures standards, the following quarantine measures are hereby decided:

I. Quarantine approval is suspended for the introduction, for production use, of propagating material such as seed potatoes of potato (Solanum tuberosum) originating from countries and regions where potato zebra chip disease occurs (see the Annex).

II. Quarantine approval is suspended for the introduction, for production use, of propagating material (hereinafter referred to as seedlings) such as seed of tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), pepper (Capsicum annuum), carrot (Daucus carota var. sativa), coriander (Coriandrum sativum), celery (Apium graveolens) and parsley (Petroselinum crispum) originating from Italy and the Republic of Korea.

After the phytosanitary authorities of Italy and the Republic of Korea provide written information on the occurrence of potato zebra chip disease in their territories and an assessment confirms that the conditions are met, quarantine approval for the introduction of seedlings from areas of the relevant countries where the disease does not occur shall be resumed. Before export of the relevant seedlings, the phytosanitary authorities of Italy and the Republic of Korea shall draw representative samples and test them using the PCR method prescribed in International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 27, “Diagnostic Protocols for Regulated Pests, DP21: Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum” (ISPM No.27—DP21), to confirm that they are free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium, and shall enter the following additional declaration on the phytosanitary certificate: “This consignment of plants for planting is originated from area free of Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum and has been tested prior to export, and is found free of Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum.” (This consignment of seedlings originates from an area free of potato zebra chip disease and, having been tested prior to export, is free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium).

III. For seedlings originating from countries and regions in the Annex other than Italy and the Republic of Korea, the phytosanitary authority shall test samples in accordance with the relevant requirements of the second paragraph of Article II to confirm that they are free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium, and shall enter the following additional declaration on the phytosanitary certificate: “This consignment of plants for planting has been tested prior to export and is found free of Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum.” (This consignment of seedlings, having been tested prior to export, is free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium). This does not apply to consignments that departed from the country or region of origin before the implementation date specified in this Announcement.

IV. Where seedlings originating from countries and regions in the Annex are exported to China via transshipment through a third country or region, phytosanitary certificates shall be issued in accordance with the following requirements:

(i) Where the seedlings are only stored or consolidated in the third country or region and have not been contaminated by pests, the phytosanitary authority of the third country or region shall issue a transit phytosanitary certificate, accompanied by the original phytosanitary certificate issued by the phytosanitary authority of the country or region of origin bearing the corresponding additional declaration, or a copy confirmed by the phytosanitary authority of the third country or region.

(ii) Where the seedlings are contaminated by pests in the third country or region, the phytosanitary authority of the third country or region shall test samples in accordance with the relevant requirements of the second paragraph of Article II to confirm that they are free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium, and shall enter the following additional declaration on the phytosanitary certificate: “This consignment of plants for planting has been tested prior to export and is found free of Candidatus Liberibacter solanacearum.” (This consignment of seedlings, having been tested prior to export, is free of Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium). This does not apply to consignments that departed from the third country or region before the implementation date specified in this Announcement.

V. All customs shall review the phytosanitary certificates accompanying seedlings from the relevant countries and regions and inspect and quarantine the consignments; where a certificate does not meet the requirements or Potato Zebra Chip Bacterium is detected, the consignment shall be subject to return or destruction in accordance with the law.

VI. Agriculture and rural affairs departments and plant quarantine institutions at all levels shall strengthen the isolated trial planting of seedlings and post-introduction epidemic monitoring, and shall take prompt lawful action upon the discovery of any epidemic.

This Announcement takes effect on 1 August 2026.

Annex: Countries and Regions Where Potato Zebra Chip Disease Occurs

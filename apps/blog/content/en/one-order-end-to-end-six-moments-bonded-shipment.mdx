A bonded order travels from product admission to settlement and payment through six moments; from release inside the zone to delivery to the consumer, the fastest order in the demo data took one day, fourteen hours and forty-eight minutes, while slower ones are still on the way.

Each moment has its own trigger, release gate and deliverable; if the previous gate is not cleared, the next one does not start. Documents must be complete at the admission stage, and delivery must be watched after release; these two ends take the most time and are the easiest to gloss over.

To judge whether an order is stuck, there is no need to search chat logs; two tables are enough: whether the three-document timeline shows a discrepancy, and how far apart release and delivery are. Both tables hold one order per row, the discrepancy is located at a specific moment, and whoever takes over can trace it back by order number.

## Which gate each of the six moments sits behind

Three numbers on this path are worth remembering: a six-step order path, four key interfaces, and one accountable A role. The six moments advance in order; the trigger decides when a step starts, the gate decides whether it can be released, and the deliverable decides what the step leaves behind.

- M1 Product selection and admission: the trigger is complete inputs, the gate is documents, the deliverable is the declaration list and HS code; we execute, and you provide the inputs.
- M2 Zone entry: the trigger is M1 approval, the gate is the bonded ledger, the deliverable is the ledger and inventory view; we execute.
- M3 Listing: the trigger is M2 completion, the gate is claims review, the deliverable is the review conclusion and disclosure text; we execute.
- M4 Order: the trigger is the listing going live, the gate is identity and disclosure, the deliverable is the identity and disclosure record; we execute.
- M5 Customs clearance: the trigger is payment completion, the gate is three-document cross-check and quota, the deliverable is the check conclusion and quota deduction; we execute.
- M6 Settlement and payment: the trigger is release, the gate is payment compliance, the deliverable is the payment list; we execute, and you confirm the rules.

The six moments are arranged by order, not by department. Shang Cai Fa [2018] No. 486 sets the regulatory framework for cross-border e-commerce retail imports; GACC Announcement No. 194 of 2018 writes declaration-list release, three-document data comparison and identity verification into the declaration process; Cai Guan Shui [2016] No. 18 sets the tax basis at the level of a single order. The three documents govern rules; the six moments govern where each rule lands in an order and who hands over what.

M1 and M2 are the easiest to confuse. M1's gate is documents; the declaration list and HS code can only be issued once inputs are complete. M2's gate is the bonded ledger; once goods enter the bonded warehouse, if the ledger and inventory view do not match, zone entry is not complete. M3 and M4 both look like online actions, but their gates differ: claims review comes before listing, identity and disclosure come at order placement. M5 is where three-document cross-check and quota come in, and M6's gate is payment compliance; you confirm the rules once at the start, and they are applied the same way afterward.

## Responsibility changes hands only at the two ends; one party orchestrates the middle

The whole chain has only one coordinating entity, and the responsibility assignment matrix has only one accountable A role. The boundaries of the four key interfaces are written into the plan: your company owns the product and decisions, the platform owns orders and settlement, the fulfillment provider owns warehousing and customs clearance, and regulators own declaration and inspection. Once the interfaces are set, no gap is left at handover.

The second half of the path sits in two demo tables: whether the order and payment document match in timing, and where the courier tracking number stands after the declaration list is released. The detail and log entries in the tables lead back to the original record of each order; these moments are not paper process.

You provide inputs only at M1 and confirm rules only at M6; there is no need to interface step by step in between. Read this sentence in reverse, and it matters more: if your team plans to interface with the platform, the fulfillment provider and regulators at every step, this arrangement is not cost-effective for you; the time cost of relaying messages through many hands will eat up the certainty the moments bring. Accounts and data stay with you, orchestration and records stay with us; this division is the premise that lets the six moments form one line.

## The three-document timeline pins each discrepancy to one step

The three-document timeline holds one order per row; the columns are order number, payment document, waybill and declaration list, followed by timing, discrepancy item and locating result. The tabs split into all orders, matched and discrepant; in daily work, look at the discrepant tab first, then return to the full list for verification. Of the six demo rows, four have all three documents consistent and two show discrepancies; a discrepancy is not written up as a vague conclusion such as slow clearance but pinned directly to one step.

An order passes through the payment document, waybill and declaration list in sequence; when the three documents sit side by side, a discrepancy shows up in the same row. For the order whose declaration list came two hours after the waybill, the locating result is declaration queue, meaning goods and documents have both arrived and the wait is at declaration; for the order whose payment document came one hour after the order, the locating result is channel callback, meaning the payment channel came back slowly, not the warehouse and not the declaration. Once an exception is traced this far, the person handling it knows whom to ask, without interrogating the whole chain again.

## Record release and delivery separately to see the time clearly

Release and delivery are two points in time, not one status. Release ends the customs side; delivery ends the consumer side; transport sits in between. The release and delivery status table holds one order per row; the columns are declaration list number, release time, courier tracking number, carrier, delivery time and delivery status, with tabs for all, released and delivered. Only by recording the two timestamps separately can you see whether the time is spent inside the zone or on the road.

The path starts at admission and ends at delivery, and this table catches the last two points. To compress the time from zone outbound to the consumer's hands, first check whether the release time was dragged by the previous gate, then check how long the delivery leg took; only by calculating the two segments separately can you know what to change.

## Walk one order from beginning to end

Take order SO260922-0417 from the demo table and walk it through. At M1 the inputs are complete and the documents clear the gate, yielding the declaration list and HS code; at M2 the goods enter the bonded warehouse, the bonded ledger is created, and the inventory view shows this batch; at M3 claims review comes before listing, and the review conclusion and disclosure text are archived together; at M4 the consumer places an order and the identity and disclosure record is generated; at M5 payment completes, the payment document, waybill and declaration list are cross-checked, and the quota is reduced accordingly; at M6, after release, the order enters settlement and payment, and the payment list is issued under the rules confirmed at the start. Along the whole path, you submit materials only at M1 and confirm rules only at M6; we orchestrate the middle.

In the three-document timeline, this order has all three documents consistent and no discrepancy; the release time is 2026-09-22 18:41 and the delivery time is 2026-09-24 10:12, one day, fifteen hours and thirty-one minutes from release to delivery.

SO260921-0088 in the same table takes another route: its declaration list is two hours later than the waybill, and the locating result is declaration queue; in the release table it was already released at 2026-09-21 21:07, the delivery time has not landed, and the status is in transit. The exception lands at the declaration step; only because release and delivery are recorded separately can you see that it is stuck at declaration, not at delivery.

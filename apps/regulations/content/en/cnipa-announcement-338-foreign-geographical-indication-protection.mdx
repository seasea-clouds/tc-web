Protection in China starts with the competent authority of the country of origin, which must be willing to recommend the product; the applicant must be the original applicant of that geographical indication there. Certificates, origin area, quality and technical requirements and test reports must be ready, and the Chinese translations of the certificates and origin area must be notarized.

CNIPA completes the formal examination within 30 working days, and the acceptance announcement opens a 60-day opposition period. If consultation fails, the Geographical Indication Expert Committee reviews and rules, and only then does the technical examination begin, combining a meeting examination with, where necessary, on-site origin verification. The approval announcement follows after it passes.

Once approved, the product name and origin must match the approval announcement, the special symbol rests on self-declaration, and the applicant files an annual report before the end of March each year. Changes to major information such as origin area or quality requirements must be applied for within 90 days. Settle the Chinese name and origin wording before shipping.

(Promulgated by the General Administration of Quality Supervision, Inspection and Quarantine (AQSIQ) on 28 March 2016; amended in accordance with Announcement No. 338 of 27 November 2019 of the National Intellectual Property Administration.)

## Chapter I General Provisions

**Article 1**　These Measures are formulated to effectively protect foreign geographical indication products sold in China, to regulate the use in China of the names and special symbols of foreign geographical indication products, and in accordance with Article 26 of the Provisions for the Protection of Geographical Indication Products.

**Article 2**　The foreign geographical indication products referred to in these Measures are products produced outside China that are already registered and protected in the country or region of origin and comply with Article 2 of the Provisions for the Protection of Geographical Indication Products.

**Article 3**　The application of a foreign geographical indication product for protection in China under these Measures shall be handled in accordance with the agreement concluded between the country to which the product belongs and the People's Republic of China, or the international treaty to which both are parties, or on the principle of reciprocity.

**Article 4**　These Measures apply to the application, acceptance, examination, approval, special symbol use, supervision and administration, amendment and cancellation of foreign geographical indication products protected in China (hereinafter referred to as “protection in China”).

**Article 5**　The names of foreign geographical indication products protected in China include a Chinese name and an original-language name.

(1) The Chinese name consists of a name with a geographical indication function and a generic name reflecting the true attributes of the product, or may be a name established by usage;

(2) The original-language name is the name that has obtained geographical indication registration protection in the country or region of origin;

(3) The above names are not generic names in China and do not conflict with other prior rights such as the names of Chinese geographical indication products.

**Article 6**　The National Intellectual Property Administration of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the National Intellectual Property Administration, CNIPA) uniformly administers the protection of foreign geographical indication products in China. Intellectual property administration departments at all levels protect foreign geographical indication products in accordance with their functions.

## Chapter II Application and Acceptance

**Article 7**　Protection in China of a foreign geographical indication product is applied for by the original applicant for geographical indication protection in the country or region of origin where the product originates, recommended by the competent geographical indication department of that country or region of origin, and submitted to the National Intellectual Property Administration.

**Article 8**　The applicant for protection in China of a foreign geographical indication product may designate its institution in China as the contact person for the protection work in China, may request staff of the official resident representative institution in China of the country or region of origin to serve as the contact person for the protection work in China, or may appoint an agent.

**Article 9**　The application for protection in China of a foreign geographical indication product requires the following written materials in Chinese:

(1) The application form for protection in China of the foreign geographical indication product;

(2) The applicant's name and address, contact telephone number, and the contact person, address and contact telephone number in China;

(3) The original official document certifying that geographical indication protection has been obtained in the country or region of origin, together with its notarized Chinese translation;

(4) The origin area issued by the competent geographical indication authority of the country or region of origin, together with its notarized Chinese translation;

(5) The quality and technical requirements of the product;

(6) Test report: the test report issued in the country or region of origin proving the sensory characteristics and physicochemical indicators of the applied product, together with its notarized Chinese translation;

(7) Other supporting materials.

**Article 10**　The quality and technical requirements of a foreign geographical indication product protected in China include:

(1) The Chinese name and original-language name of the product;

(2) The protected origin area;

(3) The product attributes and its production process;

(4) Quality characteristics, including the sensory characteristics and physicochemical indicators of the product;

(5) Reputation: the reputation and trade and sales situation of the product in the country (region) of origin, in China, and in other countries and regions of the world;

(6) Linkage: a description of the linkage between the product's quality characteristics and the natural or human factors of its origin.

**Article 11**　After receiving the application materials, the National Intellectual Property Administration organizes a formal examination of the application materials within 30 working days. The outcomes of the formal examination fall into three categories: acceptance, need for correction, and non-acceptance.

(1) Where accepted, the National Intellectual Property Administration issues a gazette and publishes the matter to the public on its official website;

(2) Where correction is needed, the National Intellectual Property Administration gives the applicant written feedback with correction opinions. After the applicant submits the corrected materials to the National Intellectual Property Administration, the Administration re-organizes the formal examination;

(3) Where not accepted, the National Intellectual Property Administration issues the applicant a written notice of non-acceptance.

**Article 12**　The opposition period for the acceptance announcement is 60 days, counted from the date on which the National Intellectual Property Administration announces acceptance. Within the opposition period, any organization or person, domestic or foreign, may file an opposition in writing with the National Intellectual Property Administration.

**Article 13**　The opposition contents include the name and unit name of the opponent, contact information, the reasons for the opposition and evidence materials, etc. The opposition shall be written in Chinese and is valid upon signature or seal.

**Article 14**　After receiving the opposition, the National Intellectual Property Administration promptly forwards the opposition contents to the applicant. The opposition is resolved through consultation between the two opposing parties, or the National Intellectual Property Administration organizes the two parties to resolve it through consultation; where consultation fails, the National Intellectual Property Administration organizes the Geographical Indication Expert Committee to review and render a ruling.

**Article 15**　Where an opposition application falls under any of the following circumstances, the National Intellectual Property Administration shall not accept it, and shall notify the opponent in writing with reasons:

(1) It is not filed within the statutory period;

(2) It has no clear opposition reasons, facts or legal basis.

**Article 16**　The National Intellectual Property Administration shall notify the opponent in writing of a rejected opposition application. A party dissatisfied with the opposition ruling may apply to the National Intellectual Property Administration for review within 30 days from receipt of the notice. The National Intellectual Property Administration makes a decision within 60 days from receipt of the application and notifies both parties in writing; the review decision is final.

## Chapter III Technical Examination and Approval

**Article 17**　Where the acceptance announcement period has expired without opposition, or the opposition has been resolved by agreement, or the opposition has been ruled unfounded, the National Intellectual Property Administration organizes experts to conduct a technical examination.

**Article 18**　The technical examination includes a meeting examination and, where necessary, origin verification, and the applicant shall cooperate. The technical examination expert panel consists of 5 or 7 persons.

**Article 19**　During the technical examination, the applicant shall invite professional technicians familiar with the product and translation personnel to participate; the time and place of the technical examination are agreed by both parties.

**Article 20**　The outcomes of the technical examination fall into three categories: passed, needs rectification, and not passed.

(1) Where the examination is passed, the National Intellectual Property Administration issues the approval announcement for protection in China of the foreign geographical indication product, and the product is protected according to law;

(2) Where rectification is needed, the National Intellectual Property Administration gives the applicant written feedback with rectification opinions. After the applicant submits the rectification materials to the National Intellectual Property Administration, the Administration again organizes a technical examination or technical confirmation;

(3) Where not passed, the National Intellectual Property Administration issues the applicant a written notice of non-pass of the technical examination.

## Chapter IV Special Symbol and Supervision Administration

**Article 21**　For a foreign geographical indication product protected in China, the product name, origin and other information marked on it shall match the information in the approval announcement of the National Intellectual Property Administration.

**Article 22**　Producers, associations and other societies within the origin area of a foreign geographical indication product protected in China may apply to the National Intellectual Property Administration for use of the geographical indication special symbol of the People's Republic of China.

**Article 23**　The use of the special symbol follows a self-declaration system. Once the name of the product protected in China and the geographical indication special symbol of the People's Republic of China are used, the product is deemed to have been self-declared as meeting the requirements of the National Intellectual Property Administration approval announcement for foreign geographical indication products.

**Article 24**　The geographical indication special symbol of the People's Republic of China is implemented in accordance with the relevant requirements of the National Intellectual Property Administration.

**Article 25**　The applicant for a foreign geographical indication product protected in China shall publish to the public in Chinese the geographical indication laws, regulations and technical standards applied by its product.

**Article 26**　The applicant for a foreign geographical indication product protected in China shall fulfill the corresponding management responsibilities, formulate management measures, and manage its name, quality characteristics and special symbol use.

**Article 27**　An annual report system applies to foreign geographical indication products protected in China. Before the end of March each year, the applicant shall report to the National Intellectual Property Administration the management measures for the current year and the implementation report for the previous year.

**Article 28**　Where a foreign geographical indication product already protected in China causes a major adverse impact in China, the National Intellectual Property Administration may, if it deems it truly necessary, organize further on-site verification of its quality characteristics and origin conditions, and the applicant shall cooperate.

**Article 29**　The National Intellectual Property Administration publishes the protection information in China of foreign geographical indication products on its official website and accepts public supervision.

## Chapter V Protection, Amendment and Cancellation

**Article 30**　Foreign geographical indication products protected in China enjoy equal protection with Chinese geographical indication products.

**Article 31**　Intellectual property administrative departments at all levels accept reports and complaints concerning infringement of the lawful rights and interests of foreign geographical indication products protected in China; the relevant departments investigate and punish violations according to law. The applicant for a foreign geographical indication product protected in China may also bring a lawsuit before a people's court.

**Article 32**　Where major information such as the origin area, quality and technical requirements, or the names and addresses of producers, associations or societies within the origin area of a foreign geographical indication product protected in China has changed, the applicant shall file an amendment application with the National Intellectual Property Administration within 90 days. After passing the technical examination, the amendment is announced by the National Intellectual Property Administration.

**Article 33**　Where a foreign geographical indication product protected in China falls under any of the following circumstances, the National Intellectual Property Administration may cancel it; any unit or person may request the National Intellectual Property Administration to cancel it and provide relevant evidence materials:

(1) The geographical indication product has had its protection cancelled in the country or region of origin;

(2) It has become a generic name within the territory of China or has evolved into a generic name;

(3) It seriously violates the relevant provisions of Chinese laws and regulations.

**Article 34**　Where a cancellation request falls under any of the following circumstances, the National Intellectual Property Administration shall not accept it, and shall notify the requester in writing with reasons:

(1) It has no clear cancellation reasons and facts;

(2) It only involves the product name becoming a generic name abroad.

**Article 35**　The National Intellectual Property Administration organizes the Geographical Indication Expert Committee to review the cancellation request and render a ruling. Where the ruling is to cancel, the National Intellectual Property Administration issues an announcement; where the ruling is not to cancel, the requester and the rights holder are notified.

## Chapter VI Supplementary Provisions

**Article 36**　These Measures take effect on the date of promulgation.

**Article 37**　Foreign geographical indication products protected in China shall also comply with the relevant provisions of China on entry-exit inspection and quarantine.

**Article 38**　These Measures are interpreted by the National Intellectual Property Administration.

Entry is allowed only for 2 types: plum and apricot. Announcement No. 143 applies from 28 September 2026: Tajikistan fresh stone fruits may be exported to China; orchards and packing houses must be audited by the Tajik side, approved and registered by the Chinese side, and come from a pest-free area or have systems approach records.

An apricot consignment: 1 apple borer moth trap and 2 grape flower-wing leafroller moth traps per hectare, checked every 2 weeks; manual selection and grading at the packing house; 2% pre-export sampling, at least 1200 fruits, 60 cut open; the Tajik side issues the ISPM 12 certificate, the additional declaration quoting the Protocol's English sentence verbatim. Live specimens of either pest mean return or destruction of the whole consignment.

Without pest-free area approval or monitoring records, do not ship; without the English additional declaration, entry is likewise blocked.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side, GACC) and the Food Safety Committee of the Republic of Tajikistan (hereinafter the Tajik side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh stone fruits from Tajikistan to China, fresh stone fruits from Tajikistan meeting the following requirements are permitted for import from the date of this announcement.

I. Legal Basis for Quarantine

(i) Biosecurity Law of the People's Republic of China;

(ii) Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(iii) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(iv) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(v) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from Tajikistan to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Food Safety Committee of the Republic of Tajikistan.

II. Permitted Commodities

Fresh stone fruits (hereinafter stone fruits) include plum (scientific name Prunus salicina, English name Plum) and apricot (scientific name Prunus armeniaca, English name Apricot).

III. Permitted Production Areas

Stone-fruit-producing areas of Tajikistan.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses exporting stone fruits to China shall be audited by the Tajik side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. Registration information includes the name, address and registration number, so as to enable accurate traceability of export consignments that do not comply with this announcement. Before each export season, the Tajik side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side which, after review and approval by the Chinese side, shall be published on the GACC website.

V. Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) apple borer moth Cydia pomonella

(2) almond kernel wasp Eurytoma amygdali

(3) small plum sawfly Hoplocampa minuta

(4) grape flower-wing leafroller moth Lobesia botrana

(5) Hall's long-shield scale insect Mercetaspis halli

(6) peach large black aphid Pterochloroides persicae

(7) Turanian brown globose scale insect Rhodococcus turanicus

VI. Pre-export Management

(i) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards exporting stone fruits to China shall, under the supervision of the Tajik side, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, implement Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), maintain orchard hygiene and promptly remove fallen and rotten fruit, and implement Integrated Pest Management (IPM), including regular pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control of pests and cultural practices and other control measures, so as to avoid or minimize the occurrence of quarantine pests of concern to China on the stone fruits.

(2) The Tajik side shall, in accordance with the requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), develop a management plan for quarantine pests of concern to China and organize orchard monitoring throughout the year. If pests or their corresponding symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated management measures including chemical, physical or biological control shall be taken to control pest population density or maintain a low level of prevalence.

(3) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel. Such personnel shall receive training from the Tajik side or its authorized institution.

(4) Orchards exporting to China shall retain pest monitoring and control records and provide them to the Chinese side through the Tajik side when needed. Pest monitoring and control records shall at least include the monitoring time, the names of pests found and the control measures taken; chemical control records shall include detailed information such as the names, active ingredients, concentrations and application dates of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(5) With regard to apple borer moth and grape flower-wing leafroller moth, stone fruits exported to China shall come from pest-free areas for apple borer moth and grape flower-wing leafroller moth or from orchards effectively controlled under a systems approach.

(1) Pest-free area

It shall be established by the National Plant Protection Organization in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 10 (ISPM 10), pest monitoring shall be carried out by the Tajik side in accordance with the relevant requirements, and it shall be subject to approval by the Chinese side.

If apple borer moth or grape flower-wing leafroller moth is found in a relevant pest-free area, the Tajik side shall immediately suspend the pest-free area status, notify the Chinese side within 48 hours and activate the emergency response plan. The pest-free area may be restored only after the Tajik side has eliminated the pest situation and obtained recognition by the Chinese side.

(2) Systems approach

If a pest-free area cannot be established, the Tajik side shall adopt a systems approach and, using pheromone trapping for monitoring, conduct monitoring of apple borer moth and grape flower-wing leafroller moth in orchards exporting to China, and shall provide the monitoring records to the Chinese side upon request.

For apple borer moth, orchards exporting to China shall conduct apple borer moth monitoring from flowering to the end of harvest. Traps shall be set at a density of 1 per hectare in the orchard; orchards with an area of less than 3 hectares must install at least 3 traps; lures shall be replaced once a month and traps inspected once every 2 weeks. If 3 or more apple borer moths are found in a trap, effective control shall be carried out immediately (such as the use of mating disruption techniques). For orchards where control is not thorough, the Tajik side shall suspend the orchard's qualification for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. Upon request, the Tajik side shall provide pest investigation reports or management records to the Chinese side.

For grape flower-wing leafroller moth, the Tajik side or its authorized personnel shall, from flowering to the end of harvest, carry out pest control in registered orchards through field surveys and trap monitoring. Traps shall be set in the orchard at a density of at least 2 per hectare and inspected at least once every 2 weeks. The Tajik side shall, as required, provide a monitoring report to the Chinese side before each annual export season. Once grape flower-wing leafroller moth is detected, effective control shall be carried out immediately (such as the use of mating disruption techniques). For orchards where control is not thorough, the Tajik side shall suspend the orchard's qualification for export to China and immediately notify the Chinese side. Upon request, the Tajik side shall provide pest investigation reports or management records to the Chinese side.

(6) With regard to aphids, scale insects and other pests, orchards exporting to China shall, under the supervision and guidance of technical personnel of the Tajik side, conduct monitoring from flowering to harvest, carrying out an orchard survey once every 2 weeks, focusing on whether the above pests occur on branches, stems, leaves and fruits. If any pest or its corresponding symptoms are found, measures shall be taken immediately, such as biological, physical or chemical control, to control the occurrence of the pests.

(ii) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and shipment of stone fruits exported to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Tajik side or its authorized personnel.

(2) Packing houses for stone fruits exported to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors, and shall have a raw material area and a finished product warehouse.

(3) Functional areas for the processing, treatment and storage of stone fruits exported to China shall be relatively separate and rationally laid out, and shall be isolated from living areas with an appropriate distance.

(4) During packaging, stone fruits exported to China shall undergo manual selection, grading and cleaning so as to remove diseased fruit, insect-damaged fruit, rotten fruit, deformed fruit, twigs and leaves or other plant residues and soil.

(5) Packaged stone fruits, if storage is required, shall be immediately placed in storage and stored separately to avoid re-infestation by pests.

(6) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system to ensure that stone fruits exported to China can be traced back to registered orchards, and shall record information such as the processing and packaging date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the export date, the export quantity, the destination country and the container number.

(iii) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall comply with China's phytosanitary requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each package shall be marked, in Chinese or English, with information such as the fruit type, variety, exporting country, production area, and the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house. Each package and pallet shall be marked, in Chinese or English, with "Exported to the People's Republic of China".

(3) Containers loaded with stone fruits exported to China shall be inspected at the time of loading to ensure good hygienic conditions, and the inspection record shall be confirmed by the officials of the Tajik side.

(iv) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Before export, the Tajik side shall sample and inspect stone fruits exported to China at a rate of 2% per consignment, with a minimum sample size of 1200 fruits, and shall cut open for inspection at least 60 of the fruits or any suspect fruits found during inspection. If no phytosanitary problems occur within 2 years, the sampling rate shall be reduced to 1%.

(2) If live quarantine pests of concern to China are detected, the consignment shall not be exported to China, and, as the case may be, the export of stone fruits from the relevant orchards and packing houses to China for the current export season shall be suspended. If the consignment comes from a pest-free area, the status of the relevant pest-free area shall be cancelled. The Tajik side shall investigate the cause and take corrective measures. At the same time, detection records shall be kept and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(v) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

For stone fruits exported to China that have passed quarantine, the Tajik side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), indicating the name or code of the orchard and packing house, and shall state in the additional declaration: "This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Stone Fruits from Tajikistan to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China." (The consignment meets the phytosanitary requirements of the Protocol for fresh stone fruits exported from Tajikistan to China and is free of quarantine pests of concern to China.)

VII. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Non-compliance Handling

When stone fruits exported to China arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(i) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verify whether the imported stone fruits have obtained the Permit for Entry of Animals and Plants.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with Chapter VI, Section (v), of this announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on packages and pallets comply with Chapter VI, Section (iii), of this announcement.

(ii) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Stone fruits exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for fruit imports.

(2) Upon arrival at a Chinese port of entry, Customs shall verify the relevant documents and marks and conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this announcement. Consignments passing inspection and quarantine shall be permitted entry.

(iii) Non-compliance handling.

(1) If a consignment is found to originate from an orchard or packing house not approved by the Chinese side, entry of that consignment shall be refused.

(2) If live apple borer moth or grape flower-wing leafroller moth is detected, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

(3) If quarantine pests of concern to China or newly occurring quarantine pests in Tajikistan are detected, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to pest elimination treatment.

(4) If non-compliance with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards is found, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

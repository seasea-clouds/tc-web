## Three Layers of Ownership and Their Boundaries

From inside out the three layers are your core assets, the shared data and interface layer, and our operating scope during the contract term. Inner-layer items go into the handover checklist and transfer on exit; the middle layer follows the minimum-necessary principle, with the interface list delivered alongside the S0 deliverables; the outer layer covers only our own systems and tools, so data definitions do not follow the system. The boundaries are written into contract annexes to keep who runs it separate from who owns it.

The first column is yours: accounts, mini programs, domains, brand assets, content, and member and marketing data, all listed in the handover checklist and transferred on exit. The page states it plainly: accounts are registered in your name or a designated entity, you supply the registration details, we complete platform registration under the agreement, and the accounts end up in your name.

The second column is the shared layer: you are the personal information handler and we are the entrusted processor. Processing follows minimum necessity and domestic priority, with no other use; the interface list is delivered with the S0 deliverables, and decisions on interface changes and data field definitions stay with you. Article 21 of the Personal Information Protection Law writes the purpose, term, method and safeguards of entrusted processing into the agreement, which is the contract basis for this column.

The third column is our scope, limited to our own systems and tools, while data definitions remain yours. A bold closing line says we create no lock in on you through asset ownership. Our part at exit is set out on the same page: all member and transaction data exported in the agreed format, local copies cleared except where retention is required by law, with no disclosure to third parties and no other use.

The two lower cards turn duties into actions. The handover card sets out data export and copy clearance at exit; the account registration card states registration in your name or a designated entity, details supplied by you, platform registration handled by us under the agreement, and accounts landing in your name. Both list acceptance criteria in the contract annexes, with scoring and payment cadence tied to these deliverables, and verbal agreements kept out of the execution layer.

## Four Triggers and Three Phases of Exit Handover

This page is Exit and Handover: A No-Lock-In Mechanism, with four triggers under the title: no renewal at term end, an adjustment you request, a missed milestone, and force majeure. A horizontal timeline has three phases: start, a transition jointly confirmed by both sides, and closure and settlement. Six handover items run along the time bars: store and accounts, member and transaction data, stock on hand and bonded ledger, outstanding payments, in-transit orders, and content assets. Dark navy bars are key items (store and account access, member and transaction data) moved first, mid-blue bars are routine items advanced in the transition phase, and a white circle at the end of each bar is an item-by-item acceptance point. Here asset ownership, exportable data and the exit plan become a mechanism, and verbal promises do not count.

The handover checklist on the right has exactly six items matching the time bars one to one: store and accounts, member and transaction data, stock on hand (including bonded ledger), outstanding payments, in-transit orders, and content assets. The disposal basis below sets three boundaries: returns and bonded ledger, where stock can be returned as is, moved to compliant channels or sold at an agreed discount, with the ban on second sale still applying; transition orders, transferred item by item against the six-item checklist once returns and after-sales are cleared; and transaction records kept for no less than three years, the same standard as Article 31 of the E-Commerce Law. Fees stop at the amount actually incurred on the handover date, and transition length and fees follow the contract. A small member data table at lower right gives three samples by member ID and cumulative spend, stored by column in the system and exported as full tables in the agreed format.

## Reading Four Pages as One Line Through a Single Member

Take the row of black card member M-100238 and read ownership, permissions, export and acceptance as one chain. Start with ownership: the store account is registered to your domestic entity, shop_admin has your domestic entity as its registered entity and sees all data, member data rights are yours, and Article 927 of the Civil Code states the duty to transfer property obtained under entrustment. Then permissions: in the member list M-100238 shows cumulative spend of 15800.00 yuan, 15230 points, last purchase 09-24 and registration 2025-11-02, with tier and spend side by side so a high-value member is clear without a separate report.

Then export: once a trigger fires, both sides enter a jointly confirmed transition, member and transaction data are a key item exported as full tables in the agreed format, and that row in the six-item checklist is accepted item by item. Transaction records are kept for no less than three years, matching Article 31 of the E-Commerce Law; local copies are cleared except where retention is required by law, with no disclosure to third parties. The ownership close: data definitions remain yours, content assets and the store account transfer by the checklist, and fees stop at the amount actually incurred on the handover date. Across four pages, who owns the account, who sees what, what leaves at exit and against which acceptance criteria it is checked all line up in the same tables and the same checklist.

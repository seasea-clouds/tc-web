NHC rules govern the safety assessment and licensing of new food ingredients, promulgated in 2013 and revised in 2017.

Importers of new food ingredients must submit, in addition to standard materials, a marketing certificate from the exporting country and manufacturer certification.

Review includes expert assessment, on-site verification and social stability risk assessment; equivalent products may end review, with one-to-three-year bans for violations.

(Promulgated on 31 May 2013 by Order No. 1 of the former National Health and Family Planning Commission, effective from 1 October 2013; revised on 26 December 2017 under the Decision of the National Health and Family Planning Commission on Amending Seven Departmental Rules including these Measures, Order No. 18)

Article 1 These Measures are formulated to regulate the review of safety assessment materials for new food ingredients under the Food Safety Law and its implementing regulations.

Article 2 New food ingredients are items without a traditional history of consumption in China: (i) animals, plants and microorganisms; (ii) components isolated from animals, plants and microorganisms; (iii) food ingredients whose original structure has been altered; and (iv) other newly developed food ingredients.

Article 3 New food ingredients shall possess the characteristics of food ingredients, meet nutritional requirements, be non-toxic and harmless, and cause no acute, sub-acute, chronic or other potential harm to human health.

Article 4 New food ingredients may be used in food production and operation only after safety review by the NHC.

Article 5 The NHC is responsible for reviewing and licensing safety assessment materials for new food ingredients. The NHC technical review institution for new food ingredients (the review institution) conducts technical safety review and proposes comprehensive conclusions and recommendations.

Article 6 Entities or individuals intending to produce, use or import new food ingredients (applicants) shall apply and submit: (i) an application form; (ii) a development report; (iii) a safety assessment report; (iv) production processes; (v) applicable standards (including safety requirements, quality specifications and test methods); (vi) labels and instructions; (vii) domestic and international research and use data and related safety assessment materials; and (viii) other materials useful for review. One unopened product sample or 30 grams of the raw material shall also be attached.

Article 7 Applicants for importing new food ingredients shall, in addition to the materials under Article 6, submit: (i) proof issued by the competent authority of the exporting country or region that the product is permitted to be produced or sold there; and (ii) proof of review or certification of the manufacturer issued by a relevant institution or organisation in the country or region where the manufacturer is located.

Article 8 Applicants shall submit materials truthfully, are responsible for their authenticity and bear legal liability.

Article 9 When submitting items (ii) to (vi) of the first paragraph of Article 6, applicants shall indicate the content that does not involve trade secrets and may be disclosed to the public.

Article 10 After accepting an application, the NHC shall solicit public comments.

Article 11 Within 60 days of acceptance, the NHC shall organise experts to review the safety assessment materials and issue a review conclusion.

Article 12 Where supplementary materials are needed during review, the applicant shall be notified in writing and shall provide them promptly. The applicant may be asked to answer technical questions on site and shall cooperate.

Article 13 Where on-site verification of production processes is needed, experts may be organised to verify the development and production site and issue an opinion, for which they bear responsibility. Provincial health supervision institutions shall cooperate. Experts participating in on-site verification shall not take part in the review vote on the product.

Article 14 Specific procedures for review and licensing shall follow the Administrative Licensing Law, the Measures for Health Administrative Licensing and other relevant laws and regulations.

Article 15 The comprehensive review conclusion of the review institution shall include the safety review result and the social stability risk assessment result.

Article 16 The NHC shall grant and announce a licence for ingredients meeting food safety requirements, and refuse with written reasons those that do not. For ingredients substantially equivalent to food or already announced new food ingredients, the review shall be terminated and the applicant notified in writing.

Article 17 Announcements may include, depending on the characteristics of the ingredient: (i) name; (ii) source; (iii) production process; (iv) main components; (v) quality specifications; (vi) labelling requirements; and (vii) other content requiring announcement.

Article 18 The NHC shall promptly organise re-review of announced new food ingredients where: (i) doubts arise as to their safety due to scientific development; (ii) evidence suggests possible safety problems; or (iii) other circumstances warrant re-review. The NHC may revoke the licence for ingredients failing re-review.

Article 19 Manufacturers shall produce new food ingredients in accordance with the announcement requirements and ensure their food safety.

Article 20 Labels of foods containing new food ingredients shall comply with national laws, regulations, food safety standards and NHC announcement requirements.

Article 21 Production or use of new food ingredients without safety assessment in violation of these Measures shall be handled under the Food Safety Law.

Article 22 Where an applicant conceals facts or provides false materials, the NHC shall refuse to accept or licence the application, issue a warning, and bar reapplication for one year. Where a licence was obtained by deception or bribery, the NHC shall revoke it and bar reapplication for three years.

Article 23 Definitions: substantial equivalence means that a newly declared ingredient is identical to food or an announced new food ingredient in species, source, biological characteristics, main components, edible parts, usage, scope and target population, with basically consistent processes and quality requirements, and may be regarded as equally safe. Traditional consumption history means a food with more than 30 years of production and operation history as packaged or unpackaged food within a province-level region, and not recorded in the Pharmacopoeia of the People's Republic of China.

Article 24 New food ingredients under these Measures exclude genetically modified foods, health foods and new varieties of food additives, which shall be managed under other national laws and regulations.

Article 25 These Measures shall take effect on 1 October 2013. The Measures for the Administration of New Resource Foods promulgated by the former Ministry of Health on 2 July 2007 shall be repealed simultaneously.

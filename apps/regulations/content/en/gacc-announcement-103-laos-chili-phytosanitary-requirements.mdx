Yes, they can enter. Announcement No. 103 admits chilies from Laos: the Lao side approves plantations and packing houses, the Chinese side reviews, registers and publishes the list; fresh chilies must be fumigated and then cold treated, and shipping to China follows only once registration and the certificate are in place.

For a consignment, check the Chinese registration numbers of the plantation and packing house and the monitoring records; a Lao phytosanitary certificate travels with the goods, its additional declaration carrying the full protocol sentence; in the first year two per cent of each lot is sampled and the rate falls to one per cent if no problems arise; every box and pallet is marked in Chinese or English with the destination statement.

At the Chinese port of entry, China Customs checks the certificate, marks and seal and carries out inspection and quarantine. Chilies from unregistered plantations or packing houses are denied entry; lots carrying live pests of concern to China, plant residues or soil are returned, destroyed or treated; lots failing Chinese food safety standards are returned or destroyed.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic (hereinafter the Lao side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of chilies from Laos to China, chilies from Laos meeting the following requirements are permitted for import into China from the date of this Announcement.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) The Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) The Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(c) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(d) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Lao People's Democratic Republic on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Chilies from Laos to China.

II. Permitted Commodity

For the purposes of this Announcement, chilies (Capsium spp.) means the fruit of chili plants grown and processed in Laos for human consumption, including fresh and dried fruit.

III. Registration of Establishments

Plantations exporting chilies to China (hereinafter plantations) and packing houses shall be examined by the Lao side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information, including names, addresses and registration numbers, shall allow accurate tracing when a consignment does not comply with this Announcement. Before trade begins, the Lao side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side which, after review and approval, shall publish it on the GACC website and update it regularly.

IV. Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(a) Fresh chilies.

(1) Spiralling whitefly Aleurodicus dispersus

(2) Cotton mealybug Phenacoccus solenopsis

(3) Guava fruit fly Bactrocera correcta

(4) Chili fruit fly Bactrocera latifrons

(5) Tomato looper Chrysodeixis chalcites

(6) Chili leaf spot Cercospora capsici

(b) Dried chilies.

(1) Khapra beetle Trogoderma granarium

(2) Chili leaf spot Cercospora capsici

(3) Dodder Cuscuta spp.

V. Pre-export Management

(a) Plantation management.

(1) Plantations shall, under the supervision of the Lao side, establish sound quality management and traceability systems, apply good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain plantation hygiene such as keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting chili production and promptly removing diseased plant debris, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including pest monitoring and surveys, physical, chemical or biological control and cultural practices.

(2) Plantations shall, under the supervision of the Lao side, carry out pest monitoring and integrated management targeted at the quarantine pests of concern to China. Monitoring and control shall be conducted under the guidance of qualified technical personnel, who shall be trained by the Lao side or its authorised institutions.

(3) Plantations shall retain pest monitoring and control records for at least 2 years and provide them to the Chinese side on request. Such records shall include at least the monitoring dates, the names of pests found, the control measures taken and the application dates, active ingredients and concentrations of the chemicals used.

(4) For spiralling whitefly and cotton mealybug, plantations shall monitor during the growing season once every 2 weeks, focusing on branches, stems, leaves and fruit. Where pests or their symptoms are found, control measures including chemical or biological control shall be applied promptly to contain the pests or keep their prevalence low.

(5) For guava fruit fly, chili fruit fly and tomato looper, plantations shall conduct visual and trapping monitoring during the chili growing season, using fruit fly pheromone traps and yellow sticky traps. Once a fruit fly is detected, effective control shall be carried out.

(6) For chili leaf spot, plantation management shall be strengthened to keep the plantation well ventilated and reduce disease incidence, and control shall be applied promptly where disease is found.

(b) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of chilies exported to China shall take place under the quarantine supervision of the Lao side or its authorised officers.

(2) Packing houses for chilies exported to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors and separate areas for raw materials and finished products.

(3) The storage, processing, packaging and handling areas shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and shall be separated from living quarters by appropriate measures and distance.

(4) During processing, chilies shall be manually sorted, screened and washed so that they are free from insects, mites, snails, diseased or rotten fruit, weed seeds, stems, leaves and other plant residues and soil.

(5) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system so that chilies exported to China can be traced back to registered plantations, recording the processing and packaging dates, the name or registration number of the source plantation, quantities, export dates, export quantities, the country of destination and the transport means or container numbers.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging material shall be clean, hygienic and unused and shall comply with the relevant Chinese phytosanitary requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each box shall bear, in Chinese or in English, traceable information such as the product name, country, place of origin, and the names or registration numbers of the plantation and packing house. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or in English with “Destined for the People's Republic of China” or “Exported to the People's Republic of China”.

(3) The transport means or containers carrying chilies exported to China shall be checked at loading for satisfactory hygiene. The transport means or containers shall be sealed, and the seals shall remain intact on arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(d) Quarantine treatment measures.

For guava fruit fly, chili fruit fly and tomato looper, fresh chilies exported to China shall undergo quarantine treatment under the supervision of the Lao side, which shall first fumigate the consignment and then apply cold treatment. The treatment specifications are as follows:

(1) Fumigation: at a fruit core temperature of not lower than 22 °C, apply methyl bromide at 32 g/m3 for 2 hours (the concentration before the end of fumigation shall not fall below 18 g/m3), or at 18 g/m3 for 5 hours (the concentration before the end of fumigation shall not fall below 13 g/m3).

(2) Cold treatment: hold the fruit core temperature at 0.56 to 2.77 °C for 4 days, or at 3.33 to 8.33 °C for 11 days.

(3) Where in-transit cold treatment is used, the Lao side shall monitor the fruit core temperature in the refrigerated transport means before export and confirm compliance. The fruit core temperature in transit shall meet the cold treatment specifications above, and in-transit temperature records shall be retained.

(e) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) Before export, the Lao side shall sample 2% of each lot of chilies exported to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within 1 year, the sampling rate may be reduced to 1%.

(2) Where quarantine pests of concern to China, plant residues or soil are found, the entire lot shall not be exported to China. The Lao side shall identify the cause and take corrective measures, and shall retain the detection records and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(f) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For chilies that pass inspection, the Lao side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), stating the names or registration numbers of the plantation and packing house and the quarantine treatment conditions applied to the lot, and shall enter the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of chilies from Lao PDR to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (This consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol on Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Chilies from Laos to China and is free from the quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) Before trade begins, the Lao side shall provide a specimen phytosanitary certificate to the Chinese side for record and verification.

VI. Entry Inspection, Quarantine and Non-compliance

On arrival at the Chinese port of entry, chilies exported to China shall be inspected and quarantined by China Customs in accordance with the following requirements.

(a) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verify that the phytosanitary certificate complies with subparagraphs (d) and (f) of Section V of this Announcement.

(2) Verify that the marks on the boxes or wooden packaging comply with subparagraph (c) of Section V of this Announcement.

(b) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) On arrival at the Chinese port of entry, Customs shall check the documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws, regulations and national standards and this Announcement. Lots that pass inspection and quarantine shall be allowed entry.

(c) Non-compliance.

(1) Where chilies are found to come from unregistered plantations or packing houses, the lot shall be denied entry.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to China or other quarantine pests, plant residues or soil are found, the lot shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(3) Where chilies fail to comply with Chinese food safety laws, regulations and national standards, the lot shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

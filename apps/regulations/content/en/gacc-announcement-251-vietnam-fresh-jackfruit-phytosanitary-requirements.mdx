Announcement No. 251 admits fresh jackfruits from Vietnam to China from 1 June 2026. Orchards and packing houses are audited by Vietnam and registered by China; 9 quarantine pests of concern to China are controlled by name.

Orchards are monitored year-round under ISPM 6, and scale insects are checked at least every 15 days from flowering to harvest; packing houses hand-clean the fruit, boxes and pallets are marked for the People's Republic of China, containers are sealed; sampling is 2% per lot, cut to 1% after a problem-free year, and certificates under ISPM 12 carry the English additional declaration.

At the port, China Customs checks the permit, certificate and marks; goods from unregistered orchards or packing houses are denied entry, lots with quarantine pests, soil or plant residues are returned, destroyed or disinfested, and lots failing food safety standards are returned or destroyed.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions of the Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam (hereinafter referred to as the Vietnamese side) on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh jackfruits from Vietnam to China, fresh jackfruits from Vietnam that meet the following relevant requirements are permitted for import into China.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(a) The Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(b) The Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry-Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(c) The Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(d) The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of the Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(e) The Protocol between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on the Phytosanitary Requirements for the Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Vietnam to China.

II. Name of the Commodity Permitted for Import

Fresh jackfruits (hereinafter referred to as jackfruits) have the scientific name Artocarpus heterophyllus and the English name Jackfruit.

III. Permitted Places of Origin

Jackfruit production areas in Vietnam.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses supplying jackfruits to China shall be examined by the Vietnamese side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. The registration information, covering the name, address and registration number, allows accurate tracing where exported consignments fail to comply with this Announcement. The Vietnamese side shall provide the registration list to the Chinese side; after review and approval by the Chinese side, it shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs and updated regularly.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to the Chinese Side

(1) Three-banded fruit fly Bactrocera umbrosa

(2) Lepelley's grey mealybug Dysmicoccus lepelleyi

(3) Grey pineapple mealybug Dysmicoccus neobrevipes

(4) Yellow-winged pyralid moth Glyphodes caesalis

(5) South Seas mealybug Planococcus lilacinus

(6) Pacific mealybug Planococcus minor

(7) West African mealybug Rastrococcus invadens

(8) Spiny mealybug Rastrococcus spinosus

(9) Star wax scale Vinsonia stellifera

VI. Pre-export Management

(a) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards supplying jackfruits to China shall, under the supervision of the Vietnamese side, establish sound quality management and traceability systems. Good agricultural practices (GAP) shall be applied to keep the orchards hygienic, for example by keeping the surroundings free of pollution sources affecting fruit production and promptly clearing fallen and rotten fruit, and integrated pest management (IPM) shall be implemented, including regular pest monitoring surveys, physical, chemical or biological pest control, and control measures such as farming operations.

(2) In accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 6 (ISPM 6), the Vietnamese side shall draw up a management plan for the quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side and organise orchard monitoring throughout the year. In addition to visual inspection, various physical or chemical methods shall be used to trap and monitor the pests. For the three-banded fruit fly, pheromone traps for fruit flies shall be hung in the orchards and yellow sticky traps shall be used for physical monitoring. For the yellow-winged pyralid moth, physical monitoring shall be carried out with light traps or yellow sticky traps. For scale insects and other pests, orchard monitoring shall be carried out from flowering to harvest at least once every 15 days, examining the fruit, stems and leaves for scale insects.

(3) Where pests or their symptoms are found during monitoring, integrated management measures including physical, chemical or biological control shall be adopted to ensure that jackfruits supplied to China are free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side. Such integrated measures against those pests shall be approved by the Vietnamese side and, before the trade begins, provided by the Vietnamese side to the Chinese side on request.

(4) Pest monitoring and control shall be carried out under the guidance of professional technical personnel, and the Vietnamese side or training institutions authorised by the Vietnamese side shall train such personnel.

(5) The Vietnamese side shall retain records of orchard pest monitoring and control and provide them to the Chinese side when needed. The control records shall include the names of the chemical agents used during the growing season, the active ingredients, and the application dosage and timing.

(b) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packaging, storage and loading of jackfruits supplied to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Vietnamese side or of personnel authorised by the Vietnamese side.

(2) Packing houses for jackfruits supplied to China shall be clean and hygienic, with hardened floors and separate areas for raw materials and finished products.

(3) The functional areas for storage, processing and handling of jackfruits supplied to China shall be relatively independent and rationally laid out, and shall be isolated from living areas by appropriate measures and distance.

(4) During packing, jackfruits supplied to China shall be manually selected, graded and cleaned to remove diseased, insect-damaged, rotten and deformed fruit, twigs and leaves or other plant residues, and soil. The fruit surface shall be effectively cleaned by blowing, spraying and brushing with high-pressure air or water guns, and where necessary the outside of the jackfruits shall be wiped by hand with a fine, soft and clean cotton cloth, so as to effectively remove insects, eggs and pathogenic spores attached to the fruit surface.

(5) Packed jackfruits that need to be stored shall be moved into the warehouse immediately and stored separately to avoid renewed infestation by pests.

(6) Containers holding jackfruits supplied to China shall be checked at loading for good hygienic conditions. Containers shall be sealed, and the seals shall remain intact on arrival at the Chinese port of entry.

(7) Registered packing houses shall establish a traceability system so that jackfruits supplied to China can be traced back to the registered orchards, recording the processing and packing date, the name or registration number of the source orchard, the quantity, the export date, the quantity exported, the country of destination and the container number.

(c) Packaging requirements.

(1) Packaging materials shall be clean, hygienic and unused, and shall meet the relevant Chinese plant quarantine and hygiene requirements. Where wooden packaging is used, it shall comply with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) Each box shall be marked in Chinese or English with information such as the fruit name, the exporting country, the place of origin (province, city), and the names or registration numbers of the orchard and the packing house. Each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with “Exported to the People’s Republic of China”.

(d) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The Vietnamese side or its authorised bodies shall sample and inspect 2% of every lot of jackfruits supplied to China. If no phytosanitary problems occur within one year, the sampling rate shall be reduced to 1%.

(2) Where quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side, twigs and leaves or soil are found, the whole lot shall not be exported to China and, as appropriate, the export of jackfruits to China from the orchard or packing house concerned shall be suspended for the remainder of the export season. The Vietnamese side shall identify the causes and take corrective measures, and shall keep the detection records and provide them to the Chinese side on request.

(e) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

For consignments that pass quarantine inspection, the Vietnamese side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM 12), stating the names or registration numbers of the orchard and the packing house, and containing the following additional declaration: “This consignment complies with the requirements specified in the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Fresh Jackfruits from Vietnam to China, and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.” (this consignment complies with the Protocol on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of fresh jackfruits from Vietnam to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side).

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine and Treatment of Non-conformities

On arrival at a Chinese port of entry, jackfruits supplied to China shall be inspected and quarantined by China Customs in accordance with the following requirements.

(a) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verification whether the imported jackfruits have obtained the Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verification whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of item (e) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verification whether the marks on the boxes and pallets comply with the provisions of item (c) of Section VI of this Announcement.

(b) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Jackfruits supplied to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruit.

(2) When jackfruits arrive at a Chinese port of entry, China Customs shall examine the relevant documents and marks and carry out inspection and quarantine in accordance with Chinese laws and regulations, national standards and this Announcement.

(c) Treatment of non-conformities.

(1) Where jackfruits are found to come from orchards or packing houses not approved by the Chinese side, the consignment shall be denied entry.

(2) Where live quarantine pests of concern to the Chinese side or other quarantine pests newly occurring in Vietnam, or soil, plant residues or the like, are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed or subjected to disinfestation treatment.

(3) Where the consignment is found not to comply with Chinese national food safety laws, regulations and standards, it shall be returned or destroyed.

This Announcement shall enter into force on 1 June 2026. It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

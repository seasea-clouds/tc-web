Cross-border retail imports are supervised as personal-use inbound articles: direct-purchase imports and goods under the online shopping bonded import model (supervision code 1210) need no first-import licence, registration or filing, except suspended epidemic-area goods and goods under emergency risk handling.

Entities and documents come first: platform, logistics and payment enterprises register locally, overseas e-commerce firms act through a domestic agent; before declaration they transmit transaction, payment and logistics data; imports clear on list verification, exports on list verification plus consolidated declaration.

Tax and returns are the core: the consumer is the taxpayer, tax collected and remitted by the platform, logistics or declaring enterprise; if no return within 30 days of release, tax is paid between the 31st and 45th days; original-condition returns within 30 days are untaxed and the annual quota restored, expired or unsellable goods are shipped back or destroyed.

To regulate cross-border e-commerce retail imports and exports and promote the sound and orderly development of cross-border e-commerce, the General Administration of Customs hereby announces the following supervision matters in accordance with the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China, the Law of the People's Republic of China on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine, the Law of the People's Republic of China on Import and Export Commodity Inspection, the E-Commerce Law of the People's Republic of China and other laws and regulations, and the Notice of the Ministry of Commerce, the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Finance, the General Administration of Customs, the State Taxation Administration and the State Administration for Market Regulation on Improving the Supervision of Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Imports (Shang Cai Fa [2018] No. 486) and other relevant national policies on cross-border e-commerce retail imports and exports:

I. Scope of Application

(1) Where cross-border e-commerce enterprises and consumers (purchasers) conclude retail import and export transactions through cross-border e-commerce trading platforms and transmit the relevant electronic transaction data as required by customs, they shall be supervised by customs under this Announcement.

II. Enterprise Administration

(2) Enterprises participating in cross-border e-commerce retail import business, such as cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises, logistics enterprises and payment enterprises, shall register with the customs at their location in accordance with the provisions on the registration and administration of customs declaration entities; an overseas cross-border e-commerce enterprise shall entrust a domestic agent (hereinafter referred to as the domestic agent of the cross-border e-commerce enterprise) to register with the customs at the agent's location.

Enterprises participating in cross-border e-commerce retail export business, such as cross-border e-commerce enterprises and logistics enterprises, shall file information with the customs at their location; where customs declaration business is required, they shall register with the customs at their location.

Logistics enterprises shall obtain the Express Business Operation Licence issued by the national postal administration department. Under the direct-purchase import model, the logistics enterprise shall be a postal enterprise or an operator of inbound and outbound express items that has completed the customs agency declaration registration formalities.

Where the payment enterprise is a banking institution, it shall hold the Financial Licence issued by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission or the former China Banking Regulatory Commission; where the payment enterprise is a non-bank payment institution, it shall hold the Payment Business Licence issued by the People's Bank of China, and its payment business scope shall include “internet payment”.

(3) Enterprises participating in cross-border e-commerce retail import and export business and registered with customs shall be included in customs credit management, and customs shall apply differentiated customs clearance measures according to their credit ratings.

III. Customs Clearance Administration

(4) Cross-border e-commerce direct-purchase imported goods and goods subject to the import policy of “online shopping bonded import” (supervision code 1210) shall be supervised as personal-use inbound articles, and the requirements for first-import licences, registration or filing shall not apply. This does not apply to goods from epidemic areas that the relevant departments have ordered suspended from import, or when emergency risk handling is initiated for goods presenting major quality and safety risks.

Goods subject to the import policy of “online shopping bonded import A” (supervision code 1239) shall be handled in accordance with the supervision requirements in the footnotes of the List of Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Import Goods (2018 Edition).

(5) Customs shall quarantine cross-border e-commerce retail imports and exports together with their containers and packaging in accordance with relevant laws and regulations, and apply necessary supervision measures as required.

(6) Before cross-border e-commerce retail imports are declared, the cross-border e-commerce platform enterprise or the domestic agent of the cross-border e-commerce enterprise, the payment enterprise and the logistics enterprise shall respectively transmit electronic information on transactions, payments and logistics to customs through the international “Single Window” or the cross-border e-commerce customs clearance service platform, and shall bear corresponding responsibility for the authenticity of the data.

Under the direct-purchase import model, postal enterprises and operators of inbound and outbound express items may, entrusted by the cross-border e-commerce platform enterprise or the domestic agent of the cross-border e-commerce enterprise and the payment enterprise, and on the premise of undertaking corresponding legal liability, transmit electronic information on transactions, payments and other matters to customs.

(7) Before cross-border e-commerce retail exports are declared, the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or its agent and the logistics enterprise shall respectively transmit electronic information on transactions, receipts and logistics to customs through the international “Single Window” or the cross-border e-commerce customs clearance service platform, and shall bear corresponding legal liability for the authenticity of the data.

(8) When cross-border e-commerce retail goods are imported, the domestic agent of the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or the customs declaration enterprise entrusted by it shall submit the Declaration List of Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Import and Export Goods of the Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Declaration List) and complete customs declaration formalities by the method of “release upon verification of the list”.

When cross-border e-commerce retail goods are exported, the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or its agent shall submit the Declaration List and complete customs declaration formalities by the method of “release upon verification of the list and consolidated declaration”; for exports of cross-border e-commerce retail goods meeting the conditions within comprehensive cross-border e-commerce pilot zones, the method of “release upon verification of the list and consolidated statistics” may be adopted.

The Declaration List shall have the same legal effect as the Customs Declaration Form for Import (Export) Goods of the People's Republic of China.

Electronic information transmitted and submitted as required by items (6) to (8) above shall bear an electronic signature.

(9) Cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises and domestic agents of cross-border e-commerce enterprises engaged in cross-border e-commerce retail import business shall verify the authenticity of transactions and of the identity information of consumers (purchasers) and bear corresponding responsibility; where the identity information has not been authenticated by a national competent department or an institution authorized by it, the purchaser and the payer shall be the same person.

(10) After cross-border e-commerce retail goods are exported, the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or its agent shall, before the 15th day of each month (where the 15th day is a statutory holiday or statutory rest day, the deadline shall be postponed to the first working day thereafter), consolidate the Declaration Lists closed in the preceding month into a Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods of the People's Republic of China and declare it to customs, according to the rules of the same consignor or consignee, the same mode of transport, the same production and sales entity, the same country of arrival and the same customs office of exit on the list header, and the same country of final destination, the same 10-digit customs commodity code and the same currency on the list body.

Where customs declaration formalities may be completed by the method of “release upon verification of the list and consolidated statistics”, no consolidated Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods of the People's Republic of China shall be formed.

(11) Amendment or cancellation of the Declaration List shall be handled with reference to the relevant provisions of customs on the amendment or cancellation of the Customs Declaration Form for Import (Export) Goods of the People's Republic of China.

Unless under special circumstances, the Declaration List and the Customs Declaration Form for Import (Export) Goods of the People's Republic of China shall be declared by paperless customs clearance operations.

IV. Tax Collection and Administration

(12) For cross-border e-commerce retail imports, customs shall levy customs duty, import value-added tax and consumption tax in accordance with the national tax policies on cross-border e-commerce retail imports; the dutiable value shall be the actual transaction price, including the retail price of the goods, freight and insurance.

(13) The consumer (purchaser) of cross-border e-commerce retail imports shall be the taxpayer. Cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises, logistics enterprises or declaring enterprises registered with customs shall act as the tax collection and remittance agents, perform the tax payment obligation on behalf of the taxpayer, and bear the corresponding obligation to pay additional tax and related legal liability.

(14) Tax collection and remittance agents shall truthfully and accurately declare to customs the tax administration elements of cross-border e-commerce retail imports, such as the name of the goods, specifications and models, tariff heading, actual transaction price and related expenses.

The declaration currency for cross-border e-commerce retail imports shall be the renminbi.

(15) To examine and determine the classification, dutiable value and other matters of cross-border e-commerce retail imports, customs may require tax collection and remittance agents to make supplementary declarations in accordance with relevant provisions.

(16) Customs shall, for cross-border e-commerce retail imports that comply with the supervision provisions, collect tax on a consolidated basis by period, and tax collection and remittance agents shall submit sufficient and valid security for tax payments to customs in accordance with law.

Where no return or amendment or cancellation of the declaration occurs within 30 days after customs release, the tax collection and remittance agent shall complete tax payment formalities with customs from the 31st to the 45th day after release.

V. Site Administration

(17) The customs supervision operation sites for cross-border e-commerce retail imports and exports must comply with relevant customs provisions. Operators of cross-border e-commerce customs supervision operation sites and warehousing enterprises shall establish computer management systems meeting customs supervision requirements and exchange electronic data as required by customs. Among them, supervision operation sites conducting cross-border e-commerce direct-purchase import or general export business shall be established according to the standards for express or postal customs supervision operation sites.

(18) Cross-border e-commerce online shopping bonded import business shall be conducted within special customs supervision areas or bonded logistics centers (Type B). Unless otherwise provided, it shall be supervised with reference to this Announcement.

VI. Quarantine, Inspection and Logistics Administration

(19) Quarantine and quarantine treatment required at the port of entry shall be completed before the goods are transported to the cross-border e-commerce supervision operation site.

(20) For online shopping bonded import business, goods entering the area at the first line shall be declared by customs declaration form; customs may strengthen physical supervision of online shopping bonded imported goods by means of video monitoring, networked verification, on-site inspection, inventory checking and the like.

(21) When customs conducts inspection, the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or its agent, the operator of the cross-border e-commerce supervision operation site and the warehousing enterprise shall provide convenience and cooperate with customs inspection in accordance with relevant provisions.

(22) Cross-border e-commerce retail imports and exports may be transited under the “cross-border e-commerce” model. In particular, the customs at the location of a comprehensive cross-border e-commerce pilot zone may enter the names of the transited goods in the master waybill as “a batch of cross-border e-commerce goods”, and a detailed electronic list of the transited goods shall be attached.

(23) Online shopping bonded imported goods may circulate between special customs supervision areas or bonded logistics centers (Type B), with circulation formalities handled in accordance with relevant provisions. Goods entering under the customs supervision code “online shopping bonded import” (supervision code 1210) shall not be transferred to cities subject to “online shopping bonded import A” (supervision code 1239) to continue cross-border e-commerce retail import business. Online shopping bonded imported goods may circulate among enterprises within the same area (center).

VII. Return Administration

(24) Under the cross-border e-commerce retail import model, the domestic agent of the cross-border e-commerce enterprise or the customs declaration enterprise entrusted by it may apply for return; the returned goods shall meet the requirements for resale and be transported in original condition to the original supervision operation site within 30 days from the date of customs release, the corresponding tax shall not be levied, and the cumulative annual transaction amount of the individual shall be adjusted.

Under the cross-border e-commerce retail export model, returned goods shall be handled in accordance with relevant provisions.

(25) Cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods that are unsuitable for domestic sale, such as those beyond the shelf life or validity period, goods or packaging damaged, or goods not conforming to the relevant supervision policies of China, as well as cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods ordered by customs to be returned, shall be shipped back out of the country or destroyed in accordance with relevant provisions.

VIII. Other Matters

(26) Enterprises engaged in cross-border e-commerce retail import and export business shall transmit true electronic data and electronic information related to their business to customs in real time, open information sharing interfaces such as real-time logistics tracking, strengthen information and data support for customs risk prevention and control, and cooperate with customs in effective administration.

Cross-border e-commerce enterprises and their agents and cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises shall establish risk prevention and control mechanisms for commodity quality and safety, strengthen monitoring of commodity quality and safety as well as abnormal transactions such as false transactions and resale, and take corresponding handling measures.

Cross-border e-commerce enterprises shall not import or export goods that endanger port public health safety, biosafety, the safety of imported and exported food and commodities, goods that infringe intellectual property rights, or other prohibited or restricted goods, and shall establish and improve commodity traceability mechanisms and bear the main responsibility for quality and safety. Cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises are encouraged to establish and improve self-regulatory supervision systems for the safety of imported and exported goods.

Consumers (purchasers) shall not resell cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods they have purchased.

(27) Customs shall conduct quality and safety risk monitoring of cross-border e-commerce retail imports, order the relevant enterprises to take risk mitigation measures for goods that are unqualified or present quality and safety problems, supervise goods not yet sold as goods, and pursue the responsibility of the relevant business entities in accordance with law; for high-risk goods found in monitoring, customs shall issue risk warnings and take corresponding control measures. Customs shall, before the sale of cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods, carry out necessary quarantine in accordance with laws and regulations and issue risk warnings as appropriate.

(28) Cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises, cross-border e-commerce enterprises or their agents, logistics enterprises, operators of cross-border e-commerce supervision operation sites and warehousing enterprises that discover suspected violations or smuggling shall promptly and proactively inform customs.

(29) Enterprises participating in cross-border e-commerce business that are suspected of smuggling or violating customs supervision provisions shall cooperate with customs investigations and open transaction and production data or original record data.

Customs shall, in accordance with law, penalize enterprises that violate this Announcement by participating in the creation or transmission of false transaction, payment and logistics “three-document” information, facilitating resale, failing to diligently verify the authenticity of consumer (purchaser) identity information, or otherwise causing personal identity information or annual purchase quotas to be misappropriated, resale to occur, or other violations of customs supervision provisions. Suspected smuggling or violations shall be handled by customs in accordance with law; where a crime is constituted, criminal liability shall be pursued in accordance with law. Where other citizens' identity information is used illegally to engage in cross-border e-commerce retail import business, customs shall treat it as smuggling or a violation and transfer it to the relevant departments in accordance with the legal provisions on illegal use of citizens' information. Where no smuggling or violation is suspected and it is the first discovery, customs shall conduct a regulatory talk or suspend the business and order rectification; where discovered again, the enterprise shall not be permitted to engage in cross-border e-commerce retail import business for a certain period, and the matter shall be referred to other competent industry departments for investigation and handling as provided.

(30) Cross-border e-commerce enterprises and their domestic agents, cross-border e-commerce platform enterprises, payment enterprises and logistics enterprises registered with customs shall accept customs audits and inspections.

(31) The meanings of the terms used in this Announcement are as follows:

“Cross-border e-commerce enterprise” means an overseas-registered enterprise that sells cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods from overseas to domestic consumers (excluding enterprises registered within special customs supervision areas or bonded logistics centers), or an enterprise that sells cross-border e-commerce retail exported goods from within China to overseas consumers, and is the owner of the title to the goods.

“Domestic agent of a cross-border e-commerce enterprise” means the domestic agency enterprise entrusted by an overseas-registered enterprise conducting cross-border e-commerce retail import business, which registers with customs, bears the responsibility for truthful declaration, accepts supervision by relevant departments in accordance with law, and bears civil liability.

“Cross-border e-commerce platform enterprise” means an operator that has completed industrial and commercial registration within China, provides both parties to a transaction (consumers and cross-border e-commerce enterprises) with webpage space, virtual business premises, transaction rules, information publishing and other services, and establishes an information network system for both parties to independently conduct transactions.

“Payment enterprise” means a bank, non-bank payment institution, UnionPay or similar that has completed industrial and commercial registration within China and, entrusted by a cross-border e-commerce platform enterprise or the domestic agent of a cross-border e-commerce enterprise, provides cross-border e-commerce retail import payment services.

“Logistics enterprise” means an enterprise that has completed industrial and commercial registration within China and, entrusted by a cross-border e-commerce platform enterprise, a cross-border e-commerce enterprise or its agent, provides cross-border e-commerce retail import and export logistics services.

“Consumer (purchaser)” means the domestic buyer of cross-border e-commerce retail imported goods.

“International ‘Single Window’” means the one-stop trade service platform built on the public platform of the electronic port under the coordination of the inter-ministerial joint conference on port work of the State Council. Declarants (including enterprises participating in cross-border e-commerce) declare once to customs and other port administration departments through the Single Window; the port administration departments share information and data through the electronic port platform, exercise functional administration, and feed back enforcement results to declarants through the Single Window.

“Cross-border e-commerce customs clearance service platform” means the platform built by the electronic port to realize data exchange and information sharing among enterprises, customs and relevant administration departments.

Cities under the import policy of “online shopping bonded import” (supervision code 1210): Tianjin, Shanghai, Chongqing, Dalian, Hangzhou, Ningbo, Qingdao, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Chengdu, Suzhou, Hefei, Fuzhou, Zhengzhou, Pingtan, Beijing, Hohhot, Shenyang, Changchun, Harbin, Nanjing, Nanchang, Wuhan, Changsha, Nanning, Haikou, Guiyang, Kunming, Xi'an, Lanzhou, Xiamen, Tangshan, Wuxi, Weihai, Zhuhai, Dongguan and Yiwu, 37 cities (regions) in total.

(32) This Announcement shall come into force on January 1, 2019; the time of entry into force shall be the time when customs accepts the declaration of the Declaration List, and matters not covered herein shall be handled in accordance with relevant customs provisions. GACC Announcement No. 26 of 2016 shall be repealed simultaneously.

For domestic cross-border e-commerce enterprises that have already signed sales contracts, the conduct of their cross-border e-commerce retail import business may be extended to March 31, 2019.

This Announcement is hereby issued.

General Administration of Customs

December 10, 2018

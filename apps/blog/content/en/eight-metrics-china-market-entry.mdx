Entering the Chinese market, the two sides often read "how far along we are" differently, and that disagreement usually shows up earlier than any disagreement about execution. Agree on what each of the eight metrics means, how it is counted, and where the data comes from; then review monthly. Progress, risk, and delivery quality can land in one overview, and next steps stop being guesswork.

## Agree on definitions first

The same sentence, "monthly sales are up," sounds like growth to the brand and may merely restate pre-refund order value to the service provider. Without shared definitions, more numbers only mean more argument. So the first task is to write down what each of the eight metrics answers, who produces the number, and how it is reconciled each month. Targets can come later.

Once definitions are clear, targets become meaningful. Targets can be set by stage; once both sides sign the definitions, every review speaks the same language.

## The eight metrics on one table

The eight metrics cover launch, product, sales, repeat purchase, compliance, promotion, returns, and settlement. Each one states what to watch and where the data comes from. The table below puts all eight side by side and marks in the last column whether the metric enters the monthly brief.

| Metric | What to watch | Data source | Monthly brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to launch | Against the planned range, judged by conditions met | Project ledger | Yes |
| Phase-1 SKUs | All core SKUs fully admitted | Tiering table | Yes |
| Monthly GMV | Confirmed transaction value agreed by both sides | Platform data | No |
| 30-day repeat rate | Subscription and auto-ship penetration | Member system | No |
| Compliance log | Enforcement records for pyramid promotion, data, and claims | Compliance ledger | Yes |
| Promoter activity | Active count and next-month retention | Promoter system | No |
| Return processing time | Days from application to arrival | Returns ledger | No |
| Payout on time | Share executed per agreement | Reconciliation statements | Yes |

## Walk the eight metrics through the table

Stay with the demo data. July sales were 262,300 yuan, produced from platform data and confirmed by both sides at the monthly meeting as that month's GMV; this is the third metric. August's repeat rate was 24.1%, the highest in six months, matching subscription and auto-ship penetration in the member system; this is the fourth.

The fifth metric, the compliance log, does not look at sales. It looks at whether the month produced any enforcement record for pyramid-style promotion, data violations, or claim violations. If there is one, log it as it is; if there is none, still record the check. Why give this its own line? The Advertising Law sets clear limits on efficacy claims, and the way promoters are recruited has boundaries too. Once an enforcement record appears, even the prettiest sales curve gives way to remediation.

The sixth metric, promoter activity, tracks active promoters and next-month retention. The promoter system exports it monthly, and both the activity definition and the retention definition go into the definition sheet.

The seventh metric, return processing time, runs from the buyer's application to the goods arriving at the warehouse. The returns ledger records it per order, and the monthly view takes both the median and the longest case. The eighth metric, payout on time, pays each party's share into the account confirmed on the reconciliation statement and keeps the statement on file.

The second metric, Phase-1 SKUs, shows at a glance whether every core SKU in the tiering table has completed admission. Without admission, later sales figures lack an explanation, which is why this metric comes before sales. The first metric, time to launch, measures against the planned range and is judged by conditions met: the qualifications prepared, the reviews passed, and the listings live, item by item. Only then is the stage complete. Dates are reference points; conditions are the test.

## How to align the basis

Among the eight metrics, the basis of counting causes the most disagreement. Monthly GMV uses confirmed transaction value; refunds show up separately in the refund rate and do not go back to change GMV. The 30-day repeat rate starts from payment time and counts a second purchase within 30 days, with subscription and auto-ship flagged separately. Return processing time runs from application to arrival, and transit time is counted as it is. Payout on time follows the reconciliation statement, with payment date and value date recorded separately.

The basis goes into the metric definition sheet and both sides sign it. After that, each monthly meeting compares numbers and does not redefine them. Data sources are written down too: platform data, member system, promoter system, returns ledger, reconciliation statements, and project ledger, each with an owner and an export time agreed in advance.

## Who owns the data and the assets

The store, accounts, members, and data belong to the brand. Whoever acts owns the consequence, and asset ownership does not change with the stage of cooperation. Member data is also handled under the Personal Information Protection Law: collection with notice, use with boundaries, exports with records, and a complete handover when cooperation ends.

![Chapter 08 divider on delivery and protection: whoever acts owns the consequence; the store, accounts, members, and data belong to the brand; exit and handover are rehearsed in advance](/images/blog/eight-metrics-china-market-entry/chapter-08-well-protected.webp)

Exit is rehearsed in advance too: how account permissions are recovered, how member data is migrated, and how in-transit orders and after-sales are handed over. Put it in a checklist, one copy for each side. A rehearsed exit is what keeps the real handover day calm.

## Review monthly, promise by condition

The review rhythm is monthly: numbers at the start of the month, a meeting mid-month, item-by-item reconciliation. The four monthly-brief metrics come first, operating numbers second: health before scale. Each quarter brings a deeper review against the definition sheet, and any change gets a new version.

The rhythm is fixed; promises are not tied to the calendar. Launch time and review duration are judged by conditions met, not frozen dates. A date without the conditions only creates the next misunderstanding.

A jar retailing at 50 US dollars carries about 4.55 US dollars in consolidated tax. The ratio follows the order, not the shipment: goods attract no such tax while they sit in a bonded warehouse, and the tax is assessed only when a consumer places an order and the declaration list is released. The 9.1% is the consolidated burden for cross-border e-commerce retail imports, and the formula sits in Cai Guan Shui [2016] No. 18: the tariff rate is provisionally set at 0%, and import VAT and consumption tax are collected at 70% of the statutory amount. Multiply 13% by 70% and you get 9.1%. Processed foods such as protein powder and soft capsules carry no consumption tax and fall in the 13% band, so 9.1% is the whole burden.

What the rate table does not say is a different set of questions: what price the tax is computed on, who bears it, who collects it, when it is paid, and which line an order crosses before a different rule applies. The answers sit in three documents, No. 18, No. 49 and No. 486, and two execution points sit in GACC Announcement No. 194 of 2018.

## The 9.1% Is Calculated, and the Statutory VAT Rate Is Still 13%

Take the consolidated tax apart and only three layers remain: tariff, VAT and consumption tax. No. 18 sets the tariff provisionally at 0%, removes the exemption threshold for VAT and consumption tax, and collects both provisionally at 70% of the statutory amount. Consolidated tax = tariff + VAT × 70% + consumption tax × 70%. The 0% tariff has a precondition: the goods must be on the list of cross-border e-commerce retail import commodities and supervised as articles for personal use. Goods off the list go through general trade or another channel, and the tax structure changes with it. Protein powder and soft capsules, both processed foods, sit in the 13% VAT band with no consumption tax, so the formula reduces to 13% × 70% = 9.1%.

The 70% is a levy ratio, not a second tax rate. The most common miscalculation adds 13% and 70% to get 83%, or treats 9.1% as a VAT bracket. The correct reading computes the statutory amount at 13% first, then multiplies by 70% to get what is actually paid. The official examples are plain: a 100 yuan item carries 9.10 yuan in consolidated tax; at an average order value of 500 yuan, the consolidated tax is about 45.50 yuan. The figures match the formula, and anyone can redo the arithmetic on the spot.

The 9.1% applies only to categories in the 13% VAT band with no consumption tax. Goods in the 9% VAT band with no consumption tax come out at 6.3%. Products classified under a tariff line that carries consumption tax need that layer added on, and that layer cannot simply be added to 9.1%; work it out separately at that category's consumption tax rate. Which band your product lands in is decided by its tariff code, not by its product name.

Two words in No. 18 deserve attention: "provisionally set" and "provisionally collected". Both the 0% tariff and the 70% levy ratio are policy arrangements, not statutory fixtures, so keep both on the review list. The 70% levy ratio has applied since No. 18 took effect in 2016, while the two limits were raised once in 2018 by No. 49. Review the rates and the limits separately, or the two will blur into one line.

## The Tax Base Is the Order Price Plus Freight and Insurance

No. 18 provides that the dutiable value is determined by the actual transaction price, including the retail price of the goods, the freight and the insurance, all three entering the tax base together. A jar listed at 50 US dollars with 3 US dollars in freight and insurance has a dutiable value of 53 US dollars, and the consolidated tax is computed on that, not on 50 US dollars.

Converted to yuan at 7.2 yuan per US dollar, 53 US dollars is about 382 yuan, and the consolidated tax about 34.7 yuan, roughly 4.8 US dollars, for conversion reference only. Whether freight is listed separately or folded into the sale price does not change the tax base; the sale price, freight and insurance on the order must be stated truthfully, and the base is their total. Conversely, when the actual transaction price falls, the tax base falls with it. Write these three lines separately on the pricing sheet, because they are the working paper for the tax base.

## Who Pays, Who Collects, Who Backs It Up

The taxpayer is the purchaser, meaning the consumer who places the order; the tax is borne by the consumer and paid at clearance with the order. The nominal burden of all three taxes rests on the purchaser, though the tariff layer is 0 on this channel, so what is actually paid is 70% of VAT and consumption tax. Taxpayer and withholding agent are two different roles: the first bears the burden, the second files and pays, and the two must not be conflated. In the standard 1210 chain, the brand does not collect this tax and neither do we as the service provider; collection falls to the platform, logistics and customs brokerage enterprises registered with customs.

No. 18 lists e-commerce enterprises, platform enterprises and logistics enterprises as parties that may withhold and remit; No. 486 divides responsibility among the participants in more detail, with the withholding enterprise responsible for collecting and remitting and liable to make up any shortfall. This chain matters to you at both ends. At one end, the price, freight and insurance on the order must be true, because the tax base will not shrink when information is filled in wrongly. At the other, the tax never passes through the brand's account, so all you need at pricing time is a clear after-tax net figure, with no separate collection step at the payment stage.

## Four Moments in the Life of an Order

The tax follows the order, and four moments decide when it appears and when it can no longer be changed.

- Order placed: the consumer submits the order, and the purchaser and the payer must be the same person.
- Three-document cross-check: order, payment and logistics documents are pushed and reconciled; order and payment go first, and logistics data is written back at the release step.
- Customs release: the declaration list is released and the tax is counted into that consumer's annual quota.
- Days 31–45: no amendment or cancellation is accepted within 30 days of release, and tax procedures are handled on days 31 to 45.

The amounts and party details on the declaration list, order, payment document and logistics document must reconcile with one another; if they do not, customs will not release the shipment and the tax cannot be computed. After release, the tax amount goes into the ledger alongside the order number, payment number and waybill number, and reconciliation uses these reference numbers as its index.

Of the four moments, the first two settle the tax base and the parties, the third decides whose quota the tax lands on, and the fourth fixes the correction window after an error. A shipment that corresponds to a given number of orders is taxed that many times. This is also where bonded retail and general trade part ways on timing: general trade pays everything at once at import, while bonded retail defers taxation to each retail order.

## Cross 5,000 Yuan or 26,000 Yuan and the Tax Logic Changes

No. 49 set two limits: 5,000 yuan per transaction and 26,000 yuan per year. At 7.2 yuan per US dollar, that is about 700 and 3,600 US dollars. Both limits are counted per purchaser, the annual quota accumulates order by order, and the check runs at clearance. At 9.1%, a consumer's 26,000 yuan annual quota corresponds to about 2,366 yuan in consolidated tax, roughly 329 US dollars. That is the scale of one consumer's tax burden in the 9.1% band, not the brand's.

An order above 5,000 yuan that still sits within the annual quota takes one of two routes. If it contains only one unit, it may still enter through the cross-border e-commerce retail channel, with tariff, import VAT and consumption tax charged in full at goods rates, the 70% levy ratio no longer applying, and the amount counted toward the annual total. If it contains more than one item, or the annual total passes 26,000 yuan, the retail regime no longer applies and the shipment is handled as general trade.

General trade has a completely different tax structure: tariff at the applicable rate, VAT levied in full at the applicable rate on a base of dutiable value plus tariff, no 70% levy ratio, and separate rules for market access and registration. Move the same product to another channel and both the tax and the procedure must be recalculated; the 9.1% does not carry over.

The direct constraint on pricing shows up in bundles and gift boxes: a single order above 5,000 yuan with more than one item leaves the retail channel entirely. Keeping a bundle under 5,000 yuan, or splitting it into several orders, is the first step when designing a promotion. At 7.2 yuan per US dollar, 694 US dollars is the single-order ceiling; a 680-dollar bundle still leaves room, while a 750-dollar bundle has already crossed the line.

## A 50-Dollar Jar, Taxed to the Cent

Back to a specific SKU. A jar retails at 50 US dollars; the order is two jars, so the goods total 100 US dollars. Freight and insurance come to 6 US dollars, making the dutiable value 106 US dollars. At 7.2 yuan per US dollar that is about 763 yuan, and the 9.1% consolidated tax about 69.5 yuan, or roughly 9.6 US dollars, about 4.8 US dollars per jar.

A run of 1,000 such two-jar orders covers 2,000 jars, and the tax is computed order by order across 1,000 orders, not once across a 2,000-jar shipment. Each order's dutiable value depends on its own price, freight and insurance, and the tax is about 9.6 US dollars per order, or about 9,600 US dollars across 1,000 orders. The inbound bonded batch records goods value, the orders record dutiable value, and the release records record tax and quota; the three sets of books have to reconcile.

Suppose the order grows to 8 jars: goods total 400 US dollars, freight and insurance still 6 US dollars, dutiable value 406 US dollars, about 2,923 yuan, and consolidated tax about 266 yuan, roughly 36.9 US dollars. It is still under 5,000 yuan, and it takes up about 2,923 yuan of that consumer's annual quota. Push it to 14 jars: goods total 700 US dollars, plus 6 US dollars in freight and insurance, 706 US dollars, about 5,083 yuan, and the single-order limit has been crossed; with more than one item in the order, the retail channel no longer applies.

## Three Variables That Change the Result

Three variables can change the result beyond the worked examples.

- Classification and consumption tax: 9.1% holds only for categories in the 13% VAT band with no consumption tax; a 9% band classification gives 6.3%, and a tariff line carrying consumption tax adds another layer.
- Order composition: a single-item order above the limit can still use the retail channel at goods rates, while a multi-item order above the limit, or one that exceeds the annual quota, moves to general trade.
- Channel choice: general trade has no 0% tariff and no 70% levy ratio, so the burden is recalculated as tariff plus full VAT.

Of the three, classification happens first and is the easiest to get wrong through a product name; the basis for classification lies in the tariff schedule, the chapter notes and classification decisions, and the same name can take a different code when the form or the use changes. Order composition is settled during promotion design, and channel choice at project launch. Once all three are fixed, 9.1% becomes a number you can write into a pricing sheet.

## Four Common Misreadings, and Where They Go Wrong

Reading 9.1% as a VAT rate is the most common one. The statutory VAT rate is 13%; 9.1% is the actual burden after the 70% levy ratio is applied. The declaration still reports VAT and consumption tax, and there is no tax category called 9.1%.

Reading 70% as a discount or a second tax is the second. The 70% multiplies the statutory amount payable, not the product price; the formula contains only the three taxes, tariff, VAT and consumption tax, with no fourth.

Reading the 0% tariff as tax-free goods is the third. The 0% is a provisional rate for the cross-border e-commerce retail import channel; switch to general trade and the tariff applies at the applicable rate, with VAT also levied in full.

Reading the brand as the withholding agent is the fourth. Collection falls to the platform, logistics and customs brokerage enterprises registered with customs; the brand's job is to report the order price, freight and insurance accurately, and the tax does not pass through the brand's account.

The tax on an order is ultimately decided by four numbers: the VAT band and consumption tax attached to the tariff code, the order's retail price plus freight and insurance, the two limits on a single order and on the year, and whether the shipment goes through bonded retail or general trade. All four can be confirmed before an order is created, and 9.1% holds only once the first two are settled. Give these four rows a place on the pricing sheet, recalculate in the same order whenever a price changes, and the tax burden will not be miscalculated when a promotion changes the price.

A regulatory inquiry turns on whether every checkpoint has an archive number that pulls the documents back, not on how many menus the back office shows. Three documents flowing back to the customs system in real time and nine interfaces logging daily health settle whether data arrives correctly; six checkpoints each carrying five document sets, retrievable by number, settle whether the process leaves a trail. Both flows land on the same order number, so an inspection can walk back from one order to the entity document filed before the filing was made. The rule that an order, a payment record and a waybill are matched one by one before release sits in Announcement No. 486 of 2018 by six ministries including the Ministry of Commerce and in General Administration of Customs Announcement No. 194 of 2018.

## What Counts as a Correct Three-Document Callback

The information flow checkpoint verifies that three documents are sent back in real time. They reach the customs clearance system live, connected to more than 50 ports and more than 70 ERP providers; when timing is checked order by order, the order and payment are pushed first and the waybill is written back at release, and reversing that order means filing again. Data stays within the country, and a buyer placed on the controlled list must be confirmed within 15 days. The nine interfaces include order push and payment callback, with health logged daily, so one unhealthy line shows on the same day instead of surfacing at the monthly reconciliation.

The interface evidence sits in two places: the matching console shows whether the three documents pair up order by order, and the interface health log shows which of the nine interfaces dropped on which day. These are working records an inquiry uses to establish when data arrived and whether it ever dropped, not decoration for the operations team. The information flow handles getting the data right; it cannot answer for the compliance flow who signed this step.

## Walking Through the GD2609-003 Row

Take the three-document consistency check completed on 2026-09-20 as something to investigate. The row says triggered before declaration, owned by the customs role, archive number GD2609-003, documents the order, the payment record and the waybill. Pull the three documents by that archive number, then go back to the information flow for the three-document order: order and payment pushed first, waybill written back at release, and no dropped line in the interface health log that day. Only then does this row hold together.

Trace forward from the same order number: entity qualification review GD2609-001 was completed on 2026-09-18, SKU access review GD2609-002 on 2026-09-19, both completions earlier than the three-document check before declaration; trace back to checkout and the risk notice DA numbers sit in pairs with their tick times. Four steps share one order number, so one lookup finds the whole chain.

Take another case: declaration list review GD2609-004 has a blank completion time and an in-progress status. The archive number is there but the documents are not yet complete, so service can only say the work is under way, not that it is finished. An archive number is the key that pulls documents back; it does not certify completion. Writing those two things apart keeps the ledger from giving an answer fuller than the facts during an inquiry.

## Set the Archive Number First

The information flow gets the data right, the compliance flow keeps the process on record, and the two flows meet at the same checkpoint. Fix three things before launch: one order number through both flows, one archive number at each of the six checkpoints, and every row stating trigger point, owner and status. The remaining interfaces can be connected later; nine interfaces log daily health, and adding one more provider does not change the shape of the ledger.

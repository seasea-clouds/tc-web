Costa Rican fresh melons are now approved for export to China. Announcement No. 174 of 2025 of the General Administration of Customs takes effect upon issuance. The melon's scientific name is Cucumis melo L.; only ten commercial varieties of thick-skinned melons are allowed, and orchards and packing houses must be reviewed by the Costa Rican side and approved and registered by the Chinese side.

Orchards implement GAP and IPM, monitor eight quarantine pests including the Mediterranean fruit fly, and conduct field monitoring every 15 days. Before export, a 2% sample is inspected and 0.5% of melons are cut open to check for fruit fly larvae; if fruit flies are found, exports to China are suspended.

Each consignment is accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate bearing the required English declaration, and boxes are marked in Chinese or English with information on export to China. If live fruit flies or bacterial fruit blotch bacteria are detected at entry, the consignment is returned, destroyed, or given quarantine treatment, and the relevant export eligibility is suspended.

In accordance with the relevant laws and regulations of China and the provisions on the phytosanitary requirements for the export of Costa Rican fresh melons to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (hereinafter referred to as the Chinese side) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock of Costa Rica (hereinafter referred to as the Costa Rican side), as of today, the import of Costa Rican fresh melons that meet the following relevant requirements is permitted.

I. Basis for Inspection and Quarantine

(1) Biosafety Law of the People's Republic of China;

(2) Law of the People's Republic of China on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine and its implementing regulations;

(3) Food Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing regulations;

(4) Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Inspection and Quarantine of Imported Fruits;

(5) Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Costa Rican Melons to China between the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock of Costa Rica.

II. Names of Commodities Permitted for Entry

Fresh melons (hereinafter referred to as melons), with the scientific name Cucumis melo L. and the English name Melon. Melons exported from Costa Rica to China are limited to thick-skinned melons, including commercial-grade fruits of the following varieties:

(1) Hami melon (Cantaloupe)

(2) Yellow honeydew melon (Yellow honey dew)

(3) Harper melon (Harper)

(4) Sweet spark melon (Sweet spark)

(5) MAG melon (MAG)

(6) Galia melon (Galia)

(7) Piel de Sapo melon (Piel de Sapo)

(8) White honeydew melon (White Honey Dew)

(9) Charantais melon (Charantais)

(10) Hami melon (Hami)

III. Approved Places of Origin

Melon-producing areas in Costa Rica.

IV. Approved Orchards and Packing Houses

Orchards and packing houses for melons exported to China must be reviewed by the Costa Rican side and approved and registered by the Chinese side. Registration information shall include the name, address, and identification code, so that accurate traceability can be ensured when exported goods do not comply with the relevant provisions of this Announcement. Before each annual export season, the Costa Rican side shall provide the Chinese side with the list of registered entities, which shall be published on the website of the General Administration of Customs after review and approval by the Chinese side.

V. List of Quarantine Pests of Concern to China

(1) Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata

(2) Passionvine mealybug Planococcus minor

(3) Melonworm Diaphania hyalinata

(4) Pickleworm Diaphania nitidalis

(5) Jack Beardsley mealybug Pseudococcus jackbeardsleyi

(6) American serpentine leafminer Liriomyza trifolii

(7) Bacterial fruit blotch Acidovorax citrulli

(8) Cucumber scab Cladosporium cucumerinum

VI. Pre-export Management

(1) Orchard management.

(1) Orchards producing melons for export to China shall, under the supervision of the Costa Rican side, establish sound quality management and traceability management systems, implement good agricultural practices (GAP), maintain orchard hygiene, promptly remove diseased melons and dead leaves and weeds, and implement integrated pest management (IPM), including control measures such as pest monitoring and surveys, chemical or biological control, and agricultural operations.

(2) Orchards producing melons for export to China shall, under the supervision of the Costa Rican side, monitor and comprehensively manage the quarantine pests of concern to China. Pest monitoring and control shall be implemented under the guidance of professional and technical personnel, who shall receive training from the Costa Rican side or its authorized bodies.

(3) Orchards producing melons for export to China shall keep records of pest monitoring surveys and prevention and control, and shall provide them to the Chinese side upon request. Chemical control records shall include detailed information such as the names, active ingredients, application dates, and concentrations of all chemical agents used during the growing season.

(4) The Costa Rican side shall establish a monitoring system for the Mediterranean fruit fly. During the growing and harvesting seasons of melons, sex attractants of the Mediterranean fruit fly shall be used, combined with systematic control measures such as fruit-cutting inspection, to effectively reduce the population density of the Mediterranean fruit fly (see the annex for specific procedures). Before the export season, the Costa Rican side shall provide the monitoring records and reports to the Chinese side.

(5) Before melons are planted, seed health testing shall be carried out to ensure that melon seeds do not carry bacterial fruit blotch or cucumber scab. For bacterial fruit blotch, during the melon growing season, surveys shall be conducted for symptoms of infection such as nearly round, dark green sunken lesions or brown withered and yellow-brown lesions on leaves, white slimy bacterial exudate and white bacterial gum secreted on the underside of lesions, and small water-soaked lesions or fruit rot on fruits. For cucumber scab, surveys shall be conducted for chlorotic, nearly round small spots on leaves, whether they perforate, whether the edges of the holes are star-shaped, or whether there are dark green sunken spots on fruits, greyish-black mould layers, and scab-like lesions. If any of the above symptoms are found, samples shall be promptly sent to the relevant plant pathology laboratory for testing and identification. If bacterial fruit blotch or cucumber scab is confirmed, exports of melons from the relevant orchards to China shall be suspended for the current export season, and the Costa Rican side shall notify the Chinese side of the relevant situation.

(6) For other pests such as the passionvine mealybug, melonworm, pickleworm, Jack Beardsley mealybug and American serpentine leafminer, field monitoring surveys shall be conducted during the melon growing season, from germination to harvest, at least once every 15 days, and specimens of diseased and insect-damaged plants with suspicious symptoms shall be collected and sent to a laboratory for testing and identification. Orchards shall take necessary control measures to ensure that the above pests are controlled during melon growth and do not cause damage to the fruit.

(2) Packing house management.

(1) The processing, packing, storage and shipment of melons for export to China shall be carried out under the quarantine supervision of the Costa Rican side or its authorized personnel. Packing houses for melons exported to China shall have good sanitary conditions and measures to prevent re-infestation by pests (such as insect-proof mesh).

(2) During the processing and packing of melons for export to China, procedures such as manual selection, fungicide dipping, air-gun blowing and brushing of the fruit surface shall be carried out to ensure freedom from insects, mites, and branches, leaves, roots and soil, and rotten and deformed fruits shall be removed.

(3) Packing requirements.

(1) Packing materials for melons exported to China shall be clean, sanitary, unused, and comply with the relevant phytosanitary and safety and hygiene requirements of China. If wood packaging is used, it shall comply with the requirements of International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (ISPM 15).

(2) If the outer packaging of melons exported to China has ventilation holes, it shall be covered with insect-proof mesh (with mesh openings smaller than 1.6 mm). Packed melons shall be kept fresh by appropriate means and stored separately to avoid infestation by pests. If melons are vacuum-packed in plastic bags, insect-proof mesh is not required for the boxes.

(3) Each box shall be marked in Chinese or English with the name of the fruit, the exporting country, the place of origin, and the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house, and each box and pallet shall be marked in Chinese or English with „For export to the People’s Republic of China“ or „Exported to the People’s Republic of China“.

(4) Pre-export inspection and quarantine.

(1) The Costa Rican side shall conduct a sampling inspection of each consignment of melons for export to China at a rate of 2% per consignment, and conduct fruit-cutting inspection on 0.5% of the melons to ensure that they are free from Mediterranean fruit fly larvae.

(2) If the Mediterranean fruit fly is found, exports of Costa Rican melons to China shall be suspended, and the Costa Rican side shall consult with the Chinese side on the revision of the phytosanitary requirements.

(3) If other quarantine pests of concern to China are found, the consignment shall not be exported to China, and exports of melons from the relevant orchards and packing houses shall be suspended until the Costa Rican side or its authorized personnel have identified the cause and taken improvement measures. At the same time, interception records shall be kept and provided to the Chinese side upon request.

(5) Phytosanitary certificate requirements.

(1) For melons that pass quarantine, the Costa Rican side shall issue a phytosanitary certificate in accordance with International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 12 (ISPM12), indicating the name or registration number of the orchard and packing house, and shall state in the additional declaration: „This consignment of melons complies with the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Costa Rican Melons to China, and is free from any quarantine pests of concern to China.“ (This consignment complies with the requirements of the Protocol of Phytosanitary Requirements for Export of Costa Rican Melons to China and is free from quarantine pests of concern to China.)

(2) Before trade formally begins, the Costa Rican side shall provide the Chinese side with a specimen of the phytosanitary certificate for record and verification.

VII. Entry Inspection and Quarantine and Treatment of Non-compliance

When melons exported to China arrive at the entry port in China, China Customs shall conduct inspection and quarantine in accordance with the following requirements.

(1) Verification of certificates and marks.

(1) Verify whether the imported melons have obtained the Entry Animal and Plant Quarantine Permit.

(2) Verify whether the phytosanitary certificate complies with the provisions of Item (5) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(3) Verify whether the marks on the boxes and pallets comply with the provisions of Item (3) of Article VI of this Announcement.

(2) Entry inspection and quarantine.

(1) Melons exported to China shall enter through ports approved by the Chinese side for the import of fruits.

(2) Inspection and quarantine shall be conducted on melons exported to China in accordance with relevant laws, administrative regulations, rules and other provisions; those that pass inspection and quarantine shall be allowed entry.

(3) Treatment of non-compliance.

(1) If melons from unapproved orchards or packing houses are found, the consignment shall not be allowed entry.

(2) If live Mediterranean fruit flies are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed, or subjected to quarantine treatment. At the same time, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the Costa Rican side, suspend exports of Costa Rican melons to China, and consult with the Costa Rican side on the revision of the phytosanitary requirements.

(3) If bacterial fruit blotch is found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed, or subjected to quarantine treatment. At the same time, the Chinese side shall immediately notify the Costa Rican side, suspend exports of melons from the relevant orchards or packing houses to China, and, as appropriate, suspend the entire programme. The Costa Rican side shall conduct an investigation, identify the cause and implement appropriate improvement measures to prevent a recurrence. The Chinese side shall evaluate the improvement measures taken by the Costa Rican side and decide, based on the evaluation results, whether to lift the suspension measures already taken.

(4) If other quarantine pests of concern to China or quarantine pests newly discovered by the Costa Rican side are found, the consignment shall be returned, destroyed, or given quarantine treatment. The Chinese side shall notify the Costa Rican side and shall conduct a joint investigation, suspending exports of Costa Rican melons to China as appropriate until both sides complete a pest risk analysis or reach agreement on the revision of the phytosanitary requirements.

(5) If the goods are found not to comply with China's food safety laws, regulations and national standards, the consignment shall be returned or destroyed.

It is hereby announced.

General Administration of Customs

22 August 2025

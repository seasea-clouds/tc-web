A first bonded year comes down to one line of arithmetic: out of every 100 yuan of GMV, fees, fulfillment and first-leg freight, taxes, and commission take 37.1 yuan, leaving 62.9 yuan of contribution margin. Cover fixed investment with that margin and you have the payback period. The three scenarios change the inputs, not the arithmetic.

## Three tiers, starting with SKU boundaries

The conservative tier holds 3 to 5 SKUs and puts validation and word of mouth first. Run one SKU end to end, then move to the baseline tier once the data comes back; watch conversion and repeat purchase before the other two items.

The baseline tier holds 8 to 12 SKUs and aims at near break-even within year one. The parameter sheet stays the same, only the inputs change, and the tier follows the validation results; the net-ratio model plugs straight in.

The optimistic tier holds all SKUs and relies on volume to dilute fixed costs. Scale only after all-SKU stocking and the promotion system are in place; the sixteen pre-launch preparations are the precondition. Moving between tiers is not a judgment call: each tier has entry conditions, and you stay in the current one until they are met.

## Three assumptions you fill in

The model needs only three assumptions from you: average order value, repeat rate, and the promotion commission cap. We provide the structure and the tools; the numbers come from your parameters, delivered as an interactive model sheet with no preset targets.

The model chain has six links. Input assumptions turn SKU count, average order value and repeat purchases into GMV; each tier computes its own sales total; four deductions come off, namely consolidated tax, platform commission, fulfillment, and promotion commission; GMV minus those four is contribution margin; fixed investment covers system integration, bulk first-leg amortization and ledger upkeep; cumulative margin covering fixed investment gives the payback period.

You run the chain yourself. Change any one assumption and everything downstream moves; the method sits in the open, which is what makes it safe to take into an internal approval.

## Every 100 yuan of GMV, 37.1 yuan in deductions

Margin composition per 100 yuan of GMV: fees 6.0 yuan, fulfillment and first leg 7.0 yuan, taxes 9.1 yuan, commission 15.0 yuan, for 37.1 yuan in total and 62.9 yuan of contribution margin. The five segments sum to 100 percent; the structure is the same across tiers, and the difference lies in magnitude and fixed-cost dilution.

The 9.1 percent in the tax segment is the combined tax burden on cross-border e-commerce retail imports: the tariff rate is provisionally zero, and import VAT and consumption tax are levied at 70 percent of the statutory amount. It is a number computed from the whole tax structure, so do not add a single tax rate to it directly.

Deductions scale with order volume. Double the volume and all four move in proportion, and so does contribution margin; fixed investment does not move with volume, which is why scale dilutes the fixed side. The shares shown here are illustrative, not a quotation, and not a promise of returns.

## Walk the conservative tier

Take the conservative tier through one calculation. Three SKUs, 25 orders per SKU per month, about 900 orders a year; at an average order value of 300 yuan, annual GMV is 270,000 yuan. At 37.1 percent, the four deductions total 100,170 yuan: fees 16,200 yuan, fulfillment and first leg 18,900 yuan, taxes 24,570 yuan, commission 40,500 yuan.

Contribution margin is 169,830 yuan. Estimate fixed investment at 150,000 yuan, covering system integration, bulk first-leg amortization and ledger upkeep; monthly contribution margin is about 14,153 yuan, cumulative margin covers fixed investment around month 11, and payback lands within year one. Change the average order value or the repeat rate and the result moves; you fill the parameters, we keep the method unchanged.

## Three billing methods

Per order: settled by order count; it suits stages with forecastable order volume and a stable SKU mix.

Share of GMV: settled as a share of sales; it suits the scale-up phase, where larger volume dilutes unit cost.

Monthly retainer plus per order: a fixed retainer covers baseline staffing and systems, and the excess stacks per order, so the budget can be set in advance.

All three methods are quoted against five factors: order volume, SKU count, speed requirements, settlement currency, and value-added services. Change the five and the quotation structure changes with them.

## Four inputs on your side

People: one to two finance and compliance staff, part-time to start, plus one business contact; a part-time start adds no permanent headcount. Needed at kickoff, in parallel with the S0 startup stage.

Funds: first stocking sized to pilot SKUs and two to three months of turnover, paid media in small steps, and customs duty security arranged per port. In place before stocking and listing.

Documents: product, testing, qualification and brand files in checklists, completed item by item at admission nodes; the four checklist templates come with the S0 deliverables.

Time: three decisions, namely entity path, target port and claims strategy, directly affect the schedule; settle them before S0 to lock the schedule, and the work moves into the M1 execution stage once they are in place.

Staffing and funds are practical ranges; the quotation follows measured conditions, the formal quotation is issued once documents are complete, and the rate detail appears only there.
